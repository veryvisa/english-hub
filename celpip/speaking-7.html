CELPIP 9+ · ≈C1 189 words词

Yes, I firmly believe grocery stores should charge for plastic bags, and I'd like to give three reasons.

First and foremost, it cuts down on waste. A plastic bag is used for maybe ten minutes, yet it can linger in a landfill for centuries. Even a small fee makes people stop and ask themselves whether they really need one, and that alone dramatically reduces how many are handed out.

Secondly, it changes habits remarkably quickly. Here in Vancouver, many stores already charge, and within a week of the rule coming in, I'd trained myself to keep a cloth bag in the car and another in my backpack. These days I hardly ever forget, and it's become second nature.

Thirdly, the cost to shoppers is trivial. At around ten cents a bag, it's hardly a burden on anyone's budget, and stores can put that revenue towards greener alternatives like paper bags.

Admittedly, it can be a nuisance when you forget your bags, and it's happened to me too. But that minor inconvenience is a small price to pay for a cleaner environment. That's why I'm in favour of the charge.

Notes on this prompt这道题的讲解
  • The first sentence gives your position plus "three reasons", so the listener has a frame in mind, and Content/Coherence in the score benefits.第一句立场＋「三个理由」，听的人心里有框架，评分里的 Content/Coherence 会受益。
  • One sentence like "Some people say ..." acknowledges the other side, and then you return to your conclusion. It shows you thought about both sides.Some people say … 一句承认反方，再回到结论，显得想过两边。
  • Common mistake: saying "three reasons" but giving only two. Don't get the number wrong. If you're not sure, say "a few reasons".常见错误：three reasons 说了却只讲两个。数字不要说错；不确定就说 a few reasons。

Yes, I'm strongly in favour of letting people work from home two days a week, for three main reasons.

First, it gives people back time and money. Across Metro Vancouver, it's common to spend two hours a day stuck in traffic or on transit. Cutting that out twice a week means an extra hour of sleep or a proper home-cooked lunch, and the savings on gas and fares can easily run to hundreds of dollars a year for a family.

Second, it makes for a far healthier work-life balance. Parents can actually be there when their kids get home from school, and stress levels drop. In my experience, people who feel trusted and less stretched tend to be more productive, not less, and companies can trim their office costs on top of that.

Third, two days strikes the right balance. With three days still in the office, teamwork and face-to-face meetings don't suffer; they simply get scheduled more deliberately.

Of course, some people feel isolated working from home, and that's a fair concern. But overall, I'm convinced the benefits far outweigh the drawbacks.

Second, your gut often notices things a list can't. On paper, a new job might look perfect, but if the manager made you uncomfortable in the interview, that feeling is information too.

So the best approach is to use both. Make the list first, ideally on paper, then ask yourself how you feel about the result. If the two agree, go ahead. If they don't, take more time and find out why.

That way, you're much less likely to regret a decision you made too quickly.

To my mind, it isn't really an either-or question. The smartest approach is to lead with a list of pros and cons, then let your gut have the final check.

The case for the list is that big decisions come with big emotions. Walking into a house with a gorgeous kitchen, it's all too easy to gloss over a ninety-minute commute or steep strata fees. Writing things down drags those facts into the light.

That said, a gut feeling is often your brain picking up on patterns you can't yet put into words. A job can look flawless on paper, yet if something about the manager left you uneasy, that unease is data worth taking seriously.

So I'd weigh up the list first, and then ask how the outcome actually feels. When the two line up, go for it. When they clash, that's your cue to dig deeper before signing anything.

It won't guarantee a good outcome, but it does make regret far less likely, and that's worth a lot.

Strategy策略

  • Read carefully and stay on topic; yes or no is fine.先看清楚问题，别跑题。Yes 或 No 都行，选你更容易讲的。
  • About three reasons, each with a detail.大约三个理由，每个理由配具体细节。
  • Use transitions to signal new ideas.用 First / In addition / Finally 串起来。

Tips提醒

  • Don't agonize over your position. Pick the side where it is easier for you to give examples.别纠结立场，选你更容易举例的那边。
  • Keep the reasons "separate": don't give three reasons that are really about the same thing.理由要「分开」：不要三个理由其实说的是同一件事。
  • 90 seconds is not short: about 25 seconds per reason, plus the opening and ending.90 秒不短：每个理由大约 25 秒，加上开头和结尾。

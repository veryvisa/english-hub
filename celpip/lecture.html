📝 Why train notes this way为什么这样练笔记
Part 6 of the test and most real lectures follow the same plan: a claim, reasons with examples, an objection, and a conclusion. Train your notes on that plan and you stop writing down every word.思培第 6 部分和大多数真实讲座都按同一个骨架走：观点、理由加例子、反方、结论。按这个骨架记笔记，就不会再想把每个词都写下来。
  1. Look at the note frame below. Do not read the text yet.先看下面的笔记框，先别看原文。
  2. Press play once. Write key words only, in English, in the frame.只播放一遍。在框里用英文写关键词。
  3. Answer the six questions from your notes.凭笔记答六道题。
  4. Then compare with the model notes and read the text.再对照参考笔记、读原文。

📝 Note frame笔记框

Topic话题wider roads → traffic?
Speaker's claim讲者观点wider road fills again in a few yrs
Reason 1 + example理由一＋例子time not distance: bus users / quiet-hour drivers switch back
Reason 2 + example理由二＋例子where people live: 40→20 min commute, families move out, drive in
Reason 3 + example理由三＋例子new trips: extra shopping, longer drives
Objection and answer反方与回应city growing → need roads? fair, but building alone can't keep up
Conclusion结论manage demand: transit + peak price + homes near jobs

✅ Questions题目

  1. What is the speaker's main claim?
    Answer答案: Wider roads in busy cities soon fill up again
    She says a wider road usually fills up again within a few years. She also says she is not against all building, so A is too strong.她说拓宽的路几年内通常又会堵满；她也说并不反对所有修路，所以 A 说过头了。
  2. According to the first reason, why do people switch back to driving?
    Answer答案: The trip becomes faster
    "Drivers respond to time, not to distance. When a trip gets faster…"原话的意思是司机看的是时间而不是距离：路程变快了，人就回来开车。
  3. What example does the speaker give for the second reason?
    Answer答案: A commute that drops from forty to twenty minutes
    The numbers forty and twenty are the example; listen for the change, not just one number.例子就是四十分钟变二十分钟；要听的是「变化」，不是单独一个数。
  4. Which kind of trip is part of the third reason?
    Answer答案: A second visit to the shops
    "A second visit to the shops" is one of the extra trips that did not happen before.「再去一趟商店」是以前不会有的额外出行之一。
  5. How does the speaker treat the objection that the city is growing?
    Answer答案: She accepts part of it but says building is not enough
    "That is a fair point… building alone does not keep up." Partial agreement is a common Part 6 answer.「这一点有道理……但光靠修路跟不上。」部分同意是第 6 部分常考的态度。
  6. Which is NOT one of the three things in her conclusion?
    Answer答案: More parking downtown
    She lists transport, a peak-hour price and homes near work. Parking is never mentioned: a classic "not said" option.她列的是公交、高峰收费、住在工作附近。停车场一句没提——典型的「没说过」干扰项。
Text of the lecture讲座原文

Good evening, everyone. Tonight I want to look at a question that sounds simple: if a road is always full, why not just make it wider?

Most people assume that more lanes means less traffic. My claim tonight is the opposite. In busy cities, a wider road usually fills up again within a few years, and I want to give you three reasons why.

The first reason is that drivers respond to time, not to distance. When a trip gets faster, people who used to take the bus, or travel at a quieter hour, switch back to driving at the busiest time. The road is quicker for a while, and then it is crowded again.

The second reason is that new roads change where people choose to live. If a commute from a distant suburb suddenly takes twenty minutes instead of forty, more families move out there, and every one of them drives in on the same road.

The third reason is trips that never happened before. When driving becomes easy, people make extra trips: a second visit to the shops, a longer drive to a cheaper store. None of these were on the road before the widening.

Now, some of you are thinking: but the city is growing, so surely we need more road space. That is a fair point, and I am not saying we should never build. What I am saying is that building alone does not keep up.

So what does work? The cities that have done best combine three things: frequent public transport, a price on driving at the busiest hours, and homes built close to where people work. In short, you manage the demand instead of chasing it.

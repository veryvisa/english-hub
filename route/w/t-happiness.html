👂 Thursday: listen first, then read周四：先听懂，再看文字

“The Science of Connection” is a text you have not read yet.「人际联结的科学」：这一篇你还没读过。 Three steps: listen without the text, answer, say the main points. Then open the text to check and shadow it.三步：不看文字听，答题，说出要点；然后打开原文核对并跟读。

1 · Answer without looking不看文字答题

1 What distinction do researchers often make?

The article explains that having many contacts is not the same as having meaningful relationships.文章解释了拥有许多联系人与拥有有意义的人际关系并不是一回事。

2 According to the article, what makes a relationship meaningful?

These three qualities are explicitly identified as important elements of meaningful relationships.文章明确指出，信任、相互尊重和情感投入是有意义的人际关系的重要组成部分。

2 · Say the main points说出要点

3 · Now look at the text再看原文

📄 Show the text显示原文

- Imagine receiving wonderful news but having nobody to share it with. The achievement might still feel satisfying, but something would be missing. Human beings are social creatures, and our understanding of a good life is closely connected to the quality of our relationships. Friends, family members, colleagues, and neighbours can all contribute to a sense of belonging, although the importance of each relationship varies from person to person. - Researchers studying well-being often distinguish between the number of social contacts and the quality of social connections. Having hundreds of online contacts does not necessarily mean having people who will listen when life becomes difficult. A meaningful relationship involves trust, mutual respect, and emotional availability. Such connections tend to make stressful experiences easier to manage and may encourage healthier habits through everyday support. - Social connection does not require constant communication. A brief conversation with a neighbour, a shared meal, or a thoughtful message can strengthen a relationship. What matters is the quality of attention we give to others. While technology makes communication easier, it can also encourage superficial interaction if people become more interested in displaying their lives than understanding one another. Meaningful relationships require presence, not merely visibility. - Building strong connections also involves accepting differences. People do not always share the same values, interests, or expectations. Healthy relationships allow disagreement without turning every difference into a conflict. They also require boundaries, because caring for others should not mean ignoring one's own needs. On the whole, a good social life is not about knowing the greatest number of people. It is about maintaining relationships in which individuals can give support and receive it in return.

🆚 After your first draft: the same content written three ways写完第一稿再看：同一个内容的三种写法

Plain平实版

I think Maya should move to the coastal town. She has a good job, but money is not everything. She wants a quieter life, and this is important. She may have less work and fewer friends there, but she can enjoy more free time. A happy life needs time for family, hobbies and rest. She can also look for new work. I think she should try it.

B2B2 版

I would support Maya's decision to move, provided that she has a realistic plan for finding work and building a social life in the coastal town. A good salary can make life comfortable, but it does not necessarily make someone happy. If her current job leaves her with little time to relax or spend with her partner, a quieter environment could improve her everyday life. She might have more opportunities to enjoy outdoor activities, develop hobbies and strengthen personal relationships. These things are not simply extras; they are an important part of long-term well-being. However, moving also involves risks. She could feel isolated, and finding a suitable job might take longer than expected. Rather than treating the move as a permanent decision immediately, Maya could spend some time in the town first. This would allow both partners to judge whether the slower lifestyle genuinely suits them, rather than just imagining it.

C1C1 版

On balance, I would encourage Maya to move, provided that the decision is supported by careful planning rather than an idealised picture of coastal life. Her well-paid position offers security, but its value depends partly on how she can use her time. If work consistently takes priority over rest, relationships and personal interests, a higher income may bring diminishing returns in terms of happiness. A less demanding environment could give her greater scope to cultivate friendships, pursue outdoor interests and spend meaningful time with her partner. These are not merely pleasant additions to an otherwise successful life; they contribute to a broader sense of fulfilment. That said, the attractions of a quieter town should not obscure its practical limitations. Reduced employment prospects and a smaller social circle could create frustrations that are difficult to anticipate from a distance. Maya would therefore benefit from experiencing the town before committing to a permanent relocation. Such a trial, which would give both partners a clearer picture of everyday life there, could help them distinguish a genuine improvement in well-being from the temporary appeal of escaping a demanding routine.

What changes from one to the next每升一档换了什么

  • Plain states only that 'money is not everything', while B2 develops this into a fuller contrast: 'A good salary can make life comfortable, but it does not necessarily make someone happy'.基础版只说'money is not everything'（钱不是一切），B2 版把它展开为更完整的对比：'A good salary can make life comfortable, but it does not necessarily make someone happy'（高薪能让生活舒适，但不一定能带来幸福）。
  • B2 only asks 'whether the slower lifestyle genuinely suits them', while C1 sharpens this into the more precise distinction between 'a genuine improvement in well-being' and 'the temporary appeal of escaping a demanding routine'.B2 只问'whether the slower lifestyle genuinely suits them'（这种更慢的生活方式是否真的适合他们），C1 把它精细化为更明确的区分：'a genuine improvement in well-being'（真正的幸福感提升）与'the temporary appeal of escaping a demanding routine'（暂时逃离繁忙生活的吸引力）之间的不同。

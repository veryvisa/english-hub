👂 Thursday: listen first, then read周四：先听懂，再看文字

“Why Heat Waves Hit Older People Hardest” is a text you have not read yet.「热浪为什么最伤老人」：这一篇你还没读过。 Three steps: listen without the text, answer, say the main points. Then open the text to check and shadow it.三步：不看文字听，答题，说出要点；然后打开原文核对并跟读。

1 · Answer without looking不看文字答题

1 Who is most at risk in a long heat wave, according to the text?

older people alone at home, in upper-floor apartments.独自在家、住高层的老人。

2 Why is the night so dangerous?

the body never gets a chance to recover.身体一直没有机会恢复。

3 Which is correct?

at home is a fixed phrase without an article.at home 是固定说法，不加冠词。

2 · Say the main points说出要点

3 · Now look at the text再看原文

📄 Show the text显示原文

- In a long heat wave, the people most at risk are rarely the ones you see on the news fainting at an outdoor concert. They are usually older people alone at home, in upper-floor apartments without air conditioning or good ventilation, where the temperature indoors keeps climbing for days after it has started to fall outside. - Age changes the body in ways that make heat more dangerous. Older people sweat less, so the body cools itself less effectively. Many feel thirst later, so they drink less than they need. Some common medicines for blood pressure and the heart can also affect how the body handles heat. None of this is visible from the outside, and many people do not feel unwell until they are seriously ill. - The most dangerous part is often the night. If the air does not cool down after sunset, the body never gets a chance to recover. The first hot day is uncomfortable; the third and fourth nights in a row, during a long hot spell, are when emergency rooms fill up. - Cities have learned from the worst summers. Many now open cooling centres in libraries and community centres, extend the hours of public pools and send volunteers to check on people who live alone. Some keep a list of residents who have asked for a call during extreme weather. The most effective measure, however, is the same in every city: someone who knows you and notices when something is wrong. - So if an older relative or neighbour lives alone, a short visit or call each day of a heat wave matters more than any advice on the news. Ask whether their home is cooler than the street, and whether they have somewhere to go. It may be the only check they get that day.

🆚 After your first draft: the same content written three ways写完第一稿再看：同一个内容的三种写法

Plain平实版

I support the Saturday car ban in our town square. Cars make the air dirty and cars can be loud. A car-free market will make the square a nicer place for people. Families and older people can walk more safely. Shops might lose customers at first. The council should test the ban for three months. It should help shops and ask people for their ideas.

B2B2 版

I support the town council’s plan to make the central market square car-free on Saturdays, although I understand why nearby shop owners are worried. A cleaner, quieter square would be more pleasant for families and older residents, and people could move around safely while the market is open. However, some shoppers may choose another shopping area if they cannot park nearby. The council could test the plan for three months and provide extra bus stops or better parking close to the square. It should collect feedback from traders and residents, then keep or change the trial based on the results. This gives local businesses a chance to benefit rather than forcing them to accept a permanent decision immediately. It should explain each decision openly.

C1C1 版

I support the town council’s plan to make the central market square car-free on Saturdays, although I appreciate the concern among nearby shop owners. A cleaner, quieter square would become a more pleasant shared space for families and older residents, while allowing people to move safely while the market is open. However, some shoppers may shift to another shopping area if convenient parking is unavailable. The council could therefore trial the restriction for three months, improving bus access and arranging parking close to the square. By collecting feedback from traders and residents, it could decide whether to retain or revise the measure according to its results. Rather than imposing a permanent change, this approach would give local businesses time to adjust and the community a chance to judge the real effects.

What changes from one to the next每升一档换了什么

  • B2 keeps the same support as plain, but adds a concession that plain never makes — 'although I understand why nearby shop owners are worried' — which makes the argument sound more balanced without changing the underlying position.B2 保留了与平实版相同的支持立场，但加上了平实版完全没有的让步——“although I understand why nearby shop owners are worried”（我理解附近店主为什么会担心）——这让论证显得更平衡，但立场本身没有变。
  • B2's general benefit, 'more pleasant for families and older residents', becomes C1's more specific 'a more pleasant shared space for families and older residents' — adding the shared-space framing recasts what sounded like a private comfort as a civic one, without changing the underlying claim.B2 泛泛的好处“more pleasant for families and older residents”（对家庭和老年居民更宜人），到 C1 变成更具体的“a more pleasant shared space for families and older residents”（对家庭和老年居民更宜人的共享空间）——加上“共享空间”这个框架，把原本像是私人舒适的好处，重新表述成了公共性的好处，论点本身没有变。

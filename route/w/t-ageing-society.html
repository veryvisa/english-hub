1 How do officials describe the change in towns?

The text says officials describe the change as gradual but relentless.文中说官员们形容这一变化缓慢却无情。

2 In the phrase ‘ease the pressure on’, the word ‘ease’ is closest in meaning to:

To ease pressure is to make it less severe, so ‘reduce’ fits.ease the pressure 指减轻压力，因此 reduce 最贴切。

2 · Say the main points说出要点

3 · Now look at the text再看原文

📄 Show the text显示原文

- Local councils across several rich countries are warning that an ageing population is reshaping towns faster than planners expected. A new survey of regional authorities suggests that schools are merging, bus routes are shrinking and care homes are struggling to recruit staff. Officials describe the change as gradual but relentless. Longer lives are part of the story, but falling birth rates matter just as much. Several have asked for emergency funds. - The strain is most visible in health services. Hospitals report that a growing share of beds is occupied by patients who are medically fit to leave but have nowhere suitable to go. Family doctors say appointments take longer because older patients tend to have several conditions at once. Staff shortages make matters worse. While nobody disputes the value of long life, many managers admit that budgets were not designed with it in mind. - Some places are responding creatively. One coastal town has converted an empty school into flats for older residents, with a nursery on the ground floor. Another has trained volunteers to check on neighbours who live alone. Early results suggest that younger families are also moving in, drawn by cheaper housing. Such schemes cannot solve the problem alone, but they ease the pressure on overstretched services. - Central government has promised a review of how care is funded, though it has not said when it will report. Opposition members have called the delay unacceptable. Campaigners argue that, by and large, ministers have been slow to accept that the country must shoulder the burden together. Meanwhile, residents in the worst-affected villages are organising their own transport. Councils say they will keep collecting evidence.

🆚 After your first draft: the same content written three ways写完第一稿再看：同一个内容的三种写法

Plain平实版

I think Greenfield should make this change. Older people need safe places to sit and rest. Some people cannot walk for a long time, so benches are very helpful. Shaded areas are also good in hot weather. The library is a place for everyone, not only young people. I understand that shop owners want more parking spaces. Customers may find it harder to visit their shops. But the town should also think about older residents. Families can use these spaces too. Children and parents can sit together and enjoy the area. I think a few parking spaces can be removed. The town should listen to local people and make a good plan.

B2B2 版

I support Greenfield's plan to replace some parking spaces near the library with benches, shaded areas and better access for older residents. Public spaces should serve people at different stages of life, and this change would make the library area more welcoming to elderly visitors. Some older people may avoid visiting if walking long distances or finding somewhere to rest is difficult. Families would also benefit from having a comfortable place to spend time together. However, the shop owners have a reasonable concern: reducing parking could make shopping less convenient, particularly for customers with limited mobility. The town should therefore avoid removing too many spaces at once. It could consult residents and businesses before deciding how much parking to retain. A small pilot scheme would also let the town see how much difference the changes actually make before committing further funds. On balance, improving access and comfort is worth the investment, provided that the needs of local businesses are considered as well.

C1C1 版

Greenfield should proceed with the proposed improvements, although the number of parking spaces removed deserves careful consideration. A library is more than a destination for borrowing books; it is a shared public resource whose surroundings should accommodate residents with different physical abilities and everyday needs. For older people, the availability of seating, shade and accessible walking routes can determine whether a visit feels manageable or becomes an exhausting undertaking. Families, too, would gain a more pleasant place to pause and spend time together. The shop owners' objections should not be dismissed, since convenient parking may influence where customers choose to shop, particularly when carrying heavy purchases or travelling with someone who has mobility difficulties. Rather than treating accessibility and commercial convenience as competing priorities, the town could assess parking demand and retain sufficient spaces while redesigning the surrounding area. Such consultation would also give businesses an opportunity to identify practical concerns. On balance, the investment is justified: a town's public spaces should reflect the needs of its ageing population, without unnecessarily excluding other users.

What changes from one to the next每升一档换了什么

  • The plain version says only that older people 'need safe places to sit and rest', while the B2 version expresses the broader principle that 'Public spaces should serve people at different stages of life'.基础版只说老年人'need safe places to sit and rest'（需要可以坐下休息的安全地方），而 B2 版把它提升为更广的原则：'Public spaces should serve people at different stages of life'（公共空间应该服务于处于不同人生阶段的人）。
  • B2 only says reduced parking could 'make shopping less convenient', while C1 develops this into the more specific claim that it may 'influence where customers choose to shop'.B2 只说停车位减少会'make shopping less convenient'（让购物更不方便），C1 把它展开为更具体的说法：这会'influence where customers choose to shop'（影响顾客选择去哪家店购物）。

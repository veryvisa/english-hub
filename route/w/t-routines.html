2 · Say the main points说出要点

3 · Now look at the text再看原文

📄 Show the text显示原文

- More people are paying attention to their morning routines as they look for ways to manage busy lives. Some begin the day by checking messages, while others prefer a quiet breakfast or a short walk. Although there is no single perfect routine, health and lifestyle experts often suggest starting the morning with activities that support both physical and mental well-being. - A simple morning routine does not need to take much time. Getting up at a regular time, drinking a glass of water, and preparing a healthy breakfast are useful starting points. People who prepare their clothes and bags the night before may also find that their mornings become less stressful. These small decisions can save time and reduce unnecessary pressure. - However, changing several habits at once can be difficult. A better approach is to introduce one new activity and repeat it until it feels natural. For example, someone who usually stays in bed until the last minute might try getting up just ten minutes earlier. After a few weeks, this change may become part of a more comfortable daily routine. - The most successful morning routine is not necessarily the most complicated one. It should fit a person's working hours, family responsibilities, and energy levels. Some people enjoy exercise before breakfast, while others feel better after eating. The important point is to choose realistic activities and make enough room for them in everyday life.

🆚 After your first draft: the same content written three ways写完第一稿再看：同一个内容的三种写法

Plain平实版

I think the new morning meeting is a good idea, but it must be short. At my office, the team does not talk much now. So a meeting every day can help us. We can say what we are doing and ask for help. I understand my colleagues who like quiet mornings. They want to make a plan for the day first. That is important too. But the meeting is only 15 minutes. After it, they still have time to plan. I like to know what other people are doing. It makes my work easier. I want the meeting to stay short. If it gets long, people will be unhappy, and the rule will not work.

B2B2 版

On balance, I think the new 8:30 meeting is a sensible rule, provided it really stays at fifteen minutes. Our team has drifted apart lately, and a short daily check-in gives everyone a chance to say what they are working on and ask for help before small problems grow. I understand why some colleagues are unhappy, since a quiet start helps them plan priorities. However, the meeting is brief, so they can still use the rest of the morning for that. The real risk is that the meeting slowly becomes longer and less focused, and then people will resent it. To prevent that, I would suggest speaking for only a minute each and reviewing the rule after a month.

C1C1 版

On balance, I would back the new 8:30 stand-up, provided it genuinely stays at fifteen minutes. Our team has drifted apart lately, and a brief daily check-in lets people flag difficulties before they snowball, which is precisely what scattered colleagues tend to miss. I sympathise with those who prize a quiet start, since planning priorities does require uninterrupted thought; yet the meeting leaves most of the morning untouched, so the loss is smaller than it first appears. The danger lies in gradual overrun: once updates swell, goodwill evaporates and the ritual becomes a chore. To forestall that, I would cap each person at about a minute and revisit the arrangement after a month, adjusting it if quieter members still feel squeezed.

What changes from one to the next每升一档换了什么

  • The plain 'a good idea, but it must be short' becomes a single sentence with the adjective 'sensible' and a condition introduced by 'provided it really stays'.简单版的 'a good idea, but it must be short' 在B2版中变成带条件从句 'provided it really stays at fifteen minutes' 的一个句子，并用 'sensible' 替换了 'good'。
  • B2 flags the risk in a plain verb phrase ('the meeting slowly becomes longer and less focused, and then people will resent it'), while C1 renames it as a noun ('gradual overrun') and spells out the mechanism with vivid verbs ('goodwill evaporates', 'the ritual becomes a chore').B2 用普通动词短语指出风险（'会议逐渐变长、变得不聚焦，大家就会反感'），C1 把它重新命名为名词短语 'gradual overrun'，并用更生动的动词说明机制（'goodwill evaporates' 善意蒸发、'the ritual becomes a chore' 仪式变成苦差事）。

“The Call That Was Not from the Bank” is a text you have not read yet.「那通不是银行打来的电话」：这一篇你还没读过。 Three steps: listen without the text, answer, say the main points. Then open the text to check and shadow it.三步：不看文字听，答题，说出要点；然后打开原文核对并跟读。

📄 Show the text显示原文

- It was nearly nine in the evening when Aunt Lin's phone rang. The screen showed the name of her bank. Her nephew Tom, who was staying with her that week, heard her side of the call and asked her to put it on speaker. - "Good evening, this is the fraud department. We have noticed a suspicious transaction on your card, a purchase made in another city an hour ago. To sort out the problem, I need to verify your identity. Could you read me the code we have just sent to your phone?" Aunt Lin reached for her phone. Tom shook his head. - "Sorry, could you tell me which branch you are calling from?" Tom asked. "Sir, this is urgent. If you hang up, the money will be lost." "Then I will deal with it myself," Tom said. "I am going to hang up and call the number on the back of the card." The caller tried once more, and then the line went dead. - The real bank, reached through that number ten minutes later, confirmed what Tom had figured out. There was no suspicious transaction, and the balance was exactly as it should be. The code on her phone was a password reset code; whoever got it could have taken over her account. The agent pointed out three things real banks do not do: they do not ask you to read out codes, they do not rush you, and they do not mind when you hang up and call back. Aunt Lin now checks every statement herself, and she has written those three lines on a card next to the phone.

🆚 After your first draft: the same content written three ways写完第一稿再看：同一个内容的三种写法

Note:注意： a sample written for this site, not yet read by a second reviewer.样板，本站自写，还没有过第二位审稿。

Plain平实版

I think you should get a credit card in your first month. A credit card is very important in Canada. You need it for many things. For example, you need it to rent a car and to buy things online. It also helps your credit score. A good credit score is very useful when you want to rent an apartment. But a credit card is dangerous too. Some people spend too much money. Then they must pay a lot of interest. So you should pay the bill every month. Don't buy things you can't pay for.

B2B2 版

In my view, a newcomer should apply for a credit card in the first month, as long as the card has no annual fee. The main reason is that a credit history takes time to build: landlords and phone companies often check your credit score, and without a card you have nothing to show them. A card also covers small emergencies before your first pay cheque arrives. However, there is a real risk. If you only make the minimum payment, the bank charges interest on the balance, and the debt grows quickly. Although the limit may look like extra money, it is a loan. My advice is simple: set up automatic payment in full, and treat the card as a way to build a record, not as income.

C1C1 版

Getting a credit card in your first month is, on balance, a sensible move, provided that you treat it as a way of building a record rather than as spare cash. What newcomers tend to underestimate is how much here depends on a credit history: a lease, a phone plan, even some background checks may turn partly on a score that takes months to build, so the sooner the record starts, the better. That said, the risk is not trivial. Interest is charged on any balance carried past the due date, which means that a few careless months can cost more than the card will ever save you. Much as the bank's limit may feel like income, it is debt waiting to happen. Paid in full and on time, however, a card costs nothing and quietly works in your favour.

What changes from one to the next每升一档换了什么

  • Plain states flatly that you should 'get a credit card in your first month', while B2 limits the same view with a condition: 'as long as the card has no annual fee'.平实版只是直接说应该'get a credit card in your first month'（第一个月就办信用卡），B2 版给同一个看法加上了条件：'as long as the card has no annual fee'（只要这张卡没有年费）。
  • B2 uses 'balance' to mean money owed in 'charges interest on the balance', while C1 reuses the same word in the idiom 'on balance, a sensible move', and hedges with 'tend to'.B2 里的 'balance' 指还欠着的钱，如 'charges interest on the balance'（对欠款收利息），C1 把同一个词用在习语里：'on balance, a sensible move'（总的来说是明智之举），并用 'tend to' 留有余地。
  • B2 explains ideas in separate sentences, while C1 packs them more tightly with a fronted structure such as 'What newcomers tend to underestimate is' at the start of a sentence.B2 把每个意思分开用句子说，C1 则用前置结构把信息压得更紧，比如句首的 'What newcomers tend to underestimate is'。

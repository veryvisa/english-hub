👂 Thursday: listen first, then read周四：先听懂，再看文字

“Don’t Let the Machine Take the Blame” is a text you have not read yet.「别让机器背黑锅」：这一篇你还没读过。 Three steps: listen without the text, answer, say the main points. Then open the text to check and shadow it.三步：不看文字听，答题，说出要点；然后打开原文核对并跟读。

1 · Answer without looking不看文字答题

1 What is the author’s main complaint about how we talk about technology?

The author says such phrasing quietly removes people from the picture.作者指出这种说法悄悄地把人从画面中抹去，让人显得不负责任。

2 What does ‘consent in name only’ mean?

’In name only’ describes something that exists officially but not in reality.“in name only”指名义上存在而实际上并非如此。

2 · Say the main points说出要点

3 · Now look at the text再看原文

📄 Show the text显示原文

- We have a habit of speaking about technology as though it had a will of its own. This is more than a quirk of language. The algorithm decided, the system failed, the software made a mistake. Such phrasing is convenient, but it quietly removes people from the picture, and that is precisely where the ethical danger lies. - Every tool reflects choices made by someone: which data to collect, which goals to optimise, which errors to tolerate. While it is true that modern systems can behave in ways their designers did not foresee, unpredictability is not an excuse. A company that releases a product it cannot fully explain has, by and large, accepted the risk that comes with it. - Consider consent. We click ‘agree’ hundreds of times a year without reading a word, and then we are told that we gave permission. This is consent in name only. Genuine consent requires that people understand what they are agreeing to and can refuse without losing access to basic services. - Nor should we imagine that regulation alone will solve the problem, however well it is drafted. Rules tend to lag behind invention, and a clever firm can usually find a way around them. That is why independent oversight matters, as does a culture in which engineers feel able to raise concerns without fear of punishment. - None of this means we should fear progress. Technology has, on the whole, made life longer and easier for millions of people around the world. But the benefits do not cancel the harms, and the people who profit most should be the first to be held accountable when things go wrong. The machine cannot take the blame; we must.

🆚 After your first draft: the same content written three ways写完第一稿再看：同一个内容的三种写法

Plain平实版

I do not want the face cameras at the front door. Someone took some bicycles, and that is bad. But the cameras will watch every neighbour every day. Some neighbours are old and some are shy. They will feel bad about being watched. Also, the company will keep our faces on a computer. Maybe other people can see them. I think a normal camera is enough. It can look at the storage room, not at our faces. We can also buy a better lock for the door. This is cheaper and simpler. So my answer is no to the face cameras. Let us try the lock and the normal camera first. Then we can talk again.

B2B2 版

I would oppose the facial-recognition cameras at our front door, at least until we have tried simpler measures. The theft of the bicycles is a real problem, and I understand why some neighbours want strong action. However, a system that scans every resident's face each day changes the feeling of the building, because people start to behave as if they are being judged. Unless the committee can show exactly where our faces are kept and who can see them, we are asking people to trust something they cannot check. Visitors who never agreed to this are affected too. The risk is that a small problem with the storage room leads to a permanent loss of privacy for everyone. A better first step would be a standard camera pointed at the storage room, together with a stronger lock and a sign-in sheet for the key. Although this is less impressive, it deals directly with the real problem. If the thefts continue after that, the committee could meet again and discuss something stronger.

C1C1 版

On balance, I would oppose facial recognition at our front door, at least until cheaper remedies have been tried. The stolen bicycles are a genuine grievance, and neighbours who want decisive action are hardly being unreasonable. Even so, a system that scans every resident's face daily alters the character of the building, since people tend to behave differently once they feel assessed. The storage of that data is a further worry: unless the committee can demonstrate where our faces are kept and who may view them, residents are being asked to take on trust something they have no means of verifying. Visitors, who never consented, are affected too. The response is out of proportion: a modest theft problem would be answered with a permanent loss of privacy for everyone. A conventional camera trained on the storage room, a sturdier lock and a signed key register would address the actual weakness, which is the unsupervised door. Should the thefts continue regardless, the committee could revisit the question with better evidence.

What changes from one to the next每升一档换了什么

  • The plain version only names a vague feeling ("feel bad about being watched"), while the B2 version explains the effect on behaviour using "start to" and "as if".初级版只说了一个笼统的感受（"feel bad about being watched"），B2 版则用 "start to" 和 "as if" 解释了这种监视对人们行为的实际影响。
  • B2's active "we are asking people to trust" becomes a passive with the agent dropped, "trust" becomes the fixed phrase "take on trust", and "cannot check" becomes the noun phrase "no means of verifying".B2 的主动句 "we are asking people to trust" 变成了省略施动者的被动句，"trust" 变成固定搭配 "take on trust"，"cannot check" 则变成名词化的 "no means of verifying"。

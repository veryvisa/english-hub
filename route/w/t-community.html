👂 Thursday: listen first, then read周四：先听懂，再看文字

“What the Snow Review Found” is a text you have not read yet.「暴风雪复盘发现了什么」：这一篇你还没读过。 Three steps: listen without the text, answer, say the main points. Then open the text to check and shadow it.三步：不看文字听，答题，说出要点；然后打开原文核对并跟读。

2 · Say the main points说出要点

3 · Now look at the text再看原文

📄 Show the text显示原文

- When forty centimetres of snow fell on the town in a single night last January, some streets were not cleared for four days. Residents in the hillside neighbourhoods, many of them older people living alone, could not reach a pharmacy or a bus stop. This week the town council published an independent review of what went wrong. - The review is blunt. Only on the third day did the town ask a private company for extra ploughs, although the forecast had warned of heavy snow a week earlier. Nor was there a list of residents who might need help. Volunteers who offered to check on neighbours had no way of knowing where to start. - "Had the contract been signed in December, most of the hillside would have been open by the second morning," the report says. It also notes that the lack of clear information made the situation worse: the town's website was updated only twice in four days. - The review recommends that the town sign snow contracts before the start of each winter, and that a voluntary register of residents with limited mobility be kept by the community centre. It is essential, the authors write, that the register be checked every autumn. Should a similar storm hit again, volunteers will receive a list of streets within hours. - At a public meeting on Tuesday, a retired nurse who spent the storm phoning her neighbours said the report was fair. "Nobody needed to be a hero," she said. "We just needed a list."

🆚 After your first draft: the same content written three ways写完第一稿再看：同一个内容的三种写法

Plain平实版

I think neighbours should try to know each other. It can make the area safer and friendlier. Neighbours can help each other and notice problems. But neighbours should not feel pressure. Some people want privacy. I take privacy seriously, so I like simple greetings and small acts of help.

B2B2 版

I think neighbours should make an effort to know one another, provided that everyone is comfortable with the level of contact. A familiar neighbourhood is safer and more welcoming, because people are more likely to notice problems, help in an emergency, or include someone who feels isolated. However, friendliness should not become pressure. Not everyone wants regular visits or personal conversations, and a good community should respect quiet boundaries as much as it encourages connection. I take privacy seriously, so I would prefer simple, natural gestures such as greetings, occasional help, or a neighbourhood event. These actions can build trust gradually without making anyone feel obliged to share more than they want. Over time, small acts of kindness can make people feel that they belong.

C1C1 版

On balance, neighbours should make a reasonable effort to know one another, provided that familiarity develops at a pace acceptable to everyone. A connected neighbourhood can be safer and more welcoming, since greater social trust makes people more likely to notice problems, offer practical help, or prevent isolation. However, pressure can undermine the trust that community efforts seek to establish. Much as social contact benefits many residents, it should not demand regular visits or the disclosure of personal details. I take personal privacy seriously, so I would favour low-pressure gestures such as greetings, occasional practical help, or an inclusive local event. In this way, trust may grow naturally while preserving the freedom to remain private. Over time, modest acts of kindness can support a sense of belonging.

What changes from one to the next每升一档换了什么

  • Plain states only that 'neighbours should try to know each other', while B2 turns this into a conditional view: 'provided that everyone is comfortable with the level of contact'.基础版只说'neighbours should try to know each other'（邻居应该尝试相互了解），B2 版把它变成有条件的说法：'provided that everyone is comfortable with the level of contact'（前提是大家都对彼此接触的程度感到舒适）。
  • C1 reuses "contact" as "social contact," uses "on balance" and "much as" to hedge and structure the argument, and condenses the idea of sharing personal information into "the disclosure of personal details."C1版将“contact”具体化为“social contact”，用“on balance”和“much as”表明判断并安排句子结构，还用“the disclosure of personal details”压缩表达个人信息分享。

👂 Thursday: listen first, then read周四：先听懂，再看文字

“The Quarrel Over One Name” is a text you have not read yet.「为一个署名而起的争执」：这一篇你还没读过。 Three steps: listen without the text, answer, say the main points. Then open the text to check and shadow it.三步：不看文字听，答题，说出要点；然后打开原文核对并跟读。

3 · Now look at the text再看原文

📄 Show the text显示原文

- Three years ago I fell out with a colleague over a project that neither of us wanted to lose. We had each done a fair share of the work, but the presentation would carry only one name, and we both believed it should be ours. For a week we exchanged polite, icy emails, and each of us waited for the other to back down. - I remember treating my version of events as a bargaining chip. I had saved every message, and I was ready to produce them like evidence in court. Looking back, that was exactly the wrong instinct. The more proof I gathered, the less I listened, and the more she hardened. - The turning point came by accident. We ended up sharing a lift, and in the awkward silence she said, ‘I don’t actually care about the name. I care that nobody thinks I did less.’ It had never occurred to me that her real concern was recognition, not ownership. Had she said so earlier, we would have saved ourselves a week of misery. - So we found common ground. I conceded that her contribution deserved to be named on the first slide, and she agreed that I would present, since I had drafted most of the text. It was not a perfect deal, but it was one we could both defend, and on the whole it held up well. - What I took from the episode is simple. Most quarrels are not about what they appear to be about. Although it is tempting to defend your position, a single honest question, asked early enough, tends to achieve more than a folder full of evidence.

🆚 After your first draft: the same content written three ways写完第一稿再看：同一个内容的三种写法

Plain平实版

Mei should talk to the landlord and stay calm. She should not shout or say she will leave. Her shop brings many people to the street. She can tell him this and ask for a smaller rise. She can also say she will stay for a long time. This is good for him too. There is a risk. He may say no. Anger makes the problem bigger. So I think a calm talk is best.

B2B2 版

I think Mei should negotiate with her landlord calmly rather than threaten to leave, as long as she has a realistic alternative in mind. Her shop has made the street busier and more attractive, so she can fairly argue that she deserves a smaller increase. She should also offer something in return, such as a longer lease, because landlords usually value a tenant who stays. That way both sides gain something, and the talk feels less like a fight. However, there is a real risk. The landlord may refuse to move, and then Mei would have to decide whether to accept the higher rent or look for another place. Although a threat might seem powerful, it could damage trust and make him more stubborn. For that reason, I would keep her other options private and use them only to stay confident. In my view, a calm, practical conversation gives her the best chance of keeping the shop and the relationship.

C1C1 版

Mei would be wise to negotiate rather than threaten to leave, provided that she has a workable alternative in the background. Her bookshop has helped make the street busier and more appealing, which gives her a fair basis for resisting a steep increase, and she can sweeten the case by offering a longer lease, since landlords tend to prize a reliable tenant. Framed that way, the exchange becomes a joint problem rather than a contest. There is, however, a genuine risk: the landlord may simply refuse, leaving Mei to choose between paying more and relocating. Threats, though they can look forceful, tend to erode trust and harden positions, so she would do better to keep her alternatives in reserve and let them steady her nerve rather than serve as a weapon. On balance, a calm, practical conversation, which respects the landlord's interests without conceding her own, offers the best chance of preserving both the shop and the relationship.

What changes from one to the next每升一档换了什么

  • The plain answer only says Mei will stay a long time, while the B2 version turns this into a trade, using "offer something in return" and a concrete example introduced by "such as".基础版只说梅会长期留下；B2 版把它变成一种交换，用 "offer something in return" 加上 "such as" 引出具体例子（更长的租约）。
  • The B2 phrase "other options private" becomes "alternatives in reserve", where "alternatives" is more precise and "in reserve" suggests something held back for later use rather than just hidden.B2 的 "other options private"（其他选择保密）在 C1 中变成 "alternatives in reserve"："alternatives" 更精确，"in reserve" 表示留作后手、备而不用，而不只是不公开。

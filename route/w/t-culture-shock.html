👂 Thursday: listen first, then read周四：先听懂，再看文字

“The Culture Shock Curve Is Not a Curve” is a text you have not read yet.「文化冲击曲线并不是一条曲线」：这一篇你还没读过。 Three steps: listen without the text, answer, say the main points. Then open the text to check and shadow it.三步：不看文字听，答题，说出要点；然后打开原文核对并跟读。

1 · Answer without looking不看文字答题

1 According to the text, what improves over time?

What does change is how fast people come back up.变的是回升的速度。

2 Why are people who expect a zigzag less surprised?

A zigzag includes going down again.锯齿线本身就包含再次下跌。

2 · Say the main points说出要点

3 · Now look at the text再看原文

📄 Show the text显示原文

- Most guides to moving abroad show the same picture: a curve. First comes the honeymoon, when everything is new and exciting. Then comes the dip, when the language tires you and the food is never quite right. Then a slow climb, and finally a line that stays flat and happy. It is a neat picture. For many people it is not what happens. - People who work with newcomers often describe something messier: the line moves up and down for years. A bad week at work, a call from a parent who is ill, a week of isolation in a dark winter: each can send someone back into the dip, even in the fifth year. This is not failure. It is the normal shape of settling in. - What does change is how fast people come back up. The first dip may last months; later ones may last days. Two things seem to help most. One is awareness: knowing that the low mood has a name and that it passes. The other is flexibility: being ready to adapt, keeping some old habits and dropping others, rather than choosing between "my culture" and "theirs". - Expectations matter too. People who arrive expecting a smooth curve often feel they are doing something wrong when the dip returns. People who expect a zigzag are less surprised, and they recover with more confidence. - So if you feel homesick in your third year, it does not mean you made the wrong choice. It means you are human, living in two places at once, and still finding your way home in both.

🆚 After your first draft: the same content written three ways写完第一稿再看：同一个内容的三种写法

Plain平实版

I think a person should learn the local customs and make friends. This can help the person feel less alone. You should also keep parts of your own culture. It can feel strange at first. You can join a club or ask people to explain traditions. I take this seriously. Belonging helps people feel safe and confident.

B2B2 版

I believe that a person can feel more at home when they gradually learn local customs and build genuine friendships. Staying curious and asking for help may feel uncomfortable at first, but it gives the newcomer a clearer understanding of daily life and reduces confusion. It is also important to keep familiar routines and relationships, because belonging does not mean losing one's identity. In fact, shared meals, regular calls, or cultural activities can provide stability while they adapt. However, becoming accepted can take time, and constant efforts to fit in may create stress or make differences seem like personal failures. For this reason, I take balance seriously: adapting should feel like a personal choice, not an obligation. A person should move at a manageable pace and seek support when needed.

C1C1 版

In my view, a newcomer is likely to feel at home more quickly if they engage with local customs while preserving routines and relationships that provide continuity. Learning by asking questions and forming genuine connections can turn unfamiliar situations into shared experiences, although progress may be uneven. Familiar meals, regular calls, or cultural activities offer an additional source of stability as they adapt, so that change does not become disorientation. The risk is that, in pursuing acceptance, a person may mistake conformity for belonging and overlook the distinct identity they carry. Belonging, for which I take responsibility as both an individual and a community member, is better understood as a connection that grows through respect in both directions. Provided that adaptation remains a choice rather than pressure, it can widen a person's sense of home without demanding a complete reinvention.

What changes from one to the next每升一档换了什么

  • Plain gives the short instruction to 'learn the local customs and make friends', while B2 adds the risk in its own sentence: 'constant efforts to fit in may create stress'.基础版只给出简短建议'learn the local customs and make friends'（学习当地习俗、交朋友），B2 版单独用一句话补上了风险：'constant efforts to fit in may create stress'（一直努力融入可能带来压力）。
  • C1 gives “belonging” a more specific meaning as a two-way connection and adds the hedge “provided that,” as well as the non-defining phrase “for which I take responsibility.”C1版将“belonging”的含义进一步具体化为双向连接，并加入了保留余地的结构“provided that”，以及非限制性结构“for which I take responsibility”。

👂 Thursday: listen first, then read周四：先听懂，再看文字

“Easy to Start, Hard to Stop” is a text you have not read yet.「开通容易取消难」：这一篇你还没读过。 Three steps: listen without the text, answer, say the main points. Then open the text to check and shadow it.三步：不看文字听，答题，说出要点；然后打开原文核对并跟读。

1 What is the "familiar pattern" in the complaints?

signs up … in two clicks … the button is hidden several pages deep.开通容易，取消难。

2 What do industry groups say about some rules?

some of these rules go too far.有些规定管得太宽。

3 Why might the real number be higher?

their figures only include people who chose to complain.只统计了投诉的人。

2 · Say the main points说出要点

3 · Now look at the text再看原文

📄 Show the text显示原文

- Complaints about subscriptions that are easy to start but hard to cancel have risen sharply over the past year, according to a report released on Monday by a national consumer group. The group said it has received more complaints about cancellations than about any other single issue since it began keeping records. - Many of the complaints describe a familiar pattern. A customer signs up for a free trial online in two clicks, forgets about it, and is charged when the trial ends. When they try to cancel, the button is hidden several pages deep, or they are told they must phone during office hours. "I have spent longer cancelling a streaming service than I spent choosing it," one woman told researchers. - Several governments have already introduced or proposed "click to cancel" rules, which require cancelling to be as easy as signing up. Industry groups have argued that some of these rules go too far, and that customers sometimes cancel by mistake. The report suggests that clearer reminders before a trial ends may help both sides. - The group's advice for consumers has not changed. Put the end date of every free trial in your calendar, read the fine print before you enter card details, and check your statements every month. If a company makes cancelling difficult, keep a record of every attempt, with dates and screenshots, and ask your card provider whether it can help. - The report's authors admit that their figures only include people who chose to complain. The real number of unwanted subscriptions, they said, is likely to be much higher.

🆚 After your first draft: the same content written three ways写完第一稿再看：同一个内容的三种写法

Plain平实版

Customers should complain about seriously poor products or services. It is right to complain. I would first tell the seller about the problem. I would keep the receipt and take photos. A calm complaint is more likely to get a good result. It can fix the mistake and protect other customers. But complaints can waste time and cause stress. So I complain only for serious problems. I take my evidence and the final result seriously.

B2B2 版

I believe customers should complain when a product or service is seriously poor, because staying silent can allow unfair practices to continue. I would first give the seller a chance to respond and explain the problem clearly. If it was damaged before use or did not match the description, for example, I would keep the receipt and provide photos. This approach is usually practical, since many firms are willing to correct a mistake when the complaint is specific. However, a complaint is not always a simple choice. Repeated small problems can waste time, and an angry message may weaken an otherwise fair case. For that reason, I take the tone and the evidence seriously, as well as the result I am seeking. A calm, factual complaint gives my concern the best chance of being taken seriously without turning the situation into an unnecessary conflict.

C1C1 版

On balance, customers should complain when a product or service falls seriously short, provided that they make a reasonable effort to resolve the matter directly. I would tend to give the seller a chance to respond, particularly because prompt action can often prevent an isolated mistake from becoming a pattern. If goods arrived damaged or did not match their description, for example, I would keep a clear record of the purchase and submit evidence showing exactly what went wrong. However, complaints require judgment: repeatedly pursuing minor faults may waste time and weaken a consumer's position. Much as a firm is entitled to explain or deny an allegation, I take tone and evidence seriously because clarity makes a complaint more credible. A calm account of the desired outcome, in my view, is more likely to achieve a fair resolution without needlessly turning a disagreement into conflict.

What changes from one to the next每升一档换了什么

  • Plain simply asserts 'It is right to complain', while B2 explains why with the reasoned clause 'staying silent can allow unfair practices to continue'.基础版只断言'It is right to complain'（投诉是对的），B2 版给出了理由：'staying silent can allow unfair practices to continue'（保持沉默会让不公平的做法持续下去）。
  • The C1 answer uses "clear" again as an adjective in "clear record," adds hedging such as "provided that" and "tend to," and uses "On balance" and "Much as" for more sophisticated structure.C1答案再次使用“clear”，但在“clear record”中表示完整、清楚的记录，并添加了“provided that”和“tend to”等缓和表达，还使用了“On balance”和“Much as”等更复杂的结构。

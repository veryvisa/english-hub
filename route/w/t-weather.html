👂 Thursday: listen first, then read周四：先听懂，再看文字

“Should We Change Our Weekend Plans?” is a text you have not read yet.「我们应该改变周末计划吗？」：这一篇你还没读过。 Three steps: listen without the text, answer, say the main points. Then open the text to check and shadow it.三步：不看文字听，答题，说出要点；然后打开原文核对并跟读。

1 · Answer without looking不看文字答题

1 Why are Maya and Daniel worried about hiking?

They are concerned that rain could make the paths slippery and visibility poor.他们担心降雨会使山间小路湿滑，并降低能见度。

2 What is their alternative plan?

They decide that visiting a museum and having lunch nearby would be a suitable alternative.他们认为参观博物馆并在附近吃午餐是合适的替代方案。

2 · Say the main points说出要点

3 · Now look at the text再看原文

📄 Show the text显示原文

- Maya: Have you checked the weather forecast for Saturday? I was hoping we could go hiking in the mountains. Daniel: I checked it this morning. It looks as if we might get heavy rain in the afternoon. The morning should be mild, but the conditions could change quickly. Maya: That is disappointing. I have been looking forward to this trip all week. - Daniel: I understand, but we should think about safety first. The mountain paths may become slippery after heavy rain, and visibility could be poor. Maya: You are right. I do not want us to take unnecessary risks. Perhaps we could leave earlier and finish before the rain starts. Daniel: That might work, although we should check the updated forecast before making a final decision. - Maya: What if the rain arrives earlier than expected? We could spend the day at the local museum instead. Daniel: That is a good alternative. We could also have lunch at the small cafe nearby. Maya: Great idea. I would still prefer hiking, but there is no point in ignoring the forecast. We can save the mountain trip for another weekend. - Daniel: Exactly. We should prepare for both possibilities. Let's bring light jackets and check the forecast again on Friday evening. Maya: Agreed. If the weather improves, we can go hiking. Otherwise, we will visit the museum. Daniel: Perfect. Having a backup plan makes the weekend easier to organise, especially when the weather is unpredictable. Maya: I am glad we have a plan that works either way.

🆚 After your first draft: the same content written three ways写完第一稿再看：同一个内容的三种写法

Plain平实版

My view is simple. The club should meet later in winter. Winter mornings are dark and very cold. The paths are icy, so they are not safe. Two members already fell down. That is a big problem. Some people say the early start is special. I understand that. But the club is for friends, and friends must stay safe. Late morning is warmer and brighter. More people can come, too. In summer, we can run early again. This plan is good for everyone.

B2B2 版

I think the club should move its winter runs to late morning, provided that we return to the early start once the ice has gone. The main reason is safety: on dark, frozen paths even experienced runners can slip, as two members have just found out. Nobody joined the club to get hurt, and one injury can keep a runner away for weeks. However, I can see why the early start matters to some people, because meeting before the town wakes up has always felt like a shared adventure. Although a later start may lose some of that atmosphere, a club with fewer injuries and more regular members is worth more.

C1C1 版

On balance, the club should shift its winter runs to late morning, reverting to the early start as soon as the ice clears. Safety is the decisive consideration: on dark, frozen paths even seasoned runners are liable to slip, as two members have just found out, and a single injury can sideline a runner for weeks. Admittedly, the early start has real appeal, since gathering before the town stirs has always given our Saturdays a sense of shared adventure, which a later hour may dilute. Yet that loss seems modest beside the risk of members limping home, and a club that keeps everyone running is worth more than a tradition kept at their expense.

What changes from one to the next每升一档换了什么

  • The bare 'I understand that. But' becomes a proper concession: 'However, I can see why...' also says why the other side cares, instead of just acknowledging that they exist.简单的 'I understand that. But' 变成了真正的让步表达：'However, I can see why...' 还点明了对方在意的原因，而不只是承认对方存在。
  • The phrasal idea 'keep a runner away' is replaced by the single verb 'sideline', and 'one injury' becomes the more emphatic 'a single injury'.短语式的 'keep a runner away' 被单个动词 'sideline' 取代，'one injury' 也变成语气更强的 'a single injury'。

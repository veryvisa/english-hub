👂 Thursday: listen first, then read周四：先听懂，再看文字

“My Mother's Cupboard of Supplements” is a text you have not read yet.「我妈的保健品柜子」：这一篇你还没读过。 Three steps: listen without the text, answer, say the main points. Then open the text to check and shadow it.三步：不看文字听，答题，说出要点；然后打开原文核对并跟读。

1 · Answer without looking不看文字答题

1 What made the writer finally bring the subject up?

she stopped taking her blood pressure pills for a week.她停了一周降压药。

2 What worked better the second time?

I asked her to help me figure out who had written it.请她一起弄清是谁写的。

3 Which sentence is softer?

A shared question invites her in; the others attack.一起提问是邀请，另两句是攻击。

2 · Say the main points说出要点

3 · Now look at the text再看原文

📄 Show the text显示原文

- My mother keeps a cupboard of supplements in her kitchen. There is fish oil, a powder for her joints, and a herbal tea that promises to "clear the blood". Most of them arrived through the family group chat, where a relative forwards a long message every morning, usually with a headline in red and a photo of a doctor nobody has heard of. - For years I said nothing. Then she stopped taking her blood pressure pills for a week because a message said the tea worked better. That was when I decided to bring it up. I did it badly. I called the messages rubbish, she called me arrogant, and we did not speak for two days. If I had been calmer that first time, we would have saved ourselves a lot of hurt. - My second try went better. Instead of arguing about the claim, I asked her to help me figure out who had written it. We looked together. The doctor in the photo was selling the tea. The study the message mentioned did not seem to exist. I did not have to point out what that meant; she saw it herself. - We now have a simple way to deal with new messages. Before she buys anything, she reads it to me on the phone, and we ask two questions: who is selling this, and where is the evidence? Some things pass. Her doctor is happy with the fish oil. Others she turns down on her own, which she seems to enjoy more than I expected. - I wish I had understood earlier that she was not foolish. She was worried about getting old, and the messages offered her a feeling of control. If I were in her position, far from the country I grew up in, I might want those promises too. What she needed was not a lecture. It was a partner in checking.

🆚 After your first draft: the same content written three ways写完第一稿再看：同一个内容的三种写法

Plain平实版

In my view, the supermarket should remove those green labels. The bars have protein, but a lot of added sugar is still bad for health. Busy shoppers are tired after work, so they see the 'doctor-approved' sign on the front and pick up a bar quickly. They do not read the small ingredient list on the back. People trust the bright label because it sounds safe, and then they eat too much sugar. Some customers want fast help in the aisles, and that makes sense. Still, the store can put a clear warning for high sugar on the front instead. That way, shoppers save time and get the real facts.

B2B2 版

In my view, the supermarket should remove those green labels unless the snack bars actually meet balanced nutrition standards. Highlighting only one positive ingredient like protein creates a false impression of the whole product. When busy shoppers rush through the store after work, they naturally rely on big signs on the front instead of checking the small ingredient list on the back. Because a medical badge feels trustworthy, it leads customers to treat a sweet bar as an everyday health food. However, taking away every quick label could frustrate people who genuinely want fast help in the aisles. Instead of using vague approval badges, the store should place plain labels on the front showing both high protein and high sugar. This approach still saves shoppers time, while giving them an honest picture of what they are buying.

C1C1 版

On balance, the supermarket should withdraw its 'doctor-approved' labels unless these snack bars meet strict overall nutrition standards. Elevating a single desirable nutrient, which is essentially what the current packaging does, obscures the broader nutritional cost of heavy added sugar. Tired shoppers navigating crowded aisles tend to take medical phrasing at face value rather than scrutinizing the fine print on the back, turning a sugary indulgence into an everyday habit. Admittedly, removing all visual guidance from the shelf could inconvenience customers who genuinely value quick cues during a rushed grocery trip. Yet keeping selective health marketing in check does not mean leaving buyers without practical direction. Replacing vague medical endorsements with transparent front labels for both protein and added sugar preserves shopping convenience while restoring honest accountability to the store.

What changes from one to the next每升一档换了什么

  • Moving from plain to B2 shifts the reason into a fronted 'Because' clause ('Because a medical badge feels trustworthy') and replaces the simple 'and then they eat' chain with 'leads customers to treat a sweet bar as an everyday health food' to explain the behavioral result.从基础版升到 B2 时，句子将原因前置为“Because”从句（“Because a medical badge feels trustworthy”），并用“leads customers to treat a sweet bar as an everyday health food”替代简单的并列动作“and then they eat”，从而说明这一行为后果。
  • From B2 to C1, the sentence inserts a non-restrictive relative clause ('which is essentially what the current packaging does') and replaces 'creates a false impression of the whole product' with the denser phrasing 'obscures the broader nutritional cost of heavy added sugar'.从 B2 升到 C1 时，句子插入了一个非限制性定语从句（“which is essentially what the current packaging does”），并用更凝练的表达“obscures the broader nutritional cost of heavy added sugar”替代了“creates a false impression of the whole product”。

2 · Say the main points说出要点

3 · Now look at the text再看原文

📄 Show the text显示原文

- Ask people who have lost money to a scam how it happened, and many begin with the same sentence: "I'm not stupid, but…" They are right. Research on fraud suggests that education and intelligence give surprisingly little protection. Scams do not usually work by fooling the clever part of the brain; they work by switching it off. - Most scams pull some mix of three levers. The first is authority: a caller who claims to be from a bank, a government office or the police. The second is urgency: a deadline in minutes, a frozen account, a warning that something terrible will happen today. The third is emotion, whether fear, hope or love. Under pressure, people make quick decisions and skip the checks they would normally do. - Confidence can make things worse. People who are sure they would spot a scammer are sometimes less careful, because they do not expect to be targeted. Older adults are often described as the most vulnerable group, but younger people seem to lose money to online shopping and job scams at least as often; they simply tend to lose smaller amounts each time. - What helps is not more knowledge but a habit. Experts recommend one simple rule: when a message creates urgency, treat that as a red flag in itself. Hang up, close the chat, and contact the organisation through a number you already know is reliable. A real bank will show a willingness to wait; a scammer will not. - Public awareness campaigns tend to focus on the details of the latest scam, but the details change every month. The pattern does not. If you remember the three levers, and keep the expectation that real organisations let you slow down, you will recognise most scams you meet, including ones nobody has invented yet.

🆚 After your first draft: the same content written three ways写完第一稿再看：同一个内容的三种写法

Plain平实版

My answer is no. It is Mrs. Lin's card and her money. But she needs a simple safety rule. Sometimes a caller asks for money fast. Then Mrs. Lin must stop and hang up. Next, she calls a family member. She uses a number from her own phone book. This is useful, because bad callers want her to feel afraid and hurry. But she may feel scared and forget the rule. So her family should practise it with her. They can also put the numbers near the phone.

B2B2 版

In my view, the daughter should not take the card, as long as her mother is able to manage her money and agrees to a clear safety rule. Whenever a caller urgently asks for money, Mrs. Lin should end the call and contact a relative on a number she already knows. This matters because scammers rely on panic: they push people to act before they can check the story. A short pause gives her time to find out whether her grandson really needs help, while she still controls her daily spending. However, there is a risk that she will feel too frightened to remember the rule during a real call. For this reason, the family should practise it with her and keep important numbers next to the phone.

C1C1 版

Although the daughter's worry is understandable, I would, on balance, leave the card with Mrs. Lin, provided that she can still manage her finances and is willing to adopt a clear safeguard against such calls. Any urgent request for money should prompt her to hang up and verify the story with a relative, using a number she already knows to be genuine. This brief pause targets the scammer's main weapon, panic, without depriving her of control over everyday spending, which taking the card would do. Admittedly, even a simple rule can easily slip someone's mind in a frightening moment, particularly when a loved one appears to be in danger. Rehearsing the response together, with trusted numbers displayed beside the phone, would therefore make it far easier to follow under pressure.

What changes from one to the next每升一档换了什么

  • The general phrase 'bad callers' becomes 'scammers', and the simple wish for her to 'feel afraid and hurry' becomes an explanation using 'rely on panic' and a 'before' clause.笼统的 bad callers 换成了 scammers（骗子），原来简单地说对方想让她 feel afraid and hurry（害怕并着急），现在用 rely on panic（利用恐慌）和 before 从句解释了骗术如何起作用。
  • The structure 'too frightened to remember' becomes the expression 'slip someone's mind', and 'can' presents forgetting as a possibility for anyone, not only for Mrs. Lin.too frightened to remember（吓得记不起来）换成了表达 slip someone's mind（被人一时忘记），并用 can 把忘记规则说成任何人都可能遇到的情况，而不只是针对林太太。

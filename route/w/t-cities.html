👂 Thursday: listen first, then read周四：先听懂，再看文字

“Let Our Street Grow a Little” is a text you have not read yet.「让我们这条街长大一点」：这一篇你还没读过。 Three steps: listen without the text, answer, say the main points. Then open the text to check and shadow it.三步：不看文字听，答题，说出要点；然后打开原文核对并跟读。

1 · Answer without looking不看文字答题

1 What does the writer mean by the character of a neighbourhood?

It is also who can afford to live there.还包括谁住得起。

2 What does the writer argue for?

gentle density: a townhouse here, a fourplex there.温和加密：联排屋、四户楼。

2 · Say the main points说出要点

3 · Now look at the text再看原文

📄 Show the text显示原文

- On my street of detached houses, almost every proposal for something denser meets the same response at council meetings: it will ruin the character of the neighbourhood. I used to agree. I have changed my mind, and I want to explain why without calling anyone unreasonable. - The character of a neighbourhood is not only its buildings. It is also who can afford to live there. On our street, the teachers, nurses and young families who gave it life twenty years ago have mostly been priced out. Their children cannot rent here, let alone buy. A street of beautiful houses where nobody under fifty can afford to live is not preserving its character; it is slowly losing it. - I am not arguing for towers on every corner. I am arguing for gentle density: a townhouse here, a fourplex there, basement suites that are legal and safe. These buildings look like houses, and they would let a family stay in the neighbourhood when its needs change. My expectation is not that this would make housing cheap, but that it would add a little supply, make homes a little more affordable and give families a little more flexibility. - Some neighbours worry about parking, shade and noise, and those worries deserve serious answers, not slogans. But I would ask them to show the same willingness to imagine a different street that we expect from newcomers. Most of us live in houses that someone once objected to. - There is no perfectly reliable way to predict what a few more homes will do to one street. That is fine. Cities have always changed; the question is who gets to be part of the change. I would like the answer to include the people who teach our children.

🆚 After your first draft: the same content written three ways写完第一稿再看：同一个内容的三种写法

Plain平实版

I support turning the closed hotel in the city centre into affordable homes. Many workers and teachers cannot find homes near their jobs. It would help the neighbourhood. But building will make noise and may close streets. The council should plan the work carefully. It should keep delivery areas open and help small shops during construction.

B2B2 版

I support the council’s plan to turn the closed hotel in the city centre into affordable homes, but it should address the concerns of local shop owners first. Many residents need a reasonable place to live near their jobs, and an empty building is a wasted opportunity in a crowded city centre. If the council pays for traffic signs, keeps loading areas open, and schedules noisy work outside busy shopping hours, the change may cause less disruption during construction. However, moving the project forward without a clear plan could hurt small businesses in the area. Therefore, I would approve it only if the final plan includes practical support for shops and a clear timetable for construction. The council should publish its schedule early.

C1C1 版

On balance, I support the council’s plan to turn the closed hotel in the city centre into affordable homes, provided that residents’ needs are weighed against the pressures faced by local shop owners. The building could offer a stable place to live near work for many people, so leaving it empty seems wasteful in a crowded city centre. Even so, major construction could reduce foot traffic and damage small businesses if disruption is poorly managed. The council could limit that risk by paying for clearer signs, keeping loading areas open, moving noisy work outside busy shopping hours, and giving shop owners advance notice of street closures. I would therefore approve the project, but only where practical shop support is included as an enforceable condition.

What changes from one to the next每升一档换了什么

  • Plain states the view and then lists the risk in short separate sentences. B2 folds the objection into the opening sentence as a concession ('but it should address the concerns of local shop owners first'), turns the three mitigations into one conditional sentence ('If the council pays for traffic signs, keeps loading areas open, and schedules noisy work outside busy shopping hours, the change may cause less disruption during construction'), and closes with a conditional approval ('I would approve it only if the final plan includes practical support for shops and a clear timetable for construction').基础版先表态、再用几个短句分别列出风险。B2 把反对意见收进开头那一句作让步（“but it should address the concerns of local shop owners first”），把三项缓解措施合并成一个条件句（“If the council pays for traffic signs, keeps loading areas open, and schedules noisy work outside busy shopping hours, the change may cause less disruption during construction”），结尾改成有条件的同意（“I would approve it only if the final plan includes practical support for shops and a clear timetable for construction”）。
  • C1’s real upgrade is not swapping 'However' for 'Even so' — both are B2-level connectors, and treating that swap as a difficulty marker teaches a false grading. The genuine C1 features are the passive, nominalised condition 'provided that residents’ needs are weighed against the pressures faced by local shop owners' and the closing phrase 'as an enforceable condition', which turn a personal opinion into a formally hedged policy position.C1 真正的升档不是把 ‘However’ 换成 ‘Even so’——这两个词都是 B2 级的连接词，把这处替换当作难度标志，等于教了一个假梯级。真正的 C1 特征是被动态、名词化的条件句 “provided that residents’ needs are weighed against the pressures faced by local shop owners”，以及结尾的 “as an enforceable condition”，这两处把个人意见改写成了正式的、带限定条件的政策立场。

- Subject: Could we have a word about my workload? Hi Karen, I hope your week is going well. Could we have a word this week about my workload? I wanted to put a few thoughts in writing first, so that our conversation can be useful rather than emotional. - Since March I have taken responsibility for two extra projects while we hire for the open position. I was glad to help, and I think the results have been good. But for the last six weeks I have been working most evenings and part of each weekend, and I am starting to notice the effects. I find it hard to switch off at night, I am sleeping badly, and I made two careless mistakes in last week's report. - I am telling you this now because I would rather raise it early than wait until it becomes burnout. I am not asking to drop anything immediately. I would like us to look at the list together and decide what can wait, what someone else could take on, and what I should keep. - I have also made an effort on my side. I now take a proper break at lunch, and I have stopped answering messages after nine unless something is urgent. These changes help, but they cannot fix a workload that is simply too large for one person to cope with. - I would really value your view on this, including if you see the situation differently. Would Thursday afternoon work for you? Thank you for reading, and for the support you have already given me this year. Best wishes, Ming

🆚 After your first draft: the same content written three ways写完第一稿再看：同一个内容的三种写法

Plain平实版

In my view, the clinic should change its shift schedule. Nurses work very hard, so they need enough sleep after a late evening shift. They cannot rest properly before an early morning shift, and they feel stressed. Poor sleep hurts their mental health, and tired staff also make more mistakes with patients. Some senior staff worry about busy periods, but the clinic can solve this problem. Managers can make the schedule earlier, and they can call part-time nurses during busy weeks. Rested nurses stay calm and healthy, because good sleep protects both their minds and their patients.

B2B2 版

In my opinion, the clinic should stop scheduling early shifts directly after late ones, as long as managers plan carefully for peak demand. Without an adequate overnight break, nurses cannot properly switch off and get deep sleep, which keeps their stress levels high and gradually harms their mental health. In addition, when staff arrive exhausted, they struggle to maintain focus on patients and remain calm under pressure. However, the senior staff raise a valid concern, since dropping short turnarounds leaves fewer people available to cover sudden absences during busy weeks. Even so, the clinic can manage this risk by releasing work schedules earlier and keeping a small group of part-time nurses on call. In the long run, protecting nurses' rest is worth the extra coordination, because healthier staff deliver safer and more dependable care.

C1C1 版

On balance, the clinic should phase out back-to-back late and early shifts, provided that it builds sufficient cover for peak periods. Brief overnight turnarounds tend to prevent deep restorative sleep, triggering sustained stress that gradually erodes nurses' mental health. Consequently, fatigued staff struggle to preserve clinical focus and composure under pressure, increasing the likelihood of patient-care errors. Although senior staff maintain that tight rotations make sudden absences easier to absorb during busy weeks, relying on sleep-deprived clinicians merely shifts the strain onto patient safety. Rather than treating staff wellbeing and reliable cover as competing goals, managers can reconcile them through earlier scheduling and a modest pool of on-call part-time nurses. Ultimately, safeguarding recovery time is worth the administrative effort, since sustainable, safe care depends on a mentally resilient team.

What changes from one to the next每升一档换了什么

  • While plain states a general stance ("change its shift schedule"), B2 names the exact schedule change ("stop scheduling early shifts directly after late ones") and adds a limiting condition with "as long as".Plain版只笼统提出“change its shift schedule”，B2版则具体说明要改的内容（“stop scheduling early shifts directly after late ones”），并用“as long as”加上了前提条件。
  • Instead of introducing the fix with "Even so" and "by" + "-ing" clauses, C1 opens with a fronted "Rather than" phrase, frames the trade-off with "reconcile", and compresses the actions into noun phrases ("earlier scheduling", "a modest pool").C1没有沿用B2的“Even so”和“by + 动名词”结构来引出解决办法，而是以“Rather than”前置对比结构开篇，用“reconcile”点出兼顾双方的思路，并将具体做法压缩为名词短语（“earlier scheduling”“a modest pool”）。

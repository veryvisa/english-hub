👂 Thursday: listen first, then read周四：先听懂，再看文字

“Who Does the Housework?” is a text you have not read yet.「家务应该由谁来做？」：这一篇你还没读过。 Three steps: listen without the text, answer, say the main points. Then open the text to check and shadow it.三步：不看文字听，答题，说出要点；然后打开原文核对并跟读。

1 · Answer without looking不看文字答题

1 What is the main problem described by the reader?

The reader works full-time but also manages most household tasks and family planning.读者虽然从事全职工作，却还承担了大部分家务和家庭安排。

2 What does mental responsibility include?

The article explains that planning and remembering family tasks are also forms of work.文章指出，计划和记住家庭事务同样属于劳动。

2 · Say the main points说出要点

3 · Now look at the text再看原文

📄 Show the text显示原文

- Dear Family Adviser, my partner and I both work full-time, but I seem to do most of the housework. I cook, clean, organise appointments, and remember what our children need for school. My partner says that work is exhausting and promises to help more, but the situation rarely changes. I am becoming frustrated, and small disagreements are turning into bigger arguments. What should I do? - Dear Reader, the first step is to recognise that housework includes more than visible tasks. Remembering appointments, planning meals, and checking school messages are also forms of family work. This mental responsibility can be tiring, even when other people do not notice it. Instead of arguing about who works harder, try making a complete list of the tasks that keep your household running. - Next, discuss how responsibilities could be shared more fairly. You might agree that one person prepares dinner while the other cleans the kitchen. Children can also take part by putting away their toys or helping with simple household tasks. Although an equal division is not always possible, a fair arrangement should take everyone's working hours, abilities, and personal needs into account. Flexibility is often more useful than a rigid schedule. - Finally, review the arrangement regularly. A family schedule that works during a quiet month may become difficult when someone changes jobs or a child starts a new activity. Notice what is working and what needs to change. Remember that sharing responsibilities is not about keeping score. It is about creating a home where everyone feels respected, supported, and able to rest.

🆚 After your first draft: the same content written three ways写完第一稿再看：同一个内容的三种写法

Plain平实版

I think families should eat together often. In the Chen family, everyone is busy, but they can still find time for dinner. They can talk about their day and share their news. This helps parents and children understand each other. I know people need free time, and they may have different plans. But eating together does not need to happen every day. Even one or two meals together each week can make a difference. Family time is important, and a shared meal is an easy way to stay close.

B2B2 版

I believe the Chen family should try to have dinner together regularly, even if they cannot do it every day. A shared meal gives everyone a chance to talk about work, personal plans and daily problems. It also helps parents and their adult children stay connected as their lives become busier. However, family dinners should not become another responsibility that people feel forced to complete. If someone has work or other plans, the family should be flexible. In my view, agreeing on a few regular meals would give the Chen family a healthy balance between independence and family connection.

C1C1 版

On balance, the Chen family would benefit from setting aside regular evenings for dinner, provided that the arrangement remains flexible. Eating together creates a natural opportunity to exchange news, discuss everyday concerns and maintain a sense of connection, particularly as adult children develop increasingly independent lives. The value of the meal lies less in its formality than in the attention family members give one another. That said, turning dinner into an obligation could undermine the very closeness it is intended to encourage. Allowing occasional absences while protecting some shared evenings would, I think, preserve both personal freedom and meaningful family relationships.

What changes from one to the next每升一档换了什么

  • Plain says family members can 'talk about their day and share their news', while B2 develops this into the more specific 'talk about work, personal plans and daily problems'.基础版只说可以'talk about their day and share their news'（聊聊一天和分享消息），B2 版展开为更具体的'talk about work, personal plans and daily problems'（谈工作、个人计划和日常问题）。
  • B2 warns that dinners should not become 'another responsibility that people feel forced to complete', while C1 reframes the risk more precisely as something that could 'undermine the very closeness it is intended to encourage'.B2 只是警告聚餐不该变成'another responsibility that people feel forced to complete'（让人被迫完成的另一项任务），C1 把这个风险说得更精确：这可能会'undermine the very closeness it is intended to encourage'（削弱它本想促成的亲密关系）。

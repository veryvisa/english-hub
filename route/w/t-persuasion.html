👂 Thursday: listen first, then read周四：先听懂，再看文字

“The Power of Framing the News” is a text you have not read yet.「新闻框架的力量」：这一篇你还没读过。 Three steps: listen without the text, answer, say the main points. Then open the text to check and shadow it.三步：不看文字听，答题，说出要点；然后打开原文核对并跟读。

1 · Answer without looking不看文字答题

1 What does framing refer to in news reporting?

Framing concerns which aspects of an issue are selected and how they are presented.框架效应涉及对问题哪些方面进行选择，以及如何呈现这些方面。

2 Why might journalists use dramatic headlines?

Dramatic headlines often attract attention and encourage readers to click.戏剧性的标题往往能够吸引注意力并促使读者点击。

2 · Say the main points说出要点

3 · Now look at the text再看原文

📄 Show the text显示原文

- Two news reports can describe the same event and leave readers with completely different impressions. Imagine a city introducing a new public transport policy. One headline announces that the government is investing in cleaner and more efficient travel. Another warns that commuters will face higher costs and fewer parking spaces. Both reports may contain accurate information, yet their framing encourages different emotional responses. - Framing refers to the selection and presentation of particular aspects of an issue. Journalists cannot include every available detail, so they make decisions about which facts deserve attention. These choices are not necessarily dishonest. However, repeated emphasis on certain consequences can make them appear more important than others. Over time, audiences may begin to understand a complicated issue through a narrow perspective. - Although journalists have a responsibility to report significant developments, the pressure to attract attention can influence editorial decisions. Headlines often highlight conflict, uncertainty or personal consequences because such elements are likely to generate clicks. While these techniques can make important stories accessible, they may also simplify complex debates. A dramatic headline is therefore not a reliable summary of the complete report. - Media analysts increasingly encourage readers to compare coverage across different sources. They recommend examining which facts are included, which perspectives are missing and whether the headline accurately represents the article. Readers should also distinguish between factual reporting and interpretation. On the whole, understanding framing does not require rejecting the media. It requires recognising that every act of presentation involves choices.

🆚 After your first draft: the same content written three ways写完第一稿再看：同一个内容的三种写法

Plain平实版

I think the radio station should say that the messages are paid. The messages about recycling are useful, and I like that. But the listeners trust the presenter. They think he is giving his own opinion. If he does not say it is paid, they will feel tricked. I do not want that. It is easy to say one short sentence before the message. For example, he can say, "This message is from the council." Then the listeners know the truth, and they can decide for themselves. I think people will still listen to the recycling advice. Also, the radio station will keep the trust of the town. Trust is very important for a small local station. If people stop trusting it, they will stop listening.

B2B2 版

I think the station should go ahead with the segment, provided that it states clearly that the council is paying for the messages. The advice about recycling is useful, so I understand why some listeners are not worried. However, the presenter is popular, and people tend to trust him because he sounds like a friend rather than an advertiser. If he reads the messages without saying who paid, listeners may feel misled when they find out, even though the content itself is harmless. A short sentence at the start, such as "This message comes from the council", would solve the problem. This would cost the station nothing and would take only a few seconds to say each week. Listeners could then judge the advice for themselves. Although some people might take the messages less seriously, I think honesty is worth that small risk. A local station depends on its listeners' trust, and once that trust is damaged, it is very hard to win back.

C1C1 版

On balance, I would let the segment go ahead, provided that the station discloses the council's payment up front. The recycling advice is genuinely useful, which is why some listeners see no harm in it. Yet the presenter's appeal rests on familiarity, so his words carry a personal authority that a plain advertisement would lack. Should he read the messages without attribution, many listeners would feel misled once they found out, however harmless the content. A one-line introduction, along the lines of "This message comes from the council", would remove that problem at almost no cost. Such a disclosure would take only a moment to read aloud, yet it would settle the matter for good. Admittedly, some people may discount the advice once they know who is behind it, but that is a fair price for transparency. A small local station, whose standing depends almost entirely on its listeners' goodwill, has far more to lose from a reputation for quiet deception than from a few sceptical listeners.

What changes from one to the next每升一档换了什么

  • The plain sentence is a simple direct statement, while the B2 version adds a condition with "provided that" and spells out who is paying.简单版只是直接陈述观点；B2 版用 "provided that" 加入了条件，并明确说出是市政府在付费。
  • The B2 clause with "because" and "rather than" becomes a denser statement using the abstract nouns "familiarity" and "authority" and the verb phrase "rests on".B2 里用 because 和 rather than 的从句，在 C1 中变成更紧凑的表达，用了抽象名词 familiarity 和 authority，以及动词短语 rests on。

👂 Thursday: listen first, then read周四：先听懂，再看文字

“A Call to the Landlord” is a text you have not read yet.「给房东打的一通电话」：这一篇你还没读过。 Three steps: listen without the text, answer, say the main points. Then open the text to check and shadow it.三步：不看文字听，答题，说出要点；然后打开原文核对并跟读。

2 · Say the main points说出要点

3 · Now look at the text再看原文

📄 Show the text显示原文

- The heating in Mei's apartment stopped working on a Sunday in January. She had put off calling the landlord twice before, about smaller things, because she was nervous about her English. This time she could not wait. Here is the call, more or less as it happened. - "Hello, Mr Davies? It's Mei from unit 4. Sorry to bother you on a Sunday. I'm calling because we have no heat." "Oh. Since when?" "Since this morning. I've checked the thermostat, and it's set to twenty-one, but the radiators are cold." "Right. Have you tried turning it off and on again?" "Yes, twice. Nothing happens." - "Okay. I can't come today, but I'll have a word with my plumber." "Thank you. Could you tell me roughly when he might come? It's minus ten tonight, and I have a small child." "I'll find out and call you back within the hour." "That would be great. And if he can't come today, could we sort out a heater for tonight?" "Fair enough. I'll bring one over." - After she hung up, Mei did one more thing: she followed up with a text message that repeated the time, the problem and what he had promised. Nothing in the call was complicated. What made it work was the order: who I am, what is wrong, since when, what I have already tried, what I need, and by when. She had made an effort to prepare those six points on paper first. The plumber came at four. The landlord, she has noticed, has taken her calls more seriously ever since.

🆚 After your first draft: the same content written three ways写完第一稿再看：同一个内容的三种写法

Plain平实版

In my view, a small flat in the city centre is better for a new start. I want a short trip to work and shops, because long rides every day make life hard. With more free time, I can walk around my street, meet neighbours, and feel at home. Big houses far away have more space, but they take away your energy. Central flats also have clear risks. Busy streets are noisy, and old buildings bring extra bills for repairs. So I check the walls and ask about all extra costs first.

B2B2 版

When moving to a new city, I believe choosing a smaller flat near the centre is the smarter option, as long as the rent fits your budget. The main reason is that living close to work and a reliable means of transport saves precious hours each week. Rather than spending your evenings stuck in traffic, you gain the time and energy to explore local shops, build friendships, and settle into a steady routine. As a result, a smaller home can help a newcomer feel grounded much sooner than a larger house in a quiet suburb. However, central flats carry serious downsides that deserve careful attention, especially noise from busy streets and unexpected repair bills in older buildings. Because strong demand for central housing pushes prices up, tenants must inspect the property thoroughly and confirm all monthly fees before signing a lease.

C1C1 版

On balance, newcomers to a city tend to settle more smoothly in a compact central flat than in an outer suburb, provided that the rent remains within their means. Much as a spacious house farther out may appeal, the daily drain of a long commute quietly erodes both time and energy. Proximity to workplaces and transit hubs frees up hours each week, which in turn allows residents to learn their neighbourhood, build social ties, and establish a steady routine. That said, central living carries tangible trade-offs that warrant serious scrutiny. Older properties along busy streets often suffer from poor soundproofing and unexpected repair costs, while fierce competition for central addresses can mask hidden service fees. Consequently, conducting a thorough inspection of daytime noise and lease terms is essential before committing to any long-term agreement.

What changes from one to the next每升一档换了什么

  • The B2 answer adds a limiting condition ('as long as the rent fits your budget'), connects ideas with B2 transitions ('Rather than', 'As a result', 'However'), and expands the time-saving reason and noise-and-cost downside into fuller cause-and-effect sentences.B2版本加入了限制条件（'as long as the rent fits your budget'），使用B2衔接词（'Rather than'、'As a result'、'However'）串联逻辑，并将节省时间的原因和噪音及费用隐患展开为更完整的因果句。
  • The C1 version hedges claims ('On balance', 'tend to', 'provided that'), uses a fronted 'Much as...' clause, a non-restrictive relative clause ('...which in turn allows...'), and nominalisations ('Proximity to', 'inspection of'), while reusing 'means' in a more specific financial sense ('within their means' instead of 'means of transport').C1版本通过'On balance'、'tend to'和'provided that'弱化绝对语气，运用前置让步句'Much as...'、非限制性定语从句（'...which in turn allows...'）及名词化表达（'Proximity to'、'inspection of'），并将B2中表示交通方式的'means'（'means of transport'）复用为更具体的经济能力义（'within their means'）。

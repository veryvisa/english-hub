👂 Thursday: listen first, then read周四：先听懂，再看文字

“One Line on a Form Doubles Sign-ups” is a text you have not read yet.「表格上改一行，参与翻一倍」：这一篇你还没读过。 Three steps: listen without the text, answer, say the main points. Then open the text to check and shadow it.三步：不看文字听，答题，说出要点；然后打开原文核对并跟读。

3 Which sentence is correct?

2 · Say the main points说出要点

3 · Now look at the text再看原文

📄 Show the text显示原文

- When a mid-sized city in Ontario changed one line on a form, the number of households using its food-waste program more than doubled. Residents were not offered money or prizes. The city simply made the program the default: instead of asking people to sign up, it sent every home a green bin and allowed people to opt out. - City staff say the results were well above their expectations. Under the old system, about a third of households had joined after three years of advertising. Within a year of the change, most households were using the bins, and only a small number had asked to opt out. "We did not change people's opinions," one manager said. "We changed which option needed effort." - Researchers who study decision-making are not surprised. Defaults work because doing nothing is the easiest choice, and because many people assume the default is what experts recommend. The same pattern has been found with workplace savings plans and in other areas where the sensible choice is clear but easy to put off. - Not everyone is pleased. Some residents complained that the city had made the decision for them, and one local councillor called the approach "unreasonable pressure". Supporters reply that every form has a default anyway; the only question is whether it is chosen with care. The city says flexibility matters, and anyone can return the bin with one phone call. - Experts say the lesson goes beyond garbage. Awareness of defaults helps in everyday life: check which plan your phone company puts you on, what happens when a free trial ends, and which box is already ticked on a form. Often the most important decision is the one you did not know you were making.

🆚 After your first draft: the same content written three ways写完第一稿再看：同一个内容的三种写法

Plain平实版

I think the library should try the new hours. Many people work during the day, so they cannot visit the library before it closes. If it stays open later, they can borrow books or study after work. This is helpful for families and students too. However, the library should not make this a permanent change right away. It needs more money and more work for the staff. Some evenings may also be very quiet. The library can try the new hours for a short time and ask visitors what they think. If enough people come and the staff are happy with the plan, the library can keep the later hours. I think this is a fair way to make the decision.

B2B2 版

I believe Riverside Public Library should test the later opening hours before making a permanent decision. People who work during the day often have little time to visit the library, and staying open until 9 p.m. would give them more flexibility. It could also provide students with a quiet place to study in the evening. However, longer opening hours would increase operating costs and might put extra pressure on staff, particularly if visitor numbers remain low. Rather than guessing whether the change would be worthwhile, the library could introduce a trial period and collect feedback from both visitors and employees. It should then compare attendance with the additional costs. If the benefits justify the expense and staff can manage the schedule, keeping the later hours would be a sensible decision.

C1C1 版

On balance, Riverside Public Library should introduce the extended hours on a trial basis rather than commit to a permanent change immediately. For residents whose working schedules leave little opportunity for daytime visits, access until 9 p.m. could make the library considerably more useful; students, too, would gain a quiet place to study. However, greater accessibility comes at a cost: additional staffing and operating expenses may be difficult to justify if evening attendance proves disappointing. The library should therefore assess actual demand instead of relying solely on residents' stated preferences. A trial would allow it to weigh visitor numbers, employee feedback and financial implications together. Provided that demand is sufficient and the revised schedule remains manageable for staff, retaining the later hours would be justified. This approach makes the decision responsive to evidence without overlooking the people expected to deliver the service.

What changes from one to the next每升一档换了什么

  • The simple suggestion to 'try the new hours' becomes a more precise proposal to 'test the later opening hours before making a permanent decision', clarifying that the change should begin as a trial.原文用“try the new hours”简单提出尝试新时间；B2改为“test the later opening hours before making a permanent decision”，更明确地表达了先试行、再决定是否长期实施的方案。
  • The B2 answer compares attendance with costs, while the C1 answer broadens this assessment to include employee feedback and the wider financial implications through the more analytical verb 'weigh'.B2主要比较到访人数和额外开支；C1则通过“weigh”将评估扩展为综合权衡访客人数、员工反馈和更广泛的财务影响，分析维度更加完整。

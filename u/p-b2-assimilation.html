Sounds that change and disappear in fast speech 快语速里的同化与省音

Recognise the sounds that change, merge and drop out in fast speech, and use the common ones yourself so you sound relaxed rather than over-careful. 认出快语速里变化、合并、脱落的音，自己也用上常见的几种，听起来放松而不是一字一板。

B2 · CELPIP 7–8⏱ 25 minP09 Words are not linked; each one is said alone不连读，一词一顿

📏 The rule规则

📏 The point in one line一句话要领
At natural speed, a sound takes on features of the sound next to it (ten boys → /tem bɔɪz/, did you → /dɪdʒu/), and /t/ and /d/ between consonants often disappear (next door → /neks dɔː/). These are not mistakes; they are how the words are normally said.自然语速下，一个音会带上邻音的特征，夹在辅音之间的 /t/、/d/ 常常脱落。这不是错误，而是这些词平常的说法。
  • Easy to miss易漏 ✗ nex-tuh week (adding a vowel to keep /t/) → ✓ nex' week

📖 Why, and when细讲：为什么、什么时候用

Open the full explanation: 5 points展开细讲：5 段讲解

A student who scores well in reading hears a colleague say "Did you get my message?" and catches only "...jew...". She knew every word; she did not know that did you is usually /dɪdʒu/. Most listening failure at upper-intermediate level is not about vocabulary. It is about words that do not sound like their dictionary form.

一个阅读分数很高的学员听同事问「你收到我的消息了吗」，只听到一个「jew」。每个词她都认识，只是不知道 did you 通常读 /dɪdʒu/。中高级的听力困难，多半不在词汇，而在词的实际读音和词典读音不一样。

Three changes cover most cases. First, /t/, /d/ and /n/ at the end of a word take the lip shape of a following /p/, /b/ or /m/, or the back-of-the-tongue shape of a following /k/ or /ɡ/: ten boys /tem bɔɪz/, good morning /ɡʊb ˈmɔːnɪŋ/, in case /ɪŋ keɪs/. Second, /t/ or /d/ plus /j/ become /tʃ/ or /dʒ/: don't you /dəʊntʃu/, would you /wʊdʒu/. Third, /s/ or /z/ before /ʃ/ or /j/ becomes /ʃ/ or /ʒ/: this shop /ðɪʃ ʃɒp/, is your /ɪʒ jɔː/.

三种变化覆盖大多数情况：词尾 /t/、/d/、/n/ 随后面的双唇音或软腭音变口型；/t/、/d/ 加 /j/ 变成 /tʃ/、/dʒ/；/s/、/z/ 在 /ʃ/ 或 /j/ 前变成 /ʃ/、/ʒ/。

Elision means a sound disappears. The most common case is /t/ or /d/ squeezed between two consonants: next week, last night, handbag, old man, I don't know /aɪ dəʊ nəʊ/. Weak vowels also drop in common words: camera /ˈkæmrə/, family /ˈfæmli/, probably /ˈprɒbli/ in casual speech.

省音就是音消失。最常见的是夹在两个辅音之间的 /t/、/d/；常用词里的弱元音在随意说话时也会脱落。

Chinese speakers tend to do the opposite of what helps here. Because Mandarin syllables are separate and mostly open, learners either add a small vowel to keep the final consonant (nex-tuh week) or stop completely between words. Both make speech slower and harder to follow. When you speak, you do not have to produce every assimilation, but let the /t/ in next week go, and say would you as /wʊdʒu/; these two habits alone make you sound much more natural.

华人在这里常常反着来：为了保住词尾辅音加个小元音，或者词与词之间完全停住。两种都让话更慢、更难跟。说的时候不必每处都同化，但先把 next week 的 /t/ 放掉、把 would you 读成 /wʊdʒu/，这两个习惯就能让你自然很多。

For listening, learn to undo the changes. When you hear /tem b/, think "ten b..."; when you hear /dʒu/ after a verb, think "d you" or "would you"; when a /t/ seems missing, ask which word would need it. The drills below give you the most frequent forms; once your ear knows them, fast speech begins to break into words.

听力上，学会把变化「还原」。下面的跟读给出最常见的形式；耳朵熟悉之后，快语速就开始能断成词了。

⚠️ Common slips for Chinese speakers华人常错

✗ nex-tuh week (adding a vowel to keep /t/)
✓ nex' week
The added vowel adds a syllable and sounds foreign.
加元音等于多一个音节，听起来很外国。
✗ Did. You. Get. My. Message? (full forms with stops)
✓ /dɪdʒu ˈɡet maɪ ˈmesɪdʒ/
Over-careful speech is slower and, oddly, harder to follow.
过分仔细反而更慢、更难跟。
✗ Thinking /tem bɔɪz/ is a different number
✓ It is ten boys
The change is predictable; undo it in your head.
变化有规律，在脑子里还原即可。
✗ Assimilating in slow, formal reading of key numbers
✓ Say key numbers and names clearly
Assimilate the grammar words, not the information you must not lose.
同化功能词，别同化不能丢的信息。

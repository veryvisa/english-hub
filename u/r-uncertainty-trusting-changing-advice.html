Dear Dr. Marsh, I am a little embarrassed to write this. Every few months, health experts seem to reverse their advice, and I have started to ignore all of it. My sister says I am being foolish. Am I, or is it reasonable to stop listening when the experts cannot agree? Yours, Confused in the Suburbs

亲爱的马什博士：我有点不好意思写这封信。每隔几个月，健康专家似乎就会推翻自己的建议，我已经开始对这些建议一概不理。我姐姐说我很愚蠢。专家们意见不一时，我不再听他们的，是否合理？此致 郊区的困惑者

Dear Confused, You are not foolish, but I think you are drawing the wrong conclusion. While it is true that advice changes, that is not the same as saying that experts disagree about everything. Most guidance is provisional by design: it reflects the best evidence available today and is likely to be adjusted when better evidence arrives.

亲爱的困惑者：你并不愚蠢，但我认为你得出了错误的结论。建议会变化确实没错，但这并不等于专家们在所有事情上都有分歧。大多数指导意见在设计上就是暂定的：它反映的是当下可得的最佳证据，当更好的证据出现时很可能会被调整。

Think of it as a map that is redrawn as explorers report back. The broad outline, by and large, stays the same, and only the details move. Advice that has been confirmed many times by different teams is robust, and it deserves your attention. Advice based on a single small study should be held more loosely, and any sensible expert will tell you so.

不妨把它想成一张随探险者带回消息而不断重绘的地图。总体而言，大致轮廓保持不变，移动的只是细节。被不同团队多次证实的建议是稳固的，值得你重视；仅基于一项小型研究的建议则应更谨慎地看待，任何明智的专家都会这样告诉你。

So what should you do in practice? First, notice how the claim is worded. Honest sources tend to use phrases such as ‘may reduce’ or ‘is associated with’, and they usually mention the margin of error. Second, ask whether the advice has changed once or repeatedly. Finally, if you are unsure, talk to someone who knows your circumstances rather than relying on headlines.

那么实际上该怎么做？首先，留意说法的措辞。诚实的来源往往使用“可能降低”或“与……相关”之类的表述，并且通常会提到误差范围。其次，看看这条建议是只变过一次还是反复变过。最后，如果拿不准，就去问了解你具体情况的人，而不是依赖新闻标题。

As for your sister, she may be right that ignoring everything is risky. That said, she should not be too hard on you. Healthy doubt is a good instinct; it simply needs to be paired with a willingness to check. Keep asking questions, and do not mistake a change of advice for a failure of the whole system. Warm regards, Dr. Marsh

至于你姐姐，她说得没错，一概不理确实有风险。话虽如此，她也不该对你太苛刻。健康的怀疑是好本能，只是需要配合愿意核实的态度。请继续提问，不要把建议的变化误当作整个体系的失败。此致敬礼，马什博士

Long sentences, unpacked长句拆解

While it is true that advice changes, that is not the same as saying that experts disagree about everything.
The ‘While’ clause concedes a fact, and the main clause uses ‘that is not the same as saying that...’ to separate two ideas that are easily confused.
While 从句承认一个事实，主句用 that is not the same as saying that... 区分两个容易混淆的观点。
Most guidance is provisional by design: it reflects the best evidence available today and is likely to be adjusted when better evidence arrives.
The colon introduces an explanation, ‘available today’ is a reduced relative clause after ‘evidence’, and ‘is likely to be adjusted’ is a hedged passive.
冒号引出解释，'available today' 是修饰 evidence 的省略式定语从句，'is likely to be adjusted' 是带限定语气的被动结构。

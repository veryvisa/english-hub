My Grandfather’s First Smartphone 祖父的第一部智能手机

🧠 Learn the 4 new words of this unit, one card at a time逐张学本单元的 4 个新词

Teaching my grandfather to use a smartphone taught me patience and showed me his hidden strengths. 教祖父用智能手机让我学会了耐心，也让我看到了他的潜在优点。

Last winter my grandfather, who is eighty-one, finally agreed to buy a smartphone. He had refused for years, saying that a phone was for calling people and nothing else. I was surprised when he rang me one evening and asked, in a small voice, whether I could teach him.

去年冬天，我八十一岁的祖父终于同意买一部智能手机。多年来他一直拒绝，说手机只是用来打电话的，别无他用。一天晚上他打电话给我，小声问我能不能教他，我很惊讶。

At first it was a disaster. He pressed everything twice, held the phone far from his face and complained that the screen was too clever for its own good. I felt my patience wearing thin, and more than once I nearly said, ‘Just let me do it.’ Then I remembered how many hours he had spent teaching me to ride a bike.

起初简直是一场灾难。他每件事都按两次，把手机拿得离脸很远，还抱怨屏幕聪明过头了。我感觉自己的耐心快被磨光了，不止一次差点说出：“还是让我来吧。”后来我想起他曾花了多少个小时教我骑自行车。

So I slowed down. We sat side by side every Sunday, and I let him make his own mistakes. In return, he told me stories I had never heard: how he left his village at seventeen, how he learned a new language at work, and how scared he felt on his first day in a strange country. I realised he had always been a person who could keep up with change; he simply needed someone to be patient.

于是我放慢了节奏。每个星期天，我们并肩而坐，我让他自己犯错。作为回报，他给我讲了许多我从没听过的故事：他十七岁时怎样离开村子，怎样在工作中学会一门新语言，以及第一天到陌生国家时有多害怕。我意识到，他一直是个能跟上变化的人，只是需要有人耐心相待。

Now he sends me photos of his garden with long messages full of mistakes, and I would not change a single one. The gap between us has not disappeared, but it has become a bridge rather than a wall. I think we both learned something.

现在，他会给我发花园的照片，配上满是错误的长长消息，而我一个字都不想改。我们之间的差距并没有消失，但它已经从一堵墙变成了一座桥。我想我们都学到了一些东西。

/ˈweərɪŋ θɪn/ phr.

patience wears thin; start to wear thin
His excuses are wearing thin. 他的借口越来越站不住脚了。
/ɪn rɪˈtɜːn/ adv. phr.
作为回报
give something in return; offer help in return
She cooked dinner, and I washed the dishes in return. 她做晚饭，我作为回报洗碗。
/saɪd baɪ saɪd/ adv. phr.
并肩地
sit side by side; work side by side
The old and the young worked side by side in the garden. 老人和年轻人在花园里并肩劳动。
/kiːp ʌp wɪð/ phr. v.
跟上
keep up with change; keep up with friends
It is not easy to keep up with a fast-growing company. 要跟上一家快速发展的公司并不容易。
/ˈrɑːðə ðæn/ conj.
而不是

She chose to talk rather than argue. 她选择交谈而不是争吵。

Long sentences, unpacked长句拆解

Then I remembered how many hours he had spent teaching me to ride a bike.
The past perfect ‘had spent’ shows an earlier time than ‘remembered’, and ‘how many hours’ begins a noun clause.
过去完成时 had spent 表示比 remembered 更早的时间，how many hours 引导名词性从句。
I realised he had always been a person who could keep up with change; he simply needed someone to be patient.
The relative clause ‘who could keep up with change’ describes ‘a person’, and the semicolon joins two linked ideas.
关系从句 who could keep up with change 修饰 a person，分号连接两个相关的意思。

📏 The rule规则

📏 The point in one line一句话要领
Most accent differences come from five places: whether the r after a vowel is spoken, the vowel in bath and dance, the vowels in lot and thought, the /t/ between vowels, and, for Canada, a raised start to price and mouth vowels before voiceless sounds.口音差别大多来自五处：元音后的 r 读不读、bath 和 dance 的元音、lot 与 thought 的元音、元音之间的 /t/，以及加拿大口音在清辅音前把 price、mouth 两类元音的起点抬高。

📖 Why, and when细讲：为什么、什么时候用

Open the full explanation: 6 points展开细讲：6 段讲解

Rhoticity is the biggest single difference. In standard Southern British English an r is only pronounced before a vowel: car /kɑː/, hard /hɑːd/, water /ˈwɔːtə/. In North America it is pronounced everywhere: /kɑr/, /hɑrd/, /ˈwɑɾər/. Most textbooks taught in China use British transcription, so learners in Canada often hear work, first and never as unfamiliar because of the strong /r/ colouring.

元音后的 r 是最大的单项差别。标准南部英式英语只在元音前读 r；北美处处都读。国内教材多用英式音标，所以在加拿大的学习者常因浓重的 r 色彩而认不出 work、first、never。

The vowel in bath, ask, dance, can't and half is long /ɑː/ in southern British English and short /æ/ in North America. Can't is the one that matters most: British /kɑːnt/ is clearly different from can; American and Canadian /kænt/ differs from can /kæn/ mainly by the final /t/, which is often not released. North American listeners rely on stress: can is weak (/kən/), can't is strong.

bath、ask、dance、can't、half 的元音，英式是长 /ɑː/，北美是短 /æ/。最要紧的是 can't：英式 /kɑːnt/ 和 can 差别明显；北美 /kænt/ 与 can 主要差在不爆破的词尾 /t/，北美听者靠重音分辨：can 弱读，can't 重读。

lot, cot and caught. British English has three distinct vowels: lot /ɒ/ (short, rounded), thought /ɔː/ (long), and father /ɑː/. American English merges lot with father (/ɑ/) and, for many speakers, keeps thought separate. Canadian English merges all three: cot and caught, Don and dawn, stock and stalk are the same word. If you hear a Canadian say I got caught, both vowels are identical; do not search for a difference.

英式三个元音分明；美式把 lot 和 father 合并，很多人仍分开 thought；加拿大英语三者全并：cot 与 caught、Don 与 dawn 同音。听加拿大人说 I got caught，两个元音完全一样，不用去找差别。

The /t/ between vowels after a stress is a flap in North America (better /ˈbeɾər/, city, meeting), and a clear /t/ or, in many British accents, a glottal stop (be'er, ci'y for some speakers in casual London speech). The flap also makes writer and rider, latter and ladder sound almost the same in North America; Canadians often keep them apart by the vowel: see the next point.

重读后元音之间的 /t/，北美读闪音，英式读清楚的 /t/，或在不少英国口音的随意说话中读喉塞音。闪音也让北美的 writer 与 rider、latter 与 ladder 几乎同音；加拿大人常靠元音区分，见下一条。

Canadian raising. Before voiceless consonants, many Canadians start the vowels of price and mouth higher: about /əˈbʌʊt/, house /hʌʊs/, out, price /prʌɪs/, writer /ˈrʌɪɾər/. Before voiced sounds the vowel stays low: loud, houses (with /z/), rider. This is why visitors joke that Canadians say a-boot; the real vowel is not /uː/ but a raised /ʌʊ/. You do not need to copy it, but recognising it keeps out and about from sounding like new words.

加拿大式抬高：在清辅音前，很多加拿大人把 price、mouth 两类元音的起点抬高。浊音前仍是低起点。这就是外人取笑加拿大人说「a-boot」的由来，实际不是 /uː/，而是抬高的 /ʌʊ/。不必模仿，但要认得出。

Which model to speak? Pick one and be consistent; mixing a British can't /kɑːnt/ with an American r is noticed more than either accent alone. If you live in Canada and talk mostly to Canadians, a general North American model is the practical choice. Your listening, however, should cover all three, because work calls, podcasts and colleagues will.

说哪一种？选一种并保持一致；英式 can't 配美式 r 混着用，比单用任何一种都更显眼。生活在加拿大、主要和加拿大人说话，通用北美口音是实际的选择。但听力要三种都覆盖，因为工作电话、播客和同事都会用到。

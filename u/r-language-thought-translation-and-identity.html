When I first moved abroad, I believed that becoming fluent in another language would be enough to make me feel at home. I studied grammar, memorised expressions and practised conversations whenever possible. Yet I soon discovered that vocabulary alone could not explain why certain jokes felt uncomfortable or why ordinary invitations sometimes carried meanings I failed to recognise. I could translate individual sentences accurately while still misunderstanding the social expectations behind them.

刚搬到国外时，我相信只要能够流利地使用另一种语言，就足以让我产生归属感。我学习语法、记忆表达，并尽可能练习对话。然而，我很快发现，单靠词汇无法解释为什么某些笑话令人不舒服，也无法解释为什么普通的邀请有时包含我没有意识到的含义。我能够准确翻译单个句子，却仍然无法理解这些句子背后的社会期待。

One afternoon, a neighbour invited me to visit. I assumed the invitation referred to a specific appointment and asked for an exact time. She looked surprised, explaining that she had simply wanted to express friendliness. I felt embarrassed, but the encounter taught me something about the relationship between language and social context. Expressions do not operate independently of the situations in which they occur. Their meaning is often shaped by relationships, expectations and shared cultural knowledge.

一天午后，一位邻居邀请我去她家做客。我以为这个邀请指的是一次具体约定，于是询问了确切时间。她显得有些惊讶，并解释说，她只是想表达友好。我感到有些尴尬，但这次经历让我学到了一些有关语言与社会语境之间关系的知识。表达并不是独立于发生情境之外的。它们的含义往往受到人际关系、预期和共享文化知识的影响。

Gradually, I began to notice that certain expressions carried emotional associations that dictionaries could not fully capture. Some phrases suggested respect, distance, affection or obligation, depending on who used them and when. While a dictionary provides useful definitions, it rarely explains every social implication. I started keeping a notebook of expressions that confused me, recording the situation, the speaker's intention and my interpretation. This simple habit helped me recognise recurring patterns rather than treating each misunderstanding as an isolated failure.

渐渐地，我开始注意到，有些表达带有字典无法充分体现的情感联想。某些短语会根据使用者和使用时间的不同，暗示尊重、距离、亲密或责任。虽然字典提供了有用的定义，但它很少解释所有社会层面的含义。我开始用笔记本记录令我困惑的表达，写下当时的情境、说话者的意图以及自己的理解。这个简单的习惯帮助我识别反复出现的模式，而不再把每次误解都看成一次孤立的失败。

Today, I no longer think of bilingualism as the ability to replace one set of words with another. It is more like learning to move between different systems of meaning. My identity has not become divided; instead, it has become more flexible. I can appreciate the emotional force of an expression without assuming that every language has an equivalent. By and large, living between languages has made me more patient with uncertainty and more curious about other people's experiences. Fluency, I have learned, also involves knowing when to ask what someone really means.

如今，我不再认为双语能力就是用一套词语替换另一套词语。它更像是学习在不同的意义体系之间移动。我的身份认同并没有因此变得分裂，反而变得更加灵活。我能够欣赏某种表达的情感力量，而不再假设每种语言都有完全对应的说法。总的来说，在不同语言之间生活使我面对不确定性时更加耐心，也让我对他人的经历更加好奇。我逐渐认识到，语言流利还意味着懂得在什么时候询问别人真正想表达什么。

How the Brain Rebuilds the Past 大脑如何重建过去

Many people picture memory as a kind of recording: an event happens, the brain stores it, and years later we press play. Psychologists have long argued that this picture is misleading. Remembering is closer to reconstruction, in which the brain assembles fragments of a scene and fills the gaps with what seems plausible.

许多人把记忆想象成一种录像：事件发生，大脑存储，多年后我们按下播放键。心理学家早就指出，这个比喻具有误导性。回忆更接近于重建：大脑把一个场景的碎片拼合起来，再用看似合理的内容填补空缺。

Evidence for this comes from experiments in which volunteers watch the same short film and are later asked about it. Merely changing a verb in the question, from ‘touched’ to ‘smashed’, for example, can alter how fast people recall a vehicle to have been moving. Their memories are not random; they are quietly adjusted by the wording they have just heard.

这方面的证据来自一些实验：志愿者观看同一段短片，之后被问及相关问题。比如，仅仅把问题中的一个动词从“碰到”改成“撞碎”，就可能改变人们回忆中车辆行驶速度的快慢。他们的记忆并非随意，而是被刚刚听到的措辞悄悄调整了。

The same mechanism operates at the level of groups. When families and communities repeat a story, each retelling tends to strengthen the parts that fit the group’s identity and weaken those that do not. Over time, this process produces a shared account that feels vivid and certain, even though it may differ in important respects from what actually occurred.

同样的机制也在群体层面起作用。当家庭和社群反复讲述一个故事，每一次复述往往会强化符合群体身份的部分，削弱不符合的部分。久而久之，这一过程会生成一个鲜活而确定的共同叙述，尽管它在重要方面可能与实际发生的事情有所不同。

None of this means that memory is worthless. By and large, our recollections of major events are broadly accurate, and written records, photographs and independent witnesses help correct the errors that creep in. While confidence is often mistaken for accuracy, the two are only loosely related; a person can be entirely sincere and still be wrong about a detail.

这并不意味着记忆毫无价值。总体而言，我们对重大事件的回忆大体上是准确的，而书面记录、照片和独立的见证者有助于纠正悄然混入的错误。虽然人们常把自信误当作准确，但两者只有松散的关联；一个人可以完全真诚，却仍然记错某个细节。

The practical lesson is a modest one. When we revisit the past, whether our own or a nation’s, we are not opening a vault but rebuilding a house from its plans, and it is wise to check the plans against other sources.

实际的启示很朴素。当我们回顾过去，无论是自己的还是一个国家的，我们并不是在打开一个保险库，而是在依据图纸重建一座房子，明智的做法是把图纸与其他来源相互核对。

Long sentences, unpacked长句拆解

While confidence is often mistaken for accuracy, the two are only loosely related; a person can be entirely sincere and still be wrong about a detail.
’While’ opens a concession clause with a passive verb (‘is mistaken for’), and the semicolon joins a related second point about sincerity and error.
While 引出让步从句，其中 is mistaken for 为被动结构；分号后接着补充另一层意思：真诚并不等于正确。
Remembering is closer to reconstruction, in which the brain assembles fragments of a scene and fills the gaps with what seems plausible.
’In which’ begins a non-defining relative clause that explains what reconstruction involves, and ‘what seems plausible’ is a noun clause serving as the object of ‘with’.
in which 引导非限制性定语从句，解释“重建”的含义；what seems plausible 是名词性从句，作介词 with 的宾语。

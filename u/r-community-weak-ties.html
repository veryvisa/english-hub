Why the People You Barely Know Matter 点头之交为什么重要

Think about the people you said hello to this week: the cashier at the grocery store, the dog owner you meet on your morning walk, the neighbour who waves from her porch. Sociologists call these relationships "weak ties", and research suggests they may matter more to our wellbeing than most of us expect.

想想你这周打过招呼的人：超市收银员、早上散步时遇到的遛狗人、在门廊上冲你挥手的邻居。社会学家把这类关系叫作「弱关系」，研究显示，它们对我们身心状态的影响，可能比大多数人以为的要大。

In several studies, people reported feeling happier and more connected on days when they had more short, friendly conversations with acquaintances, even when they spent the same time with close friends. One explanation is simple: each small exchange reminds us that we belong to a place. Over time, this builds a quiet confidence that the people around us are reliable, and that someone would notice if something went wrong.

在几项研究里，人们说，在和熟人有更多简短、友好交谈的日子里，自己更开心、也更觉得和周围有联系，哪怕那天和好朋友相处的时间并没有变。一种解释很简单：每一次小小的交流都在提醒我们，自己属于这个地方。久而久之，人会生出一种安静的信心，觉得身边的人靠得住，出了事会有人察觉。

Weak ties are also how information travels. Your close friends mostly know what you know. A neighbour you barely know, on the other hand, may have heard about a job opening, a family doctor taking new patients, or a resident down the street who needs help with snow. This may be one reason why newcomers who keep to themselves often take longer to settle in, even when their English is good.

弱关系也是信息流动的渠道。好朋友知道的，大多你也知道。而一个你几乎不认识的邻居，可能听说过某个职位空缺、某位在收新病人的家庭医生，或者街那头某位需要人帮忙铲雪的居民。这也许就是为什么独来独往的新移民往往要更久才能安顿下来，哪怕他们英语不错。

The research has limits. Most studies are small, and it is hard to separate cause from effect: happier people may simply talk to more strangers. Still, the cost of testing it yourself is low. It takes some willingness to look slightly silly, and a lower expectation of each conversation. Not every chat will turn into a friendship, and it does not need to.

这些研究也有局限。大多数样本不大，因果也很难分开：也许只是更快乐的人本来就更爱跟陌生人说话。不过，自己试一试的代价很低。只需要愿意显得有点傻，再把对每次聊天的期待放低一点。不是每次闲聊都会变成友谊，也不需要。

Awareness is the first step. Next time you pass a familiar face, try one more sentence than usual. A neighbourhood is built out of these small moments.

意识到这一点是第一步。下次遇到一张熟面孔，试着比平时多说一句。一个街区就是由这些小小的时刻搭起来的。

Words词

/ˈbeəli/ adv.
几乎不；刚刚
barely know sb; barely enough
I barely knew my neighbours before the storm. 暴风雪之前我几乎不认识邻居。
/wiːk ˈtaɪ/ n.
弱关系（点头之交式的联系）
strong and weak ties; the strength of weak ties
Many people find jobs through weak ties. 很多人是通过弱关系找到工作的。
/bɪˈlɒŋ/ v.
属于；有归属感
belong to a place; a sense of belonging
It took her years to feel she belonged here. 她过了好几年才觉得自己属于这里。
/fəˈmɪliə feɪs/ n. phr.
熟面孔
a familiar face; see a few familiar faces
It was good to see a familiar face at the meeting. 在会上看到一张熟面孔挺好的。
/ˈwɪlɪŋnəs/ n.
意愿；乐意
willingness to help; show willingness
What matters most is a willingness to try. 最要紧的是愿意去试。

Long sentences, unpacked长句拆解

Over time, this builds a quiet confidence that the people around us are reliable, and that someone would notice if something went wrong.
confidence that + clause: the noun is followed by what you feel sure about. would … if … went is an imagined situation, not a past one.
confidence that 后接从句说明对什么有信心。would … if … went 是设想的情况，不是过去。
It takes some willingness to look slightly silly, and a lower expectation of each conversation.
Two nouns from the word families willing → willingness and expect → expectation. It takes + noun: this is what you need. Note expectation of, not expectation to.
两个名词来自词族 willing → willingness、expect → expectation。It takes 加名词＝需要这个。注意是 expectation of，不说 expectation to。

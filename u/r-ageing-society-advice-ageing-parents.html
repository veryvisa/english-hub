How Do I Talk to My Parents About the Future? 我该如何和父母谈论未来？

🧠 Learn the 4 new words of this unit, one card at a time逐张学本单元的 4 个新词

Talking to ageing parents about their future works best when you listen first, ask practical questions and share the load with siblings. 与年迈的父母谈论未来，最好的做法是先倾听，提出实际的问题，并与兄弟姐妹分担责任。

Hear the whole topic in a row整个主题连着听

A reader writes: My parents are in their seventies and still live in the family home. I worry about them but they refuse to discuss the future. Every time I mention care or moving, they change the subject. How do I raise it without causing a quarrel?

读者来信：我的父母已年过七十，仍住在家里的老房子里。我很担心他们，可他们拒绝谈论未来。每次我一提到护理或搬家，他们就转换话题。我该怎样提起这件事，又不引发争吵？

You are not alone. Many adult children find that this conversation is harder than expected, partly because it reverses a lifetime of roles. While your concern is perfectly natural, parents tend to hear worry as a loss of independence. Start by listening, not by presenting solutions, and ask what matters most to them about staying at home.

你并不孤单。许多成年子女发现这场谈话比预想的更难，部分原因在于它颠倒了一生中的角色。你的担心完全自然，但父母往往会把担忧听成是对独立的剥夺。先从倾听开始，而不是抛出解决方案，并问问他们，留在家里对他们来说最重要的是什么。

Choose a calm moment, preferably not during a family celebration. Frame the talk around practical questions, such as who they would want to make decisions if one of them fell ill, and whether the house will still suit them in ten years. Questions like these are likely to feel less threatening than a list of your own fears. Be ready for several short conversations rather than one big one.

选一个平静的时刻，最好不要在家庭庆祝活动期间。围绕实际问题展开交谈，比如如果其中一人生病，他们希望由谁来做决定，以及十年后这所房子是否仍然适合他们。这类问题往往比一连串你自己的恐惧更不容易让人感到威胁。要做好准备进行几次简短的谈话，而不是一次大谈。

It also helps to involve siblings early, so that no single person is left to shoulder the burden alone. Agree who will do what, and be honest about your own limits. Caring for someone is rewarding, but it can take its toll if it is never shared. If your parents still decline to talk, accept that for now and try again later; patience tends to work better than pressure.

尽早让兄弟姐妹参与也有帮助，这样就不会只有一个人独自承担重担。商量好谁做什么，并坦率说明自己的极限。照顾他人是有回报的，但如果从不分担，也会造成消耗。如果父母仍然不肯谈，暂时接受，稍后再试；耐心往往比施压更有效。

Finally, remember that an ageing population is made of individuals, and your parents are entitled to make choices that you would not make. Your role is to make sure those choices are informed, not to make them on their behalf. That balance is difficult, but it is the one that preserves both safety and dignity.

最后，请记住，老龄人口是由一个个具体的人组成的，你的父母有权做出你不会做的选择。你的角色是确保这些选择是在知情的前提下做出的，而不是代替他们做选择。这种平衡很难把握，但它能同时保护安全与尊严。

Long sentences, unpacked长句拆解

While your concern is perfectly natural, parents tend to hear worry as a loss of independence.
The ‘While’ clause concedes that the concern is natural, and ‘tend to’ softens the claim about what parents hear.
While 从句承认担忧是自然的，tend to 则缓和了关于父母如何理解这种担忧的说法。
Questions like these are likely to feel less threatening than a list of your own fears.
’Are likely to’ hedges the prediction, and ‘less threatening than’ is a comparative structure with a noun phrase after ‘than’.
are likely to 使预测带有保留；less threatening than 是比较结构，than 后接名词短语。

📏 The rule规则

📏 The point in one line一句话要领
Positive can is weak and unstressed /kən/, with the stress on the verb; can't is strong /kɑːnt/ (British) or /kænt/ (North American), stressed along with the verb. For numbers, -teen is stressed and ends in a long /iːn/; -ty is stressed on the first syllable and ends in a short /ti/ or /di/.肯定的 can 弱读 /kən/，重音在动词上；can't 强读，和动词一起重读。数字里 -teen 重读、以长 /iːn/ 结尾；-ty 重读第一节、以短 /ti/ 或 /di/ 结尾。

📖 Why, and when细讲：为什么、什么时候用

Open the full explanation: 5 points展开细讲：5 段讲解

A caller asks a dental office, "Can you see me on Friday?" and hears "I can see you at four." Or was it "I can't"? In North American speech the final /t/ of can't is often swallowed, so the letter that seems to make the difference is barely there. What really separates them is rhythm: I can SEE you has a weak, quick can; I CAN'T SEE you has two strong beats in a row.

打电话问诊所周五能不能看，听到「四点可以」——还是「不行」？北美口语里 can't 的 /t/ 常被吞掉，看似决定差别的字母几乎听不到。真正区分它们的是节奏：can 弱而快，can't 和后面的动词连着两个强拍。

Chinese speakers often stress can because it feels important (it is the answer to the question). But a stressed can with a full vowel sounds exactly like can't to many listeners. Make positive can smaller than you think: /kən/, almost /kn/. Make can't full and long, and do not worry if the /t/ is light. The exceptions are short answers and contrast: Yes, I CAN. / I CAN come, but I'd rather not.

华人常重读 can，因为它感觉重要。可是重读、元音饱满的 can，很多听者会听成 can't。把肯定的 can 说得比你以为的更小，把 can't 说得饱满拉长，/t/ 轻一点没关系。例外是简短回答和对比。

Numbers from 13 to 19 and 30 to 90 confuse almost everyone, native speakers included, which is why careful speakers repeat them. In isolation: thirTEEN with a long /iːn/, THIRty with a short end. North American speakers often turn the t in thirty into a quick /d/-like flap, /ˈθɜːrdi/, which is another clue. Saying the number with the right stress is half; the other half is the habit of confirming: That's one-five, fifteen?

13–19 和 30–90 几乎人人会混，母语者也一样，所以讲究的人会重复确认。单独说时 -teen 长、-ty 短；北美人常把 thirty 的 t 读成闪音，这也是线索。重音说对是一半，另一半是确认的习惯。

Be careful: in a phrase, -teen numbers can lose their final stress. FOURteen DOLlars and thirteen PEOple shift the stress forward because the noun after them is stressed. So the long /iːn/ matters more than the stress position. If you hear a long, clear -een with an /n/, it is a teen; if you hear a short -ty / -dy, it is a ten.

注意：在短语里，-teen 数字可能失去词尾重音，因为后面的名词要重读。所以长 /iːn/ 比重音位置更可靠：听到长而清楚的 -een 加 /n/，就是十几；听到短的 -ty，就是几十。

For prices, times, addresses, flight numbers and dates, the safe routine is: say it, then spell or split it. It's at fourteen, one-four, Main Street. The total's fifty, five-oh. This is not a sign of weak English; it is what professionals do on the phone.

价格、时间、地址、航班号、日期，稳妥的做法是：说一遍，再拆开或逐位说一遍。这不是英语差的表现，是电话里专业人士的做法。

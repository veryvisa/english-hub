Intonation in questions and lists 疑问与列举的语调

📘 In your coursebook: English File 5e · Upper-Intermediate · 1A “A tricky question?”. Use the texts and recordings with your coursebook; this page adds new words, grammar and practice.教材定位：English File 5e · Upper-Intermediate · 1A《A tricky question?》。课文与录音请配合教材学习，本页补充新词、语法与练习。

Use falling and rising tunes correctly in questions, lists and choices, so that people know whether you are asking, checking or finished. 在疑问句、列举和选择里用对升调与降调，让对方知道你是在提问、在确认，还是说完了。

B2 · CELPIP 7–8⏱ 25 minP10 Flat or wrong intonation; the important word is not stressed语调平，重点词不突出

📏 The rule规则

📏 The point in one line一句话要领
Wh- questions usually fall; yes/no questions usually rise. In a list every item rises except the last, which falls. In a choice, A or B? rises on A and falls on B; if it rises on both, it means "A or anything like it?"特殊疑问句通常降调，一般疑问句通常升调。列举时除最后一项外都升，最后一项降。选择问句前升后降；两个都升，意思就变成「A 之类的东西要不要？」。
  • Easy to miss易漏 ✗ Would you like tea or coffee ↘? — Yes, please. → ✓ Tea, please.

Where do you work? ↘ /ˈweə də ju ˈwɜːk/
Normal wh- question: fall on WORK. 普通特殊疑问句，WORK 上降。
Where do you work? ↗ /ˈweə də ju ˈwɜːk/
Checking: "Sorry, where?" 确认：没听清，再说一次？
Are you from Vancouver? ↗ /ə ju frəm vænˈkuːvə/
Rise starts on VAN and keeps climbing. 升调从 VAN 开始一直往上。
Are you coming? ↘ /ə ju ˈkʌmɪŋ/
A fall sounds direct, even impatient. 降调直接，甚至不耐烦。
Would you like tea ↗ or coffee ↘? /wʊdʒu laɪk ˈtiː ɔː ˈkɒfi/
Closed choice: pick one. 二选一。
Would you like tea or coffee ↗? /wʊdʒu laɪk ˈtiː ɔː ˈkɒfi/
Open offer: anything to drink? 开放询问：喝点什么吗？
We need milk ↗, bread ↗, eggs ↗ and butter ↘. /wi niːd ˈmɪlk ˈbred ˈeɡz ən ˈbʌtə/
Rise, rise, rise, fall. 升、升、升、降。
I've been to Toronto ↗, Ottawa ↗ and Montreal ↘. /aɪv bɪn tə təˈrɒntəʊ ˈɒtəwə ən ˌmɒntriˈɔːl/
The fall closes the list. 降调结束列举。
It's cold today, isn't it ↘? /ɪts ˈkəʊld təˈdeɪ ˈɪzənt ɪt/
Sure, inviting agreement. 有把握，请对方附和。
You've sent it, haven't you ↗? /juv ˈsent ɪt ˈhævənt ju/
Not sure, a real question. 没把握，真在问。
What time does it start? ↘ /ˈwɒt ˈtaɪm dəz ɪt ˈstɑːt/
The fall on START is enough; no rise at the end. START 上降即可，句末别再上扬。
Is it on Monday or Tuesday? /ɪz ɪt ɒn ˈmʌndeɪ ɔː ˈtjuːzdeɪ/
MON↗, TUES↘: one of the two. 前升后降，二者选一。
Do you take cards? ↗ /də ju teɪk ˈkɑːdz/
Polite yes/no question with a rise. 礼貌的一般疑问句用升调。
Sorry, how much? ↗ /ˈsɒri haʊ ˈmʌtʃ/
Rise to ask for a repeat. 升调请对方重复。
First you fill in the form ↗, then you book a time ↗, and then you bring your documents ↘. /ˈfɜːst ju fɪl ɪn ðə ˈfɔːm ðen ju bʊk ə ˈtaɪm ən ðen ju brɪŋ jə ˈdɒkjəmənts/
Steps in a list follow the same pattern. 步骤列举同样规律。
Red, white, or blue? /ˈred ˈwaɪt ɔː ˈbluː/
Rise, rise, fall: choose one of three. 升、升、降：三选一。

📖 Why, and when细讲：为什么、什么时候用

Open the full explanation: 5 points展开细讲：5 段讲解

A waiter asks, "Would you like tea or coffee?" with a fall at the end, and the guest says, "Yes, please." The waiter is confused: a fall on coffee means "which one?", a closed choice. A rise on both, tea or coffee↗, would have meant "anything to drink?", and Yes, please would be fine. The words are identical; the tune decides the question.

服务员问「茶还是咖啡？」，在 coffee 上降调，客人答「好的」，服务员就糊涂了：降调是「选哪个」；两个都升才是「要喝点什么吗」。字一样，问的是什么由语调决定。

Mandarin uses pitch for tones on each syllable, so learners often keep their pitch in a narrow band for whole sentences, or end everything with the same small rise. In English the big movement happens once per phrase, on the nucleus (the last stressed word), and continues across the syllables after it. In Where do you WORK? the fall starts on work; in Are you from VANcouver? the rise starts on VAN and keeps climbing through -couver.

普通话的音高用在每个字的声调上，所以学习者整句音域偏窄，或者句末都带同样的小上扬。英语每个短语只在核心重音上做一次大的音高移动，并延续到后面的音节。

The default is not a law. A wh- question with a rise sounds like you are checking something you missed: Where do you work↗? means "sorry, where did you say?". A yes/no question with a fall sounds direct, sometimes impatient: Are you coming↘? Use the rise on wh- questions for repeating and checking, and soften yes/no questions with a rise when you are not sure of the answer.

默认不是铁律。特殊疑问句用升调，是在确认没听清的内容；一般疑问句用降调，听起来直接，有时显得不耐烦。

Lists work like a door that stays open. Each rise says "more is coming"; the final fall shuts the door. We need milk↗, bread↗, eggs↗ and butter↘. If you fall on every item, the listener thinks you have finished after milk. If you rise on the last item too, it sounds as if the list is open: "...and so on".

列举像一扇开着的门：每个升调都在说「还有」，最后的降调把门关上。每项都降，对方会以为你说完了；最后一项也升，听起来像「等等」。

Tag questions follow the same logic: a fall means "I'm sure, please agree" (It's cold today, isn't it↘?); a rise means "I really want to know" (You've sent it, haven't you↗?). In listening, these tunes tell you whether you are expected to answer or just to nod.

附加疑问句同理：降调是「我确定，你附和一下」，升调是「我真的想知道」。听力上，语调告诉你对方是要你回答，还是点头就行。

⚠️ Common slips for Chinese speakers华人常错

✗ Would you like tea or coffee ↘? — Yes, please.
✓ Tea, please.
A fall on the last option asks which one; yes is not an answer.
末项降调问的是选哪个，答 yes 不对题。
✗ milk ↘, bread ↘, eggs ↘ (falling on every item)
✓ milk ↗, bread ↗, eggs ↘
Each fall sounds like the end, and the listener stops listening.
每个降调都像结束，对方就不再听了。
✗ What time does it start ↗? (rising out of habit)
✓ What time does it start ↘?
A rise on a wh- question sounds as if you did not hear the answer before.
特殊疑问句升调听起来像没听清前面的回答。
✗ Small rise at the end of every sentence
✓ One clear movement on the nucleus
A habitual rise makes statements sound uncertain.
习惯性上扬让陈述听起来没把握。

录一段你可能真会打的电话：两个特殊疑问句、一个一般疑问句、给出两个时间供选，再复述三样要带的东西。先在纸上标箭头，回放时逐个核对，再问自己：单凭语调，对方能不能听出你什么时候说完？

Why Clever People Fall for Scams 聪明人为什么也会上当

🧠 Learn the 3 new words of this unit, one card at a time逐张学本单元的 3 个新词

Read a short explainer on the psychology of scams, and learn the noun forms (awareness, confidence, willingness, expectation) that make an argument sound measured rather than absolute. 读一篇讲诈骗心理的说明文，学会 awareness、confidence、willingness、expectation 这些名词形式，让论述有分寸，不把话说死。

Ask people who have lost money to a scam how it happened, and many begin with the same sentence: "I'm not stupid, but…" They are right. Research on fraud suggests that education and intelligence give surprisingly little protection. Scams do not usually work by fooling the clever part of the brain; they work by switching it off.

问那些被骗过钱的人是怎么回事，很多人开口第一句都一样：「我不傻，但是……」他们说得对。关于诈骗的研究表明，学历和智力提供的保护少得出奇。骗局通常不是靠骗过大脑里聪明的那部分起作用，而是让它关机。

Most scams pull some mix of three levers. The first is authority: a caller who claims to be from a bank, a government office or the police. The second is urgency: a deadline in minutes, a frozen account, a warning that something terrible will happen today. The third is emotion, whether fear, hope or love. Under pressure, people make quick decisions and skip the checks they would normally do.

多数骗局会同时拉动三根杠杆中的几根。第一是权威：来电者自称来自银行、政府部门或警方。第二是紧迫：几分钟内的期限、被冻结的账户、今天就会发生可怕事情的警告。第三是情绪，恐惧、希望或爱。在压力之下，人会很快做决定，跳过平时会做的核查。

Confidence can make things worse. People who are sure they would spot a scammer are sometimes less careful, because they do not expect to be targeted. Older adults are often described as the most vulnerable group, but younger people seem to lose money to online shopping and job scams at least as often; they simply tend to lose smaller amounts each time.

自信反而可能让事情更糟。那些确信自己一眼能认出骗子的人，有时反而不够小心，因为他们没想到自己会成为目标。老年人常被说成最容易受骗的群体，但年轻人在网购和招聘骗局里被骗的频率似乎至少一样高，只是每次往往被骗得少一些。

What helps is not more knowledge but a habit. Experts recommend one simple rule: when a message creates urgency, treat that as a red flag in itself. Hang up, close the chat, and contact the organisation through a number you already know is reliable. A real bank will show a willingness to wait; a scammer will not.

有用的不是多懂一点，而是一个习惯。专家推荐一条简单规则：只要一条消息在制造紧迫感，就把这本身当作危险信号。挂电话、关掉聊天，用你本来就知道可靠的号码去联系那家机构。真银行愿意等你；骗子不愿意。

Public awareness campaigns tend to focus on the details of the latest scam, but the details change every month. The pattern does not. If you remember the three levers, and keep the expectation that real organisations let you slow down, you will recognise most scams you meet, including ones nobody has invented yet.

提高公众意识的宣传往往聚焦于最新骗局的细节，但细节每个月都在变，套路却不变。记住这三根杠杆，并且始终预期真正的机构会允许你慢下来，你遇到的大多数骗局都能认出来，包括那些还没人发明出来的。

Long sentences, unpacked长句拆解

Scams do not usually work by fooling the clever part of the brain; they work by switching it off.
work by + -ing explains the method. The semicolon sets two methods side by side: fooling vs switching off. usually hedges the claim.
work by + -ing 说明方式。分号把两种方式并列：fooling 与 switching off。usually 给结论留了余地。
A real bank will show a willingness to wait; a scammer will not.
willingness is the noun of willing (-ness). The second half drops the repeated verb: will not (show a willingness to wait).
willingness 是 willing 的名词（加 -ness）。后半句省略了重复的部分：will not（show a willingness to wait）。

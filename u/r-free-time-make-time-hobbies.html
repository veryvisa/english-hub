Too Busy for a Hobby? 忙得没时间发展爱好？

🧠 Learn the 2 new words of this unit, one card at a time逐张学本单元的 2 个新词

You can find time for a hobby by planning it on purpose, starting small, and not worrying about being good. 只要有意识地安排时间、从小处开始、不担心做得好不好，你就能为爱好找到时间。

Dear Reader, you wrote to say that you have no time for hobbies. You work long hours, you look after your family, and by the evening you are too tired to do anything. I understand, and many people feel the same way.

亲爱的读者，你来信说你没有时间发展爱好。你工作时间很长，还要照顾家人，到了晚上累得什么都不想做。我理解，很多人都有同感。

My first piece of advice is simple: do not wait for free time to appear. It almost never does. Instead, make time for your hobby the way you make time for a meeting. Choose one short slot, perhaps twenty minutes after dinner, and write it in your diary.

我的第一个建议很简单：别等空闲时间自己冒出来，它几乎从不会。相反，要像安排会议那样为你的爱好留出时间。选一个短短的时段，比如晚饭后二十分钟，并把它写进日程本。

Second, start small. If you take up something that needs expensive equipment or a long journey, you will probably give up quickly. A notebook for drawing, a pair of trainers for walking, or a simple guitar is enough at the beginning.

第二，从小处开始。如果你开始做的事需要昂贵的装备或很长的路程，你很可能很快就会放弃。一本画画用的本子、一双走路用的运动鞋，或一把简单的吉他，一开始就足够了。

Third, do not worry about being good. Adults often stop trying because they feel embarrassed. But everyone is a beginner once, and people tend to get better at a skill faster than they expect when they practise a little every week.

第三，别担心自己做得好不好。成年人常常因为觉得难为情而不再尝试。但每个人都有当初学者的时候，而且只要每周练习一点，人们往往比自己预期的更快进步。

Finally, remember why you are doing this. A hobby is not another job on your list. It is a way to unwind and to remember that you are more than your work. Try it for a month, and write to me again to tell me how it went. I am looking forward to hearing from you.

最后，记住你为什么这样做。爱好不是你清单上的又一份工作，而是一种放松的方式，提醒你自己不只是工作。试一个月，然后再写信告诉我进展如何。我期待收到你的来信。

/meɪk taɪm fɔː/ phr.
为……抽出时间
make time for friends; make time for exercise
Try to make time for a short walk every day. 每天尽量抽出时间短暂散个步。
/teɪk ʌp/ phr. v.
开始从事（爱好）
take up a sport; take up a new skill
Many people take up gardening in spring. 许多人在春天开始养花种草。
/ɡɪv ʌp/ phr. v.
放弃
give up easily; never give up
Do not give up if the first lesson is difficult. 如果第一节课很难，别放弃。
/ɡet ˈbetə æt/ phr.
在……方面变得更好
get better at a skill; get better at speaking
You will get better at swimming if you go twice a week. 如果你每周去两次，游泳会越来越好。
/ɪmˈbærəst/ adj.
尴尬的，难为情的
feel embarrassed; embarrassed about
He felt embarrassed when he forgot the words to the song. 他忘了歌词时觉得很尴尬。

Long sentences, unpacked长句拆解

If you take up something that needs expensive equipment or a long journey, you will probably give up quickly.
This is a first conditional sentence, and ‘that needs expensive equipment or a long journey’ is a relative clause describing ‘something’.
这是第一条件句；that needs expensive equipment or a long journey 是修饰 something 的定语从句。
But everyone is a beginner once, and people tend to get better at a skill faster than they expect when they practise a little every week.
’Tend to’ is followed by the base verb and makes the claim less absolute, while ‘faster than’ compares two things.
tend to 后接动词原形，使说法不那么绝对；faster than 用来做比较。

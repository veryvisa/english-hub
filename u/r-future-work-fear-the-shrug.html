Hear the whole topic in a row整个主题连着听

Every few years, a fresh wave of anxiety washes over the workplace, and the cause is almost always a new machine. Today it is software that can write, draw and analyse. It is tempting to conclude that millions of jobs are about to vanish, but history suggests a more complicated story.

每隔几年，新一轮焦虑就会席卷职场，而起因几乎总是一种新机器。如今它是能写作、绘画和分析的软件。人们很容易断定数以百万计的工作即将消失，但历史讲述的故事要复杂得多。

That said, I do not want to dismiss the worry. Technology has a habit of destroying tasks faster than it creates new ones, and the people who lose out are rarely the ones who designed the change. A displaced worker cannot simply adapt to a different industry overnight, however many inspiring speeches are made about lifelong learning.

话虽如此，我并不想轻视这种担忧。技术往往摧毁任务的速度快于创造新任务的速度，而吃亏的人很少是设计这场变革的人。一个被取代的工人无法一夜之间适应另一个行业，无论人们发表多少关于终身学习的激励演讲。

What worries me more is the attitude of those in charge. On the whole, employers talk about reskilling as if it were a favour rather than a shared responsibility. Workers are told to build a broader skill set in their spare evenings, while the company that benefits from it takes no part in the effort.

更让我担心的是当权者的态度。总体而言，雇主谈论技能再培训时，仿佛那是一种恩惠而非共同责任。员工被告知要利用业余的夜晚拓宽自己的技能组合，而从中受益的公司却不参与这份努力。

A sensible approach would look different. Firms would pay for training during working hours, governments would support people moving between sectors, and schools would teach judgement and collaboration alongside technical knowledge. None of this is cheap, yet the cost of doing nothing is likely to be far higher, because a disillusioned workforce rarely produces good work.

一种明智的做法会截然不同。公司会在工作时间内出钱培训，政府会支持人们在行业之间流动，学校会在技术知识之外教授判断力与协作能力。这一切都不便宜，但无所作为的代价很可能高得多，因为心灰意冷的员工队伍很少能做出好成绩。

So I would urge readers to resist both panic and complacency. The future of work will not be decided by machines. It will be decided by the choices we make about who pays for change, who gets a say in it, and who is left behind when the dust settles.

因此我想劝读者既不要恐慌，也不要自满。工作的未来不会由机器来决定，而将由我们的选择来决定：谁来为变革买单，谁在其中有发言权，以及尘埃落定时谁被落在后面。

Long sentences, unpacked长句拆解

That said, I do not want to dismiss the worry.
’That said’ is a discourse marker that concedes a point after the previous statement, preparing the reader for a qualified view.
That said 是话语标记语，在前文之后做出让步，为接下来有所保留的观点做铺垫。
None of this is cheap, yet the cost of doing nothing is likely to be far higher, because a disillusioned workforce rarely produces good work.
The sentence joins a concession (‘None of this is cheap, yet’) with a hedged claim (‘is likely to be’) and a reason clause introduced by ‘because’.
句子把让步（None of this is cheap, yet）、带限定的判断（is likely to be）和由 because 引导的原因从句连在一起。

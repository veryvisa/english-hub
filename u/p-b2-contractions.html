Hearing and saying contractions 缩略形式的听与说

Hear the tiny contracted sounds that change tense and meaning (I'd, we'll, they've), and use contractions in speech so that you sound natural instead of formal. 听出那些改变时态和意思的细小缩略音（I'd、we'll、they've），说话时也用缩略形式，听起来自然而不生硬。

📏 The rule规则

📏 The point in one line一句话要领
In speech, auxiliaries are almost always contracted: I'll, she's, we'd, they've. The contracted part is often a single consonant, and it carries the tense, so missing it changes the meaning: I'd go is not I go.口语里助动词几乎总是缩略。缩略部分常常只剩一个辅音，却承载时态，漏掉就改了意思。

I'd like a window seat. /aɪd ˈlaɪk ə ˈwɪndəʊ siːt/
'd = would: /d/ links to like. 'd 是 would。
I'd left before he arrived. /aɪd ˈleft bɪˈfɔːr i əˈraɪvd/
'd + past participle = had. 'd 后接过去分词是 had。
They've already paid. /ðeɪv ɔːlˈredi ˈpeɪd/
Hold the /v/. 保住 /v/。
We've been here for years. /wiv bɪn ˈhɪə fə ˈjɪəz/
/v/ + /b/: do not add a vowel. /v/ 接 /b/，别加元音。
She's working today. /ʃiz ˈwɜːkɪŋ təˈdeɪ/
's = is (before -ing). -ing 前的 's 是 is。
She's worked here since 2020. /ʃiz ˈwɜːkt hɪə sɪns ˌtwenti ˈtwenti/
's = has (before a past participle). 过去分词前的 's 是 has。
You're right. /jɔː ˈraɪt/
Sounds like your. 听起来像 your。
There's been a change. /ðəz bɪn ə ˈtʃeɪndʒ/

📖 Why, and when细讲：为什么、什么时候用

Open the full explanation: 5 points展开细讲：5 段讲解

A tenant reads a message aloud from the landlord: "We'll fix the heater." He tells his partner, "They fix the heater," as if it were something the landlord does every winter, not a repair booked for next week. The whole future lived in one /l/. Contractions are short, but they are where English keeps its tense, its conditionals and its perfect aspect.

租客把房东的消息念给伴侣听，把 We'll fix 说成 They fix。整个「将来」都在一个 /l/ 里。缩略形式虽短，英语的时态、条件和完成体都存在这里。

The common contractions reduce to one sound: 'll is a dark /l/, 'd is /d/, 've is /v/, 's is /s/ or /z/, 're is /ə/ or nothing. Chinese speakers often drop these because Mandarin has no such endings, and because a final consonant followed by another consonant is hard (I'd like, we've been). The fix is the same as for final consonants: hold the sound and link it to the next word, without adding a vowel.

常见缩略形式只剩一个音。华人常漏掉，一是普通话没有这类词尾，二是辅音后再接辅音不好发。办法和词尾辅音一样：把音保住，连到下一个词上，不加元音。

'd and 's each stand for two different words. 'd can be would or had: I'd like = would, I'd left = had (followed by a past participle). 's can be is or has: she's working = is, she's worked = has. You tell them apart by what follows, so listen past the contraction to the verb form.

'd 可以是 would 或 had，'s 可以是 is 或 has。靠后面的动词形式分辨，所以要越过缩略形式去听后面的动词。

Full forms are not more polite; they are emphatic. I do not agree in conversation sounds angry or ironic, and I will come sounds like a promise under pressure. Keep full forms for stress (I WILL be there), for short answers (Yes, I have.) and for formal writing. Everywhere else, contract. Negatives have two options, she isn't and she's not; the second puts more weight on not.

全写形式并不更礼貌，而是带强调。对话里说 I do not agree 显得生气或讽刺。全写留给强调、简短回答和正式写作，其他时候都缩略。否定有两种缩略，后一种更突出 not。

In listening, expect contractions where you see pronouns. After I, you, we, they, he, she, it and there, a tiny sound before the verb is probably an auxiliary, and it usually decides the time of the action. If a sentence seems to have the wrong tense, replay it and listen for /l/, /d/ or /v/ right after the pronoun.

听力上，代词后面预期有缩略。代词和动词之间那个小音多半是助动词，通常决定动作的时间。觉得时态不对，就回放，专听代词后的 /l/、/d/、/v/。

⚠️ Common slips for Chinese speakers华人常错

✗ I like a window seat. (meaning: I'd like)
✓ I'd like a window seat.
Without /d/ it is a general preference, not a request.
没有 /d/ 就成了一般喜好，不是请求。
✗ I do not know. (in a casual chat)
✓ I don't know.
The full form sounds emphatic or annoyed.
全写听起来强调或不耐烦。
✗ Yes, I've.
✓ Yes, I have.
Contractions cannot end a short answer.
简短回答不能以缩略形式结尾。
✗ Hearing "She's worked" as present
✓ 's + past participle = has
Listen to the verb form after the contraction.
听缩略形式后面的动词形式。

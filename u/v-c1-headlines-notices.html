📖 Why, and when细讲：为什么、什么时候用

Open the full explanation: 5 points展开细讲：5 段讲解

Headlines usually drop articles and the verb 'to be', so 'Minister to resign' means 'The minister is going to resign'. The infinitive 'to' signals a future event, and the simple present tense usually describes something that already happened, as in 'Minister resigns' (meaning 'resigned'). Watch out for -ed forms like 'Hundreds evacuated': without more context you cannot always tell whether this is simple past (the people left on their own) or a reduced passive (they were evacuated by officials); only the rest of the article tells you which. Knowing these conventions, and knowing when a headline stays ambiguous, helps you decode most headlines.

标题通常省略冠词和 be 动词，所以 'Minister to resign' 的意思是「部长将要辞职」。不定式 to 表示将来，一般现在时通常表示已经发生的事，如 'Minister resigns'（意思是「已经辞职」）。要留意像 'Hundreds evacuated' 这样的 -ed 形式：不看后文，无法确定这是一般过去时（这些人自己离开了）还是省略了 were 的被动（他们被官方疏散了），要靠正文内容判断。掌握这些规律、也知道哪些标题本身就有歧义，能帮助你读懂大多数标题。

Headline writers favour short, dramatic verbs because space is limited: 'slash' means cut, 'probe' means investigate, 'bid' means attempt, 'axe' means cancel or dismiss, and 'vow' means promise. They are common throughout news writing, not only in the headline itself, and 'probe' and 'bid' also work as ordinary nouns outside the news (a space probe, a takeover bid). But in casual speech and in a formal essay or email, the plainer verbs — cut, investigate, attempt, cancel/dismiss, promise — usually sound more natural, so recognise the news verbs but choose the plain ones for your own writing.

由于版面有限，标题作者偏爱简短、有冲击力的动词：slash 指削减，probe 指调查，bid 指尝试，axe 指取消或解雇，vow 指承诺。这些词不仅出现在标题里，整篇新闻报道中都很常见；probe 和 bid 在新闻之外也可以作普通名词（a space probe 探测器，a takeover bid 收购要约）。但在口语和正式的作文或邮件中，更朴素的词——cut、investigate、attempt、cancel/dismiss、promise——通常更自然，所以能认出这些新闻用词即可，自己写作时选朴素的词。

A common mistake among Chinese learners is to treat abbreviations as interchangeable. 'e.g.' introduces examples, while 'i.e.' restates something in other words. Likewise, 'ASAP', 'FYI', 'RSVP' and 'TBC' are standard in notices and emails, but some, such as 'ASAP', can sound pushy if you send them to a senior colleague. Check the register before you use them.

华人学习者常见的错误是把缩写当成可以互换的。e.g. 用来引出例子，i.e. 则是换一种说法重述。同样，ASAP、FYI、RSVP 和 TBC 在告示和邮件中都很常见，但像 ASAP 这样的缩写发给上级时可能显得催促。使用前要先考虑语体。

Signs and notices use fixed formulas rather than full sentences: 'No parking', 'Keep off the grass', 'Staff only', 'Out of order', 'Mind the gap'. Many are negative imperatives or noun phrases, and they are almost never translated word for word from Chinese. Learners often produce 'Please do not to step on the grass', which sounds unnatural. Memorise the standard formula for each situation instead of building it from scratch.

标识和告示使用固定套语，而不是完整句子：No parking、Keep off the grass、Staff only、Out of order、Mind the gap。其中很多是否定祈使句或名词短语，几乎不可能从中文逐字翻译。学习者常说出 'Please do not to step on the grass' 这样不自然的句子。应当记住每种场景的标准说法，而不是自己拼凑。

Formal notices rely on a particular register: 'with effect from', 'until further notice', 'we regret to inform you', 'failure to comply may result in'. These chunks signal authority and distance. Chinese learners often make notices too friendly or too long, adding apologies and explanations that a native-speaking reader does not expect. Keep the key facts first: what, who, when, and what happens if the rule is ignored.

正式告示依赖特定的语体：with effect from、until further notice、we regret to inform you、failure to comply may result in。这些固定搭配体现权威感和距离感。华人学习者常把告示写得过于友好或冗长，加入母语读者并不期待的道歉和解释。应当先给出关键信息：什么事、涉及谁、何时、违反规定会怎样。

⚠️ Common slips for Chinese speakers华人常错

✗ Please do not to step on the grass.
✓ Please keep off the grass.
After 'do not' we use the bare verb without 'to', and notices prefer fixed formulas such as 'Keep off the grass' to long, built-from-scratch sentences.
do not 后面接动词原形，不能加 to；而且告示更喜欢用 Keep off the grass 这类固定说法，而不是自己拼出的长句。
✗ Many fruits, i.e. apples and oranges, are on sale.
✓ Many fruits, e.g. apples and oranges, are on sale.
'e.g.' introduces examples from a larger group, while 'i.e.' restates or defines something. Apples and oranges are only examples, so 'e.g.' is correct.
e.g. 用来引出某一类中的例子，i.e. 则是重述或解释。苹果和橙子只是例子，所以应当用 e.g.。
✗ The lift is broken down. Out of order.
✓ The lift is out of order.
'Out of order' already means the machine does not work. Combining it with 'broken down' is redundant, and on a sign the short form alone is the standard.
out of order 本身就表示设备不能使用，再加 broken down 是重复；在告示上直接用短语形式才是标准写法。
✗ The company axes 500 workers yesterday. (as normal prose)
✓ The company cut 500 jobs yesterday.
'Axe' is headline language. In an essay or an email you should use neutral verbs such as 'cut' or 'dismiss', and use the past tense for a past event.
axe 是标题用语。在作文或邮件里应使用 cut、dismiss 等中性动词，并且用过去时表示过去的事。
✗ Please RSVP me before Friday.
✓ Please RSVP by Friday.
'RSVP' is used with 'to' plus the host, or without an object, and a deadline is introduced with 'by'. It is not followed directly by a personal pronoun.
RSVP 后面可以接 to 加主办方，或者不接宾语，截止时间用 by 引出，后面不能直接接人称代词。
✗ We have regret to inform you that the class is cancelled.
✓ We regret to inform you that the class has been cancelled.
The formula is 'regret to inform', not 'have regret'. 'Regret to + verb' is used for announcing bad news, and the cancellation is naturally passive.
固定说法是 regret to inform，而不是 have regret。regret to + 动词用于宣布坏消息，取消这件事自然用被动语态。

Final consonants without an extra vowel 词尾辅音不拖元音

End words on the consonant, without an extra vowel after it and without dropping it, so that like it, big house and bad sound clean. 词尾停在辅音上，既不在后面加元音，也不把它吞掉，让 like it、big house、bad 听起来干净。

📏 The rule规则

📏 The point in one line一句话要领
Close the mouth for the final consonant and stop there: no puff of vowel after it, and for b, d, g, v, z make the vowel before it a little longer instead.词尾辅音做出口型就停住，后面不带出元音；b、d、g、v、z 结尾时，改为把前面的元音拉长一点。

📖 Why, and when细讲：为什么、什么时候用

Open the full explanation: 5 points展开细讲：5 段讲解

Mandarin syllables end in a vowel or in n or ng, nothing else. When an English word ends in /k/, /t/, /d/ or /ɡ/, the mouth wants to finish the syllable the Chinese way and adds a small vowel: like-uh, big-uh, good-uh. To the listener every word now has one more syllable, the rhythm breaks, and short words stop being recognisable.

普通话的音节只以元音或 n、ng 结尾。英语词以 /k/、/t/、/d/、/ɡ/ 收尾时，嘴想按汉语的方式把音节收完，就加了一个小元音：like-uh、big-uh。对听者来说每个词多了一个音节，节奏乱了，短词也认不出来了。

The other half of the problem is the opposite: the final consonant is dropped altogether. bad, back and bat all become ba; seat and seed become see. Here the problem is not rhythm but meaning, and it shows up most in past tenses and plurals, covered in the next unit.

另一半问题正相反：词尾辅音整个吞掉，bad、back、bat 都成了 ba。这时伤的不是节奏而是词义，在过去式和复数上最明显（见下一单元）。

The native solution is an unreleased stop. For /p/, /t/, /k/ at the end of a phrase, make the closure (lips together, tongue on the ridge, back of tongue up) and simply hold it, with no puff. Before another consonant, as in good night or that man, do the same and go straight into the next sound. Before a vowel, link instead: like it is lai-kit, with the /k/ starting the next word.

母语者的做法是「不爆破」：短语末的 /p/、/t/、/k/ 做好闭合（双唇合上、舌尖抵齿龈、舌根抬起）就停住，不送气。后面接辅音时（good night、that man）同样闭合后直接进入下一个音。后面接元音时则连读：like it 读成 lai-kit。

How does a listener hear bad versus bat if the final consonant is not released? Mostly from the vowel. Before a voiced consonant (/b/, /d/, /ɡ/, /v/, /z/) the vowel is clearly longer; before a voiceless one (/p/, /t/, /k/, /f/, /s/) it is short and clipped. So the fix for bad is not a loud /d/, it is a longer /æ/. Mandarin speakers often make all vowels the same length here, which merges the pairs.

词尾不爆破，听者怎么分 bad 和 bat？主要靠元音。浊辅音前元音明显更长，清辅音前元音短而利落。所以修 bad 不是把 /d/ 读响，而是把 /æ/ 拉长。华人在这里往往元音一样长，词对就合并了。

For listening, this explains why fast speech seems to swallow word endings: the native speaker did say the /t/ in I can't do it, but only as a closure and a shorter vowel. Train yourself to hear the vowel length and the small silence, not a pop.

对听力来说，这解释了为什么快语速像是吞掉了词尾：母语者确实说了 I can't do it 里的 /t/，但只是一个闭合加一个更短的元音。要训练自己去听元音长短和那一下微小的停顿，而不是等爆破声。

⚠️ Common slips for Chinese speakers华人常错

✗ I like-uh it very much.
✓ I like it very much. (lai-kit)
The extra vowel adds a syllable and breaks the rhythm.
多出的元音加了一个音节，打乱节奏。
✗ It's a big-uh problem.
✓ It's a big problem.
Close for /ɡ/ and go straight into /p/.
/ɡ/ 闭合后直接接 /p/。
✗ The food was ba. (final /d/ dropped, short vowel)
✓ The food was bad. (longer vowel, closure)
With no ending and a short vowel, bad sounds like bat or back.
没有词尾、元音又短，bad 听着像 bat 或 back。
✗ prize said with a short vowel
✓ prize /praɪz/ with a longer vowel than price /praɪs/
The vowel length is what separates the prize from the cost.
元音长短才是 prize 与 price 的区别。

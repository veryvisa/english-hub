Why do people sometimes believe a message simply because they have encountered it repeatedly? The answer involves several psychological processes. Human attention is limited, and the brain often relies on mental shortcuts to process information efficiently. When a statement appears familiar, people may perceive it as more credible, even when they have not examined the supporting evidence. Familiarity, however, should not be confused with truth.

为什么人们有时仅仅因为反复接触某条信息就相信它？答案涉及多种心理过程。人类的注意力有限，大脑经常依靠思维捷径来高效处理信息。当一句话看起来很熟悉时，即使人们没有检查支持它的证据，也可能认为它更加可信。然而，熟悉感不应该与真实性混为一谈。

Another mechanism is social proof. People tend to look at the behaviour of others when deciding what is acceptable or sensible, particularly in uncertain situations. A message suggesting that thousands of people support an idea can therefore become more persuasive. Although popularity may provide useful information about public attitudes, it does not establish whether an argument is logically sound or factually correct.

另一种机制是社会认同。人们在决定什么是可以接受或合理的事情时，往往会观察其他人的行为，尤其是在不确定的情况下。因此，声称有成千上万人支持某个观点的信息可能更具说服力。尽管受欢迎程度能够提供有关公众态度的有用信息，但它并不能证明一个论点在逻辑上合理或在事实上正确。

Emotional appeals also play an important role. Fear, hope, anger and sympathy can influence which details people notice and remember. Advertisements may present an ordinary product as a source of confidence, while public campaigns may use personal stories to make distant problems feel immediate. Such approaches are not inherently manipulative, but they can become problematic when emotion replaces evidence.

情感诉求也发挥着重要作用。恐惧、希望、愤怒和同情会影响人们注意和记住哪些细节。广告可能把普通产品描述为自信的来源，而公共宣传活动可能利用个人故事，让遥远的问题显得迫在眉睫。这些方法本身并不必然具有操纵性，但当情绪取代证据时，它们就可能产生问题。

Understanding these mechanisms can help audiences become more independent thinkers. Instead of immediately accepting a persuasive message, readers can ask who benefits from it, what evidence supports it and which emotions it attempts to activate. On the whole, persuasion tends to be most effective when psychological shortcuts operate without conscious examination. Recognising them creates an opportunity to pause, reflect and make a more considered decision.

理解这些机制能够帮助受众成为更加独立的思考者。读者不应立即接受具有说服力的信息，而可以询问谁会从中受益、有什么证据支持它，以及它试图激发哪些情绪。总体而言，当心理捷径在未经有意识审视的情况下发挥作用时，说服往往最为有效。识别这些机制能够创造暂停、反思并作出更加审慎决定的机会。

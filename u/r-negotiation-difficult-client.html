Dear Alex: The Client Who Wants More 亲爱的亚历克斯：总想要更多的客户

Hear the whole topic in a row整个主题连着听

Dear Alex, I run a small design studio, and one long-standing client keeps asking for extra work without paying for it. Each time I say yes to keep the peace, and each time I resent it more. I am afraid that if I push back, I will lose the contract altogether. How should I raise the subject without starting a fight?

亲爱的亚历克斯：我经营一家小型设计工作室，有一位长期客户总是要求额外的工作却不付费。每次我为了维持和气都答应了，而每次我心里的怨气都更重。我担心一旦我反驳，就会彻底失去这份合同。我该如何提起这件事而不引发争吵？

Dear Worried, You are in a more comfortable position than you think. Clients who ask for extras are rarely acting out of malice; they are more likely testing where the boundary lies, and you have never shown them. That is not a criticism. Many of us find it easier to say yes than to risk an awkward moment.

亲爱的担心者：你的处境比你想的要好。要求额外工作的客户很少是出于恶意；他们更可能是在试探边界在哪里，而你从未给他们划出过边界。这不是批评。我们很多人都觉得，说「好」比冒着尴尬的风险更容易。

Begin by naming the pattern calmly, without blame. Something like: ‘I have noticed that several recent requests fall outside our agreement. I would like to find a way that works for both of us.’ This opens the door to cooperation rather than confrontation. Then offer options. You might propose a small fixed fee for extras, or a limited number of revisions included in the price. By and large, reasonable clients accept a clear structure far more readily than a sudden refusal.

先平静地、不带责备地点明这种模式。可以这样说：「我注意到最近有几项要求超出了我们的约定。我希望找到一个对我们双方都行得通的办法。」这会打开合作而非对抗的大门。然后给出选择。你可以提议为额外工作收取一笔小额固定费用，或在价格中包含有限次数的修改。总体而言，通情达理的客户接受清晰的结构，远比接受突然的拒绝更容易。

Be ready to meet halfway on details, but do not abandon the principle. If the client holds out for free work, you will need to decide what the relationship is worth to you. Although the fear of losing the contract is understandable, a client who will only stay when they are not paying fairly is not giving you much security. Knowing your alternatives, even vaguely, is your best source of leverage.

准备好在细节上各让一步，但不要放弃原则。如果客户坚持要求免费工作，你就需要决定这段关系对你值多少。虽然担心失去合同可以理解，但一个只有在不公平付费时才肯留下的客户，并不能给你多少安全感。了解自己的替代选择，哪怕只是模糊的，也是你最好的筹码来源。

Finally, choose your moment. Raise the subject after a successful delivery, not in the middle of a crisis. Good luck, and let me know how it goes.

最后，选对时机。在一次成功交付之后提出这件事，而不是在危机当中。祝你好运，告诉我进展如何。

Long sentences, unpacked长句拆解

Clients who ask for extras are rarely acting out of malice; they are more likely testing where the boundary lies, and you have never shown them.
‘Who ask for extras’ is a defining relative clause, ‘rarely’ and ‘more likely’ hedge the claim, and the semicolon links two related statements.
「who ask for extras」是限定性关系从句，「rarely」和「more likely」使断言留有余地，分号连接两个相关的陈述。
Although the fear of losing the contract is understandable, a client who will only stay when they are not paying fairly is not giving you much security.
‘Although’ sets up a concession, and the defining relative clause ‘who will only stay when...’ specifies which kind of client is meant.
「Although」引出让步，限定性关系从句「who will only stay when…」说明所指的是哪一类客户。

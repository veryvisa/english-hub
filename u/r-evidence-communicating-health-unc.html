The Courage to Say We Do Not Know 坦然承认我们尚不知道

Hear the whole topic in a row整个主题连着听

Ladies and gentlemen, public health professionals face a difficult responsibility. They must communicate information that can influence people's behaviour, protect communities and guide important decisions. Yet scientific knowledge is rarely complete. New findings emerge, earlier assumptions are reconsidered and recommendations sometimes change. In such circumstances, saying that we do not yet know something should be understood as intellectual honesty, not professional weakness.

女士们、先生们，公共卫生专业人员肩负着艰巨的责任。他们必须传播可能影响人们行为、保护社区并指导重要决策的信息。然而，科学知识很少是完整的。新的研究发现不断出现，过去的假设会被重新审视，建议有时也会发生变化。在这种情况下，承认某些事情尚不清楚，应当被视为学术诚实，而不是专业能力不足。

The difficulty is that uncertainty can be misunderstood. Some members of the public may interpret changing guidance as evidence that experts have failed. Others may assume that an uncertain finding has no value at all. Both reactions are problematic. While uncertainty does limit what can responsibly be claimed, it does not eliminate the usefulness of the information already available.

困难在于，不确定性可能被误解。一些公众可能将不断变化的指导意见理解为专家失职的证据。另一些人则可能认为，不确定的研究发现毫无价值。这两种反应都有问题。尽管不确定性确实限制了我们能够负责任地作出的判断，但它并不会消除现有信息的价值。

We should therefore distinguish established findings from preliminary observations and informed predictions. We should explain where the evidence comes from, what its limitations are and which questions remain unanswered. On the whole, people are more likely to trust communication that acknowledges uncertainty than messages that present every conclusion with absolute confidence. Clear explanations also make it easier to update advice without appearing inconsistent.

因此，我们应当区分已经确立的研究发现、初步观察结果和有依据的预测。我们应解释证据的来源、存在的局限以及仍未回答的问题。总体而言，与那些对每个结论都表现出绝对信心的信息相比，人们更可能信任承认不确定性的沟通方式。清晰的解释也使更新建议变得更容易，而不至于显得前后矛盾。

There is, of course, a need for timely action. Waiting for perfect information may create unnecessary risks, especially when a potential threat is serious. However, action should be proportionate to the available evidence and accompanied by an explanation of its reasoning. Public confidence is not built by promising certainty. It develops when institutions demonstrate that they can listen, evaluate new information and correct their decisions when necessary.

当然，我们仍然需要及时采取行动。等待完美的信息可能造成不必要的风险，尤其是在潜在威胁十分严重的时候。然而，行动应当与现有证据相称，并附带对决策理由的解释。公众信心并不是通过承诺确定性建立起来的，而是在机构展示出倾听、评估新信息并在必要时纠正决策的能力时逐步形成的。

📏 The rule规则

📏 The point in one line一句话要领
The ending copies the voicing of the sound before it: /t/ or /s/ after voiceless sounds, /d/ or /z/ after voiced ones, and a full extra syllable, /ɪd/ or /ɪz/, only when the word already ends in /t d/ or in a hissing sound.词尾跟着前一个音的清浊走：清音后读 /t/、/s/，浊音后读 /d/、/z/；只有词本身以 /t d/ 或嘶擦音结尾，才多出一个音节 /ɪd/、/ɪz/。

📖 Why, and when细讲：为什么、什么时候用

Open the full explanation: 5 points展开细讲：5 段讲解

Most intermediate learners know these endings in writing and still drop them when speaking, because Mandarin marks time and number with words (yesterday, two, le) rather than with sounds on the verb or noun. Yesterday I walk to work is clear to a Chinese ear; to an English ear it is a grammar mistake, and in a sentence without yesterday, it is ambiguous.

中级学习者大多书面上会写这些词尾，口语里照样吞掉，因为普通话用词（昨天、两个、了）表示时间和数量，而不是在动词名词上加音。对华人耳朵 Yesterday I walk to work 很清楚，对英语耳朵这是语法错误；句子里没有 yesterday 时，就会产生歧义。

The rule for -ed. After /t/ or /d/: add a syllable, /ɪd/: wanted, needed, decided. After other voiceless sounds (/p k f s ʃ tʃ θ/): /t/: stopped /stɒpt/, worked, laughed, missed, watched. After voiced sounds and vowels: /d/: lived, played, called, changed. The most common error is adding /ɪd/ everywhere: work-ed, stop-ped. That gives the word an extra syllable it does not have.

-ed 规则：/t/、/d/ 后多一个音节 /ɪd/；其他清音后读 /t/；浊音和元音后读 /d/。最常见的错是到处加 /ɪd/：work-ed、stop-ped，凭空多出一个音节。

The rule for -s (plurals, third person, possessive 's, and is / has contracted). After hissing sounds (/s z ʃ ʒ tʃ dʒ/): a syllable, /ɪz/: buses, changes, watches, George's. After other voiceless sounds: /s/: books, helps, months. After voiced sounds and vowels: /z/: days, calls, lives, goes. Mandarin speakers tend to say /s/ everywhere; a hissing /s/ on days or calls is understood, but it makes speech sound effortful.

-s 规则（复数、第三人称、所有格 's、is/has 缩写）：嘶擦音后多一个音节 /ɪz/；其他清音后读 /s/；浊音和元音后读 /z/。华人倾向于一律读 /s/，能听懂，但听着费劲。

In connected speech the ending often joins the next word, which is both a help and a trap. walked in is walk-tin: easy to say, easy to hear. walked to work has /t/ next to /t/, and native speakers say it once: walk-to work. As a listener, you will often not hear a separate -ed, so use the vowel length and the context; as a speaker, keep at least the linked form.

连读时词尾常并入下一个词，这既是帮助也是陷阱。walked in 读成 walk-tin，好说也好听；walked to work 里两个 /t/ 相邻，母语者只发一次。听的时候常听不到单独的 -ed，要靠元音长短和上下文；说的时候至少保住连读的形式。

Clusters make it harder: asked /ɑːskt/, texts /teksts/, months /mʌnθs/. Native speakers simplify these too (ast, tex), so do not over-practise every consonant, but keep the last sound, because the last sound carries the grammar.

辅音堆叠更难：asked、texts、months。母语者自己也会简化，不必每个辅音都苦练，但要保住最后那个音，因为语法意思在最后那个音上。

⚠️ Common slips for Chinese speakers华人常错

✗ Yesterday I walk to the station.
✓ Yesterday I walked to the station. (/wɔːkt/)
The time word does not replace the ending; the listener expects both.
时间词不能代替词尾，听者两样都要。
✗ I work-ed there for two years.
✓ I worked /wɜːkt/ there for two years.
/ɪd/ only after /t/ and /d/; here it adds a syllable that is not there.
只有 /t/、/d/ 后才读 /ɪd/，这里凭空多了一个音节。
✗ He go to the gym three time a week.
✓ He goes /ɡəʊz/ to the gym three times /taɪmz/ a week.
Two missing /z/ endings: third person and plural.
少了两个 /z/：第三人称和复数。
✗ the bus-s (one syllable)
✓ the buses /ˈbʌsɪz/
After a hissing sound, -es is a full syllable.
嘶擦音后，-es 是完整的一个音节。

induce verb

US /ɪnˈdus/ UK /ɪnˈdjuːs/ NAWL · band 1 · #22

to cause a particular state or reaction; to persuade someone to do something (formal) 引起，诱发；劝诱，促使（正式）

Forms词形: induces, induced, inducing

How it is used怎么用

Two patterns. Induce plus a noun means cause, usually something in the body or mind: induce sleep, induce vomiting, induce panic. Induce someone to do something means persuade, and is mostly used in the negative: nothing would induce me to go back.两种句型。induce＋名词是「引起」，多指身体或心理反应：induce sleep、induce vomiting、induce panic。induce sb to do sth 是「劝动」，多用于否定：nothing would induce me to go back。

The adjective ending -induced is very productive: stress-induced headaches, drug-induced sleep.-induced 这个后缀很能产：stress-induced headaches（压力引起的头痛）、drug-induced sleep。

Examples例句 10

  • A warm bath an hour before bed helps to induce sleep. 睡前一小时泡个热水澡有助于入睡。
  • Nothing would induce me to drive through the city centre at five o'clock. 说什么我也不会在五点钟开车穿过市中心。
  • The doctors decided to induce labour two weeks early. 医生决定提前两周引产。
  • Bright screens late at night induce a state of alertness just when you need to wind down. 深夜的亮屏幕偏偏在你该放松的时候让人变得清醒。
  • Discounts are designed to induce shoppers to buy more than they planned. 折扣的设计，就是为了诱使顾客买得比原计划多。
  • Do not try to induce vomiting unless a doctor tells you to. 除非医生让你这么做，否则不要自行催吐。

Where it comes from词源与派生

From Middle English enducen, borrowed from Latin indūcere (“lead in, bring in, introduce”), from in + dūcō (“lead, conduct”). Compare also abduce, adduce, conduce, deduce, produce, reduce etc.

Derived words派生词: inducee, coinduce, deinduce, reinduce, preinduce, subinduce, autoinduce, induceable, hyperinduce, self-induce, chondroinduce, inducive, enzyme-inducing medication

theorem noun

US /ˈθiərəm/ UK /ˈθɪərəm/ NAWL · band 1 · #31

a statement in mathematics or logic that can be shown to be true by reasoning 定理

Forms词形: theorems

How it is used怎么用

A theorem is proved; a theory is tested. Mixing up the two words is common, and it changes the meaning: a theory can turn out to be wrong, a proved theorem cannot.theorem（定理）是「证明」出来的，theory（理论）是「检验」出来的。这两个词常被混用，意思却不一样：理论可能被推翻，证明过的定理不会。

The verbs are prove, state and apply. The word begins with the th of think, not with s.搭配的动词是 prove、state、apply。开头是 think 里的 th，不是 s。

Examples例句 8

  • It took the class a whole lesson to prove the theorem. 全班用了整整一节课才证出这条定理。
  • You can apply the theorem to any triangle with a right angle. 这条定理适用于任何直角三角形。
  • A theorem that looks obvious can be surprisingly hard to prove. 看上去显而易见的定理，证起来可能出奇地难。
  • She could state every theorem in the book but struggled to use them. 书里的每条定理她都背得出，用起来却很吃力。
  • The proof of the theorem runs to more than a hundred pages. 这条定理的证明长达一百多页。
  • Unlike a theory, a theorem does not change when new evidence appears. 和理论不同，定理不会因为出现新证据而改变。
  • His teacher drew a diagram to show why the theorem had to be true. 老师画了一张图，说明这条定理为什么必然成立。

Where it comes from词源与派生

From Middle French théorème, from Late Latin theōrēma, from Ancient Greek θεώρημα (“speculation, proposition to be proved”) (Euclid), from θεωρέω (“to look at, view, consider, examine”), from θεωρός (“spectator”), from θέα (“a view”) + ὁράω (“to see, look”).

Derived words派生词: theoremic, nontheorem, antitheorem, metatheorem, theorematic, theoremhood, countertheorem

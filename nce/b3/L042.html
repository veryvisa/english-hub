Lesson 42 · Modern Cavemen 现代穴居人

📘 New Concept English · Book 3 · Lesson 42 “Modern Cavemen”新概念英语 第三册 Lesson 42《Modern Cavemen》

B2🧠 34 new words个新词📝 Full notes有完整讲解

🎯 Before you open the lesson开课前
Cave exploring, or pot-holing as it is now called, is a fairly recent sport. Perhaps it is the wish to be alone, or the hope of finding something unexpected, that draws people underground; the pot-holer's motives are as hard to explain as the climber's love of high mountains. Really deep caves are not for Sunday walkers: they need planning like a military operation, and fixing ladders and supply points can take eight days. The lesson then turns to the Gouffre Berger, at that time the deepest cave anyone knew of near Grenoble, over three thousand seven hundred feet deep, with an entrance only six feet across that might never have been noticed but for the French caver Berger. It has become the pot-holers' Everest. We follow a team down: a narrow corridor, streams and pools, a waterfall into an underground lake crossed by rubber boat, a booming waterspout, and at last a cavern as large as a concert hall, where powerful lights show stalagmites over forty feet high and limestone shining in every colour, in silence broken only by dripping water. This is exposition followed by description: first the sport, then a guided descent. The grammar to learn is come to be known, It is impossible to, the same ... which or as, can take as long as, inversion with had not in place of if, Since its discovery, and the size of.
洞穴探险是一项相当新的运动。也许是向往孤独，也许是期待意外的发现，把人们引向大地深处——探洞者的动机很难解释清楚，就像高山对登山者的吸引一样，触动的是说不太清的本能。探真正的深洞不是星期天下午散步的人能干的，得像军事行动那样精密筹划：放绳梯、建补给站可能就要八天。课文介绍的是当时已知最深的洞——格勒诺布尔附近的贝尔热洞，深 3,723 英尺，入口只有六英尺宽，若不是法国著名探洞家贝尔热发现，可能永远不为人知，如今已成了探洞者的「珠穆朗玛峰」。随后跟着一支探洞队往下走：窄廊、浅溪、深潭、瀑布、地下湖，橡皮艇载着装备漂过去，最后挤过岩缝，进入一个音乐厅那么大的洞厅，强光下石笋高达四十多英尺，石灰岩闪着彩虹的颜色，只听见水从穹顶上滴落。本课是说明 + 描写：先讲这项运动，再带读者走一遍。学 come to be known、It is impossible to、the same … which / as、can take as long as、had not … been done（省略 if 的倒装虚拟）、Since its discovery、the size of。
Questions to keep in mind as you read读的时候带着这几个问题：
  1. Why does the writer say that deep caving is not for the Sunday afternoon walker?作者为什么说深洞探险「不是星期天下午散步的人能干的」？
  2. Why might the Gouffre Berger never have been found?贝尔热洞为什么差一点永远不为人知？

📖 Understanding the piece读懂这篇

Written for the course by L. G. Alexander, but the cave is real. The Gouffre Berger lies on the Vercors plateau above Grenoble in the French Alps; it is named after Joseph Berger, one of the group of cavers who found its entrance in 1953.

课文为编者 L. G. 亚历山大为教材自撰，但这个洞是真的。贝尔热洞位于法国阿尔卑斯山区格勒诺布尔上方的韦科尔高原，以约瑟夫·贝尔热命名——他是 1953 年发现洞口的那群探洞者之一。

🌍 Where it comes from出处与背景

Within a few years of its discovery, the Gouffre Berger became the first cave in the world to be explored past a depth of one thousand metres, and for a time it was the deepest known. The lesson's figure of about three thousand seven hundred feet is a little over eleven hundred metres, which matches the depth reached in that early period. It did not keep the record. Deeper systems have since been found, and the deepest known cave today is Veryovkina in the Arabika massif of Abkhazia, in the western Caucasus, explored in 2018 to a little over two thousand two hundred metres — roughly twice the Berger. The lesson's sense of danger is accurate. The Berger is famous among cavers for sudden flooding: heavy rain on the plateau can turn its streams into torrents within hours, and expeditions have been trapped and cavers have died there. The rest of the world learned how dangerous flooded caves can be in 2018, when twelve boys and their coach were trapped for more than two weeks in the Tham Luang cave in northern Thailand before a large international team of divers brought them out.

发现后没几年，贝尔热洞就成了世界上第一个被探到一千米以下的洞，一度是已知最深的洞。课文给的约三千七百英尺是一千一百多米，与早期达到的深度相符。它没有一直保持纪录。此后又发现了更深的洞系；今天已知最深的是西高加索阿布哈兹阿拉比卡山地的韦尔约夫金娜洞，2018 年被探到两千二百多米——大约是贝尔热洞的两倍。课文对危险的感觉是准确的。贝尔热洞在探洞者中以突发洪水出名：高原上一场大雨，几小时内就能把洞里的溪流变成急流，曾有探险队被困、有探洞者死在里面。世界其他地方是在 2018 年才认识到被淹的洞穴有多危险的：泰国北部睡美人洞（Tham Luang）里，十二名少年和他们的教练被困两个多星期，最后由一支庞大的国际潜水队救了出来。

✨ How it is written赏析

The lesson is built like the descent it describes. The first paragraph stays on the surface, in general words — motives, instincts, undertakings — and even admits that the motive cannot be explained, which leaves a little mystery to carry us down. The second paragraph moves from the sport to one cave and gives us facts: its place, its depth, its tiny entrance, its discoverer. Then, in the third, the writer stops explaining and starts showing, and the sentences become a chain of verbs of movement: climbed, edge, wading, swimming, plunged, squeezing. Each obstacle is different — narrowness, water, cold, noise, rubble — so that the journey feels long without being repetitive. The climax is light and silence after darkness and noise. The final sentence is the quietest in the lesson: the booming has gone, and all that is left is the sound of water dripping from the dome, which is also, quietly, the sound of the cave still being made.

这一课的结构就像它描写的那次下降。第一段停在地面上，用的是概括的词——动机、本能、事业——甚至承认动机无法解释，留一点神秘带我们往下走。第二段从这项运动收到一个洞，给出事实：它在哪、多深、洞口多小、谁发现的。到第三段，作者不再解释而开始展示，句子变成一串表示移动的动词：爬下、侧身挪、蹚水、游过、跳进、挤过。每一道障碍都不一样——狭窄、水、寒冷、噪声、碎石——于是旅程显得漫长而不重复。高潮是黑暗与噪声之后的光与寂静。最后一句是全课最安静的一句：轰鸣已经远去，只剩下水从穹顶滴落的声音——这也悄悄地是这个洞仍在形成之中的声音。

🧭 What it argues, and does it still hold?解读

The lesson calls the motives of pot-holers impossible to explain, and today we might push back a little. Cavers themselves usually give clear reasons: the chance to stand where no one has stood, the puzzle of following water through rock, the trust that grows inside a team. Scientists have added another: caves preserve records of past climate in their stalagmites, and cave exploration has fed real research. What the lesson does not say is who pays when things go wrong. A rescue deep underground can involve hundreds of people and take days, as Tham Luang showed, and one of the volunteer divers died during it. That raises a fair question about any dangerous sport. The people who choose the risk are not the only ones who carry it; the rescuers carry it too. Most caving clubs answer with training, careful planning and weather checks — exactly the military foresight the lesson praises — which suggests the writer's second paragraph is the real moral, not the first.

课文说探洞者的动机无法解释，今天我们可以稍加反驳。探洞者自己通常给出清楚的理由：站在无人站过之处的机会，追着水穿过岩石的那种解谜之乐，在团队里长出来的信任。科学家又补了一条：洞穴里的石笋保存着过去气候的记录，探洞活动也为真正的研究提供了材料。课文没说的是：出了事，谁来买单。地下深处的一次救援可能牵动数百人、持续好几天，睡美人洞就是例子，一名志愿潜水员在救援中牺牲了。这给任何危险运动都提出了一个合理的问题：选择风险的人并不是唯一承担风险的人，救援者也在承担。多数探洞俱乐部的回答是训练、周密的计划和查看天气——正是课文所称赞的那种军事行动般的远见——这说明作者的第二段才是真正的寓意，而不是第一段。

③ How the hard sentences are built难句结构

Sentence 1第 1 句 (begins开头 “Cave exploration, or …”)

  • or: that is, giving another name.or：即，也就是（引出另一个名称）。
  • The phrase means 'as people now call it'; come to do means to gradually become.as it has come to …：现在人们这样称呼它（come to do 渐渐变得）。

Sentence 2第 2 句 (begins开头 “Perhaps it is …”)

  • It is ... that ...: a cleft sentence that puts the weight on the subject.It is … that …：强调句，强调主语。

Sentence 4第 4 句 (begins开头 “For him, caves …”)

  • the same ... which: which introduces a clause about fascination; the same ... as or that is more usual.the same … which：此处 which 引导定语从句修饰 fascination（更常见的写法是 the same … as / that）。

④ Grammar in this lesson本课语法

Inverted conditions without if: Had, Were or Should plus the subject省略 if 的倒装条件句：Had / Were / Should + 主语
In an unreal condition containing had, were or should, you can leave out if and put that word before the subject: Had I known, Were I you, Should you need help. In the negative, not usually goes after the subject, as in Had it not rained; sentence 15, with had not before its subject, uses an older order.
虚拟条件句里含 had、were、should 时，可以省掉 if，把这些词放到主语前面：If I had known → Had I known；If I were you → Were I you；If you should need help → Should you need help。否定时 not 一般放在主语后：Had it not rained…；本课第 15 句 had not 放在主语前，是较旧的写法。

In sentence 15 the cave might have stayed unknown if Berger had not spotted its entrance.

本课：第 15 句：若不是贝尔热发现了入口，这个洞也许永远不会被发现。

  • Had I known you were coming, I would have cooked more.
  • Were I in your place, I would accept the offer.
  • Should you need any help, please call me.
It is ... that ...: cleft sentences for emphasisIt is … that …：强调句
It is or was plus the part you want to stress plus that or who plus the rest picks out a subject, object or adverbial. If you remove It is ... that and the sentence is still complete, it is a cleft sentence.
It is / was + 被强调部分 + that / who + 其余部分，用来突出主语、宾语或状语。去掉 It is … that 后句子仍然完整，是判断强调句的办法。

Sentence 2 stresses what draws people underground — perhaps solitude, perhaps the chance of a find.

本课：第 2 句强调吸引人们入洞的「也许是孤独，也许是发现的机会」。

  • It was my brother who broke the window, not me.
  • It is in winter that this road is most dangerous.
  • It was only last year that I learnt to swim.
It takes / can take as long as ...; the size of ...
as long as plus a time, as much as plus an amount and as many as plus a number stress how large a figure is. the size, height or age of plus a noun means 'as big, tall or old as', often placed beside a noun or after be.
as long as + 时间、as much as + 数量、as many as + 数目：强调数字之大（「长达、多达」）。the size / height / age of + 名词：「和……一样大/高/老」，常作同位语或表语。

Sentence 8 says preparations can take up to eight days, and sentence 29 compares the cavern to a large concert hall.

本课：第 8 句说准备工作可能要长达八天；第 29 句说洞厅有一座大音乐厅那么大。

  • The journey can take as long as three days.
  • As many as a thousand people came to the meeting.
  • They found a stone the size of a football.

⑤ Patterns to reuse可以套用的句型

  • X, or Y as it is now known, is … X，现在人们叫它 Y，是……
    Rock music, or rock as it is now called, began in the 1950s.
  • Perhaps it is … or … that lures people … 也许是……或……把人们吸引到……
    Perhaps it is the quiet or the clean air that draws people to the hills.
  • Such undertakings require the … of … 这样的事情需要……般的……
    Such journeys require the patience of a saint.
  • X might never have been … had not … 要不是……，X 也许永远不会……
    The letter might never have been found had not a child dropped her toy behind the cupboard.
  • Since its discovery, X has become a sort of … 自被发现以来，X 已成为某种……
    Since its opening, the café has become a sort of village hall.

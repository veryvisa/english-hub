📖 Understanding the piece读懂这篇

Written for the course by L. G. Alexander; the gallery is not named and we could not match the visit to a particular exhibition, so the occasion is unverified. What the writer describes, though, is a documented kind of art with documented makers, and the Greek sculptor Takis is the closest fit for the magnetised spheres.

课文由编者 L. G. 亚历山大为教材自撰；画廊没有点名，我们也没能把这次参观对上某一场具体展览，所以场合未核。但作者描述的东西是一个有案可查的艺术门类、有案可查的作者，而那些带磁的金属球，最接近希腊雕塑家塔基斯的作品。

🌍 Where it comes from出处与背景

Sculpture that moves had a particular moment, and this lesson sits inside it. Takis, a Greek artist working in Paris, began his Signals in the middle nineteen fifties — long thin rods that sway and bend in moving air, which he said came out of looking at the masts and lights of a railway station at Calais. From nineteen fifty-eight he worked with magnets, calling the results telemagnetic, and the point of those works is exactly the one the lesson records: two objects acting on each other across a gap of empty air, with no visible cause. The movement in them is real, not depicted, and it never settles. London had a gallery named after that series: Signals, which opened as the Centre for Advanced Creative Study and ran for about two years in the middle sixties, showing kinetic work from Europe and South America. A notice forbidding visitors to touch, on the grounds of danger, is in keeping with the hardware. Magnets strong enough to swing a steel ball will erase a bank card and pull at a pacemaker; coloured lamps on tall frames and boxes that throw sparks are mains electricity in an open frame. The warning in the lesson is a gallery doing its job, and the writer is quite right to find it funny.

会动的雕塑有过一个特定的时刻，而本课就坐在那个时刻里面。塔基斯是一位在巴黎工作的希腊艺术家，他的《信号》系列始于五十年代中期——细长的金属杆在流动的空气里摆动、弯折；他说灵感来自观看加来火车站的桅杆与灯。从一九五八年起他开始用磁铁，把成果称为「远磁的」，而那些作品的要点正是课文记下的那一点：两个物体隔着一段空无的空气彼此作用，看不见原因。它们的运动是真的，不是画出来的，而且永不停歇。伦敦曾有一家以这个系列命名的画廊：Signals，开办时叫「高等创作研究中心」，在六十年代中期运营了约两年，展出来自欧洲与南美的动态作品。一条以危险为由禁止触摸的告示，和这些硬件是相称的。强到能把钢球甩起来的磁铁，会抹掉银行卡，也会拉扯心脏起搏器；高架上的彩灯和会冒火花的盒子，是敞在开放框架里的市电。课文里的那条警告是画廊在尽本分，而作者觉得它好笑，一点也没错。

✨ How it is written赏析

The piece is a tour of a room, and the tour is organised by where the writer's eyes go, not by any argument. Count the positions: what met him at the entrance, then the row along the wall, then the middle of the room, and finally the sparks and the lamps. That is how people actually look at a room, and it is why the description does not feel like a catalogue. Notice also the long approach. Four sentences are spent establishing that modern sculpture is boring and familiar, including the dry observation that some of it has been on view for the best part of a century, so that the writer's surprise is earned rather than asserted; the admission in brackets, including myself, does a lot of work in three words. The comparisons are domestic rather than artistic: traffic lights that have gone mad, prehistoric electronic equipment. He never reaches for a critic's vocabulary, and the effect is that you trust him as an eye rather than as a judge. Then the last sentence collects the payment. Shock has been waiting since the notice at the door, and the whole description turns out to have been a very long set-up for a pun. When you describe a place in English, let the room order the paragraph, keep the comparisons ordinary, and if you have a joke, plant it early and pay it off last.

这一篇是一次沿着房间走的参观，而参观的次序由作者的目光决定，不由任何论证决定。数一数位置：进门第一眼看到的、靠墙排开的、大厅中央的，然后是火花与红灯。人真实地打量一个房间就是这个顺序，这也是为什么这段描写不像展品目录。也注意那段很长的接近。作者花了四句话去确立「现代雕塑既无聊又眼熟」，其中还有一句干巴巴的观察：有些展品已经摆了近一个世纪——于是他的惊讶是挣来的，不是宣称的；括号里那句「包括我自己」，三个词干了很多活。他用的比喻是家常的，不是艺术的：发疯的红绿灯、史前的电子设备。他从不去抓评论家的词汇，效果是你信他这双眼睛，而不是信他这个裁判。然后最后一句来收钱。shock 这个词从门口那张告示起就一直在等，整段描写原来是一个很长的铺垫，为了一个双关。你用英文描写一个地方时，让房间去安排段落，让比喻保持平常；如果你有个笑话，早早埋下，最后再兑现。

🧭 What it argues, and does it still hold?解读

The judgement in the first half has become more true than the writer could have meant. He wrote that modern sculpture rarely surprises anyone, and that certain so-called modern pieces had by then been on view for close to eighty years; read today, that phrase reaches back to the middle of the twentieth century rather than to the eighteen eighties, which means the kinetic work he found so startling is itself now the old art in the room. Takis lived into his nineties and had a full retrospective at Tate Modern in the year he died, which is the museum's way of saying that a style has finished being dangerous. The shock has gone in the other direction too. Galleries now build whole rooms you are meant to walk into, touch and set off, and the notice at the door is more likely to invite contact than to forbid it; what is still forbidden is usually a matter of conservation rather than of voltage. The deeper point the lesson stumbles on is worth keeping. Art that moves by itself asks to be experienced over time, like music, which is why a still photograph of it is almost useless and why it has always been under-represented in books. Readers in China can place it in a much older line without strain: machinery displayed to produce wonder goes back at least to the water-driven clock tower built at Kaifeng in the eleventh century, with its figures that came out to mark the hours — the same bargain, a mechanism that a crowd gathers to watch, long before anybody called it sculpture.

前半边的判断，变得比作者本意更真。他写现代雕塑很少再让人吃惊，还写有些「所谓现代」的展品已经展出近八十年；今天读这句话，它回望的是二十世纪中叶，而不是一八八〇年代——也就是说，让他大吃一惊的那些动态作品，如今自己才是房间里的旧艺术。塔基斯活到九十多岁，在他去世那一年于泰特现代美术馆办了完整的回顾展，而这正是美术馆宣布「某种风格已经不再危险」的方式。震撼也朝另一个方向跑了。今天的美术馆会整间整间地造出让你走进去、去摸、去触发的房间，门口的告示更可能是邀请你接触而不是禁止；仍被禁止的，通常是出于保存而不是出于电压。课文无意中碰到的那个更深的点值得留住。自己会动的艺术要求人花时间去经历它，像音乐一样——所以它的静态照片几乎没用，也所以它在书里一直被严重低估。中国读者可以不费劲地把它放进一条古老得多的脉络：用机械造出惊奇，至少可以追到十一世纪开封那座水力驱动的钟楼，到点就有小人出来报时——同样的交易：一具让人群围着看的机械，远在有人管它叫雕塑之前。

💬 Discuss and write讨论与写作
  1. The writer trusts his eye and never uses an art critic's words. Describe one thing you have seen in a gallery or a public square in that way, in five sentences, without saying whether it is good.
    作者信自己的眼睛，从不用艺评家的词。用五句话这样描述一件你在美术馆或广场上见过的东西，不说它好不好。
  2. Write 120 words about a work of art, a building or a machine that startled you. Use cannot have failed to once and not only ... but ... as well once.
    写 120 词，讲一件让你吃惊的艺术品、建筑或机器。用一次 cannot have failed to，用一次 not only … but … as well。
  3. A notice told visitors not to touch because the exhibits were dangerous. Would a work of art that can hurt you be better or worse art for it? Give an example to support your answer.
    一张告示以展品危险为由禁止触摸。一件会伤到你的作品，因此成了更好的艺术，还是更差的？举例支持你的回答。

Sentence 2第 2 句 (begins开头 “The idea that …”)

  • that ...: an appositive clause spelling out what the idea is; the backbone is The idea ... is mistaken.that…：同位语从句，解释 idea 的内容；主干是 The idea … is mistaken。
  • can only be seen: a modal with a passive.can only be seen：情态 + 被动。

  • go mad: a linking verb of change plus an adjective.go mad：发疯（go 作系动词表变化）。

④ Grammar in this lesson本课语法

Deductions about the past: must have done / cannot have done / may have done对过去的推测：must have done / cannot have done / may have done
must have done = it certainly did happen; cannot or couldn't have done = it could not possibly have happened; may or might have done = it possibly did. Put fail to inside the negative and you get a double negative: cannot have failed to do = certainly did.
must have done ＝那时一定……；cannot/couldn't have done ＝那时不可能……；may/might have done ＝那时可能……。和 fail to 连用时变成双重否定：cannot have failed to do ＝一定做了。

In this lesson, sentence 3: even those with no feeling for the subject cannot have failed to notice the sculpture standing in public places.

本课：第 3 句：哪怕对艺术不感兴趣的人，cannot have failed to notice 公共场所的现代雕塑。

  • The ground is wet. It must have rained last night.
  • She can't have seen me; she didn't say hello.
  • He may have missed the bus.
get used to and be used to doing, against used to doget used to / be used to doing 与 used to do
get used to + noun or -ing = gradually grow accustomed to (the process); be used to + noun or -ing = already accustomed to (the state); used to do = did regularly in the past. In the first two, to is a preposition.
get used to + 名词/doing ＝渐渐习惯（过程）；be used to + 名词/doing ＝已经习惯（状态）；used to do ＝过去常常。前两个的 to 是介词。
  • I'm used to cold winters now.
  • It took her months to get used to driving on the left.
Participle phrases as modifiers: attached to / lined up / suspended from分词短语作定语与状语：attached to / lined up / suspended from
A past participle phrase can follow a noun directly (= which are ...) or stand at the front of the sentence describing a state. At the front, check which noun it logically belongs to.
过去分词短语可以紧跟名词作后置定语（= which are …），也可以放在句首描写状态。放句首时要注意它逻辑上修饰的是哪个名词。
  • The man dressed in black is my uncle.
  • Surrounded by mountains, the village is very quiet.

⑤ Patterns to reuse可以套用的句型

Summary writing摘要写作

In no more than 80 words, describe the show the writer visited, and finish on the pun.

用不超过 80 个词描述作者参观的那次现代雕塑展，结尾点出双关。

Points a good summary covers好摘要要覆盖的要点
  • Odd shapes stand everywhere in public now; people have stopped noticing them and are seldom startled.现代雕塑在公共场所随处可见，人们早已习惯，很少再感到惊奇。
  • At one show, though, the writer got a fright: a notice at the door said the exhibits must not be touched, since some were dangerous.可作者在一次展览上大吃一惊：门口告示说不许触摸展品，有些是危险的。
  • The works moved: magnetised balls pulled at and pushed away from one another, coloured lights blinked without stopping, small black boxes threw sparks and red lamps came on and off.展品是会动的雕塑：带磁的金属球互相吸引排斥，彩灯不停闪烁，小黑盒子冒火花，红灯一明一灭。
  • The pieces looked built to jolt the visitor's feelings, and to deliver a jolt of electricity too.这些作品不仅要在情感上震撼观众，还似乎要让人挨电击。
Our model summary本站范文

Modern sculpture is everywhere in public places, so it seldom amazes us. However, one exhibition surprised me. A sign at the entrance warned visitors not to touch anything, as some pieces were dangerous. Magnetic metal balls kept pulling and pushing one another, tall frames held lights flashing like crazy traffic signals, and small black boxes threw out sparks. The works seemed meant to shock people not only in their feelings but with real electricity too.（75 词）

Lesson 39 · Nothing to Worry About 不必担心

📘 New Concept English · Book 3 · Lesson 39 “Nothing to Worry About”新概念英语 第三册 Lesson 39《Nothing to Worry About》

B2🧠 27 new words个新词📝 Full notes有完整讲解

🎯 Before you open the lesson开课前
The rough track across the plain grows so bad, strewn with boulders and holes, that we beg Bruce to turn back. He is not worried at all: a glance at the map tells him the next village is only twenty miles off. It is not that he underestimates difficulty, the writer says; he simply has no sense of danger, and believes a car should always go as fast as it can. Stones bang under the floor, and we keep looking back for a trail of oil. The boulders end, only for a great crack to appear ahead; we get out and measure it — fifty yards long, two feet wide, four feet deep. Bruce drops into low gear and races along it with the front wheels on either side of the gap. Finally he charges into a shallow pool, the car grinds to a stop in the middle, a yellow light flashes, and Bruce cheerfully announces that there is no oil in the engine. This is a travel story about a character, in which the comedy comes from setting our fear against Bruce's calm, and the last line turns the title upside down. The grammar to learn is get someone to do, not in the least, It was not that ..., No matter what, What a relief ... when, give way to, and there was worse to come.
横穿平原的土路越来越糟，满是大石头和坑，我们求布鲁斯掉头回村，他却一点不慌，看看地图说：下一个村子只有二十英里。作者说，布鲁斯不是低估困难，而是压根没有危险意识——不管路况如何，车都该开到最快。石头砸在车底咚咚响，我们一路回头看有没有漏油；好不容易石头没了，前面又横着一条大裂缝，我们下车量了：五十码长、两英尺宽、四英尺深。布鲁斯照样挂低速挡，让两个前轮跨着裂缝、顺着它的之字形冲了过去。最后，车冲进一片浅水塘，在中央嘎地停下，仪表板上黄灯直闪，布鲁斯高高兴兴地宣布：发动机没油了！本课是人物与旅途叙事，靠「我们」的担心和布鲁斯的满不在乎来回对照制造笑点，结尾把题目翻了个面。学 get sb to do、not in the least、It was not that … 、No matter what、What a relief … when、give way to、there was worse to come。
Questions to keep in mind as you read读的时候带着这几个问题：
  1. What makes Bruce different from other people, according to the writer?作者说布鲁斯和别人有什么不同？
  2. How did Bruce get across the great crack in the ground?布鲁斯是怎么开过那条裂缝的？
  3. Why is the last line, about the oil, funny?最后那句「发动机没油了」为什么好笑？

📖 Understanding the piece读懂这篇

Written for the course by L. G. Alexander; Bruce and the journey are invented, and no country is named. The character, though, is one every reader has met, and psychologists have studied the habit of mind he represents.

课文为编者 L. G. 亚历山大为教材自撰；布鲁斯和这趟旅程都是编的，连国家都没点名。不过这个人物每个读者都见过，他所代表的那种心态，心理学家也研究过。

🌍 Where it comes from出处与背景

Two well-known findings fit Bruce closely. In 1981 the Swedish psychologist Ola Svenson asked groups of student drivers in the United States and Sweden to compare their own driving skill and safety with that of the others in the room. A large majority of the American group placed themselves above the middle, which cannot be true of most people at once. A year earlier the American psychologist Neil Weinstein had described what he called unrealistic optimism: people generally believe that bad events, from illness to accidents, are less likely to happen to them than to others like them. Neither study found that confident people are always wrong; both found that most people's sense of their own risk is tilted in a hopeful direction. Bruce is an extreme case, and the lesson makes the point by contrast: the passengers see every danger, Bruce sees none, and the car, which has no opinions, pays for it. A yellow or red oil light in a real car usually means low oil pressure, and driving on is one of the surest ways to ruin an engine.

有两项著名研究与布鲁斯十分贴合。1981 年，瑞典心理学家奥拉·斯文森请美国和瑞典的几组学车学生，把自己的驾驶技术与安全程度同在场其他人相比。美国组里绝大多数人把自己排在中位以上——这不可能对大多数人同时成立。前一年，美国心理学家尼尔·温斯坦描述了他所谓的「不切实际的乐观」：人们普遍相信，坏事——从生病到出事故——落在自己头上的可能比落在同类人头上小。两项研究都没有发现自信的人总是错的；它们发现的是，大多数人对自身风险的估计朝乐观一侧倾斜。布鲁斯是一个极端的例子，课文用对比来点明这一点：乘客看见每一处危险，布鲁斯一处也看不见，而那辆没有任何主见的车替他买了单。真车上的黄灯或红色机油灯通常表示机油压力过低，继续开下去是毁掉发动机最可靠的办法之一。

✨ How it is written赏析

The comedy rests on two voices that never meet. The narrator speaks for the group — we tried, we felt sure, we kept looking back — and fills the story with careful measurements and fears: twenty miles, fifty yards, two feet, four feet. Bruce speaks only in announcements, and every one of them is about distance on the map, as if the only fact that mattered were how far there was to go. The writer stops twice to explain him, and the explanation is exact: it is not that he misjudges difficulties, but that he does not see danger at all. The story is shaped as a series of obstacles, each worse than the last, with a moment of false relief between them, and the signposting phrase 'there was worse to come' tells us so. The ending works because it reverses the pattern. For once the danger is not outside the car but inside it, Bruce is the one who announces it, and he does so cheerfully — which turns the title, Nothing to worry about, into the punch line.

这出喜剧建立在两个永不相交的声音上。叙述者代表这群人说话——我们设法、我们确信、我们不停回头——并给故事塞满了谨慎的测量与担忧：二十英里、五十码、两英尺、四英尺。布鲁斯只用宣布的口气说话，而每一次宣布都是地图上的距离，好像唯一要紧的事实就是还有多远。作者两次停下来解释他，而且解释得精确：不是他错估了困难，而是他根本看不见危险。故事被做成一连串障碍，一个比一个糟，中间夹着一刻虚假的轻松，「更糟的还在后头」这个路标式的短语明白告诉了我们。结尾之所以成立，是因为它把模式反了过来：这一次危险不在车外而在车内，宣布它的是布鲁斯自己，而且他宣布得兴高采烈——于是题目「不必担心」成了笑点。

🧭 What it argues, and does it still hold?解读

Readers tend to laugh at Bruce and side with the passengers, and the lesson invites it. Yet there is a question underneath: why do the passengers stay in the car? They beg, they plead, they measure the crack and report the figures, and then they get back in. That is the more common human situation. Most of us are not Bruce, but we often go along with someone more confident than we are, because objecting is awkward and because confidence is persuasive. Research on group decisions describes the same pattern: a single assured voice can carry a group past warnings that most of its members privately share. The useful lesson for a modern reader is less about Bruce, who will never change, than about the people beside him. Measuring the danger is not enough; at some point someone has to refuse to get back in the car.

读者往往笑布鲁斯而站在乘客一边，课文也这样引导。可是下面藏着一个问题：乘客为什么一直坐在车里？他们恳求、哀求，下车量裂缝、报出数字，然后又坐了回去。这才是更常见的人类处境。我们大多数人不是布鲁斯，却常常跟着一个比自己更自信的人走，因为反对很尴尬，而自信本身就有说服力。关于群体决策的研究描述了同样的模式：一个笃定的声音，能把一群人带过他们大多数人私下都认同的警告。对今天的读者来说，更有用的一课不在布鲁斯——他永远不会变——而在坐在他旁边的那些人。把危险量出来是不够的；到某个时刻，总得有人拒绝再坐回车里。

④ Grammar in this lesson本课语法

get someone to do / have someone do / make someone doget sb to do / have sb do / make sb do：使役
get someone to do means to persuade or manage it, with to; have someone do means to arrange for it; make someone do means to force it, without to, though the passive be made to do needs to.
get sb to do：说服或设法让某人做（带不定式符号 to）；have sb do：安排某人做；make sb do：强迫某人做（主动句不带 to，被动句 be made to do 要带 to）。

In sentence 1 they try, and fail, to persuade Bruce to turn back.

本课：第 1 句：我们设法让布鲁斯开回去（没成功）。

  • I got my brother to help me with the move.
  • We had the mechanic check the brakes.
  • The teacher made us rewrite the essay.
No matter what / how / where ...: whatever, however, wherever
no matter plus a question word introduces a clause meaning 'it makes no difference what, who, how, where or when'. It can be replaced by whatever, whoever, however, wherever or whenever.
no matter + 疑问词（what / who / how / where / when）引导让步状语从句，「无论……」。可以换成 whatever / whoever / however / wherever / whenever。

In sentence 6 the state of the road makes no difference to how fast Bruce thinks a car should go.

本课：第 6 句：无论路况如何，他都认为车该开到最快。

  • No matter what you say, I won't change my mind.
  • No matter where she goes, she takes her dog.
  • However hard it rains, he cycles to work.
It is not that ...; What a ... it was when ...
It is or was not that ..., often followed by but that ..., rules out one possible explanation before giving the real one. The exclamation What a plus a noun plus it was when ... expresses strong feeling about a moment.
It is / was not that … (but that) …：「并不是……（而是……）」，用来先否定一个可能的解释。感叹句 What a + 名词 + it was when …：「……时真是……啊」。

Sentence 4 rules out the idea that Bruce misjudged difficulties, and sentence 11 uses the exclamation for the moment the stones end.

本课：第 4 句用 It was not that 先排除「低估困难」；第 11 句用 What a relief 感叹句写如释重负。

  • It's not that I don't like you; I'm just tired.
  • It wasn't that the film was bad — it was just too long.
  • What a shock it was when the lights went out!

⑤ Patterns to reuse可以套用的句型

  • X got so bad that we begged Y to … X 糟到我们想让 Y……
    The noise got so bad that we begged the neighbours to stop.
  • X was not in the least … X 一点也不……
    She was not in the least surprised by the news.
  • It was not that … He simply … 并不是……，他只是……
    It was not that he was lazy. He simply hated mornings.
  • What a relief it was to … ……的时候真是如释重负
    What a relief it was to hear the exam results.
  • Worse was still to come. 但更糟的还在后头
    We missed the train. Worse was still to come.

In no more than 80 words, tell what happened on this journey.

用不超过 80 个词讲述这次旅途中发生的事。

Points a good summary covers好摘要要覆盖的要点

  • When the rocks ended, a crack fifty yards long and two feet wide lay ahead.石头路结束后，前面出现一条长五十码、宽两英尺的大裂缝。
  • Bruce raced along it at a frightening speed with the front wheels on either side.布鲁斯让前轮跨着裂缝，以吓人的速度冲了过去。

Our model summary本站范文

The track across the plain was full of rocks and holes, but Bruce, who had no sense of danger, ignored our pleas to turn back and drove as fast as possible. Stones crashed against the car. Then we reached a long, deep crack in the ground; Bruce raced along it with a wheel on each side. Finally he charged into a shallow pool, where the car stopped dead and a warning light showed that the engine had no oil.（79 词）

Lesson 23 · One Man's Meat is Another Man's Poison 各有所好

📘 New Concept English · Book 3 · Lesson 23 “One Man's Meat is Another Man's Poison”新概念英语 第三册 Lesson 23《One Man's Meat is Another Man's Poison》

B2🧠 22 new words个新词📝 Full notes有完整讲解

🎯 Before you open the lesson开课前
The title is an English proverb: what one person enjoys can make another person ill. People are not at all reasonable about what may and may not be eaten. Round the Mediterranean octopus is a treat, while northerners think it horrible; northerners, in turn, cook potatoes in animal fat, and the thought of that is enough to upset a southern stomach. Most of us eat what we were fed as children and go on doing so for life. Snails are the clearest case: boiled in wine they are a luxury in some countries, while in the writer's friend Robert's country they are looked down on — and yet Robert loves them and has spent years asking the writer to gather some from his garden. After a downpour the writer suddenly gives in, picks up a few dozen in a paper bag and takes them over. The two men talk for two hours; Robert asks him to stay for dinner, with snails as the main dish. Then they step out of the room and find that the creatures have climbed out of the bag and are all over the hall. The writer has never been able to face a snail since. The piece is an argument first, a story second. Learn the unreal conditional (If you lived ..., you would ...), find + object + adjective, be brought up to do, associate A with B, happen to do and take possession of.
标题是英语谚语「一个人的美食是另一个人的毒药」。什么能吃、什么不能吃，人们其实很不讲道理：地中海的人把章鱼当美味，北方人觉得恶心；反过来，北方人用动物油炸土豆，地中海的人想想就反胃。大多数人从小吃什么，一辈子就吃什么。蜗牛最典型：有的国家拿酒烹蜗牛当珍馐，朋友罗伯特的国家却看不起蜗牛，可他偏爱吃，求作者从花园里给他捉。一场大雨后，作者一时兴起捉了几十只装进纸袋送去，两人聊了两个小时，罗伯特留他吃晚饭，主菜当然是蜗牛——可一出客厅，发现蜗牛早已爬出袋子，占领了整个门厅。从此作者再也不敢看蜗牛。本课是先议论后叙事的结构。学 if 虚拟（If you lived…, you would…）、find + 宾语 + 形容词、be brought up to do、associate A with B、happen to do 和 take possession of。
Questions to keep in mind as you read读的时候带着这几个问题：
  1. Why does the writer say that people are not reasonable about food?作者为什么说人们在食物问题上很「不讲逻辑」？
  2. Why did he gather snails for his friend on that particular day?作者为什么那天偏偏去给罗伯特捉蜗牛？
  3. How does the story end, and how does the writer feel about snails now?故事最后发生了什么？作者从此对蜗牛有什么感觉？

Book Three texts were written for the course by L. G. Alexander, so the snail story has no outside author and we found no earlier printed version of it; the title, however, is a proverb far older than the course, and the food customs behind the lesson can all be checked.

第三册课文由编者 L. G. 亚历山大为教材自撰，这则蜗牛故事没有外部作者，我们也没有查到更早的印本；但标题是一句比教材老得多的谚语，课文背后的饮食风俗也都可核。

🌍 Where it comes from出处与背景

The thought in the title is about two thousand years old. The Roman poet Lucretius wrote in his long poem on nature that what is food to one creature may be sharp poison to another, and English writers were using the saying as something already worn out by sixteen hundred and four. A dictionary of proverbs printed in sixteen sixteen gives it in almost the wording we use now. The eating customs are real as well, and more mixed than the lesson lets on. France is famous for cooked snails and gets through something between sixteen thousand and thirty thousand tonnes of them a year, depending on whose figure you take; but between ninety and ninety-five per cent are imported, a good share from Romania and the Baltic countries, while French farms supply perhaps a thousand tonnes from a few hundred growers. The animals are not the garden pest of the lesson either: the kinds on the table are mainly the large Burgundy snail and two of its relatives.

标题里的想法大约有两千年了。罗马诗人卢克莱修在他那首讲自然的长诗里写过：一种生物的食物，可能是另一种生物的烈毒；到 1604 年，英国作家引用这句谚语时已经嫌它陈旧。1616 年的一本谚语辞书里，它的措辞和今天几乎一样。饮食风俗也是真的，而且比课文说的更复杂。法国以煮蜗牛闻名，一年吃掉大约一万六千到三万吨（看你采信谁的数）；但其中九成到九成五是进口的，相当一部分来自罗马尼亚和波罗的海国家，法国本土农场大概只产一千吨，出自几百个养殖户。盘子里的也不是课文里那种花园害虫：主要是个头大的勃艮第蜗牛和它的两个亲戚。

✨ How it is written赏析

Look at how the piece is put together: four paragraphs of argument, then one short story that proves the argument, and a last line that pays for everything. The argument half does something clever with the word you. The writer does not say 'people in the south like octopus'; he says if you lived there you would like it, which drops the reader inside another kitchen and makes the disagreement feel like a matter of luck rather than taste. Then the generalisation narrows to one animal, the animal narrows to one friend, and the friend narrows to one wet afternoon. Notice the joke hidden in the vocabulary: the snails are discovered taking a stroll on the prize plants, as if they were guests in a garden, and at the end they take over the hall like an occupying army. Giving small creatures the manners of people is one of the cheapest and best comic tools in English. And the final sentence does the work of a whole paragraph of feeling: it does not tell us the writer was disgusted, only that he has never managed to look at one since.

看它是怎么搭的：四段议论，然后一个短故事替议论作证，最后一句话把全部账结了。议论那一半在 you 这个词上做了手脚。作者不说「南方人爱吃章鱼」，而说「如果你住在那儿，你就会觉得它是美味」——一句话把读者塞进别人的厨房，于是这场分歧看起来像运气问题，不是品味问题。接着，泛论收到一种动物身上，动物收到一个朋友身上，朋友收到一个下雨的下午。请注意词语里藏的玩笑：蜗牛是在名贵花草上「散步」时被发现的，像花园里的客人；到结尾它们又像占领军一样接管了门厅。给小动物配上人的举止，是英文里最便宜也最好用的喜剧手段之一。最后那一句顶得上一整段抒情：它没说作者恶心，只说他从此再没能正眼看过一只蜗牛。

🧭 What it argues, and does it still hold?解读

The lesson's claim — that what counts as food is mostly a matter of how you were raised — still holds, but it is no longer the whole story, because the question has moved into law and into animal science. In twenty twenty-four the state of Washington became the first place in the world to forbid the farming of octopus, and California went further in the same year, banning both the farming and the sale of farmed octopus. Two years earlier the United Kingdom had put cephalopod molluscs and decapod crustaceans into its sentience act, after a review of more than three hundred studies reported very strong evidence that octopods can feel. So the Mediterranean treat of the lesson is now also an argument about whether the animal suffers, and that argument is not settled by upbringing at all. The second thing worth noticing is how fast tastes travel. Raw fish was a byword for the unthinkable in English a lifetime ago and is now sold in supermarkets. Chinese readers have the mirror image of the whole lesson at home: river snails are street food in the south, stewed with chilli or served in a bowl of rice noodles, while the same animal is a pest to be picked off cabbages elsewhere; and preserved eggs and fermented bean curd, which are breakfast at home, turn up regularly on foreign lists of the most frightening things to eat. The lesson is right that none of this is reasonable. It is less right that nothing changes.

课文的判断——什么算食物，主要取决于你从小被怎么喂大——今天依然成立，但不再是全部，因为这个问题已经走进了法律和动物科学。2024 年，华盛顿州成为世界上第一个禁止养殖章鱼的地方；同年加州走得更远，连养殖章鱼的销售也一起禁了。再往前两年，英国在一次审阅三百多项研究、认定「章鱼能感受」的证据非常有力之后，把头足类和十足类写进了动物感知法。于是课文里那道地中海美味，如今同时是一场「它会不会痛」的争论，而这场争论跟从小吃什么毫无关系。第二件值得注意的事是口味跑得有多快：生鱼在英语里曾是「不可思议」的代名词，现在超市就有卖。中国读者家里就有这一课的镜像：田螺在南方是街头小吃，辣炒的、泡在米粉碗里的；而同一种动物在别处是要从菜叶上捉走的害虫；至于家常早餐里的皮蛋和臭豆腐，则常年出现在外国人「最可怕的食物」名单上。课文说这些事都不讲道理，说对了；说它们不会变，就没那么对。

💬 Discuss and write讨论与写作
  1. Name one food you grew up with that visitors find hard to accept, and one you still cannot face yourself. What, if anything, would change your mind about the second one?
    说一样你从小吃、而外地客人难以接受的食物，再说一样你自己至今不敢碰的。什么样的理由会让你改变对第二样的看法？
  2. If there is good evidence that an animal can feel pain, should that change what we are willing to eat? Give the strongest argument against your own answer before you give your answer.
    如果有充分证据说明一种动物能感到疼痛，这该不该改变我们愿意吃什么？先说出反对你自己立场的最强理由，再给你的答案。
  3. Write a hundred and twenty words about a gift that went wrong. Keep the argument out of it: start with the thing itself, and let the last sentence carry the feeling, as this lesson does.
    写 120 词，讲一件送错了的礼物。不要议论：从东西本身写起，把情绪留给最后一句，像这一课那样。

③ How the hard sentences are built难句结构

Sentences are counted in the lesson text, title not included. Find the sentence in your book, then read our notes on its structure.句子按课文正文数（不含标题）。在教材里找到这一句，再看本站的结构讲解。

Sentence 1第 1 句 (begins开头 “People become quite …”)

  • what can be eaten: a what-clause as the object of decide, with a modal in the passive.what can be eaten：what 引导宾语从句，作 decide 的宾语；情态 + 被动。

Sentence 2第 2 句 (begins开头 “If you lived …”)

  • A supposition that is not true now: If + past, would do.与现在事实相反的假设：If + 过去式, would do。
  • consider + object + noun: regard something as being something.consider + 宾语 + 名词：认为……是……。

Sentence 4第 4 句 (begins开头 “On the other …”)

  • Somebody's stomach turns: they feel sick. at the idea of doing: as soon as they think of it.sb's stomach turns：反胃，恶心。at the idea of doing：一想到……。
  • What follows the dash is in apposition and explains the cooking just mentioned.破折号后是同位语，解释 frying potatoes in animal fat。

Sentence 5第 5 句 (begins开头 “The sad truth …”)

  • The truth is that ...: a that-clause used as the complement.The truth is that…：表语从句。
  • be brought up to do: be raised from childhood to do. stick to: keep to the same thing.be brought up to do：从小被培养成做……。stick to：一直坚持。

Sentence 6第 6 句 (begins开头 “No creature has …”)

  • A negative plus a comparative works as a superlative: no other animal has been praised and insulted so much.否定词 + 比较级 = 最高级：没有一种动物比蜗牛受到更多的……（蜗牛受到的毁誉最多）。

Sentence 11第 11 句 (begins开头 “For years he …”)

  • Present perfect continuous with for years: he has kept on asking, again and again.现在完成进行时 + for years：多年来一直在（反复）请求。
  • ask sb to do ... and (to) do ...: the second to is left out.ask sb to do … and (to) do …：第二个 to 省略。

Sentence 13第 13 句 (begins开头 “I happened to …”)

  • happen to do: do by chance; happen to be doing: be doing by chance.happen to do：碰巧；happen to be doing：碰巧正在做。
  • notice something doing: see it in the middle of an action; the snails are treated as people out for a walk.notice sth doing：注意到……正在……；taking a stroll 拟人化（蜗牛在「散步」）。

Sentence 14第 14 句 (begins开头 “Acting on a …”)

  • act on an impulse: do something because the wish came suddenly; the participle phrase works as an adverbial.act on (an) impulse：凭一时冲动行事；现在分词短语作状语。
  • Three verbs in a row give the sentence a quick, light rhythm.三个动词并列，节奏轻快。

④ Grammar in this lesson本课语法

The unreal conditional about now: If + past, would do与现在事实相反的虚拟：If + 过去式, would do
To suppose a situation that does not exist at present, put the if-clause in the past simple (with were for be) and the main clause in would / could / might plus the base form. When you is used to mean anyone at all, the pattern invites the reader to stand in someone else's shoes.
假设一种现在并不存在的情况，从句用一般过去时（be 用 were），主句用 would/could/might + 动词原形。用 you 泛指「任何人」时，常用来让读者设身处地。
  • If I had more time, I would learn to play the piano.
  • If she were here, she would know what to do.
find + object + adjective / consider + object + nounfind + 宾语 + 形容词／consider + 宾语 + 名词
find / consider / think / make + object + complement (an adjective or a noun) says how you judge something or what you turn it into. If the object is an infinitive or a clause, it is usual to put it first as an empty object: find it hard to do.
find/consider/think/make + 宾语 + 宾补（形容词或名词），表示「觉得/认为/使……怎样」。如果宾语是不定式或从句，常用 it 作形式宾语：find it hard to do。

In this lesson, sentence four: consider octopus a great delicacy; sentence five: some people find it repulsive.

本课：第 2 句 consider octopus a great delicacy（认为章鱼是美味）；第 3 句 some people find it repulsive（有人觉得它恶心）。

  • I find this book very interesting.
  • They consider him a hero.
  • She found it hard to sleep in the noisy room.
happen to do / happen to be doing: by chancehappen to do / happen to be doing：碰巧
happen to do means to do something by chance; happen to be doing, to be doing it at that moment by chance; happen to have done, to have done it already. The same idea can be put as It happened that ...
happen to do（碰巧做）、happen to be doing（碰巧正在做）、happen to have done（碰巧已经做过）。也可改写为 It happened that…。

In this lesson, sentence fifteen: the writer happened to be walking in the garden, and so caught sight of a crowd of snails.

  • It happened that I was at home when he called.

⑥ Dictation and retelling听写与复述

  1. Listen to the whole recording once with the book closed. Say in one sentence what it is about.合上书完整听一遍录音，用一句话说出讲了什么。
  2. Dictate sentence by sentence: pause after each sentence and write it down. Replay a sentence at most three times.逐句听写：每句后暂停写下来，同一句最多重放三遍。
  3. Check against the text in your coursebook. Mark every mistake by type: a word you did not know, a word you knew but did not hear (linking, weak forms), or spelling.对照教材课文自查，把每个错按类型标出：不认识的词、认识却没听出的词（连读、弱读）、拼写。
  4. Words you did not hear go into your word book above. Re-listen to those sentences and shadow them.没听出的词加进上面的生词本；把那几句再听、跟读。
  5. Retell from the keywords below without looking at the text, then compare and retell once more.只看下面的关键词、不看课文复述一遍，对照后再复述一遍。

Keywords for retelling复述关键词

  • what we eat: octopus in the Mediterranean, potatoes fried in animal fat in the north
  • snails: a luxury in some places, despised in others
  • Robert keeps asking me for snails
  • after a heavy shower: several dozen in a paper bag
  • two hours of talk; snails for dinner — but they are all over the hall

Summary writing摘要写作

In no more than eighty words, tell the story of the snails the writer took to Robert.

用不超过 80 个词讲述作者给罗伯特送蜗牛的故事。

Points a good summary covers好摘要要覆盖的要点
  • Snails are looked down on where Robert lives, he has no garden in the town, and for years he has asked the writer to gather some for him.罗伯特住在一个看不起蜗牛的国家，城里没有花园，多年来一直请作者给他捉蜗牛。
  • After heavy rain the writer saw a great many snails on his plants, gathered a few dozen on a sudden impulse, and carried them over in a paper bag.一场大雨后，作者在花园里看到很多蜗牛，一时冲动捉了几十只，装进纸袋给罗伯特送去。
  • The paper bag stayed in the hall; the two men talked in the sitting room for two hours, and Robert asked him to stay for a dinner of snails.作者把袋子放在门厅，两人在客厅聊了两个小时；罗伯特留他吃晚饭，主菜是蜗牛。
  • When they came out, the snails had got out of the bag and were all over the hall, and the writer has not been able to face one since.他们走出客厅，发现蜗牛逃出纸袋，爬满了门厅；作者从此再也不敢看蜗牛。

🎯 Before you open the lesson开课前
A play can run for years, with the same cast saying the same words evening after evening, so you would think every one of them must be word-perfect. In this story a well-known actor plays a nobleman who has spent twenty years in prison. In the final act the jailer brings him a letter, and the actor always insists on being handed the whole text so that he can read it aloud. One evening the actor playing the jailer decides to test him and hands him a blank sheet instead. The nobleman looks at it, half closes his eyes, says the light is too poor and asks the jailer to read it for him. The jailer cannot remember a word of it either, so he says he must fetch his glasses, hurries off, and comes back with the glasses and the proper letter. This is a piece of stage mischief that rebounds on the joker. Learn insist that ... (should) do, play a joke on, find out if, be expected to do, much to one's amusement and proceed to do.
一出戏连演多年，演员夜夜重复同样的台词，按理早该背得滚瓜烂熟。可有位名演员演一个在巴士底狱关了 20 年的贵族，最后一幕狱卒会递给他一封信，他每场都坚持要把信的全文写出来照着念。一天晚上，演狱卒的同事想开个玩笑，看他到底背没背下来，递上去的是一张白纸。「贵族」盯着看了几秒，眯起眼说「光线太暗，你念给我听」，把纸递回去。狱卒自己也一个字都记不得，只好说「我去拿眼镜」，跑下台，拿来眼镜和那封原信念了起来。本课是以牙还牙的舞台趣事，学 insist that … (should) do（虚拟）、play a joke on、find out if、be expected to do、much to one's amusement 和 proceed to do。
Questions to keep in mind as you read读的时候带着这几个问题：
  1. Why is a play that runs for years bad news for the actors in it?为什么说长演不衰的戏对演员来说并不是好事？
  2. Why did the jailer hand over a blank sheet?狱卒为什么要递一张白纸？
  3. Who ended up reading the letter aloud, and how did the nobleman manage it?最后是谁念了信？那位「贵族」是怎样化解的？

🌍 Where it comes from出处与背景

Long runs are not an exaggeration. One London murder play opened in November nineteen fifty-two and is still being performed; it reached its thirty-thousandth performance in March twenty twenty-five, and it has held the record for the longest theatrical run in the world since April nineteen fifty-eight. In a cast like that, one speech may be delivered several thousand times by the same person. Theatres have always known the danger, which is why a prompter sits with the book in the wings, and why the letters, newspapers and telegrams carried on stage are usually real props with real words printed on them. The prison in the lesson is real too. When the crowd forced its way in on the fourteenth of July, seventeen eighty-nine, the Bastille held only seven men: four forgers, one old prisoner held over a plot against an earlier king, an Irishman believed to be mad, and a young count whose own family had him locked away under a royal warrant. Noble prisoners put away at a relative's request were a fact of the period, which is why the costume on stage is not absurd.

长演不衰不是夸张。伦敦有一出凶杀剧 1952 年 11 月开演，至今还在演；2025 年 3 月演到第三万场，从 1958 年 4 月起一直是世界上连演最久的戏。在这样的班子里，同一个人可能要把同一段台词说上几千遍。剧场一向知道这个风险：所以侧台有提示员守着台本，所以舞台上拿的信件、报纸、电报通常是印着真字的实物道具。课文里那座监狱也是真的。1789 年 7 月 14 日人群冲进巴士底狱时，里面只关着七个人：四个伪造文书的、一个因为早年谋刺国王而长期关押的、一个被当成疯子的爱尔兰人，还有一个被自己家里人用王室密札关进来的年轻伯爵。贵族因亲属请求而被关押，是那个年代的实情——所以舞台上那套戏服并不荒唐。

✨ How it is written赏析

The shape of this story is a boomerang: the man who sets the trap walks into it. Alexander builds it in three moves. First comes a general claim, that actors in long runs ought to be word-perfect; then comes the word yet, which warns you that a counter-example is on its way. Next comes the slow set-up, in which every detail that will matter later is planted in advance: the letter is always supplied complete, the cell is dark, and there is a second actor standing on the stage. Only after that do we get dialogue, and the dialogue is very short. Notice that the funniest line in the piece is not a joke at all; it is an ordinary complaint about the lighting. The nobleman never admits that he has forgotten anything, and that is exactly why an audience laughs: both men are rescued by good manners. If you want to write comedy in English, this is the lesson to copy. Plant the props early, keep the closing line flat, and let the second character pay for the first one's trick.

这个故事的形状像回旋镖：设陷阱的人自己撞上去。亚历山大分三步搭起来。先给一个一般判断——长演的戏里演员早该一字不差；接着一个 yet，预告反例要来了。然后是慢慢铺垫，后面要用的细节全部提前埋好：信每场都备全文、牢房昏暗、台上还站着第二个演员。到这之后才出现对话，而且对话极短。请注意，全篇最好笑的那一句根本不是笑话，只是一句关于光线的寻常抱怨。那位「贵族」始终没承认自己忘了词，观众恰恰为此而笑：两个人都被客气话救了场。要用英文写喜剧，这一课值得照学——道具早埋，收尾句写得越平淡越好，让第二个人替第一个人的恶作剧付账。

🧭 What it argues, and does it still hold?解读

Does the story still hold? The craft does; the technology has moved on. Productions today lean on memory less than the tale suggests: scripts are read from tablets in rehearsal, a line can be fed to an actor through a tiny earpiece, and in a musical the words are often cued by the band. What has not changed is why performers go blank. The cause is rarely a poor memory; it is repetition, which turns a practised action into something that runs without attention. Anyone who has driven a familiar road and arrived with no memory of the journey knows the feeling. Look also at what the two men do once the words have gone: they improvise inside character, and improvisation is now taught as a craft of its own. For Chinese readers the lesson has a familiar ring, because reciting a text until it is fixed in the mind carries real prestige in Chinese schooling, and there is an old argument about whether reading something a hundred times produces understanding or only fluent noise. This story takes the sceptical side, and takes it gently: the two actors have spoken those words a thousand times and can produce none of them, yet they finish the scene by thinking on their feet. Memory failed; presence of mind did not.

这个故事今天还成立吗？手艺成立，技术变了。今天的演出没那么依赖记忆：排练时剧本放在平板上，台词可以通过微型耳机送进演员耳朵，音乐剧的词常由乐队给提示。没变的是演员为什么会断片：原因很少是记性差，而是重复——重复会把练熟的动作变成不需注意就能跑完的东西。凡是开车走过熟路、到了地方却想不起一路经过什么的人，都懂这种感觉。还要看这两个人词忘了之后干了什么：他们在角色里即兴，而即兴今天是当作一门专门手艺来教的。对中国读者，这一课听着耳熟：把文章读到烂熟在中文教育里地位很高，而「读书百遍是不是真能其义自见」也是一桩老争论。这个故事站在怀疑的一边，而且很温和——两位演员把那些词说过上千遍，一个字也交不出来，却靠临场的脑子把这一场演完了。失灵的是记忆，没失灵的是反应。

💬 Discuss and write讨论与写作

    你有没有把一句话重复到它完全失去意义的经历——工作中的问候、安全提示、婚礼上的致辞？你后来怎么处理的？

④ Grammar in this lesson本课语法

insist / suggest / demand that + (should) do
Verbs of requiring, suggesting and ordering (insist, suggest, demand, order, require) take that + (should) + base form, and should is often dropped, especially in American English. When insist means 'say firmly that something is true', normal tenses are used instead.
表示「要求、建议、命令」的动词（insist、suggest、demand、order、require）后的 that 从句用 (should) + 动词原形，should 可省（美式多省）。insist 若表示「坚持说（某事是事实）」则用正常时态。

In this lesson, sentence nine: the nobleman always demands the complete text, and the sentence uses insisted that + should + base form.

本课：第 7 句那位贵族总是坚持要把信全文写出来（原句的结构＝insisted that + should + 动词原形）。

Compare this with the rule above: here insist means 'say firmly that something is true', so the tense is normal.

对比：He insisted that he had paid the bill.（坚持说付过了，事实陈述）

  • The doctor insisted that he (should) stay in bed.
  • She suggested that we (should) leave early.
find out / see / wonder if: whether or notfind out / see / wonder if：是否
Both if and whether can introduce an object clause meaning 'whether or not', and the verbs in front are usually find out, see, wonder, ask or check. The clause keeps statement word order, and it takes the past perfect when its action happened before the main verb.
if 和 whether 都可引导宾语从句「是否」；动词常用 find out、see、wonder、ask、check。从句用陈述语序；动作在主句之前完成时用过去完成时。

In this lesson, sentences ten and eleven: the jailer wants to find out if the other man has learned it; in sentence seventeen he is anxious to see if his colleague has the words at last.

本课：第 8–9 句狱卒想 find out if 同事到底背下来没有；第 15 句他 anxious to see if 同事终于记住了台词。

  • Can you find out if the shop is open on Sunday?
  • I wonder whether she got my message.
to somebody's + feeling noun: to one's amusement / surprise / horrorto sb's + 情感名词：to one's amusement / surprise / horror
(much) to + a possessive + amusement / surprise / delight / horror / dismay means 'and this greatly amused (surprised, delighted ...) that person'. much comes before to and makes the phrase stronger.
(much) to + 物主代词/名词所有格 + amusement/surprise/delight/horror/dismay：令某人……的是；much 加强语气，放在 to 前。

In this lesson, sentence twenty-four: the nobleman finds it very funny indeed when the jailer hurries off for his glasses and comes back able to read.

本课：第 22 句写那位贵族觉得十分好笑：狱卒下台去取眼镜，回来就念得出来了。

  • To my surprise, he remembered my name.
  • Much to our delight, the sun came out.

Keywords for retelling复述关键词

  • plays that run for years; actors repeat the same lines
  • an aristocrat in the Bastille; a letter in the last act
  • he insists the letter is written out in full
  • one night: a blank sheet of paper
  • "The light is dim." — he hands the paper back
  • "I must get my glasses." — back with glasses and the real letter

Summary writing摘要写作

In no more than eighty words, tell the story of this stage joke and say how each of the two actors reacted.

用不超过 80 个词讲述这个舞台上的玩笑，写出两个演员各自的反应。

Points a good summary covers好摘要要覆盖的要点
  • In a long-running play a famous actor played a nobleman shut up in the Bastille, and in the last act he had to read a letter brought in by the jailer; he always demanded the complete text on paper.一位名演员在长演的戏里扮演被关进巴士底狱的贵族，最后一幕要念狱卒递来的信，他总坚持信要写全。
  • One evening the actor playing the jailer wanted to see whether he had learned it, so he handed him a blank sheet.一天晚上，演狱卒的同事想看他是否已背下信的内容，递给他一张白纸。
  • The nobleman looked at the blank sheet for a few seconds, said the light was too poor, and asked the jailer to read it instead.贵族盯着白纸看了几秒，说光线太暗，让狱卒念给他听，把纸递了回去。
  • The jailer could not remember the words either, so he said he needed his glasses, went off, and came back with glasses and the real letter.狱卒也记不住，只好说去拿眼镜，下台后拿来眼镜和原信念了。
Our model summary本站范文

In a long-running play, a famous actor played a noble prisoner who had to read a letter in the final scene. He always demanded the full text on paper. One night the actor playing the jailer handed him a blank sheet to test his memory. After staring at it, he complained that the light was poor and gave it back. The jailer could not remember the words either, so he went off for glasses and returned with the real letter.（80 词）

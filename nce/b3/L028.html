Lesson 28 · Five Pounds Too Dear 五英镑也太贵

📘 New Concept English · Book 3 · Lesson 28 “Five Pounds Too Dear”新概念英语 第三册 Lesson 28《Five Pounds Too Dear》

🎯 Before you open the lesson开课前
The big ship is not even in harbour before small craft piled with goods come racing out to her, and the traders are up on deck spreading out Persian rugs, Indian silks, copper coffee pots and handmade silverware. It is hard to keep your hands in your pockets. The writer decides to buy nothing until he is ashore, and is no sooner off the ship than a man fastens on to him with a diamond ring. The stones are the size of marbles, and to prove them real the man presses one against a shop window and cuts a deep line in the glass; it takes half an hour to shake him off. Next comes a seller of pens and watches. One pen has a claim of American manufacture neatly cut into the gold cap; the asking price is fifty pounds, and then, as a special favour, thirty. The writer holds up five fingers. The man throws his arms about as though the offer were an insult, and comes down to ten; the writer shrugs and walks away, and a moment later the man runs after him, pushes the pen into his hands and takes the five pounds readily enough while complaining bitterly. The writer is delighted with his bargain until he gets back on board and finds that the pen cannot be filled with ink at all. Hence the title: even five pounds was money thrown away. This is a bargaining scene from a traveller's notebook. Learn no sooner ... than with inversion, have no intention of doing, go to great lengths, get rid of, as if and no matter how.
大客轮还没进港，载满货物的小船就飞驰而来，小贩爬上甲板，铺开波斯地毯、印度丝绸、铜咖啡壶和手工银器，让人很难不动心。作者打定主意上岸再买，可一下船就被一个卖钻戒的人缠住：钻石大得像弹珠，那人还在商店橱窗上一按，划出一道深痕来「证明」是真的，作者花了半个多小时才脱身。接着来了个卖钢笔手表的：笔帽底下刻着「美国制造」，开价 50 英镑，「特别优惠」30 英镑；作者伸出五个手指，对方连连做出荒唐的样子，降到 10 英镑；作者耸耸肩要走，那人追上来把笔塞到他手里，嘴上直叫亏，收下 5 英镑却很痛快。作者正得意，回船才发现这支笔怎么也灌不进墨水，至今一个字也没写过。标题的意思是：连五英镑都花冤了。本课是讨价还价的旅行趣事，学 no sooner … than（倒装）、have no intention of doing、go to great lengths、get rid of、as if 和 no matter how。

🌍 Where it comes from出处与背景

The seller's demonstration is a piece of folklore that was already old when the lesson was written and is still repeated today. Scratching glass proves almost nothing. On the scale of hardness that mineralogists use, ordinary window glass sits at about five and a half, while the two stones most often sold in place of diamond are harder than that: cubic zirconia is about eight and moissanite about nine. Both will cut glass quite happily, as will quartz, topaz and a good steel file. The test is also destructive, which is why the trade advises against hardness testing altogether: if the stone is not a diamond you have just damaged it, and if it is, you have learned nothing you did not know. What jewellers actually use is a probe that measures how fast heat passes through the stone, a reading of how much it bends light, a loupe to look for the tiny flaws that natural stones have, and a laboratory certificate. The other detail in the lesson is just as telling: the words cut into the cap of the pen. A statement of origin is the cheapest thing on any counterfeit, because it costs the same to engrave a true one as a false one.

卖家的那套演示是一则民间常识，这一课写作时它就已经很老了，今天还在被重复。划玻璃几乎什么都不能证明。在矿物学家用的硬度标尺上，普通窗玻璃约为五点五，而最常用来冒充钻石的两种石头都比它硬：立方氧化锆约八，莫桑石约九。两者都能轻松划开玻璃，石英、黄玉和一把好钢锉也能。这个测试还会造成损坏，所以业内干脆不建议做硬度测试：若不是钻石，你刚刚毁了它；若是钻石，你什么新东西也没学到。珠宝业真正用的是：测热量通过石头有多快的探针、折光程度的读数、用放大镜找天然石特有的细小瑕疵，以及实验室证书。课文里另一处细节同样说明问题：笔帽上刻的那几个字。产地声明是任何假货上最便宜的一样东西，因为刻一个真的和刻一个假的，成本一样。

✨ How it is written赏析

The scene is almost entirely made of numbers and gestures, and that is what makes it work. Count the prices: fifty, thirty, five, ten, five. The whole plot is in that sequence, and the reader does the arithmetic without being told what to feel. Then notice that the two men barely speak. One holds up five fingers; the other throws his arms about and later throws them up in despair; the buyer shrugs and walks away. A bargain is being struck across a language barrier, and the writing shows it rather than explaining it, which is why it is funny instead of merely informative. There is one piece of wordplay worth keeping: the writer admits to being impressed, in one sense, by how large the stones are, and a few lines later one of them makes an impression, in the other sense, in a shop window. The same word does duty for a state of mind and a dent, and the joke is never pointed out. Finally, look at the shape of the ending. The last sentence does not say that the pen was a fake; it says, in a quiet aside, that the thing has yet to produce one word of writing, and the closing phrase about the present day turns a one-off mishap into a long and comic grievance.

这个场景几乎全由数字和动作构成，而这正是它成立的原因。数一数价格：五十、三十、五、十、五。整个情节就在这串数里，读者自己做完算术，不需要被告知该有什么感受。再注意，两个人几乎不说话：一个伸出五个手指；另一个挥舞手臂，后来又把手臂往上一摊表示绝望；买家耸耸肩走开。一桩交易是跨着语言隔墙谈成的，而文字是把它演出来，不是解释出来——所以好笑，而不只是提供信息。有一处文字游戏值得留着：作者被石头的大小 impressed（打动），几行之后那石头在玻璃上 made an impression（压出痕迹）。同一个词既管心境又管凹痕，而这个玩笑作者从不点明。最后看结尾的形状。最后一句没说那支笔是假的，只是轻轻带一句：至今它一个字也没写过；而 to this day 这个短语，把一次性的倒霉事变成了一桩漫长而好笑的怨气。

🧭 What it argues, and does it still hold?解读

What has lasted here is not the story but the mechanism. The seller's two moves are still the two moves. The first is a demonstration that looks like evidence and is not; the second is a price that starts high so that a fall looks like a gift. Both have aged into new forms. The fake proof is now a certificate photographed on a phone, a review written to order, a seal on a box, a figure in a laboratory report nobody can read; the test of a good claim is still whether it could come out the other way. The anchored price has moved online and attracted the law: in the European Union any announced reduction must now state the lowest price the trader actually charged over at least the previous thirty days, precisely because an imaginary original price does the selling. For readers used to haggling, the lesson also contains a practical correction to a common belief. The writer thinks he has won because he pushed the price from fifty to five. In fact he lost, and the reason he lost is contained in the detail he reports but does not notice: the man accepted five pounds readily. A seller who gives in that easily is telling you what the thing is worth, and the right question at that moment is not how low the price can go but why he is so willing to let it. This is also the answer to the old market rule that you should never ask the price before you have asked whether the thing is genuine.

这里经受住时间的不是故事，而是机制。卖家的两招还是那两招。第一招：做一个看起来像证据、其实不是证据的演示；第二招：先报一个高价，好让降价看起来像赠与。两招都已经长出新形态。假证据如今是手机上拍的一张证书、一篇定做的评价、盒子上的一枚封签、一份没人读得懂的化验报告里的数字；而判断一个断言好不好，标准依旧是：它有没有可能得出相反的结果。锚定价搬到了网上，并且招来了法律：在欧盟，任何「降价」声明如今都必须写出商家在此前至少三十天里实际收取过的最低价——正因为一个虚构的原价本身就在卖货。对习惯讲价的读者，这一课还附带纠正一个常见信念。作者以为自己赢了，因为他把价钱从五十压到五。实际上他输了，而输的理由就藏在他报告了却没留意的那个细节里：那人很痛快地收下了五英镑。一个让得这么轻松的卖家，正在告诉你这东西值多少钱；那一刻该问的不是「还能压到多低」，而是「他为什么这么愿意让」。这也正是那条老市场规矩的道理：先问真假，再问价钱。

④ Grammar in this lesson本课语法

no sooner ... than / hardly ... when: the moment one thing happened, the next didno sooner … than / hardly … when：一……就……
The main clause goes into the past perfect and the other clause into the past simple: I had no sooner done ... than I did ... When no sooner or hardly stands at the front of the sentence, the clause is inverted: No sooner had I ... than ...; Hardly had I ... when ...
主句用过去完成时，从句用一般过去时：I had no sooner done … than I did …。no sooner 或 hardly 放句首时要倒装：No sooner had I … than …；Hardly had I … when …。
  • She had no sooner sat down than the phone rang.
  • No sooner had we arrived than it started to rain.
have no intention of doing / go to great lengths to do / get rid of
Three useful phrases: have no intention of doing, which is firmer than not wanting to; go to great lengths to do, meaning to take enormous trouble; and get rid of, which works for people as well as things. Note that the first takes doing and the second takes to do.
三个常用短语：have no intention of doing（无意做，比 don't want to 更坚决）；go to great lengths to do（千方百计做）；get rid of（摆脱掉，可用于人或物）。注意前一个接 doing，第二个接 to do。
  • I have no intention of selling my car.
  • They went to great lengths to keep the party a secret.
  • How can I get rid of these ants?
no matter how / however + adjective or adverbno matter how / however + 形容词/副词
no matter how + adjective or adverb + subject + verb means the same as however ...: it does not matter to what degree. The same goes for no matter what, who and where, which equal whatever, whoever and wherever.
no matter how + 形容词/副词 + 主语 + 动词 ＝ however …：不管多么……。同类：no matter what/who/where = whatever/whoever/wherever。
  • No matter how tired I am, I always read before bed.
  • However hard he works, he never gets a pay rise.

⑤ Patterns to reuse可以套用的句型

  • I had no sooner … than … 我刚……就……
    I had no sooner got home than it began to pour.
  • No matter how hard X tried, … 不管 X 多么努力，都……
    No matter how hard she tried, the jar would not open.

Summary writing摘要写作

In no more than eighty words, tell the story of how the writer came to buy the pen.

用不超过 80 个词讲述作者买钢笔的经过。

Points a good summary covers好摘要要覆盖的要点
  • He had decided to buy nothing until he was ashore, and the moment he landed a man with a diamond ring fastened on to him, and took half an hour to shake off.作者决定下船后再买东西；一下船就被卖钻戒的人缠住，花了半个多小时才摆脱。
  • Then a second man offered him pens; one had a claim of American manufacture cut into its gold cap, and the price went from fifty pounds to thirty.接着一个人向他推销钢笔，笔帽上刻着「美国制造」，开价 50 英镑，又说可以 30 英镑卖给他。
  • The writer would go no higher than five; the man pretended to be insulted, came down to ten, then ran after him and pushed the pen into his hands for five.作者只肯出 5 英镑；那人装作觉得荒唐，降到 10 英镑，后来追上来把笔塞给他，收下了 5 英镑。
  • He was very pleased with himself until he got back on board, where he found that the pen would take no ink, and it has never written a word since.作者很得意，可回到船上发现这支笔根本灌不进墨水，至今一个字也没写过。
Our model summary本站范文

I waited until I left the ship to shop. After escaping from a man selling huge diamonds, I met another offering pens. One looked real and was marked "made in the U.S.A.". He wanted £50, then £30, but I held up five fingers. He waved his arms as if insulted and came down to £10. When I walked off, he chased me and took my £5. Later I discovered the pen could never be filled with ink.（77 词）

Lesson 14 · A Noble Gangster 高尚的歹徒

📘 New Concept English · Book 3 · Lesson 14 “A Noble Gangster”新概念英语 第三册 Lesson 14《A Noble Gangster》

B2🧠 21 new words个新词📝 Full notes有完整讲解

🎯 Before you open the lesson开课前
In twentieth-century Chicago, shopkeepers paid gangs for 'protection', and a shop whose owner did not pay was wrecked. That crime, says the writer, is nothing new. A fourteenth-century Englishman, Sir John Hawkwood, took a band of soldiers to Italy; while the city-states fought one another he rented his men to whichever prince would meet his price. In peacetime, when trade was slack, he marched into a city-state, burnt a few farms, and then let it be known that he would leave if he were paid. The odd part is that the Italians treated him as a hero, and at his death the city of Florence buried him with the honours of a statesman. The lesson is a historical essay that uses the past to explain the present, and its irony hides in the title. Learn would rather ... than, used to and would for past habit, There was a time when, come to be known, and have something done.
20 世纪的芝加哥，店主要向黑帮交「保护费」，不交就砸店。作者说这不是现代才有的罪行：14 世纪的英国人约翰·霍克伍德爵士带一队兵到意大利，城邦打仗时他把兵租给出得起高价的王公；太平时生意不好，他就带人开进某个城邦，烧几座农庄，然后表示交了保护费就走。奇怪的是意大利人把他当英雄，他 80 岁去世时佛罗伦萨人为他办了国葬。本课是以古证今的历史随笔，讽刺意味藏在标题「高尚的歹徒」里。学 would rather … than、used to 与 would 表过去习惯、There was a time when、come to be known 和 have sth done。

📖 Understanding the piece读懂这篇

Written for the course by L. G. Alexander, but the central figure is real: Sir John Hawkwood, the English mercenary captain the Italians called Giovanni Acuto. We checked his dates, his funeral and the painting against reference accounts; two of the lesson's details do not survive the check, and they are set out under 'view'.

课文由编者 L. G. 亚历山大为教材自撰，但核心人物是真的：英国雇佣兵首领约翰·霍克伍德爵士，意大利人叫他乔瓦尼·阿库托。我们按工具书核了他的生卒、葬礼与那幅画；课文有两处细节没能通过核对，写在「解读」里。

🌍 Where it comes from出处与背景

Fourteenth-century Italy was not a country but a quarrel of city-states rich enough to buy armies and too small to keep them. That market created the condottiere, the contract captain, and Hawkwood was the most successful of them. Born at Sible Hedingham in Essex around the year thirteen twenty, he crossed the Alps after the English and French crowns made peace and spent some thirty years in the Italian wars, ending as captain-general of the Florentine army. The trade had two sides, and the lesson names both honestly. In war a prince hired the company; in peace the company hired itself out to nobody and had to eat, so it camped on somebody's land and sold its own departure. Contemporaries did not pretend this was anything but what it was, yet they also understood that a captain who kept his word was worth more than one who did not, and Hawkwood's reputation rested on being reliable rather than merciful. When he died in Florence in March thirteen ninety-four, the city gave him a funeral of the kind normally reserved for its own great men, and buried him in the cathedral.

十四世纪的意大利不是一个国家，而是一群争吵的城邦：富到买得起军队，又小到养不住军队。这个市场造出了「condottiere」——契约队长，而霍克伍德是其中最成功的一个。他约在一三二〇年生于埃塞克斯的锡布尔·赫丁汉，英法两国王室休战后越过阿尔卑斯山，在意大利的战争里待了约三十年，最后做到佛罗伦萨军队的总队长。这门生意有两面，课文老实地把两面都说了。打仗时王公雇这支队伍；太平时没人雇，队伍还要吃饭，于是它驻到某家的地上，把「自己离开」卖掉。同时代的人并不假装这是别的什么，但他们也明白：一个守信的队长比一个不守信的值钱得多，而霍克伍德的名声靠的是可靠，不是仁慈。一三九四年三月他死在佛罗伦萨，这座城给了他通常只留给本城伟人的葬礼，并把他葬在主教座堂里。

✨ How it is written赏析

The essay works by inversion, and the title does it in two words. 'Noble' is used twice over: he was a knight, and he was anything but noble. The structure is a trap. Three sentences of Chicago put the reader in a world where the moral labels are already fixed — gangsters, destroyed, had to pay — and then one short sentence swings the frame: this is not a modern crime. Everything after that rides on the labels the reader has already accepted, which is why the Italian material needs no comment at all. Look at the verbs in the peacetime paragraph: march in, burn down, offer to go away. The sentence is built so that 'offer' arrives sounding almost polite, and the politeness is the joke. The last sentence is the best piece of work in the lesson. The writer does not tell us the Italians were wrong; he simply quotes the inscription, with its two superlatives and its mangled version of the Englishman's name, and lets the quotation convict everybody. When you want to pass judgement in English without stating it, this is the move: hand the reader a document and stand back.

这篇随笔靠反转起作用，而标题两个词就把它做掉了。noble 用了两层意思：他是骑士，而他一点也不高尚。结构是个陷阱。写芝加哥的三句话把读者放进一个道德标签已经钉死的世界——歹徒、砸店、不得不交钱——然后一个短句把画框一甩：这不是现代才有的罪行。之后的一切都骑在读者已经接受的那些标签上，所以意大利那段根本不需要评论。看太平段落里的动词：开进、烧掉、提出离开。句子是这样搭的：等「提出」出现时，它听起来几乎很客气——而这份客气就是笑点。最后一句是全课最好的手艺。作者没有说意大利人错了，他只是引了那段铭文，连着两个最高级和那个被写歪的英国人名字，让引文去给所有人定罪。你想用英文下判断而不说出判断，正门就是这一手：把一份文件交给读者，然后退开。

🧭 What it argues, and does it still hold?解读

Two of the lesson's details do not hold. The age is one. Hawkwood is usually put down as born around thirteen twenty, which makes him about seventy-four at his death, not eighty; one scholarly discussion does allow that he may have been in his eighties, so this is an open question rather than a plain error, but 'at the age of eighty' is firmer than the evidence. The painting is the other. The famous image of him in Florence Cathedral is Paolo Uccello's fresco, and Uccello painted it in fourteen thirty-six, some forty-two years after the funeral, replacing an earlier painted figure of thirteen ninety-five; the marble monument the city first voted for was never built, because in thirteen ninety-five Richard the Second asked for the body back and Florence agreed — whether the remains ever reached Essex is still argued over. So the lesson's neat sequence, died then honoured then painted, compresses four decades and two different pictures. The larger claim, though, is in better shape than anyone would wish. Paying a criminal organisation for the right to trade is still a live cost of doing business in parts of southern Italy, where it has its own word, the pizzo, and its own resistance movements among shopkeepers. The sharpest comparison is with the same century in China. While Italian cities were renting their armies by contract, the new Ming state founded in thirteen sixty-eight went the opposite way, absorbing soldiers into hereditary garrison households under state command so that no captain could own an army and sell it. Both systems were answers to one question — who may keep armed men, and who pays them — and the Italian answer produced Hawkwood.

课文有两处细节站不住。一是年龄。霍克伍德通常记作约一三二〇年生，这样他去世时约七十四岁，不是八十；有一篇学术讨论确实允许他可能已八十多岁，所以这是个未定问题而不是明错，但「八十岁去世」比证据更硬。二是那幅画。佛罗伦萨主教座堂里他那幅著名的像出自保罗·乌切洛，画于一四三六年，比葬礼晚了约四十二年，取代了一三九五年画的一幅旧像；城里最初表决要立的大理石纪念碑从未建成，因为一三九五年理查二世索回遗体、佛罗伦萨答应了——遗骨究竟有没有运到埃塞克斯，至今还有争论。所以课文那条干净的顺序「死去—受荣—画像」，压掉了四十年和两幅不同的画。但更大的那个判断，状况比谁都希望的要好。为做生意的权利向犯罪组织付钱，在意大利南部一些地方仍是实实在在的经营成本，那里有自己的词叫 pizzo，也有店主自己的抵抗运动。最锋利的对照来自同一个世纪的中国。意大利的城邦在按契约租军队时，一三六八年建立的明朝走了相反的路：把士兵编进世袭的卫所军户、由国家统辖，让任何将领都不可能拥有一支军队再把它卖掉。两套制度回答的是同一个问题——谁可以拥有武装的人，谁给他们发钱——而意大利的那个答案，造出了霍克伍德。

  2. Write 120 words contrasting two ways of handling the same danger — paying to be left alone, or refusing and taking the risk. Use would rather ... than once and in spite of once.
    写 120 词，对比处理同一种威胁的两种做法：交钱求安，还是拒绝并承担风险。用一次 would rather … than，用一次 in spite of。

    意大利城邦租军队，明朝直接拥有自己的兵。你更愿意生活在哪一种安排下？各自的代价是什么？

③ How the hard sentences are built难句结构

Sentences are counted in the lesson text, title not included. Find the sentence in your book, then read our notes on its structure.句子按课文正文数（不含标题）。在教材里找到这一句，再看本站的结构讲解。

Sentence 1第 1 句 (begins开头 “There was a …”)

  • There was a time when ...: there used to be a period in which ... (the when clause describes time).There was a time when…：曾经有一段时期……（when 引导定语从句修饰 time）。
  • in return for: as payment for. The quotation marks round protection mean it was protection in name and extortion in fact.in return for：作为对……的回报。protection 加引号，意思是名为保护、实为勒索。

Sentence 2第 2 句 (begins开头 “If the money …”)

  • would: a habitual action in the past — every time the money was late, this is what followed.would：表示过去的习惯行为（每次不交钱就会……）。
  • by doing: by means of.by doing：通过……的方式。

Sentence 4第 4 句 (begins开头 “As long ago …”)

  • would rather do A than do B: prefer A to B; bare infinitives on both sides.would rather do A than do B：宁愿 A 也不愿 B，两边都用动词原形。
  • have sth done: here it is something suffered, not arranged — a lifetime's work destroyed for you.have sth done：自己的心血被毁（遭遇）。

Sentence 6第 6 句 (begins开头 “He soon made …”)

  • come to be known as: gradually get called (come to do = gradually reach a state).come to be known as：逐渐被称为（come to do 表示渐渐变得）。
  • be known to sb: be familiar to somebody; be known as: be called, be famous under a name.be known to sb：为某人所知；be known as：作为……而出名，被叫做……。

Sentence 7第 7 句 (begins开头 “Whenever the Italian …”)

  • used to do: did regularly in the past and does not now. hire sth to sb: let somebody have it for payment.used to do：过去常常（现在不了）。hire sth to sb：把……租给。
  • Two layers of relative clause: who ... describes princes; he demanded describes price, with that left out.两层定语从句：who…修饰 princes；he demanded 修饰 price（省 that）。

Sentence 8第 8 句 (begins开头 “In times of …”)

  • Both instances of would are past habit. after doing: preposition plus gerund.两个 would 都表过去习惯。after doing：介词 + 动名词。
  • business was bad refers to his business of fighting — a dry joke.business was bad 指他们的「打仗生意」不好——这是幽默说法。

Sentence 11第 11 句 (begins开头 “When he died …”)

  • have sth done: they got a painter to make the picture.have sth done：请人画了一幅画。
  • which describes picture, separated from it by painted.which 修饰 picture，被 painted 隔开了。

④ Grammar in this lesson本课语法

would rather do ... than do ...: prefer one thing to anotherwould rather do … than do …：宁愿……也不……
would rather + bare infinitive + than + bare infinitive; the negative is would rather not do. The short form is I'd rather. Another way to say it: prefer to do ... rather than do ...
would rather + 动词原形 + than + 动词原形；否定 would rather not do。缩写 I'd rather。另一种说法 prefer to do … rather than do …。

In this lesson, sentence 4: people would rather pay heavily than have a lifetime's work wrecked.

本课：第 4 句：人们 would rather pay 大笔钱 than have 毕生心血被毁。

  • I'd rather walk than take a crowded bus.
  • She would rather not talk about it.
used to do and would do: habits in the pastused to do 与 would do：过去的习惯
used to do covers a past habit or a past state, and implies it is over; would do covers only repeated past actions, never states (you cannot write I would live in Paris). be used to doing means be accustomed to, which is something else entirely.
used to do 表过去的习惯或状态，暗示现在不这样了；would do 只表过去反复发生的动作，不表状态（✗ I would live in Paris）。be used to doing ＝习惯于……，完全不同。
  • I used to live in Shanghai.（状态，只能用 used to）
  • Every summer we would swim in the lake.（动作）
  • I'm used to getting up early.（习惯于）
have sth done (get it done, or suffer it) and come to do (gradually)have sth done（请人做）与 come to do（逐渐……）
have + object + past participle: somebody else does the thing for you, or the thing happens to you. come to do: gradually begin to, and it goes with know, be known, realize and like.
have + 宾语 + 过去分词：让别人完成某事，或遭遇某事。come to do：渐渐开始，常与 know / be known / realize / like 等连用。

In this lesson, sentence 4 have their life work destroyed (suffered); sentence 11 had a picture painted (arranged); sentence 6 came to be known as Giovanni Acuto (gradually).

本课：第 4 句 have their life work destroyed（遭遇）；第 11 句 had a picture painted（请人画）；第 6 句 came to be known as Giovanni Acuto（渐渐被叫做）。

  • We had our house painted last month.
  • Over time, I came to like my new school.

⑤ Patterns to reuse可以套用的句型

  • There was a time when … 曾经有段时间……
    There was a time when nobody had a mobile phone.
  • … is not a modern crime / idea. ……并不是现代才有的罪行／想法
    Online shopping is not a modern idea; mail order existed long ago.
  • As long ago as the … century, … 早在……世纪，……
    As long ago as the 15th century, people used printed books.
  • X came to be known as … X 渐渐被人称为……
    The young singer came to be known as "the voice of Toronto".
  • In spite of this, … regarded him as … 尽管如此，……仍把他看作……
    In spite of his mistakes, the fans regarded him as a hero.

Summary writing摘要写作

In no more than 80 words, tell Hawkwood's story: how he made his money, and how the Italians saw him.

用不超过 80 个词讲述霍克伍德的故事，说明他怎样赚钱以及意大利人对他的态度。

Points a good summary covers好摘要要覆盖的要点
  • Taking money for 'protection' is no modern invention; a fourteenth-century Englishman, Hawkwood, had already found that people pay rather than watch a lifetime's work burn.收保护费不是现代才有的罪行；14 世纪的英国人霍克伍德就发现，人们宁愿付钱也不愿被毁掉心血。
  • He brought a band of soldiers to Italy and, while the city-states fought, rented them out to any prince who would meet his price.他带一队士兵到意大利，城邦打仗时把士兵租给出得起高价的王公。
  • In peacetime he marched his men into a city-state, burnt a few farms, and offered to leave for a fee.和平时他就带兵进入城邦，烧几座农庄，再提出交了保护费就离开。
  • Even so the Italians made a hero of him; at his death the city of Florence buried him with full honours.尽管如此，意大利人把他当英雄；他 80 岁去世，佛罗伦萨为他举行了国葬。
Our model summary本站范文

Protection money is an old crime. Six centuries ago, the Englishman John Hawkwood took his soldiers to Italy and lived near Florence. During wars between the city-states, he rented his army to any prince who paid well. In peacetime he would burn a few farms and then agree to leave once he was paid. Yet the Italians admired him, and when he died at eighty, Florence honoured him with a state funeral.（72 词）

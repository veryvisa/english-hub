Lesson 33 · A Day to Remember 难忘的一天

📘 New Concept English · Book 3 · Lesson 33 “A Day to Remember”新概念英语 第三册 Lesson 33《A Day to Remember》

B2🧠 23 new words个新词📝 Full notes有完整讲解

🎯 Before you open the lesson开课前
We have all had days when one small thing starts a run of trouble. The writer first puts you in a kitchen: you are cooking and minding the baby, the phone rings, the baby pulls off the tablecloth and breaks the plates, the dinner burns, and then your husband walks in with three guests. Next comes a real case from Parramatta, near Sydney. Two cars collide, a learner driver behind them panics and stops, the next car brakes so hard that a cake flies out through the windscreen, a lorry driver who sees it in the air stops dead, and his load of empty bottles slides onto the road. The only winners are two stray dogs. This is explanatory narrative that moves from a general claim to a homely example to a real event, and the comedy is built from a chain of causes. The grammar to learn is as if with unreal forms, What happens is that, -ing phrases of result, happen to be, and draw up and pull up.
人人都有过这样的日子：一件小事开头，后面的麻烦一桩接一桩。作者先用家里的场景让你代入——你在做饭、看孩子，电话一响，孩子扯下桌布打碎碗碟，饭也烧焦了，丈夫偏偏又带了三位客人回来。接着讲悉尼郊区帕拉马塔的一次真事：两车相撞，后面一位学车的女司机慌了急刹，再后面一辆车里太太手上的大蛋糕飞出挡风玻璃，旁边的卡车司机看见蛋糕在天上飞也猛刹车，一车空啤酒瓶滑落满地。最后得了便宜的只有两条流浪狗。本课是先概括、再家常例子、再真实事件的说明叙事，靠一连串因果把笑点堆起来。学 as if（虚拟）、What happens is that、现在分词作结果状语、happen to be 和 draw up / pull up。
Questions to keep in mind as you read读的时候带着这几个问题：
  1. Why does the writer give the kitchen example before the accident in Parramatta?作者为什么先写家里的例子，再写帕拉马塔的车祸？
  2. How did each event in Parramatta lead to the next?帕拉马塔那一串事故是怎么一环扣一环发生的？
  3. In what sense were two stray dogs the only ones to gain?为什么说「只有两条流浪狗从中得益」？

📖 Understanding the piece读懂这篇

Written for the course by L. G. Alexander. Parramatta is real — an old town west of central Sydney, now one of its main business districts — but we have found no record of this particular pile-up, and the episode reads like a comic set piece rather than reporting.

课文为编者 L. G. 亚历山大为教材自撰。帕拉马塔确有其地——悉尼市中心以西的一座老城，如今是悉尼主要的商务区之一——但我们没找到这场连环事故的任何记录，这段情节读起来更像一出喜剧小品，而不是新闻报道。

🌍 Where it comes from出处与背景

The idea that things go wrong all at once has a popular name, Murphy's law: anything that can go wrong will. The saying is usually traced to the late 1940s and an American air force research project, though its exact first wording is disputed. Safety scientists take the feeling more seriously and explain it differently. The psychologist James Reason, writing about human error around 1990, described accidents with what is now called the Swiss cheese model: every system has several layers of defence, each with holes, and a disaster happens only when the holes in all the layers happen to line up. One slip is rarely enough. The Parramatta chain fits this well — a minor collision, a nervous beginner, a cake held on a lap, a driver distracted by something strange in the air, an unsecured load — and a single sound defence at any point would have stopped it. The learner is also a real figure of the roads: in New South Wales, learner drivers must show L plates and drive with a fully licensed supervisor beside them.

「坏事总是扎堆来」这种想法有个通俗的名字，叫墨菲定律：凡是可能出错的事，就一定会出错。这个说法一般追溯到二十世纪四十年代末美国空军的一个科研项目，不过最初的确切措辞有争议。安全科学家把这种感觉当真，但解释不同。心理学家詹姆斯·里森约在 1990 年研究人为差错时，用后来所谓的「瑞士奶酪模型」描述事故：每个系统都有好几层防线，每层都有洞，只有当所有层上的洞恰好对齐时，灾难才会发生；单一失误很少足以酿祸。帕拉马塔这条链正合此理——一次小碰撞、一位紧张的新手、一个放在腿上的蛋糕、一个被空中怪东西分了心的司机、一车没捆牢的货——其中任何一环有一道像样的防线，事情就停了。那位新手司机在现实交通里也确有其人：在新南威尔士州，学车的人必须挂 L 牌，并由持正式驾照的人坐在旁边陪驾。

✨ How it is written赏析

The structure is the joke's engine. The writer states a general law, then builds a model case in the second person so that the reader is inside it, and only then offers the real event as proof. By the time we reach Parramatta we already know the shape and are waiting for each new link, which is what makes the chain funny rather than alarming. Each link is given its own short sentence, so that the rhythm is that of dominoes falling. The cake is the inspired detail: it is harmless, absurd and visible from a distance, and it is the one object that can explain why a lorry driver would stop dead. Nobody in the story is hurt, nobody is blamed, and the final sentence gives the only reward to the two creatures with no part in it. The closing exclamation turns the whole thing back into the shrug of everyday speech that the opening began with.

结构是这个笑话的发动机。作者先陈述一条通则，再用第二人称搭一个模型场景，让读者身在其中，然后才拿出真实事件作证。等我们到了帕拉马塔，已经知道事情会是什么形状，就等着下一环出现——正因如此，这条链才可笑而不吓人。每一环都给了一个单独的短句，于是节奏就像多米诺骨牌一块块倒下。蛋糕是神来之笔：它无害、荒唐、远远就看得见，而且正是这件东西能解释卡车司机为什么会猛然停车。故事里没有人受伤，没有人被追责，最后一句把唯一的好处给了两只与此事毫无关系的动物。结尾那句感叹又把全篇收回到开头那种日常口语式的耸肩。

🧭 What it argues, and does it still hold?解读

The lesson treats a run of bad luck as a matter of fate, and it is worth asking how much of it is really chance. Psychologists point out that we remember the days when several things went wrong and forget the far more common days when one thing did, so the feeling that trouble comes in groups is partly a trick of memory. The Swiss cheese view adds a more useful lesson: chains like this are made more likely by the way things are arranged — the cake on a lap, the load not tied down, the hurried phone call while a baby is loose in the kitchen. Seen this way, the kitchen example is not bad luck but a mother asked to do three jobs at once, and the joke at its end, the husband bringing guests without warning, says more about the household of the 1960s than about chance. A modern reader might laugh at the cake and still notice who is left to clear up in both stories.

课文把一连串倒霉事当作命运，值得问一句：其中多少真是偶然？心理学家指出，我们记得几件事一起出错的日子，却忘了多得多的只出一件事的日子，所以「祸不单行」的感觉部分是记忆耍的花招。瑞士奶酪的视角还带来一个更有用的教训：这种链条之所以更容易发生，在于事情是怎么安排的——放在腿上的蛋糕、没捆好的货、孩子在厨房乱跑时匆忙接起的电话。这样看，厨房那个例子不是运气不好，而是一位母亲被要求同时干三件事；结尾那个笑点——丈夫不打招呼带客人回家——说的更多是二十世纪六十年代的家庭，而不是偶然。今天的读者可以笑那个蛋糕，同时也注意到：两个故事里，留下来收拾残局的都是谁。

💬 Discuss and write讨论与写作
  1. Think of a bad day you have had. Which link in the chain could you have broken, and how?
    回想你经历过的一个倒霉日子：哪一环本来可以被你掐断？怎么掐？
  2. Write a chain of five events, each in one short sentence, where a small cause leads to a silly result.
    写一条由五件事组成的链，每件一个短句，从一个小起因引出一个滑稽的结果。
  3. Is 'bad luck' a useful explanation, or does it stop us from looking for causes?
    「运气不好」是有用的解释，还是会让我们不再去找原因？

③ How the hard sentences are built难句结构

④ Grammar in this lesson本课语法

as if / as though with unreal formsas if / as though + 虚拟：好像
When what follows is untrue or unlikely, as if takes a past form, often were for be, for the present, and had done for the past. If it may really be so, ordinary tenses are fine, as in He looks as if he is tired. The set phrase As if that were not enough means 'and, worse still'.
说的事与事实相反或不大可能时，as if 后用过去式（be 常用 were）表示与现在相反，用 had done 表示与过去相反。如果只是可能如此，也可以用真实时态（He looks as if he is tired）。固定说法 As if that were not enough = 好像这还不够似的。

Sentence 4 uses as if with a past form for the unreal picture of one event setting off the rest, and sentence 11 uses the set phrase to bring on something worse.

本课：第 4 句用 as if 加过去式说「好像一件小事引发了连锁反应」；第 11 句用 As if that were not enough 引出更糟的事。

  • He talks as if he knew everything.
  • She looked at me as if I had said something terrible.
  • It rained all day; as if that were not enough, the roof began to leak.
-ing phrases that show a result现在分词作结果状语
An -ing phrase at the end of a sentence can show what followed from the main action, meaning 'and so'. Its subject is the subject of the main clause, so the two must match. Common ones are killing, leaving, causing and making.
句末的现在分词短语可以表示前一个动作自然带来的结果（= and so …）。它的逻辑主语就是主句的主语，所以要保证主语一致。常见：…, killing / leaving / causing / making …

In sentences 7 and 10 the baby pulls the cloth away, and the breaking and the cut follow from it.

本课：第 7–8 句：孩子扯下桌布，结果打碎了一半碗碟，还划伤了自己。

  • The storm hit the coast, destroying hundreds of homes.
  • He dropped the glass, cutting his hand on the pieces.
  • The bus broke down, leaving us stuck in the rain.
What happens is that ...; happen to do
What plus a verb plus is that ... puts the weight on the that clause. happen to do or be means 'by chance'; you can also say It happened that ....
What + 动词 + is that … 是把重点放到 that 从句上的强调句式。happen to do / be：碰巧做/是；也可以换成 It happened that …。

Sentence 3 uses the what clause to stress that everything goes wrong at once, and sentence 14 tells us the driver was by chance a learner.

本课：第 3 句用 What … happens is that 强调「同一刻出错」；第 14 句用 happened to be 说那位女司机碰巧是新手。

  • What I need is a long holiday.
  • What happened was that the alarm didn't go off.
  • I happened to be in town, so I called in.

⑤ Patterns to reuse可以套用的句型

  • We have all known days when … 我们都有过……的日子
    We have all known mornings when nothing seems to go right.
  • It is as if … set up a chain of … 好像……引发了一连串……
    It was as if one rumour set up a chain of panic in the town.
  • As if that were not enough, … 好像这还不够似的，……
    My bike was stolen; as if that were not enough, it started to rain.
  • X happened to be … X 碰巧是……
    The man next to me happened to be a doctor.
  • It took X nearly … to get … moving again. X 花了将近……才让……重新动起来
    It took the engineers nearly a day to get the trains moving again.

Our model summary本站范文

During the evening rush hour in Parramatta, two cars collided and their drivers began to quarrel. A learner driver behind them panicked and stopped, so the man behind her had to brake sharply. His wife's cake shot through the windscreen. Seeing it, a lorry driver stopped suddenly, and hundreds of empty bottles fell onto the road, causing another argument. The police needed almost an hour to clear the traffic. Only two stray dogs gained: they ate the cake.（78 词）

Lesson 29 · Funny or Not 是否可笑

📘 New Concept English · Book 3 · Lesson 29 “Funny or Not”新概念英语 第三册 Lesson 29《Funny or Not》

B2🧠 23 new words个新词📝 Full notes有完整讲解

🎯 Before you open the lesson开课前
Whether a joke strikes you as funny depends very largely on where you were raised: a sense of humour is tied up, in some way nobody can quite explain, with national character, so that a Frenchman may not laugh at a Russian joke and a Russian may see nothing at all in one that reduces an Englishman to tears. Some comic situations, though, work everywhere — the early films of Charlie Chaplin, for instance. Lately a new kind of joke has come in from the United States, called sick humour, which makes fun of tragedies such as violent deaths and serious accidents, and many people find it repellent. The writer offers one example and leaves the verdict to the reader. A man breaks his right leg a few weeks before Christmas and spends his time in hospital badgering the doctor about when he can go home; Christmas comes, the leg is still in plaster, and he lies there thoroughly miserable. The next day the doctor comforts him with the news that he has a good chance of getting out before the new year. Sure enough, on the last evening of the year he limps along to a party, drinks a few more than he should to make up for the hospital, and keeps telling everyone how much he hates the place. He is still muttering about hospitals as the party ends, when he slips on a patch of ice and breaks the other leg. The lesson is an argument followed by an illustration. Learn Whether ... or not as a subject, be bound up with, find it hard to do, No matter where, keep on doing, dread doing, and the than-clause used in comparisons.
一个笑话好不好笑，很大程度上取决于你在哪里长大：幽默感和民族性格神秘地连在一起，法国人未必笑得出俄国笑话，让英国人笑出眼泪的笑话俄国人可能毫无感觉。不过有些滑稽情景是全世界通用的，比如卓别林的早期电影。近来又从美国流行起一种「病态幽默」，拿暴死、重大事故这类悲剧开玩笑，很多人觉得反感。作者举了个例子让读者自己判断：一个男人圣诞节前几周摔断右腿住院，天天缠着医生问什么时候能回家；圣诞节还打着石膏，他在床上过得很惨。第二天医生安慰说新年前出院很有希望。他果然在除夕一瘸一拐去了派对，为弥补住院之苦多喝了几杯，一直跟人说他多讨厌医院；派对快结束时他还在念叨医院，结果踩到一块冰滑倒，摔断了左腿。本课是先议论后举例的说明文。学 Whether … or not 作主语、be bound up with、find it hard to do、No matter where、keep on doing、dread doing，以及比较从句里的 than（多喝了几杯那一句）。
Questions to keep in mind as you read读的时候带着这几个问题：
  1. What does the writer think decides whether a joke makes us laugh?作者认为什么决定了我们觉得一个笑话好不好笑？
  2. What is sick humour, and how do people react to it?什么叫「病态幽默」？人们怎么看它？
  3. Why did the man end up in hospital again, and why does the story count as sick humour?例子中的男人最后为什么又进了医院？这个故事为什么算「病态幽默」？

🌍 Where it comes from出处与背景

Sick humour was a label pinned on a group of performers, and the pin can be dated: an American news weekly ran a piece on the new comedians in July nineteen fifty-nine and coined the word sicknik for them, built on the word beatnik, which had itself been built on the name of the Soviet satellite launched two years earlier. The magazine swept seven very different performers into one basket, and at least one of them objected publicly to being filed under that heading; he then put the word on the cover of his next record, which is how it entered general use. So the fashion the lesson calls recent was already a decade or so old by the time this course was written, and what the lesson describes as new was in fact already being absorbed into the mainstream. Note also the example the lesson picks for humour that travels. Chaplin's early films are silent. Nothing in them has to be translated, so there is nothing for a national sense of humour to get in the way of — which is a clue the lesson does not follow up.

「病态幽默」是贴在一群表演者身上的标签，而这枚别针可以定年：1959 年 7 月，一家美国新闻周刊写了一篇关于新一代喜剧演员的报道，给他们造了 sicknik 这个词——它仿自 beatnik，而 beatnik 又仿自两年前升空的那颗苏联卫星。杂志把七位风格迥异的表演者扫进同一个筐里，其中至少一位公开反对被归进这个名目；他随后把这个词印上了自己下一张唱片的封面，于是它进入了通用语。所以这一课说「近来」流行的风尚，在教材写作时已经有十来年历史；而它当作新鲜事物描述的东西，其实已经在被主流吸收。还要注意这一课为「能跨国界的幽默」挑的例子：卓别林的早期电影是无声的。里面没有任何东西需要翻译，于是也没有东西能让民族幽默感插手——这是一条线索，而这一课没有往下追。

✨ How it is written赏析

The lesson is doing two difficult things at once, and the second is the interesting one. First it argues; then it tells a joke without endorsing it. Watch the sentence that hands over responsibility: the following example will let you judge for yourself. That move protects the writer, keeps the classroom calm, and also makes the reader complicit, because anyone who laughs has answered the question. The joke itself is built with real care. Everything needed for the ending is planted early and in the right order: Christmas, so that there is a deadline; the right leg, so that the other one is available; the plaster, so that we can see him; the ice, which arrives in the last line and has not been mentioned before, yet feels inevitable because it is winter. The punch is carried by a single word, left, and the symmetry of right and left is the whole joke. Note too how flat the last sentence is. No exclamation, no comment, no moral. English comic writing nearly always ends this way, with the worst news delivered in the most ordinary voice.

这一课同时在做两件难事，而第二件才有意思。它先议论，然后讲一个自己并不表态支持的笑话。看那句交出责任的话：下面这个例子能让你自己判断。这一手既保护了作者、也让课堂不至于失控，同时把读者变成了共犯——因为任何笑出来的人都已经作答了。笑话本身造得很用心：结尾需要的一切都提前埋好，而且顺序正确——圣诞节，于是有了一个期限；右腿，于是另一条腿还空着；石膏，于是我们看得见他；而冰出现在最后一行、此前从未提及，却毫不突然，因为那是冬天。全部力道由一个词担着：左。右与左的对称，就是整个笑话。还要注意最后一句有多平：没有感叹，没有评论，没有教训。英文的喜剧写作几乎总是这样收尾——最坏的消息，用最寻常的语气说出来。

🧭 What it argues, and does it still hold?解读

The opening claim is the part worth testing, and it has been tested. Early this century a British psychologist ran an experiment on the internet for about a year, collecting over forty thousand jokes and close to two million ratings from many countries. Two findings matter here. One supports the lesson: there were real differences between countries in what people rated highly, and overall ratings differed by nationality, with German participants marking the whole set funniest. The other complicates it: the joke that came out on top was a short exchange between a detective and his assistant about a missing tent, a piece of nonsense with nothing national about it at all. So the tastes differ, but they are not sealed off from one another, and the material that travels is situational rather than verbal — exactly the point hidden in the lesson's choice of silent films. It is worth adding that the sample was self-selected, which is a real weakness: people who answer a joke survey online are not a cross-section of any nation. As for sick humour, the lesson's tone of mild disapproval now sounds quaint. Jokes about death, illness and disaster are ordinary stage material, and the live argument has moved to a different question: not whether a tragedy may be joked about, but who is allowed to make the joke and how close they stand to the harm. For Chinese readers the cross-border problem is familiar from the other side. A pun in Chinese almost never survives translation, and neither do the comic forms built on rhythm and rapid talk; what crosses are situations — the man who hates hospitals, standing on the ice. If you want to test the lesson's thesis yourself, try telling a joke you love in your second language, and notice which parts you have to explain.

开篇那个判断是最值得检验的部分，而它已经被检验过。本世纪初，一位英国心理学家在网上做了约一年的实验，收集到四万多个笑话、近两百万次评分，来自许多国家。其中两项发现与这一课有关。一项支持它：各国在「什么好笑」上确有差别，整体评分也因国别而异，德国参与者给全部笑话打的分最高。另一项让它复杂起来：拔得头名的那个笑话，是一位侦探和他的助手关于一顶不见了的帐篷的简短对话——一段毫无民族色彩的胡话。所以口味确实不同，但彼此并不封闭，而能跨境的素材是情景型的，不是语言型的——这正是这一课挑了无声电影却没说出的那一点。还得补一句：那个样本是自选的，这是实打实的弱点：会在网上回答笑话问卷的人，不是任何民族的横截面。至于「病态幽默」，这一课那种温和的不赞成，今天听起来有点古雅。拿死亡、疾病、灾难开玩笑是舞台上的常规素材，而活的争论已经换了题目：不是悲剧能不能被开玩笑，而是谁有资格开这个玩笑、他离那场伤害有多近。对中国读者来说，跨境这个难题是从另一侧熟悉的：中文的谐音几乎无法翻译，靠节奏和快嘴搭起来的喜剧形式也一样；能过境的是情景——那个讨厌医院的人，站在冰上。想自己检验这一课的论点，不妨用第二语言讲一个你很喜欢的笑话，然后留意哪些部分你必须解释。

💬 Discuss and write讨论与写作
  1. Tell a joke you find funny to someone raised elsewhere, or imagine doing so. Which parts need explaining, and is it still funny afterwards?
    把一个你觉得好笑的笑话讲给在别处长大的人听，或者想象一下。哪些部分需要解释？解释完之后还好笑吗？
  2. Can a serious misfortune be a fit subject for a joke? Set out the strongest case against your own view before you state it.
    严重的不幸能不能成为笑话的题材？先把反对你自己看法的最强理由摆出来，再说你的看法。
  3. Write a hundred and twenty words that plant every detail of a joke in advance and then deliver the ending in one flat, ordinary sentence.
    写 120 词：把一个笑话需要的所有细节提前埋好，然后用一句平淡寻常的话把结尾交出来。

④ Grammar in this lesson本课语法

Whether ... or not as a subject; depend on + a question wordWhether … or not 作主语；depend on + 疑问词从句
A whether-clause can be the subject, the object or the complement of a sentence, and as a subject it is whether rather than if. depend on can be followed by a clause beginning with what, where or how.
whether 引导的从句可以作主语、宾语、表语；作主语时一般不用 if。depend on 后可以接 what / where / how 等疑问词引导的从句。
  • Whether he comes or not doesn't matter.
  • Our plans depend on what the weather is like.
keep (on) doing / dread doing / be able to do
keep (on) doing means to do something repeatedly, and keep on suggests that it has become tiresome. dread doing takes the -ing form. be able to can be used with will, would and have been, which fills the gap left by can, since can has no future form.
keep (on) doing：反复、不停地做（keep on 更强调烦人）；dread doing：害怕做（后接动名词）；be able to 可以和 will / would / have been 等连用，弥补 can 没有将来时的缺口。
  • She kept on asking me the same question.
  • I dread having to speak in public.
  • You will be able to walk again soon.
The than-clause in comparisons, where than does the work of a relative pronoun比较结构里的 than（他喝得比对他有好处的量多）
than can be followed directly by a clause with no subject of its own, in which case than is doing the work of a relative pronoun: more than is necessary, more than was expected, more than suited him.
than 后面可以直接接一个缺主语的从句，此时 than 起关系代词作用：more than is necessary（比必要的多）、more than was expected（比预期的多）。

In this lesson, sentence twenty-seven says that the quantity he drank was greater than suited him, and this is the structure it uses.

本课：第 27 句他喝得超过了对他有好处的量，用的就是这个结构。

  • Don't spend more money than is necessary.

Points a good summary covers好摘要要覆盖的要点

Our model summary本站范文

A man broke his right leg shortly before Christmas and spent weeks in hospital, begging his doctor every day to let him go home. He hated the idea of a hospital Christmas, but he healed slowly and spent the day in bed, miserable. The doctor then promised he might be out for New Year. On New Year's Eve he limped to a party, drank too much and complained about hospitals, then slipped on ice and broke his other leg.（79 词）

📘 New Concept English · Book 3 · Lesson 7 “Mutilated Ladies”新概念英语 第三册 Lesson 7《Mutilated Ladies》

B2🧠 19 new words个新词📝 Full notes有完整讲解

🎯 Before you open the lesson开课前
Put a note through the wash with your trousers, let the dog chew it, even cook it in the microwave: in Britain there is still hope. The Bank of England keeps a department for damaged money, nicknamed Mutilated Ladies. This lesson tells how Jane Butlin heated the dinner and, with it, her fiance's wallet and the three thousand pounds inside, and how every penny came back in the end. In style it is a light news feature: two questions pull the reader in, then one case, then a line from a spokeswoman to close. The language to learn is needn't, relative clauses with which, who and whose, so long as, and without + -ing.
把钞票连裤子一起扔进洗衣机、被狗嚼烂、甚至被微波炉「烤熟」——在英国都还有救：英格兰银行有个专门处理残损钞票的部门，外号 Mutilated Ladies（「残钞女士们」）。本课讲简·巴特林阴差阳错把未婚夫装着 3000 英镑的钱包和晚饭一起烤成了灰，最后钱却一分不少拿了回来。语言上是轻松的新闻特写：开头用两个问句拉读者入戏，再讲一个案例，最后引发言人的话收尾。学 needn't、which/whose 定语从句、so long as 和 without doing。

  2. Why did John put his wallet in the microwave oven, and what happened next?约翰为什么把钱包放进微波炉？后来发生了什么？
  3. On what condition does the Bank hand the money back?银行凭什么条件把钱退给人们？

📖 Understanding the piece读懂这篇

Unsigned and written for the course. The department and its nickname are real, and almost every comic detail in the lesson appears first in the Bank of England's own account of the work, published in its Quarterly Bulletin for August 1989; we have no statement from the author that he used it, so the link is not confirmed.

本课未署名，为教材自撰。这个部门和它的外号都是真的，而且课文里几乎每个笑点都先出现在英格兰银行自己写的业务介绍里（《季度公报》1989 年 8 月号）；我们没有作者本人承认取材于此的说法，关联未核。

🌍 Where it comes from出处与背景

The Bank's own article explains the whole operation. Damaged notes have always been paid in full or not at all, unlike countries that pay in proportion to the surviving area, and the promise on the note has no time limit. The work moved to the Bank's Newcastle branch in 1975 as part of a decentralisation drive. Applications peaked in the early 1970s at about 180,000 a year, needing some fifty staff; by the late 1980s the section was six people, who, in the Bank's words, refer to themselves as the Bank's 'Mutilated Ladies', and they still dealt with over 27,000 claims a year, paying out about £800,000 annually. Among the causes listed are fire, floods, burial in the garden, notes rotting under floorboards, and the appetites of household pets, camels and ferrets included. Two details will look familiar: hiding money in the oven, and these days in the microwave, and washing machines that break down with note paper jammed in the pump.

银行自己那篇文章把整套做法讲清楚了。残损钞票要么全额兑付、要么分文不付，不像有些国家按残片面积比例赔；钞票上的承诺没有时效。1975 年这项业务作为分散化的一部分迁到纽卡斯尔分行。申请量在 1970 年代初见顶，约每年十八万件，需要五十来个人处理；到 1980 年代末，这个组只剩六个人——按银行的原话，她们自称「银行的残钞女士们」——仍然每年处理两万七千多件申请，年赔付约八十万英镑。文中列出的损坏原因有火灾、水淹、埋在花园里、塞在地板下烂掉，以及家养宠物的胃口，骆驼和雪貂都算在内。其中两个细节会让人觉得眼熟：把钱藏在烤箱里、「如今还有微波炉」，以及因为钞票纸卡在泵里而坏掉的洗衣机。

✨ How it is written赏析

The lesson is a model of how to open a light news feature. It does not begin with the institution; it begins with you, in two questions about your own trousers, and only lets the Bank in once you are already implicated. The nickname is held back to the end of that paragraph so that it lands as a surprise. Then one case is told in six sentences with the laugh placed exactly where the two halves of the story collide — a wallet put somewhere for safety, and dinner cooked in the same place. Numbers arrive only at the very end, in the mouth of a spokeswoman, which is the right place for them: they stop the piece being an anecdote without ever asking the reader to do arithmetic. And the humour never has a victim. The people who cook their money are the readers, and the writer counts himself among them.

这一课是「轻松特写怎么开头」的范本。它不从机构写起，而从你写起：两句关于你自己裤子的问句，等你已经被牵连进去，才让银行出场。外号被压到那一段的末尾，落下来才有意外感。接着用六句话讲一个案例，笑点正放在故事两半相撞的地方——钱包为了安全被放进某处，而晚饭就在同一处加热。数字只在最后出现，而且出自发言人之口，这是它们该在的位置：有了它就不只是个趣闻，又不必让读者做算术。而且这幽默从头到尾没有受害者：把钱煮了的人就是读者，作者把自己也算在里面。

🧭 What it argues, and does it still hold?解读

Behind the joke sits a quietly radical rule. The Bank pays the full face value of what you destroyed, provided enough survives to identify, and it does not pay a share based on how much paper is left. Any system that paid by area would invite people to tear notes in two and claim twice, so the identification rule is doing the real work; the cheerfulness of the lesson rests on a piece of fraud control. Two cautions for readers. The figures quoted at the end are higher than the ones the Bank published in 1989, so they belong to a later year that we did not trace; treat them as the lesson's number, not as a current one. And none of this travels. Rules for damaged money differ by country, so the right move is to look up what your own central bank promises before you trust a drawer full of ash.

笑话背后是一条相当彻底的规则。只要残片足以辨认，银行照面额全赔，而不按剩下多少纸按比例赔。任何按面积赔的制度都会引诱人把钞票撕成两半、索赔两次，所以真正吃重的是「能否辨认」这一条；本课的轻快，底下垫着一套防骗设计。给读者两个提醒。结尾引用的数字高于银行 1989 年公布的数，属于我们没有追查到的更晚某一年；把它当课文里的数，不要当今天的数。还有，这套规则不通行天下：各国对残损货币的处理不同，正门是先去查自己国家的中央银行怎么承诺，再去相信抽屉里那一堆灰。

③ How the hard sentences are built难句结构

Sentences are counted in the lesson text, title not included. Find the sentence in your book, then read our notes on its structure.句子按课文正文数（不含标题）。在教材里找到这一句，再看本站的结构讲解。

Sentence 1第 1 句 (begins开头 “Have you ever …”)

  • The present perfect asks about experience: starting with Have you ever done ...? puts the reader straight into the scene.现在完成时问经历：Have you ever done…? 开头就把读者拉进场景。
  • Two past participles share the one opening Have you ever.put … and then remembered：两个过去分词共用 Have you ever。
  • The conjunction that is left out after remembered, and an object clause follows.remembered 后省略 that，引出宾语从句。

Sentence 2第 2 句 (begins开头 “When you rescued …”)

  • whiter than white: whiter than whiteness itself. It borrows the voice of washing-powder advertising, and the joke is that a note washed that clean has lost its printing.whiter than white：比白还白（套洗衣粉广告语，这里是幽默的反讽：钞票被洗得字都没了）。
  • find (that) + clause: come to see that something is so.find (that) + 从句：发现……。

Sentence 3第 3 句 (begins开头 “People who live …”)

  • needn't + bare infinitive: the negative of the modal need, meaning there is no necessity.needn't + 动词原形：情态动词 need 的否定，「不必」。
  • The do inside the brackets stands in for the whole verb phrase, so it need not be said twice.括号里 do 代替 make mistakes like this，避免重复。

Sentence 5第 5 句 (begins开头 “which deals with …”)

  • Two relative clauses sit inside each other: the first describes the team, the second describes the people.两层定语从句：which 修饰 team，who 修饰 people。
  • feed something to somebody or something: the joke is that the machine, or the dog, has eaten the money.feed sth to sb/sth：把……喂给（幽默说法：钱被机器「吃」了、被狗吃了）。

Sentence 7第 7 句 (begins开头 “A recent case …”)

  • concern as a verb here means to be about, to involve, not to worry.concern v.：涉及，和……有关（不是「担心」）。
  • whose opens a relative clause and shows belonging: the man she is engaged to.whose 引导定语从句，表所属：她的未婚夫。

Sentence 10第 10 句 (begins开头 “When they got …”)

  • without + -ing: the preposition takes a gerund, so the meaning is that she never noticed.without doing：介词 without + 动名词，「没有意识到」。
  • as well at the end of the sentence means the same as too.as well 放句末 = too。

Sentence 15第 15 句 (begins开头 “'So long as …”)

  • so long as, like as long as, states the one condition that matters, and its clause uses the present simple for future meaning.so long as = as long as：只要（条件），从句用一般现在时表将来。
  • something to identify: an infinitive after the noun, meaning something that can be identified.something to identify：不定式作后置定语（可供辨认的东西）。

④ Grammar in this lesson本课语法

needn't and don't need to: no necessityneedn't 与 don't need to：不必
needn't is a modal: it takes a bare infinitive and never changes for person. don't need to and doesn't need to use need as an ordinary verb, which is commoner in speech and in American English. Both say that something is not necessary.
needn't 是情态动词，后接动词原形，无人称变化；don't/doesn't need to 是实义动词用法，更常见于口语和美式英语。两者都表示「没有必要」。

In this lesson sentence 3 opens by telling people in Britain that there is no need to give up hope when this happens.

本课：第 3 句开头讲英国人遇到这种事 needn't despair。

Keep them apart: mustn't forbids, needn't says there is no need; needn't have done means it was not necessary, but you did it anyway.

区别：mustn't = 禁止；needn't = 不必。needn't have done = 本来不必做（但做了）。

  • You needn't bring an umbrella; it's sunny today.
  • You mustn't park here. It's a fire lane.
  • We needn't have hurried — the film started late.
Relative clauses with whose / which / whowhose / which / who 定语从句
who stands for people, as subject or object; which stands for things; whose shows belonging, for people and for things alike, and is always followed straight away by a noun.
who 指人（作主语/宾语），which 指物，whose 表所属（人或物都可），后面紧跟名词。

This lesson has four of them in a row: one for the team, one for the people, one for the woman and her fiance, and one for the bank manager.

本课：a team … which deals with claims（第 5 句）；people who fed their money…；Jane Butlin whose fiancé…；his bank manager who sent…

  • I have a friend whose sister works at the bank.
  • The house whose roof was damaged is being repaired.
  • This is the machine which ate my coins.
so long as / as long as: provided thatso long as / as long as：只要
It opens a clause of condition, close in sense to if, but stressing that this is the one thing required; the clause uses the present simple even when the meaning is future.
引导条件状语从句，意思接近 if，但更强调「唯一的条件」；从句用一般现在时表示将来。

In sentence 15 the spokeswoman uses it to state the single condition for a refund.

本课：第 15 句银行发言人用 So long as 开头说退款条件。

Note that as long as also has its literal sense of equal length.

注意：as long as 还有字面意思「和……一样长」：The bridge is as long as a football field.

  • So long as you return the book by Friday, you can borrow it.
  • You can stay here as long as you keep the room tidy.

⑤ Patterns to reuse可以套用的句型

  • Have you ever … and then remembered …? 你有没有……，然后才想起……？
    Have you ever locked the door and then remembered your keys were inside?
  • X needn't despair when … 遇到……时，X 不必绝望
    Students needn't despair when they fail one test.
  • A recent case concerns … 最近的一个案例涉及……
    A recent case concerns a tourist who lost his passport in a taxi.
  • Imagine their dismay when … 想象一下他们发现……时有多沮丧
    Imagine our dismay when we found the hotel was fully booked.
  • So long as …, we will … 只要……，我们就会……
    So long as the weather is fine, we will have the picnic outside.

Lesson 27 · Nothing to Sell and Nothing to Buy 无可卖之物，也无可买之物

📘 New Concept English · Book 3 · Lesson 27 “Nothing to Sell and Nothing to Buy”新概念英语 第三册 Lesson 27《Nothing to Sell and Nothing to Buy》

B2🧠 26 new words个新词📝 Full notes有完整讲解

🎯 Before you open the lesson开课前
Everybody, it has been said, makes a living by selling something: teachers sell knowledge, philosophers sell wisdom, priests sell comfort of the spirit. The worth of goods can be put into money, but the true worth of a service is very hard to reckon: when our life is at stake we would hand over all we own, and yet we resent the surgeon's bill for exactly that service. Society is arranged so that a skill, like a thing in a shop, has to be paid for. The one exception seems to be the tramp. A beggar all but sells his own person in order to be pitied, whereas the real tramp is no beggar: he has nothing to sell and asks nothing of anyone, and in going after his independence he gives up none of his dignity. He knows what he has chosen and what it costs, he cannot say where tomorrow's food will come from, and he is free of the thousand worries that press on everybody else; he owns little, so he moves easily, and sleeping out of doors he lives nearer to the natural world than the rest of us. We speak of such men with contempt — and yet how many of us have never once envied that life without cares? The piece is an argument that climbs step by step: a general rule, then an exception, then a question thrown back at the reader. Learn It has been said that, in the light of, ellipsis, in terms of, be such that, grudge doing and not ... but ...
有人说，人人都靠卖点什么过活：教师卖知识，哲学家卖智慧，牧师卖精神安慰。物质商品的价值可以用钱衡量，服务的真实价值却很难估：性命攸关时我们愿意倾其所有，可外科医生收高价时我们又舍不得。社会就是这样，技能和商品一样要付钱。似乎只有流浪汉例外：乞丐几乎是在出卖自己的人格去博取同情，真正的流浪汉却不是乞丐——他们无物可卖，也不求于人；追求独立却不牺牲尊严。他们清楚自己选择的后果，下一顿饭不知在哪里，却摆脱了困扰别人的千般焦虑；东西少，走到哪都方便；露宿野外，比我们更接近自然。我们常鄙视流浪汉，可又有几人敢说自己从没羡慕过他们无忧无虑的自由？本课是层层推进的议论文：先立一般规律，再找例外，最后反问读者。学 It has been said that、in the light of、省略句、in terms of、such that、grudge doing 和 not … but …。

🌍 Where it comes from出处与背景

The essay belongs to a particular moment in English writing. For about a century, from the eighteen eighties to the nineteen sixties, the man on the road was a literary figure as much as a social fact: Stevenson wrote about beggars with admiration, and later writers kept the type alive. Behind the figure stood a law. An act of eighteen twenty-four made it an offence in England and Wales to sleep out or to beg, and that act was still in force when this lesson was written, which gives the sentence about choosing one's own life an edge the modern reader can miss: the choice the writer admires was, in strict law, a crime. The law outlived the lesson by a long way. It was finally repealed on the twenty-ninth of June, twenty twenty-six, so that sleeping rough is no longer a criminal matter in England and Wales, though the government kept two of the old offences alive in new legislation to preserve police powers.

这篇随笔属于英语写作里一个特定的时刻。从 1880 年代到 1960 年代前后约一个世纪，路上的流浪汉既是社会事实，也是文学形象：史蒂文森带着赞赏写乞丐，后来的作家把这个类型一直养着。形象背后站着一部法律。1824 年的一部法案规定，在英格兰与威尔士露宿或行乞属违法；这一课写作时该法仍然有效——这让「自愿选择这种生活」那句话多出一层今天读者容易错过的锋利：作者所赞赏的那种选择，严格依法是犯罪。这部法律比这一课活得久得多，直到 2026 年 6 月 29 日才被废止，露宿在英格兰与威尔士不再是刑事问题；不过政府把其中两项旧罪写进了新法，以免警权出现空档。

✨ How it is written赏析

This is a piece of argument, and it is built like one. The first move is to state a rule so wide that it sounds like a truism, and then to push it until it becomes interesting: if everyone sells something, what exactly does a priest sell? The second move is the crack in the rule — goods can be priced, services cannot — and it is dramatised with a single sharp example, the surgeon's fee, which works because it catches the reader in a contradiction rather than accusing anybody. The third move is the exception, introduced with the word seem, which keeps the writer honest. Then comes the real engine of the essay: a definition by contrast. Not a beggar; nothing to sell; nothing required of others. English argument loves this shape, saying what a thing is not until what it is stands out in relief. Finally the ending turns on the reader. The question is rhetorical, and it does something that a statement could not: it makes us supply the admission ourselves, so that we cannot disagree without being caught out. Notice also the sentence rhythms, with their short balanced halves — he cannot say where he will eat tomorrow, but he is free of a thousand anxieties — which is how the prose makes a hard life sound enviable.

这是一篇议论，而且搭得像一篇议论。第一步，先立一条宽到近乎废话的规律，然后推它，推到有意思为止：如果人人都在卖，那牧师究竟卖什么？第二步，指出规律上的裂缝——商品可以定价，服务不能——并用一个锋利的例子把它演出来：外科医生的账单。这个例子有效，因为它把读者捉进一个自我矛盾，而不是指责任何人。第三步是例外，用 seem 引出，这让作者保持诚实。接着是全篇真正的发动机：以对比来定义。不是乞丐；无物可卖；不求于人。英语议论最爱这个形状——先说它不是什么，直到它是什么自己浮凸出来。最后，结尾转向读者。那个问句是反问，它能做陈述句做不到的事：让我们自己把那句承认说出来，于是我们不能反对而不露馅。还要注意句子的节奏，那些短而对称的两半——他可能不知道下一顿在哪里，但他摆脱了千般焦虑——正是这种文气把一种艰难的生活写得令人羡慕。

🧭 What it argues, and does it still hold?解读

Two things should be said against this essay, and one for it. Against it, first, there is an inconsistency inside the text. The tramp is defined as a man who requires nothing from others, and then, a few lines later, hunting, begging and stealing are allowed him if he must stay alive. Whatever that is, it is not self-sufficiency, and the definition is doing some quiet work to protect a romantic figure. Second, the whole argument rests on choice, and the choice is now much harder to believe in. Rough sleeping is counted today, not imagined: the official count for England found four thousand seven hundred and ninety-three people sleeping out on a single night in the autumn of twenty twenty-five, the highest figure since the count began and the fourth annual rise in a row. Read the modern accounts and the common causes are rent, the loss of a job, a hospital discharge, mental illness, addiction and leaving care — not a taste for the open road. An essay that envies such a life from a warm room now reads as a period piece, however good the prose. What survives, and it is worth keeping, is the question the essay asks about price. We really are bad at valuing what people do for us, and we really do resent paying for the services we would have given everything to receive. For Chinese readers there is a long and honourable version of the writer's ideal, but it is a different thing: the hermit of the classical tradition, the official who gives up office to grow chrysanthemums, is turning down status and salary, not shelter. The admiration there is for someone who refuses to sell his judgement, which is a sharper idea than freedom from care, and one the essay could have used.

对这篇随笔该说两条不利的、一条有利的。不利的第一条，文内自相矛盾：流浪汉被定义为「不求于人」的人，而几行之后，他可以打猎、行乞或偷窃以活下去。那不管是什么，都不是自给自足；这个定义在悄悄替一个浪漫形象打掩护。第二条，整篇论证立在「选择」上，而这个选择今天难以相信得多。露宿今天是被清点的，不是被想象的：英格兰官方统计 2025 年秋季单夜露宿 4,793 人，为该统计开始以来最高，并且是连续第四年上升。读今天的材料，常见成因是房租、失业、出院、精神疾病、成瘾、离开福利照护——不是喜欢在路上。坐在暖房里羡慕这种生活的文章，如今读来是一件时代旧物，文笔再好也一样。存活下来的，是它关于价格的那个提问，而这个提问值得留着：我们确实不擅长给别人为我们做的事定价，也确实舍不得为那些我们本愿倾家荡产去换的服务付钱。对中国读者来说，作者的理想另有一个悠久而体面的版本，但那是另一回事：古典传统里的隐士、辞官去种菊的人，放弃的是地位与俸禄，不是屋顶。那里被敬佩的是一个不肯出卖判断的人——这个念头比「无忧无虑」锋利得多，也正是这篇随笔可以用上的。

💬 Discuss and write讨论与写作
  1. The essay says we resent paying for services we would once have given everything for. Think of a bill that felt unfair at the time. What were you actually paying for, and was the price wrong?
    文章说，我们舍不得为那些自己本来愿意倾其所有去换的服务付钱。想一笔当时觉得不公道的账单：你实际上付的是什么？那个价格真的错了吗？
  2. Is it still possible to admire a way of life you would not choose and would not want for your own children? Make the case either way, with an example.
    一种你自己不会选、也不希望自己孩子去过的生活，今天还能不能被赞赏？任选一边，带一个例子来论证。
  3. Write a hundred and twenty words defining something by saying what it is not, as this lesson defines the tramp. End with a question to the reader.
    写 120 词，用「它不是什么」来定义一样东西，像这一课定义流浪汉那样。结尾给读者一个问句。

④ Grammar in this lesson本课语法

Ellipsis: leaving out a verb already used省略：承前省略相同的谓语
When clauses are joined and the verb would be the same, the verb can be left out of the later clauses, which are then linked by commas or and. The sentence becomes tighter and gains rhythm.
并列分句里，后面的分句与前面谓语相同时，可以省去谓语只留其余部分，用逗号或 and 连接，使句子紧凑有节奏。
  • Tom likes tea, and his sister (likes) coffee.
  • Some people went by bus, others (went) by train.
It + passive + that / be such that / in the same way thatIt + 被动 + that / be such that / in the same way that
It has been said / is believed / is reported that ... uses the passive to hide whoever said it, which sounds detached and objective. be such that ...: the position is this, that ... in the same way that, or as: just as.
It has been said / is believed / is reported that…：用被动把「说的人」隐去，显得客观。be such that…：情况是这样的。in the same way that / as：和……一样。

  • It is believed that the house is 200 years old.

  • Treat others in the same way that you want to be treated.
Gerunds as objects: grudge / avoid / mind doing; in doing动名词作宾语：grudge / avoid / mind doing；in doing
grudge, avoid, mind, enjoy, finish and deny are followed by the -ing form, and so is any preposition, including in, by and without. in doing means while doing or in the course of doing; by doing means by means of doing.
grudge（舍不得）、avoid、mind、enjoy、finish、deny 等后接动名词；介词 in/by/without 后也要用 doing。in doing ＝在做……时、在……过程中；by doing ＝通过做。
  • He never grudges spending money on books.
  • In trying to help, she made things worse.

Summary writing摘要写作

In no more than eighty words, sum up the writer's case: why is the tramp an exception to the rule that everybody lives by selling something?

用不超过 80 个词概括作者的观点：为什么流浪汉是「人人靠卖东西为生」的例外？

Points a good summary covers好摘要要覆盖的要点
  • Everybody makes a living by selling something, and goods, knowledge, wisdom and skills all have to be paid for.人人都靠卖点什么为生：商品、知识、智慧、技能都要付钱。
  • A beggar all but sells his own person in order to be pitied, but the real tramp is not a beggar at all.乞丐几乎出卖自己的人格来博取同情，但真正的流浪汉不是乞丐。
  • The tramp has nothing to sell and asks nothing of anybody; he keeps his dignity while going after independence, has chosen the life himself, and is free of the usual worries.流浪汉无物可卖、无求于人，追求独立又不失尊严；他们自愿选择这种生活，摆脱了种种忧虑。
  • We tend to look down on tramps, and yet hardly any of us has never envied that simple and unencumbered life.我们常看不起流浪汉，可很少有人从未羡慕过他们简单自由的生活。
Our model summary本站范文

Most people earn their living by selling something, whether goods, knowledge or skills, and every service has its price. Tramps seem to be the one exception. Unlike beggars, who use pity to get money, true tramps offer nothing and want nothing from anyone. They have chosen their life on purpose, keep their dignity, and are free from the worries that trouble the rest of us. We may look down on them, yet many of us secretly envy their freedom.（79 词）

🎯 Before you open the lesson开课前
Two hundred years ago in England, boxing meant bare fists and purses of money, which is why the men who did it were called prizefighters. Nobody had written any rules, and a fighter could be badly hurt or killed in the ring; gloves came in only when the Marquis of Queensberry's first code appeared, which the lesson dates to 1860. Mendoza, born in 1764, is one of the most colourful men in the history of the sport: he brought science into it and did much to turn a brawl into a sport. He made his name in a fight at fourteen; Humphries, then the best fighter in England, trained him, and when the pupil began to outgrow the master the two fell out and could only settle it in the ring. Mendoza lost twice before winning the third match in 1790 and becoming champion of England. He founded a successful academy, with Lord Byron among the pupils, earned sums that made him famous, spent faster than he earned, went to prison for debt and died poor in 1836. The lesson is a short life of one man, rise and fall. Learn It was not until ... that, so ... that, be known as, turn against, as much as, and alike.
两百年前的英国，拳击是赤手空拳、为奖金而打的「职业拳斗」，没有规则，会打伤甚至打死人；直到 1860 年昆斯伯里侯爵定下第一套规则，才开始戴手套。1764 年出生的门多萨是拳击史上最有色彩的人物之一：他把「科学」带进拳击，让野蛮的打斗变成运动。14 岁一战成名后，当时英国最出色的拳手汉弗莱斯收他为徒；徒弟太成功，师徒反目，只能拳台上见分晓。门多萨连输两场，直到 1790 年第三场才打赢，成为全英冠军。他开拳击学院、连拜伦勋爵都是学生，挣钱如流水，却挥霍成性、负债累累，最后因还不起债入狱，1836 年贫困而死。本课是一个人物的兴衰小传。学 It was not until … that、so … that、be known as、turn against、as much as 和 alike。

🌍 Where it comes from出处与背景

Bare-knuckle prizefighting in Georgian England was a betting business before it was a sport. Fights were staged on commons and at country inns, went on round after round until one man could not stand, and the money came from the purse put up by backers plus the wagers of the crowd, which is where the word prizefighter comes from. Daniel Mendoza was born in London in seventeen sixty-four, in the East End, and he changed how the thing was done. He was a small man by the standards of men who fought all comers, and instead of trading blows he developed guarding, side-stepping and straight hitting — what the lesson calls bringing science to the game — and he taught it, in an academy and in a book on the art of boxing. The Humphries story in the lesson is accurate in shape. Richard Humphries was the leading fighter of the day, took the boy on, and turned on him when the pupil became the better man; they met repeatedly, and Mendoza won the decisive encounter at Doncaster on the twenty-ninth of September, seventeen ninety, a fight that ran to some seventy rounds. He was recognised as champion in the years that followed, lost to the boxer known as Gentleman Jackson, and ended, as the lesson says, in debt and in prison.

乔治王朝时期英国的赤手拳斗，先是一桩赌博生意，然后才是运动。比赛摆在公共草地和乡村客栈，一轮接一轮地打下去，直到有一方站不起来；钱来自赞助人凑出的彩金，加上围观人群的赌注——prizefighter 这个词正是从这里来的。丹尼尔·门多萨一七六四年生于伦敦东区，而他改变了这件事的做法。按那些与所有来者对打的人的标准，他身量不大；他不去对砸，而是发展出防守、侧步与直击——课文称之为「把科学带进拳击」——并且把它教出去：既在一家拳击学院里，也写进一本论拳击之术的书。课文里关于汉弗莱斯的那段，形状是准确的。理查德·汉弗莱斯是当时的头号拳手，收了这个少年，又在徒弟超过自己时与他反目；两人多次交手，而门多萨在一七九〇年九月二十九日于唐卡斯特赢下决定性的一场，那一战打了约七十轮。此后几年他被公认为冠军，后来输给了那位绰号「绅士」杰克逊的拳手，并如课文所说，以负债与入狱收场。

✨ How it is written赏析

This is a life told in two movements, and the hinge is a single sentence about a quarrel. Up to that point every sentence adds something to the man: born, colourful, brought science, enjoyed popularity, adored by rich and poor, rose swiftly, attracted attention, was quick to learn. The verbs climb. Then one sentence turns the teacher into an enemy, and from there the verbs fall just as steadily: quarrelled, was defeated, lost, was always in debt, was forgotten, was sent to prison, died. Notice that the writer never explains the fall. He gives one cause, extravagance, in half a sentence, and refuses all the others — no reflection on fame, no moral about money. The restraint is what makes the last line land. Notice too the one deliberate interruption. The sentence about gloves and the Queensberry code sits oddly in the middle of the opening, breaking the narrative to tell you about something that happened long after Mendoza's death. That is a writer deciding that a reader needs the rules explained before the fights, and accepting a bump in the storytelling to do it. If you write a short life of somebody, this is a usable frame: climbing verbs, one hinge sentence, falling verbs, and no sermon at the end.

这是一段用两个乐章讲完的生平，而铰链是关于一次争吵的一个句子。在那之前，每一句都给这个人添一点东西：出生、有色彩、带来科学、享有声望、受贫富爱戴、迅速成名、引起注意、学得快。动词在往上爬。然后一句话把老师变成敌人，自那以后动词同样稳定地往下落：争吵、被击败、再输、总在欠债、被遗忘、被送进监狱、死去。注意作者从不解释这场下坠。他只在半句话里给了一个原因——挥霍——其余一概不给：不感慨名声，不就钱讲道理。正是这份克制让最后一句落得住。也注意那一处刻意的打断。关于手套与昆斯伯里规则的那句话，别扭地坐在开头的中间，打断了叙事，去讲一件发生在门多萨死后很久的事。那是一个作者判断读者需要先懂规则再看比赛，并为此接受了叙述上的一个坎。你若要写一篇短生平，这是个可用的框架：往上爬的动词，一个铰链句，往下落的动词，结尾不讲道理。

🧭 What it argues, and does it still hold?解读

Three corrections, in order of how much they matter. The small one is a date. The Queensberry code was drawn up by John Graham Chambers in about eighteen sixty-five and published in eighteen sixty-seven, not in eighteen sixty, and gloves came in with it; no source we found supports the lesson's year, so the sentence that teaches not ... until is itself attached to the wrong year. The second is Lord Byron. Byron did box, and his teacher is recorded as Gentleman John Jackson — the man who beat Mendoza — so the claim that Byron was Mendoza's pupil looks like a confusion of the two, and we could not confirm it from any reliable source. The third is the omission, and it is large. Mendoza was Jewish, billed himself as such in the ring, and was the first Jewish champion in England; his standing with the public, in a country where Jews were barred from much of public life, is the reason historians write about him at all, and he is recorded as the first boxer to win royal patronage and to be received in conversation by the king. A life of Mendoza that leaves this out keeps the rise and the fall and loses the meaning. The lesson's own claim, that he turned a brawl into a sport by bringing method to it, has held up entirely: guarding and footwork are now the first things any beginner is taught. For Chinese readers the parallel is close at hand, since the same argument about turning fighting into method, with written forms and a named school, runs through the martial arts, and so does the same unhappy ending for the teacher who can fight but cannot keep money.

三处修正，按重要程度排。小的那处是日期。昆斯伯里规则由约翰·格雷厄姆·钱伯斯约在一八六五年起草、一八六七年发布，不是一八六〇年，手套也是随它进来的；我们找到的来源没有一个支持课文那个年份——于是那句用来教 not … until 的句子，自己挂错了年。第二处是拜伦勋爵。拜伦确实练过拳，而有记载的教师是「绅士」约翰·杰克逊——正是击败门多萨的那个人——所以「拜伦是门多萨的学生」看起来是把两人弄混了，我们也无法从可靠来源确认。第三处是遗漏，而且很大。门多萨是犹太人，在拳台上就以此为号，并且是英格兰第一位犹太裔冠军；在一个犹太人被挡在许多公共生活之外的国家里，他在公众中的地位正是史家至今写他的原因，而他也被记为第一个获得王室青睐、并被国王接见交谈的拳手。一篇把这一点抹掉的门多萨小传，留住了起落，丢掉了意义。课文自己的那个判断——他靠带进方法把一场乱打变成了运动——完全站住了：防守与步法如今是任何初学者最先被教的东西。对中国读者，对照就在手边：同样「把打斗变成方法」的论证，连同成文的套路与有名有姓的门派，贯穿着武术史；而那位能打、却守不住钱的师父，也有同样不快的结局。

④ Grammar in this lesson本课语法

It was not until ... that ...: not before that momentIt was not until … that …：直到……才……
not ... until says that something did not happen before a certain point. Put the not until part into an It was ... that frame and you get It was not until ... that, followed by a positive clause. The other variant is Not until ... plus inversion of the subject and verb.
not … until 表示「直到某时某事才发生」；把 not until 部分用 It was … that 强调，就成了 It was not until … that + 肯定句。另一个变体是 Not until … did + 主语（句首倒装）。
  • I didn't know the truth until yesterday.
  • It was not until yesterday that I knew the truth.
  • Not until yesterday did I know the truth.
so ... that and such ... that; as much as plus a quantityso … that 与 such … that；as much as + 数量
so + adjective or adverb + that; such (a) + adjective + noun + that. as much as or as many as + a quantity means as high a figure as.
so + 形容词/副词 + that；such (a) + 形容词 + 名词 + that。as much/many as + 数量：多达。

  • Some phones cost as much as $2,000.
  • As many as 500 people came to the concert.
Adjectives used as nouns, and alike: rich and poor alike形容词作名词与 alike：rich and poor alike
the + adjective can stand for a class of people (the rich, the poor, the young); when two such adjectives are paired, the the is often dropped. alike is an adverb and goes after the nouns it covers, meaning in the same way.
the + 形容词可表示一类人（the rich, the poor, the young）；rich and poor 并列时常省 the。alike 作副词放在被修饰的几个名词后面，意思是「同样地」。

In this lesson, sentence 11: Mendoza was loved by rich and poor alike.

本课：第 11 句，门多萨受到 rich and poor alike 的爱戴。

  • The young should respect the old.
  • The book is popular with teachers and students alike.

⑤ Patterns to reuse可以套用的句型

  • X did much to change … into … X 为把……变成……做了很多
    She did much to change the old factory into an art centre.
  • X was adored by … alike. X 受到……一致的喜爱
    The singer was adored by parents and children alike.
  • X rose to fame swiftly after … X 在……之后迅速成名
    The young writer rose to fame swiftly after her first novel.
  • X was quick to learn. X 学得很快
    The new nurse was quick to learn.
  • It was not until … that X finally … 直到……X 才终于……
    It was not until her fourth try that she finally passed the test.

Summary writing摘要写作

In no more than 80 words, sum up Mendoza's life: what he contributed, how he became known, his contest with Humphries, and how it ended.

用不超过 80 个词概述门多萨的一生：贡献、成名、与汉弗莱斯的较量和结局。

Points a good summary covers好摘要要覆盖的要点

Our model summary本站范文

Two centuries ago English boxing had no rules and was very rough. Daniel Mendoza, born in 1764, brought skill and science to it. Famous at fourteen, he was trained by Richard Humphries, England's best boxer, but the two later became bitter enemies. Mendoza lost to him twice and only won their third fight in 1790, becoming champion. Though he earned a fortune, he spent wildly, was jailed for debt, and died poor in 1836.（74 词）

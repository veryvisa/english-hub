Lesson 36 · A Chance in a Million 百万分之一的机会

📘 New Concept English · Book 3 · Lesson 36 “A Chance in a Million”新概念英语 第三册 Lesson 36《A Chance in a Million》

B2🧠 24 new words个新词📝 Full notes有完整讲解

🎯 Before you open the lesson开课前
Novelists of the nineteenth century liked to end their stories with a string of coincidences: a humble servant turns out to be the hero's mother, and a brother thought dead has been alive all along, plotting against him. Readers today would find such endings childish. Yet real life sometimes produces coincidences no novelist would dare to use. When the writer was a boy, his grandfather told him about Franz Bussman, a German taxi driver, who stopped during a walking holiday to chat with a workman. Afterwards his wife said the man looked just like Franz and might be his brother. Franz laughed at the idea, since his brother had died in the war, but she thought there was a tiny chance, and some days afterwards she sent a boy to ask the man's name. It was Hans Bussman. Wounded near the end of the war, Hans had lost his unit and his records, walked home to find the house bombed, assumed his family were dead, and made a new life fifty miles off. This is an essay that argues first and then tells a story, setting fiction against real life. The grammar to learn is would for past habit, be thought to have been done, While with a participle or phrase, after having been done, and what had become of.
十九世纪的小说家爱用一串巧合给故事收尾：不起眼的女仆其实是主角的生母，以为死了的哥哥其实一直活着、暗中算计主角。今天的读者会嫌这种结局幼稚。可现实里偏偏有连小说家都不敢写的巧合。作者小时候听祖父讲过：德国出租车司机弗朗茨·布斯曼和太太徒步旅行时跟一个工人聊了几句，太太说那人长得真像你，没准是你哥哥。弗朗茨嗤之以鼻——哥哥早在战争中阵亡了。太太却觉得有百万分之一的可能，几天后派个男孩去问那人是不是叫汉斯·布斯曼——正是。原来汉斯战争末期受伤住院，医院被炸，部队失散、档案被毁，他徒步回到西德，老家也被炸了，以为家人都死于空袭，就在五十英里外的村子住了下来。本课是先议论、后讲故事的随笔，用小说和现实作对比。学 would 表过去习惯、be thought to have been done、While + 分词/介词短语、after having been done、what had become of。
Questions to keep in mind as you read读的时候带着这几个问题：
  1. Why do modern readers no longer accept the coincidences of nineteenth-century novels?现代读者为什么不再接受十九世纪小说式的巧合？
  2. What made Mrs Bussman think the workman might be Franz's brother?布斯曼太太凭什么认为那个工人可能是弗朗茨的哥哥？
  3. Why had Hans never gone looking for his family?汉斯为什么一直没有回家找亲人？

📖 Understanding the piece读懂这篇

Written for the course by L. G. Alexander. The Bussman brothers are given full names and a setting, but we have found no independent record of them, and the story comes to us third-hand: the writer heard it from his grandfather. Treat it as a tale of the kind that circulated widely after the war rather than as reporting.

课文为编者 L. G. 亚历山大为教材自撰。布斯曼兄弟有名有姓、有背景，但我们没找到关于他们的独立记录，而且这个故事是三手的：作者听祖父讲的。应当把它当作战后广为流传的那类故事，而不是新闻报道。

🌍 Where it comes from出处与背景

The background, at least, is entirely real. At the end of the Second World War millions of Germans had lost track of relatives — soldiers missing at the front, civilians bombed out or driven from the eastern provinces, children separated from their parents. The German Red Cross set up a tracing service to reunite them, and it is still answering enquiries about the fate of missing people today, eight decades later. Cases like the Bussmans' — a soldier listed as missing who turns out to be alive, living a short distance away — were exactly what the service existed to solve, and many families were reunited by it rather than by luck. Mathematicians, meanwhile, have explained why amazing coincidences are less amazing than they feel. The Cambridge mathematician J. E. Littlewood joked that if we count an event with odds of a million to one as a miracle, an ordinary person should expect about one a month. In 1989 the statisticians Persi Diaconis and Frederick Mosteller called this the law of truly large numbers: with a large enough number of people and chances, something astonishing is bound to happen to somebody.

至少故事的背景完全是真实的。二战结束时，数以百万计的德国人与亲人失去了联系——前线失踪的士兵、被炸得无家可归或被逐出东部省份的平民、与父母失散的孩子。德国红十字会设立了寻亲服务来帮他们团聚，八十年后的今天，它仍在答复关于失踪者下落的查询。像布斯曼兄弟这样的情况——一个被列为失踪的士兵原来还活着、就住在不远处——正是这个服务要解决的事，许多家庭是靠它而不是靠运气团圆的。与此同时，数学家解释了为什么惊人的巧合其实没有感觉上那么惊人。剑桥数学家 J. E. 李特尔伍德打趣说：如果把百万分之一概率的事件算作奇迹，那么普通人大约每个月都该遇上一次。1989 年，统计学家佩尔西·迪亚科尼斯与弗雷德里克·莫斯特勒把这称作「真正大数定律」：只要人足够多、机会足够多，惊人的事总会落在某个人头上。

✨ How it is written赏析

The essay sets a trap for itself and then walks out of it. It begins by mocking the nineteenth-century novel for its coincidences — the hidden mother, the brother presumed dead — and then tells a true story built around the very same device. The defence is in the framing: the writer gives the source of the story (a grandfather), the place and the job of the hero, and above all an explanation. The second half of the lesson is not a reunion scene at all; the meeting itself is passed over in a few words, and the space goes to Hans's account of how he came to be lost, step by step, each step plausible on its own. That is the difference the essay wants us to see between a novelist's coincidence and a real one: in a good true story the chance is single, and everything else has a cause. The phrase 'needless to say' does quiet work too, turning what would be the climax of a novel into something almost dull.

这篇随笔给自己设了个陷阱，然后又走了出来。它开头嘲笑十九世纪小说的巧合——隐身的生母、被推定死亡的哥哥——接着讲的真实故事却正是用同一个手法搭起来的。辩护在于框架：作者交代了故事的来源（祖父），主角的地点与职业，最重要的是给出了解释。课文的后半部分根本不是重逢场面：见面本身几笔带过，篇幅都给了汉斯讲他如何走失的经过，一步一步，每一步单独看都合情合理。这正是随笔想让我们看到的、小说家的巧合与真实巧合的区别：一个好的真实故事里，偶然只有一处，其余一切都有原因。「不用说」这个短语也在暗中干活：它把小说里本该是高潮的地方变得几乎平淡无奇。

🧭 What it argues, and does it still hold?解读

The lesson says modern readers are less credulous than their grandparents, and then asks them to believe a grandfather's story. It is worth taking the essay's own advice. Two things make the Bussman tale plausible: the chaos of the war, which really did separate families and destroy records, and Mrs Bussman's action, which turned a feeling into a test. She did not decide that the man was Hans; she sent someone to ask. That is the useful lesson about coincidences: notice them, then check. The modern reader is in a different position from the Victorian one. We are surrounded by stories of amazing coincidences, many shared online with no source at all, and the law of truly large numbers tells us that some will be true and many will be improved in the telling. The habit to learn from the lesson is not to disbelieve, but to ask where a story came from and what would have to be true for it to happen.

课文说现代读者比祖辈更不轻信，然后又请读者相信一个祖父讲的故事。值得用这篇随笔自己的建议来读它。有两件事让布斯曼的故事可信：战争的混乱确实拆散了家庭、毁掉了档案；而布斯曼太太的行动把一种感觉变成了一次检验——她没有认定那人就是汉斯，而是派人去问。这正是关于巧合最有用的一课：注意到它，然后去核实。今天的读者处境和维多利亚时代的读者不同：我们被各种惊人巧合的故事包围，许多是在网上转来转去、毫无出处的；「真正大数定律」告诉我们，其中有些是真的，而更多会在转述中被加工得更好听。从这一课该学的习惯不是不信，而是问一问：这个故事从哪里来？要发生这件事，必须有哪些前提为真？

  • bring something to a conclusion: to end it. by presenting someone with: by giving them.bring … to a conclusion：给……收尾。by presenting … with：通过向……呈现。

④ Grammar in this lesson本课语法

would / used to for past habitswould / used to：过去的习惯
would plus the plain verb can describe actions repeated in the past, especially in stories and memories. used to can describe both repeated actions and past states, such as used to live or used to be; would cannot be used for states.
would + 动词原形可以表示过去反复发生的习惯动作，多用于叙述与回忆；used to 既可表示过去的习惯动作，也可表示过去的状态（used to live / be），would 不能用于状态。

The would in sentence 2 describes what novelists of that century habitually did, while used to in sentence 1 describes what we once were.

本课：第 2 句的 would 讲十九世纪小说家「惯用的做法」，第 1 句的 used to 讲我们过去的状态。

  • Every summer we would go to the seaside.
  • My grandfather would tell us stories after dinner.
  • There used to be a cinema on this street.
be thought / said / believed to have been done
A subject plus be thought, said, believed or reported plus an infinitive means 'people think that ...'. The perfect infinitive to have done shows that the event came before the thinking, and to have been done makes the subject the one affected. It is the same as It was thought that ... had been ....
主语 + be thought / said / believed / reported + to do：「据认为……」。不定式的完成式 to have done 表示「被认为的事」比「认为」更早；被动式 to have been done 表示主语是承受者。等于 It was thought that … had been …

In sentence 10 Franz finds a brother whom people believed killed twenty years earlier.

本课：第 10 句：弗朗茨找到了一个被认为二十年前已阵亡的哥哥。

  • The painting is said to have been stolen in 1920.
  • He is believed to have left the country.
  • The ship was reported to have sunk near the coast.
while, when or though followed by a participle or phrase, with subject and be left outwhile / when / though + 分词或介词短语（省略主语和 be）
When the clause has the same subject as the main clause and its verb contains be, you can leave out the subject and be together: while on a tour, when heated, though tired.
当从句主语与主句主语一致、从句谓语含 be 时，可以把「主语 + be」一起省掉：While (he was) on a tour…；When (it is) heated…；Though (she was) tired…。

Sentence 11 opens with While on, leaving out he was.

本课：第 11 句以 While on 开头，省去了 he was。

  • While in London, she visited the British Museum.
  • When asked about the plan, he said nothing.
  • Though tired, they kept on walking.

⑤ Patterns to reuse可以套用的句型

  • We are less … than we used to be. 我们不像过去那样……了
    We are less patient than we used to be.
  • Modern readers would find such … totally unacceptable. 现代读者会觉得这种……完全不可接受
    Modern viewers would find such slow films totally boring.
  • X poured scorn on the plan, pointing out that … X 对这个想法嗤之以鼻，指出……
    The manager poured scorn on the plan, pointing out that it would cost too much.
  • The chance that … was one in a million. ……的可能性只有百万分之一
    The chance that the letter would arrive in time was one in a million.
  • Needless to say, … 不用说，……
    Needless to say, the train was late again.

Summary writing摘要写作

In no more than 80 words, tell how the Bussman brothers found each other again.

用不超过 80 个词讲述布斯曼兄弟重逢的经过。

Points a good summary covers好摘要要覆盖的要点
  • On a walking holiday, Franz Bussman and his wife stopped to chat with a man working by the road.弗朗茨·布斯曼和妻子徒步旅行时，停下来和一个工人聊了几句。
  • His wife thought the man looked like Franz and might be his brother, but Franz laughed, since he believed his brother had died in the war.妻子觉得那人长得很像丈夫，认为可能是他哥哥；弗朗茨不信，因为哥哥据说已在战争中阵亡。
  • Some days afterwards she sent a boy to ask the man's name, and it was indeed Hans Bussman.几天后她派一个男孩去问那人的名字，果然是汉斯·布斯曼。
  • Wounded late in the war, Hans had lost his unit and his records; finding his home bombed, he thought his family dead and settled fifty miles away.汉斯战争末期受伤，与部队失散，档案被毁；老家被炸，他以为家人已死，就在五十英里外的村子定居。
Our model summary本站范文

While on a walking holiday, Franz Bussman and his wife stopped to chat with a workman. Later she remarked on how much he looked like her husband and suggested he might be Franz's brother. Franz laughed, because his brother had supposedly died in the war. Some days afterwards she had a boy ask the man's name: it was Hans Bussman. Hans had been wounded, lost touch with his unit, found his home destroyed and, thinking his family dead, settled elsewhere.（80 词）

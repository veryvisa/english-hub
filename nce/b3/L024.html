Lesson 24 · A Skeleton in the Cupboard 柜中骷髅

📘 New Concept English · Book 3 · Lesson 24 “A Skeleton in the Cupboard”新概念英语 第三册 Lesson 24《A Skeleton in the Cupboard》

B2🧠 26 new words个新词📝 Full notes有完整讲解

🎯 Before you open the lesson开课前
English has a vivid phrase, a skeleton in the cupboard, for the dreadful thing a respectable person or family has kept from outsiders for years. Novels love it: in the last few pages we learn that the kind old lady who was good to everybody poisoned all five of her husbands when she was young. The writer points out that we all keep things from our friends, but that hardly anybody has a skeleton of that kind — the one man he knows who does is George Carlton, and George is rather pleased about it. George trained as a doctor when he was young and then became a successful writer of crime novels. The writer once spent an unforgettable weekend at his house: he opened the wardrobe in the guest room and a skeleton swung out in front of his face. He dropped his suit and ran downstairs, but George only smiled and said it was Sebastian, and had he forgotten that George was once a medical student? The humour here comes from taking a figure of speech literally: the secret of the saying turns into a real set of bones. Learn It's all very well ..., a comment clause inside a relative clause (which, he said, was ...), as if + the unreal past, be about to do and participles used as adverbials.
英语里有个生动的说法 a skeleton in the cupboard（柜中骷髅），指一个体面的人或家庭多年瞒着外人的可怕秘密。小说里常有这种情节：最后几页揭晓，那位对谁都和蔼的老太太年轻时毒死了五任丈夫。作者说，我们每个人多少都有不愿让朋友知道的秘密，但真有「柜中骷髅」的人很少——他认识的唯一一个是乔治·卡尔顿，而且乔治还很得意。乔治年轻时学医，后来成了侦探小说作家。作者在他家住过一个难忘的周末：一打开客房衣柜，一具骷髅就挂在眼前晃。他吓得扔下西装冲下楼，乔治却笑着说：「哦，那是塞巴斯蒂安。你忘了我以前是医学生。」本课是从习语字面意思出发的幽默：比喻义的「秘密」变成了字面上的真骷髅。学 It is all very well …、插入式定语从句（which, he said, was…）、as if + 虚拟、be about to do 和分词作状语。

🌍 Where it comes from出处与背景

The phrase is younger than most people assume. The Oxford English Dictionary's earliest record is from eighteen forty-five, in a piece by the novelist who wrote Vanity Fair, and his wording was a skeleton in every house rather than in the cupboard; British English later settled on cupboard, while North America says closet. A popular story links the phrase to the body-snatchers who dug up the dead and sold them to anatomy teachers before the Anatomy Act of eighteen thirty-two opened a legal supply of corpses. It is a good story and may even be true, but no source we found establishes it, and dictionaries say plainly that the origin is uncertain. What is certain is where teaching skeletons came from afterwards. For much of the twentieth century the trade ran through Calcutta, until India forbade the export of human bones with effect from the nineteenth of August, nineteen eighty-five. After that the supply dried up, and by the early nineteen nineties laboratory skeletons were already described as almost always plastic.

这句话比多数人以为的年轻。《牛津英语词典》最早的记录是 1845 年，出自《名利场》作者的一篇文字，而且他写的是「每家都有一具骷髅」，不是「柜中」；后来英式英语定在 cupboard，北美说 closet。流行的说法把它接到盗尸人身上——1832 年《解剖法》开出合法尸源之前，盗尸人挖出尸体卖给解剖教师。这故事很好，也可能是真的，但我们找到的材料都证不实它，词典干脆写「来源不确定」。确定的是后来教学骷髅从哪里来。二十世纪有很长一段时间，这桩生意经加尔各答出货，直到印度从 1985 年 8 月 19 日起禁止人骨出口。此后货源枯竭，到 1990 年代初，实验室骷髅已经被描述为「几乎总是塑料的」。

✨ How it is written赏析

The whole lesson is one joke, and the joke is a trick of language: a dead metaphor is brought back to life by being taken at its word. Alexander sets it up with care. First he explains the saying, in the flat voice of a dictionary. Then he gives the novel-reader's version of it, the kind old lady and her five husbands, which is funny because it is so unsurprising. Then comes the sentence that quietly promises a story: he knows one man who really has one, and the man is proud of it. Only then does the weekend begin. Watch the delay in the final paragraph: the door swings, the thing moves, the writer runs, and George says nothing useful for two lines. The answer, when it comes, is three words and a name, and the name is the joke. Calling a skeleton Sebastian turns horror into furniture. That is worth copying: if you want to deflate a frightening object in English, give it an ordinary name and speak of it as an old acquaintance.

整课就是一个笑话，而这个笑话是语言的手法：一个死掉的比喻，因为被照字面当真而活了过来。亚历山大铺得很小心。先用词典式的平淡口吻解释这个说法；再给小说读者版——和善的老太太与她的五任丈夫——好笑之处正在于它毫不意外；接着是那句悄悄许下故事的话：他认识一个真有这玩意儿的人，而且那人还很得意。到这儿，周末才开始。请看最后一段的延迟：门一晃，东西动了，作者夺门而下，乔治却两行里都没说出有用的话。答案来时只有三个词加一个名字，而名字就是笑点。给一具骷髅起名叫塞巴斯蒂安，恐怖立刻变成家具。这一手值得学：想在英文里给一件可怕的东西放气，就给它一个寻常名字，并且像提起熟人那样提起它。

🧭 What it argues, and does it still hold?解读

Two parts of the lesson have aged, and the aging is interesting. First, George's hobby is now largely impossible. A medical student today learns from plastic bones, from scans and from screens; the real article is regulated, and the main historical supply ended with India's export ban in nineteen eighty-five. A reader who takes the story as a picture of medical training is reading about the nineteen sixties, not about now. Second, and more important, the cupboard itself has changed. In the lesson the secret is safe for decades because secrets then lived in houses, and houses had doors. Today the hidden thing is more likely to sit in a database, an old photograph that someone else has scanned, a court record that has been indexed, a message a friend kept. The phrase has shifted with it: in the news a skeleton in the closet is now almost always something in a public person's past that a journalist or a search engine has turned up, not something a family is quietly ashamed of. For Chinese readers the second half of the lesson needs no explanation, because the idea has its own proverb: a family's shame should not be aired outside. It is worth noticing, though, that the English saying has a sharper edge. It does not advise you to keep the secret; it predicts, with a certain relish, that the cupboard will be opened.

这一课有两处旧了，而旧的方式很有意思。第一，乔治的爱好今天基本不可能。今天的医学生用塑料骨骼、断层影像和屏幕学习；真品受管制，而历史上的主要货源随 1985 年印度禁止出口而终结。把这故事当成医学训练的写照来读，读到的是六十年代，不是现在。第二点更重要：柜子本身变了。课文里秘密能稳稳藏几十年，因为那时候秘密住在房子里，而房子有门。今天那件藏起来的东西，更可能待在一个数据库里——别人扫描了的旧照片、被编了索引的法院记录、朋友留着的一条消息。这个说法也跟着挪了位：今天新闻里的「柜中骷髅」几乎总是公众人物过往里被记者或搜索引擎翻出来的事，而不是一个家庭默默羞于提起的事。对中国读者，这一课的后半不用解释，因为同一个意思自有谚语：家丑不可外扬。不过值得留意：英文那句的锋刃更利一些——它不劝你把秘密守住，它带着几分兴味地预告，柜门早晚会被打开。

    作者说，我们每个人都有连最亲近的朋友也不告诉的事。私事与「别人有权知道的秘密」之间，界线在哪里？
  2. Write a hundred and twenty words in which an ordinary object frightens the narrator and is then explained away in one short line of dialogue. Use be about to do once and as if once.
    写 120 词：让一件寻常物品先把叙述者吓一跳，再用一句很短的对话把它解释掉。用一次 be about to do、一次 as if。
  3. Old records are now easy to search. Should everything about a public figure's past be fair game, or should some of it be allowed to fade? Give the strongest point on the other side first.
    旧记录如今容易检索。公众人物的过往是否全都可以被追究，还是有些该被允许淡去？先说出相反立场最强的一点。

④ Grammar in this lesson本课语法

It's all very well ... (but ...): that is all very fine, but ...It's all very well … (but …)：……固然不错（但……）
It's all very well (for somebody) to do, or It's all very well + clause, concedes a point first: fine, that holds under those conditions. A but usually follows, saying that real life is another matter.
It's all very well (for sb) to do / It's all very well + 从句：先让一步，承认某事在某种条件下没问题，后面往往接转折，表示「放到现实中就不行了」。

In this lesson, sentence ten uses the pattern to say that such things are all right in a novel, and sentences eleven and twelve then turn to real life.

本课：第 9 句用这个句型说「这种事出现在小说里倒没什么」，接着第 10–11 句转折到现实。

  • It's all very well for you to laugh, but I have to clean up the mess.
  • It's all very well saying sorry, but the damage is done.
Comment clauses inside a relative clause: which, he said, was ...插入语与插入式定语从句：which, he said, was …
he said, I think, I believe and the like can be dropped into a relative or object clause between commas without changing the structure; the relative word is still the subject or object of its own clause.
在定语从句或宾语从句里插入 he said / I think / I believe 等，用逗号隔开，不影响句子结构；关系词仍是从句的主语或宾语。
  • This is the book which, I think, you will enjoy.
  • The man who, she said, was her uncle turned out to be a stranger.
be about to do / as if + the unreal pastbe about to do / as if + 虚拟
be about to do means to be on the point of doing, and it is not used with a stated future time. as if + past simple, or were doing, marks something as contrary to fact; when the comparison may well be true, ordinary tenses are used instead.
be about to do：马上就要（不与具体将来时间连用）。as if + 过去式/were doing：好像（与事实不符）；与事实可能相符时用陈述语气。
  • I was about to leave when the phone rang.
  • He treats me as if I were a child.

  2. Dictate sentence by sentence: pause after each sentence and write it down. Replay a sentence at most three times.逐句听写：每句后暂停写下来，同一句最多重放三遍。
  3. Check against the text in your coursebook. Mark every mistake by type: a word you did not know, a word you knew but did not hear (linking, weak forms), or spelling.对照教材课文自查，把每个错按类型标出：不认识的词、认识却没听出的词（连读、弱读）、拼写。
  4. Words you did not hear go into your word book above. Re-listen to those sentences and shadow them.没听出的词加进上面的生词本；把那几句再听、跟读。
  5. Retell from the keywords below without looking at the text, then compare and retell once more.只看下面的关键词、不看课文复述一遍，对照后再复述一遍。

Keywords for retelling复述关键词

  • novels: the kind old lady who poisoned five husbands
  • we all have secrets, but few have skeletons

Summary writing摘要写作

In no more than eighty words, tell the story of the weekend the writer spent at George's house.

用不超过 80 个词讲述作者在乔治家过的那个周末。

Points a good summary covers好摘要要覆盖的要点
  • George Carlton studied medicine when he was young and then became a writer of crime novels, and he is the only man the writer knows who really does keep a skeleton at home.乔治·卡尔顿年轻时学医，后来成了侦探小说作家；他是作者认识的唯一真有「柜中骷髅」的人。
  • The writer went there for the weekend; George took him up to a guest room that was hardly ever used and told him to unpack and come down to eat.作者到乔治家过周末，乔治带他去很少有人住的客房，让他收拾好东西下楼吃饭。
  • Opening the wardrobe to hang up a suit, the writer saw a skeleton swinging in front of him as though it meant to jump at him, dropped the suit and ran downstairs.作者打开衣柜想挂西装，看见一具骷髅在眼前晃，好像要扑出来，吓得扔下西装冲下楼。
  • George showed no sympathy at all; he smiled, said the thing was called Sebastian, and reminded the writer that he had once been a medical student.乔治毫不同情，笑着说那是塞巴斯蒂安，提醒作者他曾是医学生。

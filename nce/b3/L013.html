Lesson 13 · It's Only Me 是我

📘 New Concept English · Book 3 · Lesson 13 “It's Only Me”新概念英语 第三册 Lesson 13《It's Only Me》

B2🧠 19 new words个新词📝 Full notes有完整讲解

🎯 Before you open the lesson开课前
Mrs Richards is going to a party in costume with her husband that evening, as a ghost; the costume is nothing but a sheet, and it works rather too well. In the morning she cannot resist putting it on, and she is barely downstairs when somebody knocks — the baker, surely. Not wanting to startle him, she slips into the cupboard under the stairs. But the caller is the man who has come to read the electricity meter, and when he opens that door, a sheeted figure says 'It's only me' and he is gone with a shout. The lesson is comedy built on a misunderstanding: every laugh sits in the gap between what she assumes and what is actually the case. Learn must be for deduction, too ... to, the future-in-the-past continuous would be doing, negative participles like Not wanting, and hear sb do versus hear sb doing.
理查兹太太晚上要和丈夫去参加化装舞会，她要扮成鬼，服装只是一条床单，效果却很逼真。早上她忍不住穿上试试，刚下楼就有人敲门——一定是面包师，她怕吓着他，赶紧躲进楼梯下的储藏室。谁知进来的是电力局来抄电表的人，他一开储藏室门，看见一个「鬼」说「是我」，大叫一声逃之夭夭。本课是误会式喜剧：一切笑点都建立在「她以为」和「其实是」之间。学 must be 推测、too … to、过去将来进行时 would be doing、否定分词 Not wanting 和 hear sb do / doing。

📖 Understanding the piece读懂这篇

Written for the course by L. G. Alexander; the characters are invented. The furniture of the joke, however, is documentary — a British fancy-dress party, a baker with standing permission to walk in, and a stranger from the electricity board with a right to come and read your meter.

课文由编者 L. G. 亚历山大为教材自撰，人物是虚构的。但这个笑话的家具是纪实的：英国的化装舞会、得到授权可以自己进门的面包师，还有一个有权上门抄电表的陌生人。

🌍 Where it comes from出处与背景

The stranger in the cupboard is a period detail. Britain's electricity supply was taken into public ownership by an Act of 1947; area boards were set up under it, formally constituted at the start of 1948, and they owned the wires, sent the bills and employed the men who walked from door to door writing down numbers. That system lasted until privatisation late in the nineteen-eighties, when the Act of nineteen forty-seven was swept away. So a caller who announces himself as being from the electricity board places this story firmly between 1948 and about 1990 — which is also the window in which the course itself was written. Two other details are worth noticing. One is the bread. A delivery man with a key, or with permission to let himself in and leave the loaf on the table, was normal in a country of daily rounds, and would alarm people now. The other is the sheet. Dressing up as a ghost in a bed sheet is the cheapest costume in the English-speaking world, which is exactly why the writer chose it: the comedy needs a disguise that is both ridiculous and, in a dark cupboard, frighteningly good.

储藏室里的那个陌生人是个时代细节。英国的电力供应由 1947 年的一部法律收归公有；按该法设立的大区电力局在 1948 年初正式成立，它们拥有线路、寄出账单，也雇着那些挨家挨户记抄数字的人。这套体制一直延续到八十年代末私有化，1947 年的法律随之废止。所以一个自称「电力局来的」的来客，把这个故事牢牢钉在 1948 年到 1990 年前后之间——这也正是这套教材成书的窗口。另有两个细节值得留意。一是面包。送货的人有钥匙，或者获准自己进门、把面包放在桌上，在一个每天都有上门投递的国家里是常态，放到今天会让人不安。二是那条床单。在英语世界里，用床单扮鬼是最省钱的装扮，而作者正是为此选它：这个笑话需要一件既荒唐、又在暗储藏室里逼真得吓人的行头。

✨ How it is written赏析

The whole lesson is engineered around one word: only. 'It's only me' is what you say through a door to reassure somebody, and it is the last thing that will reassure a man who has just opened a cupboard onto a sheeted figure. Everything before it is set-up, and the set-up is built out of deductions. She knows the caller must be the baker; a moment later she is just as sure it is the meter reader. The reader is told what she thinks, never what is true, so we discover the mistake at the same instant the poor man does. Look at how the point of view swings in the last three sentences: up to then we have been inside her head, and suddenly we are watching from outside — a man lets out a cry, jumps back, flees. The writer also withholds the obvious. He never says the man thought she was a ghost, never says she looked frightening, never explains the joke. For a learner this is the lesson: in a comic story, give the reader the character's assumption, give them the fact a sentence later, and do not explain the gap.

整课是围着一个词造出来的：only。「只是我」是你隔着门安慰别人时说的话，而对一个刚打开储藏室、面对一个披床单的身影的人来说，这是最不能让他安心的一句。前面全是铺垫，而铺垫是用推测搭的。她知道「一定是面包师」。她意识到「一定是抄表员」。读者始终只被告知她怎么想，从不被告知事实如何，所以我们和那个可怜人是在同一瞬间发现错误的。看最后三句视角怎么一甩：在那之前我们一直在她脑子里，忽然就从外面看了——一个男人大叫、后退、逃走。作者还故意不说显而易见的事。他从不说那人以为她是鬼，不说她看上去可怕，也不解释这个笑话。对学习者来说，这就是这一课要教的：写喜剧，把人物的假设给读者，隔一句再把事实给他，然后不要解释那道缝。

🧭 What it argues, and does it still hold?解读

As a joke about deduction it has not aged at all — must be is still the trap it was, and the story is a neat demonstration that a confident inference built on a reasonable prior can be flatly wrong. What has aged is the doorstep. The man with a notebook is close to extinct in Britain: by the end of June 2026 there were more than forty-two million smart and advanced meters in homes and small businesses, about seventy-two per cent of all meters, sending readings down a wire instead. The incident the story turns on could still happen — somebody comes to check a faulty unit — but it is now a rare visit rather than a quarterly certainty. Readers in China will recognise the older arrangement: the person who came to copy the water or electricity figures, and knocked whether or not you were expecting anybody, is within living memory in most cities and has given way to remote reading and a scan code on the wall in much the same decades. The more durable question is the costume one. A sheet with eye-holes reads as a ghost only inside a culture that has agreed it does; the same costume in a Chinese village in 1965 would have read as laundry, or as mourning. Jokes about disguise are among the hardest things to carry across a language.

作为一个关于推断的笑话，它一点没过时——must be 今天仍然是那个陷阱，这个故事干净地演示了一件事：建立在合理前提上的自信推断，照样可以彻底错。过时的是门口那一幕。拿本子抄表的人在英国已近绝迹：到 2026 年 6 月底，家庭和小企业里的智能及高级电表超过四千二百万只，约占全部电表的七成二，读数改从线路里走。故事赖以成立的那次上门仍然可能发生——比如来查一只坏表——但它已是罕见的拜访，不再是每季一次的确定事件。中国读者对更早的那套安排不陌生：上门抄水表电表的人、不管你等不等客都敲门，在多数城市都还在记忆里，并在差不多同样的几十年里让位给了远程抄表和墙上的一个扫码。更耐久的问题是装扮那一层。一条挖了眼洞的床单之所以能读成「鬼」，只因为某个文化已经就此达成一致；同样一条床单，放在 1965 年的中国乡村，会被读成洗好的衣物，或者孝布。关于伪装的笑话，是最难跨语言搬运的东西之一。

💬 Discuss and write讨论与写作
  1. Mrs Richards makes two confident deductions and gets one wrong. Describe a time you were sure you knew who was at the door, or who had sent a message, and were wrong. What made you so sure?
    理查兹太太做了两次自信的推断，错了一次。讲一次你确信知道门外是谁、或者消息是谁发来的，结果错了的经历。是什么让你那么确信？
  2. Write 120 words of a comic misunderstanding, real or invented, in which the reader learns the truth one sentence after the character does. Use must be once and a negative participle phrase once.
    写 120 词，讲一个喜剧式的误会（真事或虚构），让读者在人物之后一句才知道真相。用一次 must be，用一次否定分词短语。
  3. The man had a right to come in and read the meter; the baker had been told to walk in. How much of a stranger's entry into your home would you accept today, and what changed?
    抄表员有权进门读表；面包师被交代可以自己进来。今天你能接受陌生人进自家门到什么程度？是什么变了？

③ How the hard sentences are built难句结构

Sentences are counted in the lesson text, title not included. Find the sentence in your book, then read our notes on its structure.句子按课文正文数（不含标题）。在教材里找到这一句，再看本站的结构讲解。

Sentence 4第 4 句 (begins开头 “She was too …”)

  • too ... to do: so much that she could not.too … to do：太……而不能。
  • would be going: the future-in-the-past continuous — seen from that morning, the party was an arrangement already in place.would be going：过去将来进行时，从过去的角度看「到时候将在去」（已经安排好的事）。

Sentence 5第 5 句 (begins开头 “She intended to …”)

  • as: because; had made: past perfect, with the night before for the evening earlier (in past narrative you do not write last night).as：因为；had made：过去完成时，the night before「前一天晚上」（叙述过去时用，不用 last night）。
  • try it on: the pronoun sits between try and on.try it on：代词放在 try 和 on 中间。

Sentence 8第 8 句 (begins开头 “She wanted to …”)

  • whether introduces the object clause: she wanted to settle the question.whether 引导宾语从句：是否。
  • comfortable to wear: adjective plus infinitive; the logical object of the infinitive is the subject it.comfortable to wear：形容词 + 不定式（穿起来舒服），不定式的逻辑宾语就是主语 it。

④ Grammar in this lesson本课语法

Deduction with modals: must be / can't be / may be情态推测：must be / can't be / may be
must be = it is certainly (a deduction you are sure of); can't be = it cannot possibly be (the negative; never mustn't be); may/might/could be = it possibly is. For deductions about the past, use must have done / can't have done.
must be ＝一定是（很有把握的肯定推测）；can't be ＝不可能是（否定推测，不用 mustn't）；may/might/could be ＝可能是。推测过去用 must have done / can't have done。

In this lesson, sentence 10: it must be the baker; then sentence 15 has it must be again, this time the meter reader. Two confident deductions, and the first one is wrong — which is where the comedy lives.

本课：第 10 句 it must be the baker；第 15 句又一次 it must be …（抄表员）。两次「一定是」，第一次是错的——笑点就在这里。

  • The lights are on. They must be at home.
  • That can't be John — he's in Paris.
  • She must have forgotten the meeting.
Negative participle phrases: Not doing ..., ...否定分词短语：Not doing …, …
When a participle phrase works as an adverbial, not goes in front of the participle: Not knowing ..., Not wanting ..., Not having done ... Such phrases usually give a reason.
分词短语作状语时，否定词 not 放在分词之前：Not knowing…、Not wanting…、Not having done…，常表原因。
  • Not knowing what to say, he kept silent.
  • Not having a key, I waited outside.
hear/see sb do and hear/see sb doing (and hear sth done)hear/see sb do 与 hear/see sb doing（含 hear sth done）
hear sb do = you heard the whole action; hear sb doing = you heard it in progress; hear sth done = you heard something being done to a thing.
hear sb do ＝听到动作的全过程；hear sb doing ＝听到正在进行；hear sth done ＝听到某物被……。

In this lesson, sentence 13: what she hears is the front door open, the whole action, and after it footsteps in the hall.

本课：第 13 句，她听到前门 open（开门的整个动作）和门厅里的脚步声。

  • I heard her sing a song.（唱完了整首）
  • I heard her singing when I passed.（正在唱）
  • I heard my name called.（名字被叫）

  • She tried to explain that it was only her, but he cried out, jumped back, and fled when she came towards him, banging the door shut.她想解释说「是我」，可那人惊叫一声后退几步，见她走过来就逃了，还砰地关上了门。

Lesson 38 · The First Calendar 最早的日历

📘 New Concept English · Book 3 · Lesson 38 “The First Calendar”新概念英语 第三册 Lesson 38《The First Calendar》

B2🧠 28 new words个新词📝 Full notes有完整讲解

🎯 Before you open the lesson开课前
Future historians writing about our age will not lack evidence but drown in it: beyond the written word they will have films, videos and discs, and will be able, so to speak, to see and hear us. The historian of the distant past faces the opposite problem and must work from a few scattered clues. It used to be assumed that calendars arose with farming, since growing crops created a real need to understand the seasons. New evidence suggests that this is wrong. Nomads of the last Ice Age, who lived by hunting and fishing, scratched dots, lines and symbols on cave walls, bones and mammoth tusks; by comparing such marks from many regions, scholars have read the code as a record of passing days and of the phases of the moon — a primitive calendar. This is an exposition in which new evidence overturns an old assumption: it first contrasts the plenty of the future with the scarcity of the past, then states its case. The grammar to learn is be in a position to, not ... solely, as it were, seem to indicate, have long been done, and than has been supposed.
将来的历史学家写我们这个时代，难处不在缺材料，而在材料太多——不光有文字，还有影片、录像和光盘，简直能「看见、听见」我们。研究远古的历史学家正相反，只能从一点点零星线索里推。过去一般认为，日历是随农业出现的，因为种地才真正需要了解季节。可新的证据显示这个假定不对：冰河时期靠打猎捕鱼为生的游牧人，在洞壁、骨头和猛犸象牙上刻下了点、线和符号，把世界各地的刻痕对照起来，历史学家读出这套「密码」和日子的流逝、月相的变化有关——它是一种原始的日历。本课是用新证据推翻旧假设的说明文：先用「未来」与「远古」对比材料的多寡，再提出论点。学 will be in a position to、not … solely、as it were、seem to indicate、have long been done、than has been supposed。
Questions to keep in mind as you read读的时候带着这几个问题：
  1. What different problems face the historian of the future and the historian of the distant past?未来的历史学家和研究远古的历史学家，各自面临什么样的难题？
  2. When did historians once believe calendars began, and why?历史学家原来认为日历是什么时候出现的？为什么？
  3. Why are the marks on bone and ivory thought to be a calendar?刻在骨头和象牙上的点线符号，为什么被认为是日历？

📖 Understanding the piece读懂这篇

Written for the course by L. G. Alexander, but this one reports real research. The idea that Ice Age marks on bone record the moon was put forward most fully by the American researcher Alexander Marshack in the late 1960s and early 1970s, and the lesson reads like a summary of his claims as they were reported at the time.

课文为编者 L. G. 亚历山大为教材自撰，但这一篇报道的是真实的研究。「冰河时期骨头上的刻痕记录了月亮」这一观点，最完整的版本是美国研究者亚历山大·马沙克在二十世纪六十年代末、七十年代初提出的；课文读起来就像当时对他的观点的一篇摘要。

🌍 Where it comes from出处与背景

Marshack examined engraved bones under a microscope, including a small plaque from the Abri Blanchard rock shelter in France, and argued that the sets of marks were made at different times with different tools and fitted a count of the moon's phases. He published the argument in a book, The Roots of Civilization, in 1972. A famous object often brought into the debate is the Ishango bone, found in the 1950s near the Congo–Uganda border and dated to roughly twenty thousand years ago, whose groups of notches some have read as a lunar count and others as simple arithmetic. The theory was influential and is still contested: later researchers, notably Francesco d'Errico, have examined the same kinds of marks and argued that many could be explained without any calendar. The dates in the lesson also need care. The last glacial period is now usually placed from about 115,000 years ago to about 11,700 years ago, much longer than the lesson's span; the figures it gives match the later part of that period, when most of the engraved objects were made.

马沙克在显微镜下检查刻花的骨头，其中包括法国布朗夏尔岩棚出土的一块小骨牌，他认为那几组刻痕是在不同时间、用不同工具刻下的，与月相的计数吻合。1972 年他把这一论证写成书出版，书名《文明之根》。辩论中常被提到的一件名物是伊尚戈骨，二十世纪五十年代在刚果与乌干达交界附近出土，年代约为距今两万年；它上面成组的刻口，有人读作月相计数，也有人读作简单的算术。这一理论影响很大，至今仍有争议：后来的研究者，尤其是弗朗切斯科·德埃里科，检查过同类刻痕，认为其中许多不需要「日历」就能解释。课文里的年代也需要小心：末次冰期现在一般定在约十一万五千年前到约一万一千七百年前，比课文给的时间跨度长得多；课文的数字对应的是这一时期的后段，大多数刻花器物正出自那个阶段。

✨ How it is written赏析

The essay is built on a contrast that it never states outright. The first paragraph imagines the historian of the future with too much evidence, and the second turns to the historian of the past with too little; by the time the bones appear, the reader feels how much is being asked of a few scratches. The writer also moves in careful steps of certainty. Historians have assumed; evidence seems to indicate; they have found; it is possible; it seems. Only one sentence, that the marks are in fact a primitive calendar, is stated flatly, and it is the weakest link in the real debate. The closing sentence turns a technical finding into a human one — people truly trying to make sense of the seasons — and pushes the date back by a round twenty thousand years, which is the kind of figure that makes a reader remember a lesson.

这篇文章建立在一个从未明说的对比上。第一段想象未来的历史学家证据太多，第二段转向过去的历史学家证据太少；等骨头出场时，读者已经感到，人们对几道划痕的要求有多高。作者还按确定程度一级一级地移动：历史学家「曾假定」，证据「似乎表明」，他们「已发现」，「有可能」，「看来」。只有一句话——这些刻痕「其实是一种原始日历」——说得斩钉截铁，而在真实的辩论里，这恰恰是最弱的一环。结尾一句把一项技术性的发现变成了一件属于人的事——人们真的在努力了解季节——并把时间往前推了整整两万年，这种整数正是让读者记住一课的那种数字。

🧭 What it argues, and does it still hold?解读

Two parts of the lesson have aged in opposite ways. The prediction about the future has come true and then some: we leave far more video, sound and data than the lesson imagined, but the CDs and CD-ROMs it names are already hard to play, and computer scientists have warned of a digital dark age in which records in obsolete formats become unreadable. Future historians may yet face the problem of the past — not too little evidence, but evidence they cannot open. The claim about calendars needs a more careful reading than the lesson gives it. It is well established that Ice Age people made regular, deliberate marks; whether those marks counted the moon is still argued, and a modern textbook would say 'may have' where this one says 'is'. Chinese readers have a useful comparison from a much later age: the oracle bones of the Shang dynasty, over three thousand years old, already record days in a sixty-day cycle. There, the writing tells us what the marks mean; with the Ice Age bones, we have only the marks.

课文有两部分以相反的方式老去了。关于未来的预言不但应验了，而且远超预想：我们留下的视频、声音和数据比课文想象的多得多，可它点名的 CD 和 CD-ROM 如今已经很难播放，计算机科学家还警告过一个「数字黑暗时代」：存在过时格式里的记录会变得无法读取。未来的历史学家也许终究会碰上过去的难题——不是证据太少，而是证据打不开。关于日历的论断，则需要比课文更审慎地读。冰河时期的人有意刻下规则的记号，这一点已有定论；这些记号是不是在数月亮，仍有争论，今天的教科书会在这本书写「是」的地方写「可能是」。中国读者手边有一个晚得多的对照：三千多年前商代的甲骨文已经用六十日一轮的干支记日。在那里，文字告诉我们记号的意思；而面对冰河时期的骨头，我们手里只有记号本身。

💬 Discuss and write讨论与写作

    一百年后，历史学家还能读到关于你生活的哪些证据？哪些会丢失？
  2. The lesson says the marks 'are' a calendar. Rewrite its conclusion in 60 words so that it is as careful as the evidence allows.
    课文说这些刻痕「是」日历。用 60 词重写它的结论，让措辞与证据所能支持的程度相当。

④ Grammar in this lesson本课语法

as it were / so to speak: phrases dropped into a sentenceas it were / so to speak：插入语
Phrases such as as it were, so to speak, in other words and that is to say are dropped into a sentence to warn the reader that a word is used loosely or will be put another way. Commas go on both sides.
as it were（可以说）、so to speak（打个比方说）、in other words（换句话说）、that is to say（也就是说）插在句中，提醒读者某个说法带比喻性或需要换种说法理解。前后用逗号隔开。

In sentence 5 future historians will, so to speak, see and hear us.

本课：第 5 句：将来的历史学家「可以说」能看见、听见我们。

  • He became, as it were, a stranger in his own home.
  • She is, so to speak, the heart of the team.
  • The shop has closed — in other words, we have lost our jobs.
have long been done / It has long been believed that
Put long after the helping verb of a perfect tense to mean 'for a long time'. The passive is have long been done, and with the empty subject it you get It has long been known, believed or thought that ....
long 放在完成时的助动词后面，表示「长期以来」。被动式 have long been done；形式主语句 It has long been known / believed / thought that …。

Sentence 12 says these marks have puzzled historians for a long time, and sentence 18 begins with It has long been known.

本课：第 12 句说历史学家长期以来一直对这些刻痕困惑不解；第 18 句以 It has long been known 开头。

  • The castle has long been admired for its gardens.
  • It has long been believed that cats can see in the dark.
  • She has long wanted to visit Japan.
than followed straight by a verbthan + 谓语（than 作关系代词）
In a comparison, than can be followed straight by a verb with no subject: more money than is needed, earlier than had been planned, better than expected. as can be used the same way: as is often the case.
比较句里 than 后面可以不要主语，直接接 is / was / has been / expected 等：more money than is needed；earlier than had been planned；better than expected。as 也有同样用法：as is often the case。

Sentence 21 ends with than plus a passive verb and no subject.

本课：第 21 句以 than has been supposed 结尾。

  • He spent more than was necessary.
  • The work was finished sooner than had been expected.
  • The results were better than expected.

⑤ Patterns to reuse可以套用的句型

  • Future X will be uniquely placed when … 将来的 X 在……时会处于独特的地位
    Future doctors will be uniquely placed when they treat old age.
  • X need not rely solely on … X 不必只依靠……
    Students need not rely solely on textbooks.
  • Even seemingly insignificant … can shed light on … 即便看似不起眼的……也能揭示……
    Even seemingly small habits can shed light on a person's past.
  • Recent evidence seems to indicate that … 最近的证据似乎表明……
    Recent evidence seems to indicate that the river is getting cleaner.
  • It has long been understood that … 人们早就知道……
    It has long been understood that sleep helps memory.

Summary writing摘要写作

In no more than 80 words, explain what has been found out about the earliest calendars.

用不超过 80 个词说明科学家对最早的日历有了什么新发现。

Points a good summary covers好摘要要覆盖的要点
  • Historians had long assumed that calendars began with farming, which needed a knowledge of the seasons.历史学家一直以为日历随农业出现，因为农业需要了解季节。
  • New evidence suggests that this belief is wrong.新证据表明这个假定不对。
  • Ice Age nomads who hunted and fished scratched dots, lines and signs on cave walls, bones and mammoth ivory.冰河时期以渔猎为生的游牧人在洞壁、骨头和猛犸象牙上刻下了点、线和符号。
  • Comparing marks from many regions showed that they record days and the moon's phases, a simple calendar; people were studying the seasons twenty thousand years earlier than once thought.把各地刻痕对照研究后发现，它们记录日子与月相，是一种原始日历——人类试图了解季节，比原先以为的早了两万年。
Our model summary本站范文

Historians used to think that calendars appeared with farming, when people first needed to understand the seasons. Recent evidence suggests otherwise. Nomads of the last Ice Age, living by hunting and fishing, scratched dots, lines and symbols on cave walls, bones and mammoth tusks. By comparing such marks from many parts of the world, scholars have found that they record the passing days and the moon's phases. They form a primitive calendar, twenty thousand years older than was believed.（79 词）

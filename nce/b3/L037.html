📘 New Concept English · Book 3 · Lesson 37 “The Westhaven Express”新概念英语 第三册 Lesson 37《The Westhaven Express》

🎯 Before you open the lesson开课前
We have learnt to expect trains to run on time: ships may be held up by storms and flights cancelled when the weather turns, yet trains are supposed to be punctual, and only very heavy snow might upset them. So when something goes wrong we are quick to blame the railway — and yet, the writer says, the mistake is usually ours. He looks at his timetable and is pleased to find a direct express to Westhaven taking just over an hour. He does not think it odd that many local people get on, nor that the train stops at a tiny station; but as it crawls from stop to stop at thirty miles an hour, he begins to wonder. At Westhaven he complains angrily to the station master, who says there is no such train; the writer triumphantly shows him the timetable, and is told to look again. A tiny star leads to a footnote: the service has been suspended. This is a narrative that states a claim and then proves it against the writer himself. The grammar to learn is all too easy, more likely ... than, could not help doing, It dawned on someone that, not ... but ..., and There was a note of.
我们早就习惯了火车准点：轮船会因风暴延误，航班会因天气取消，火车却必须准时，只有特大的雪才会暂时打乱。所以出了错，人们总爱怪铁路当局——作者说，其实错的多半是我们自己。他查时刻表，满意地看到有一趟直达韦斯特黑文的快车，只要一小时十七分钟。上车时很多本地人也上来，他没在意；火车在小站停了，他也没在意；可车一站接一站地磨蹭，时速只有三十英里。到站后他气冲冲找站长投诉，站长说没有这趟车，他得意地指着时刻表——站长让他再看一遍：一个小星号把他引到页脚，写着「本班次已停运」。本课是先立论、后自嘲的叙事：作者用自己的糊涂证明开头的论点。学 all too easy、more likely … than、could not help doing、It dawned on sb that、not … but …、There was a note of。

📖 Understanding the piece读懂这篇

Written for the course by L. G. Alexander; Westhaven and Widley are invented. The faith in timetables the lesson mocks, though, is a real piece of social history, and the British railways did more than anyone to create it.

课文为编者 L. G. 亚历山大为教材自撰；韦斯特黑文和威德利都是编的。但课文嘲讽的「对时刻表的信仰」是一段真实的社会史，而英国铁路比谁都更有功于造就它。

🌍 Where it comes from出处与背景

Before the railways, every British town kept its own time by the sun, so that noon in Bristol came about ten minutes after noon in London. That did not matter at the speed of a horse, but it made a railway timetable almost impossible to use. In the 1840s the railway companies began running their trains by London time, and the Railway Clearing House recommended that all lines should do so; within a few years most of the country had followed. Parliament made Greenwich time the legal standard for Great Britain only in 1880. The printed timetable became a national institution in the same period. Bradshaw's railway guide, first published in 1839, gathered the timetables of the different companies into one thick book, and it was famous for being both indispensable and baffling — full of small symbols, footnotes and exceptions, which is exactly the trap the writer falls into. The habit of mind the lesson describes, trusting that a printed time is a promise, was largely built by that book.

铁路出现之前，英国每座城镇都按太阳定自己的时间，布里斯托尔的正午比伦敦晚大约十分钟。马车的速度下这无所谓，可它让铁路时刻表几乎没法用。十九世纪四十年代，各铁路公司开始按伦敦时间行车，铁路清算所也建议所有线路照此办理；没过几年，全国大部分地方都跟上了。而议会直到 1880 年才把格林尼治时间定为大不列颠的法定标准时间。印刷的时刻表也在同一时期成了一项国家制度：1839 年首次出版的《布拉德肖铁路指南》把各家公司的时刻表汇成一本厚书，它出名在于既离不开又看不懂——满是小符号、脚注和例外，而这正是作者掉进去的那种陷阱。课文所描写的那种心态——相信印出来的时间就是一个承诺——在很大程度上就是这本书造就的。

✨ How it is written赏析

The lesson is a confession dressed as an argument. The first paragraph speaks in the confident voice of 'we' and ends with a warning that the fault is usually our own; the reader nods and assumes the warning is for others. Then the 'we' narrows to 'I', and we watch the writer ignore every clue the story lays out in order: the crowd of locals, the stop at a tiny station, the slow pace, a passenger who has never heard of the train. Each time he finds a reasonable excuse, and the excuses are what make him human. The anger rises with the delay, and reaches its height at the worst possible moment, when he is sure he has proof. The final sentence is quoted from the page and set apart, so that the timetable itself has the last word. The joke works because the writer has played the railway authorities' part and the passenger's part at once, and lost both arguments.

这一课是一份打扮成议论的自白。第一段用「我们」那种自信的口吻说话，结尾警告错多半在自己；读者点头称是，以为这警告是说给别人听的。接着「我们」收窄成「我」，我们看着作者把故事依次摆出来的每条线索都忽略过去：一车本地人、在小站停车、车速缓慢、一位从没听说过这趟车的乘客。每一次他都找到一个说得过去的理由，正是这些理由让他像一个真实的人。怒气随着延误一路上涨，在最糟糕的时刻达到顶点——就是他自以为握有证据的那一刻。最后一句是从页面上原样引来的，单独成段，于是由时刻表本身说了最后一句话。这个笑话之所以成立，是因为作者同时扮演了铁路当局与乘客两方，而两场争论都输了。

🧭 What it argues, and does it still hold?解读

The lesson's moral is that mistakes are usually our own, and it is worth asking whether that is fair. In this story it is, but the railway is not entirely blameless: a service that has been suspended is still printed in the main table, and the only warning is a tiny star leading to a note at the foot of the page. Designers today would call that poor information design, and a good deal of modern thinking about safety and usability starts from the opposite rule — if many people make the same mistake, the fault is in the design, not in the people. Real life also offers less comfortable cases than the writer's. Trains are not always punctual, and in many countries passengers now check live departure boards rather than printed books, because the printed time is only a plan. The lesson still teaches something useful: before you complain with a note of triumph, read the small print. But it also shows why the small print should not have to carry the most important news.

课文的寓意是「错多半在自己」，值得问一句这是否公平。在这个故事里是公平的，但铁路也不是全无责任：一趟已停运的班次仍印在正表里，唯一的提示是一个小星号，指向页脚的一条注释。今天的设计师会说这是糟糕的信息设计；而现代关于安全与易用性的许多思考，恰恰从相反的原则出发——如果很多人都犯同一个错，错在设计，不在人。现实生活也提供了比作者遭遇更不舒服的情形：火车并不总是准点，在许多国家，乘客如今看的是实时发车屏而不是印刷的时刻表，因为印出来的时间只是一个计划。这一课仍教了点有用的东西：在你带着得意口气去投诉之前，先读一读小字。但它也说明了，最重要的消息不该由小字来承担。

④ Grammar in this lesson本课语法

could not help doing / cannot help doing: unable to stop oneselfcould not help doing / cannot help doing：禁不住
can't or couldn't help plus a gerund means that you cannot stop yourself from doing something. Do not mix it up with help to do, which means to assist. Similar are can't stop doing and can't resist doing.
can't / couldn't help + 动名词 = 忍不住、不由得做某事。不要和 help (to) do（帮助做）混淆。同义：can't stop doing、can't resist doing。

In sentence 10 he cannot avoid seeing how many local people board with him.

本课：第 10 句：上车时他不由得注意到有许多本地人也上车。

  • I couldn't help laughing when he fell off his chair.
  • She can't help worrying about her son.
  • We couldn't help overhearing your conversation.
It dawns on / occurs to / strikes someone that ...: an idea comes to someoneIt dawns on / occurs to / strikes sb that …：某人突然想到
Use the empty subject it to introduce an idea: It dawned on me that ... for understanding that comes slowly, It occurred to me that ... for a sudden thought, and It struck me that .... strike can also be used as something strikes someone as plus an adjective, for an impression.
用形式主语 it 引出「想法」：It dawned on me that …（慢慢明白过来）、It occurred to me that …（忽然想到）、It struck me that …（令我想到）。strike 还可以说 sth strikes sb as + 形容词（给人……的印象）。

Sentence 11 uses strike ... as for the impression he did not have, and sentence 16 uses dawned on for the moment he finally understood.

本课：第 11 句用 strike … as 说「当时并不觉得奇怪」；第 16 句用 dawned on 写他终于明白过来。

  • It dawned on her that she had been cheated.
  • It never occurred to me that he might be lying.
  • His story struck me as rather strange.
be more likely to ... than ...; ours and theirs
be likely to do means 'probably will'; the comparative is more or less likely to ... than .... The possessive pronouns mine, yours, his, hers, ours and theirs stand for a possessive plus a noun, so the noun need not be repeated.
be likely to do：很可能做；比较级 more / less likely to … than …。名词性物主代词 mine / yours / his / hers / ours / theirs 代替「物主代词 + 名词」，避免重复。

  • This bag isn't mine — it must be yours.

Our model summary本站范文

Finding a direct express to Westhaven in his timetable, the writer boarded it, though plenty of local people boarded too. The train stopped at every station and crawled along, and after the expected journey time it was not even halfway. When he finally arrived, he complained to the station master, who said the train did not exist. Shown the timetable, the station master told him to read it again: a footnote said the service was suspended.（76 词）

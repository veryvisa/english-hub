📘 New Concept English · Book 3 · Lesson 26 “Wanted a Large Biscuit Tin”新概念英语 第三册 Lesson 26《Wanted a Large Biscuit Tin》

🎯 Before you open the lesson开课前
Nobody escapes advertising. However good we think our own taste is, our choices are being quietly steered. Advertisers have studied human nature and have sorted our small weaknesses into classes, and they worked out long ago that every one of us likes getting something without paying for it, which is why a notice that opens with the word FREE hardly ever fails. These days the gift is not only a sample but a car, a house or a journey round the world. On a radio programme, a firm of biscuit makers once asked listeners to bake a biscuit and send it in, promising ten dollars a pound for the biggest one. The answer was overwhelming. One woman arrived with nearly five hundred pounds of biscuit on a wheelbarrow; another entry filled the whole of a car boot; a seven-hundred-and-thirteen-pound one looked certain to win. Then, just before the closing date, a lorry delivered a monster of two thousand four hundred pounds, baked by a college student out of more than a thousand pounds of flour, and a crane had to lift it down. The firm paid twenty-four thousand dollars, far more than it had reckoned on. The structure is argument first, example second, and the sting is that the advertisers, who set out to exploit human nature, are caught out by it. Learn Much as ... (inversion in a concessive clause), no longer, avoid being done, make it possible for somebody to do, enable somebody to do and more ... than ... had anticipated.
谁也逃不过广告的影响：哪怕我们自以为品味很好，广告也在悄悄左右我们的选择。广告商研究过人性、把我们的小弱点分门别类，早就发现人人都想「不花钱得东西」，所以以 FREE 开头的广告很少失手；如今送的不光是样品，还有汽车、房子、环球旅行。一家饼干厂在电台节目里请听众烤饼干寄来，最大的一块按每磅 10 美元收购。反响热烈：有位女士用独轮车推来近 500 磅的饼干，有人的饼干塞满整个后备厢，713 磅那块眼看要赢；比赛截止前，一辆卡车运来一块 2400 磅的巨型饼干，是一个大学生用 1000 多磅面粉烤的，得用起重机卸车。厂家花了 24000 美元，比预想的多得多。本课是议论引出实例，结尾的反讽是：本想利用人性占便宜的广告商，被人性将了一军。学 Much as …（让步倒装）、no longer、avoid being done、make it possible for sb to do、enable sb to do 和 more … than … had anticipated。
Questions to keep in mind as you read读的时候带着这几个问题：
  1. How does the writer say advertising works on us, and which weakness do advertisers rely on?作者认为广告怎样影响我们？广告商抓住了人性的哪一点？
  2. What competition did the biscuit firm run?饼干厂办了一个什么比赛？

🌍 Where it comes from出处与背景

The lesson's central claim — that the word costs nothing and sells everything — was tested in two thousand and seven by a group of economists and psychologists who set up a stall offering two kinds of chocolate. One was ordinary and priced at one cent; the other was a good Swiss truffle at fourteen cents. Most customers took the truffle, which was the better bargain. The researchers then cut both prices by the same single cent, so that the ordinary chocolate was free and the truffle cost thirteen. On a cost-and-benefit view nothing should have changed, since the gap between the two was identical. In fact the ordinary chocolate's share jumped from about eight per cent to about thirty-one, and the truffle's fell from about thirty to about thirteen. The team concluded that a price of nothing does not merely lower the cost of a thing; it raises the apparent value of it, and the effect weakened when people were made to think the choice through. As for the biscuit, two thousand four hundred pounds was a respectable size in its day but no longer. In May two thousand and three a company in North Carolina baked one of about forty thousand pounds, covering more than eight thousand square feet, and sold pieces of it at ten dollars each to raise money for a museum.

课文的核心判断——这个词本身不要钱，却什么都卖得动——在 2007 年被一组经济学家和心理学家检验过。他们摆了个摊，卖两种巧克力：普通的一分钱，瑞士好松露十四分。多数顾客选松露，因为那更划算。研究者接着把两种价格同样降一分：普通的变成免费，松露变十三分。按成本收益算，什么都不该变，因为两者差价没动。实际结果是：普通巧克力的份额从约 8% 跳到约 31%，松露从约 30% 掉到约 13%。研究团队的结论是：零价不只是降低了东西的成本，它还抬高了东西在人眼里的价值；而当人被要求把这个选择想清楚时，这个效应就减弱了。至于那块饼干：2400 磅在当年算体面，今天不算了。2003 年 5 月，北卡罗来纳州一家公司烤出约四万磅的一块，面积八千多平方英尺，按每块十美元出售，为一家博物馆筹款。

✨ How it is written赏析

Two things are worth stealing from this piece. The first is the way the argument is built so that the example can do the arguing. Alexander never says that the competition proves anything; he simply lays out the general principle in five sentences and then lets a lorry drive into it. By the time the crane appears, the reader has already drawn the conclusion and feels clever for having done so. The second is the escalation. Look at the order of the entries: a wheelbarrow, a car boot, seven hundred and thirteen pounds, a lorry. Each one is bigger than the last and each is measured in a different unit, so the reader keeps having to re-imagine the scale; and the figures are given precisely, which makes them funnier than round numbers would be. Then the last sentence changes direction without changing tone. There is no exclamation mark and no joke word; the firm merely pays more than it had anticipated, and the flatness of that is the whole punch. If you want a comic ending in English, this is the technique: build in threes or fours, then state the reversal in the dullest possible words.

这篇有两处值得偷。第一是议论的搭法：让例子替你议论。亚历山大从不说这场竞赛证明了什么，他只用五句话把一般原理摆好，然后放一辆卡车撞进去。等起重机出场时，读者早已自己得出结论，还为此觉得自己聪明。第二是层层加码。看参赛顺序：独轮车、汽车后备厢、713 磅、一辆卡车。每一个都比上一个大，而且每一个用不同的单位计量，读者得不断重新想象这个尺度；而数字给得很精确——精确比取整更好笑。然后最后一句话换了方向，却没换语气：没有感叹号，没有一个俏皮字眼，厂家只是「付得比预想的多」，而这份平淡就是全部的力道。要在英文里收一个喜剧结尾，技术就在这儿：三四层加码，然后用最无味的词说出那个反转。

🧭 What it argues, and does it still hold?解读

The lesson has aged better than almost anything else in this book, because the weakness it describes has been measured and the measurement agrees with it. What has changed is the shape of the free offer. In the lesson something free is a physical gift: a sample, a car, a prize. Today the commonest free things are services you use every day, and what you pay with is your attention and a record of what you do; the trial that costs nothing for a month and then renews itself, and the delivery that becomes free once your basket passes a certain sum, are the same trick wearing modern clothes. Note which way that second one runs: the shopper spends more in order to be given something worth less. The law has noticed. In the United States the trade regulator's guide on the word free requires every condition attached to such an offer to be set out clearly at the start, and the European Union puts the misuse of the word on the short list of practices banned in all circumstances. Chinese readers will recognise the free-delivery threshold immediately, and the gift that arrives with a purchase nobody needed. The useful lesson is not that we are fools; it is the finding at the end of the experiment. The pull of a zero weakens when you are forced to work the choice out on paper. Doing the arithmetic out loud is a real defence.

这一课比本册里几乎任何一课都更没过时，因为它描述的那个弱点已经被测量过，而测量结果和它一致。变的是「免费」的形态。课文里的免费是实物：样品、汽车、奖品。今天最常见的免费是你每天都在用的服务，你付的是注意力和一份自己的行为记录；免费一个月然后自动续订的试用，以及购物车超过某个金额就免掉的运费，都是同一个戏法换了现代外衣。注意后面这一种的方向：顾客为了拿到一样价值更低的东西，反而多花了钱。法律也注意到了。美国贸易监管机构关于 free 一词的指引要求：这类优惠所附的每一项条件，都必须在一开始就清楚列明；欧盟则把滥用这个词列进「在任何情况下都禁止」的短名单。中国读者对「满额包邮」一眼就懂，也熟悉随单附赠的、本来没人需要的赠品。真正有用的一课不是「我们都是傻子」，而是实验结尾的那个发现：当你被迫把这笔账在纸上算一遍，零价的拉力就变弱了。把算术大声算出来，是一道真实的防线。

④ Grammar in this lesson本课语法

Much as / Although: concessive clausesMuch as / Although：让步状语从句
Much as + subject + verb means although somebody does something very much. Other inverted concessions follow the same shape: adjective or adverb + as + subject + verb, as in Tired as he was, he kept working.
Much as + 主语 + 动词 = Although … very much（尽管很……）；类似的倒装让步还有 形容词/副词 + as + 主语 + 动词：Tired as he was, he kept working。
  • Much as I like him, I can't agree with him.
  • Young as she is, she knows a lot.（= Although she is young）
make it possible for sb to do / enable sb to do
Both patterns mean to put somebody in a position to do something: make it possible for sb to do, where it is an empty object, and enable sb to do, which takes the person directly.
两种说法意思都是「使某人能够做」：make it possible for sb to do（it 作形式宾语），enable sb to do（直接接宾语）。

In this lesson, sentence thirteen: the contests will enable us to win large sums; sentence fourteen: broadcasting has made it possible for the advertiser to capture millions of listeners at once.

本课：第 13 句那些竞赛 will enable us to win 大笔钱；第 14 句电台和电视 have made it possible for 广告商 to capture 千百万人的注意。

  • The internet has made it possible for us to work from home.
  • The money enabled him to buy a house.
avoid / deny / mind / enjoy + doing, and the passive being doneavoid / deny / mind / enjoy + doing；被动 being done
Some verbs take only the -ing form after them: avoid, enjoy, mind, deny, finish, keep, practise, suggest. When the meaning is passive, use being plus the past participle.
一些动词后只接动名词（avoid、enjoy、mind、deny、finish、keep、practise、suggest）；需要被动意义时用 being + 过去分词。
  • I avoid driving in the rush hour.
  • She hates being told what to do.
  • The children enjoyed being taken to the zoo.

⑥ Dictation and retelling听写与复述

  1. Listen to the whole recording once with the book closed. Say in one sentence what it is about.合上书完整听一遍录音，用一句话说出讲了什么。
  2. Dictate sentence by sentence: pause after each sentence and write it down. Replay a sentence at most three times.逐句听写：每句后暂停写下来，同一句最多重放三遍。
  3. Check against the text in your coursebook. Mark every mistake by type: a word you did not know, a word you knew but did not hear (linking, weak forms), or spelling.对照教材课文自查，把每个错按类型标出：不认识的词、认识却没听出的词（连读、弱读）、拼写。
  4. Words you did not hear go into your word book above. Re-listen to those sentences and shadow them.没听出的词加进上面的生词本；把那几句再听、跟读。
  5. Retell from the keywords below without looking at the text, then compare and retell once more.只看下面的关键词、不看课文复述一遍，对照后再复述一遍。

Summary writing摘要写作

In no more than eighty words, tell the story of the biscuit competition and how it ended.

用不超过 80 个词讲述饼干比赛的经过和结局。

Points a good summary covers好摘要要覆盖的要点
  • On a radio programme a biscuit firm asked listeners to bake a biscuit and send it in, and offered ten dollars a pound for the biggest.一家饼干厂在电台节目里请听众烤饼干寄来，最大的一块按每磅 10 美元买下。
  • The answer was overwhelming: one woman brought nearly five hundred pounds of biscuit on a wheelbarrow, and a seven-hundred-and-thirteen-pound one looked like the winner.反响热烈：一位女士用独轮车送来近 500 磅的饼干，713 磅的那块似乎稳赢。
  • Just before the closing date a lorry brought in a giant weighing two thousand four hundred pounds, baked by a college student, which had to be lifted down by crane.比赛截止前，一辆卡车运来一块重 2400 磅的巨型饼干，是一个大学生烤的，要用起重机才能卸下。
  • The firm bought it for twenty-four thousand dollars, far more than it had reckoned on.厂家花 24000 美元买下了这块饼干，比预想的多得多。
Our model summary本站范文

Advertisers know we love getting things free, and they invent all sorts of contests. Once a biscuit company asked radio listeners to bake biscuits and offered ten dollars a pound for the largest. Thousands arrived. One woman wheeled in a 500-pound biscuit, and a 713-pound one looked like the winner. Then, just before the deadline, a student's 2,400-pound giant came by lorry and had to be lifted off by crane. It cost the company $24,000.（75 词）

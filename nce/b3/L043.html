Lesson 43 · Fully Insured 全保险

📘 New Concept English · Book 3 · Lesson 43 “Fully Insured”新概念英语 第三册 Lesson 43《Fully Insured》

B2🧠 28 new words个新词📝 Full notes有完整讲解

🎯 Before you open the lesson开课前
Insurance companies will insure almost anything: property as a matter of course, and even bad weather at an outdoor fete, with higher premiums for bigger risks. A shipping firm claiming for the salvage of a sunken ship is nothing unusual, but a local council claiming for the salvage of a sunken pie dish must be unique. The dish, eighteen feet long and six wide, had been bought so that a giant pie could be baked for a yearly fair. The committee sent it by canal and insured it for the trip, then went off to a pub to celebrate; meanwhile some teenagers climbed aboard and held a party, and their dancing tipped the dish over into seven feet of water. A garage owner came with a recovery truck and three men dived for it. Finding it was easy; lifting it was not, because its sides were too smooth to hold chains. Once they got it onto the bank it tipped and slid back in. The second time they fixed metal clamps to its sides and hauled it up vertically; water poured off it and raised a wave in the canal, and the men raced to land the dish before the wave came back. This is a story that opens with a point about insurance and builds suspense from a series of failed attempts. The grammar to learn is in the event of, the bigger ... the higher, It is not uncommon to, must surely be, more than ... could bear, so that for purpose, and be obliged to.
保险公司几乎什么都肯保：房产是常规，露天游园会还能保「坏天气」；风险越大，保费越高。航运公司为打捞沉船索赔不稀奇，可一个地方政府为打捞一只沉了的馅饼盘索赔，恐怕绝无仅有。这只盘子长十八英尺、宽六英尺，是为一年一度的集市烤巨型馅饼买的。馅饼委员会决定走运河运送，还给它上了保险；刚下水，委员们就去酒馆庆祝，一帮少年爬上盘子开起了派对，一跳舞，盘子翻了，沉到七英尺深的水里。修车厂老板开来救援车，三个人反复潜水，找到容易，拉上来难：盘边太滑，挂不上钢缆和链子；好不容易吊上岸边，它晃了晃又滑回水里。第二次在两侧装上金属夹，竖着吊起来，水哗哗往下流，在运河里掀起大浪，他们拼命赶在浪弹回来之前把盘子拖上了岸。本课是先议论、后讲趣事的叙事，用保险的道理开头，用一连串打捞失误制造悬念。学 in the event of、the bigger … the higher、It is not uncommon to、must surely be、prove to be more than … could bear、so that（目的）、be obliged to。
Questions to keep in mind as you read读的时候带着这几个问题：
  1. Why does the writer say the council's claim must surely be unique?作者为什么说这次索赔「一定是绝无仅有的」？
  2. How did the pie dish come to sink?馅饼盘是怎么沉下去的？
  3. Why did the first attempt to raise the dish fail, and what did the men do differently the second time?第一次打捞为什么失败？第二次他们是怎么改进的？

📖 Understanding the piece读懂这篇

Written for the course by L. G. Alexander. The town and the fair are not named, but the dish is almost certainly modelled on a real one: the Yorkshire village of Denby Dale has baked giant pies for special occasions since the late eighteenth century, and the dish made for its 1964 pie is usually described with the same measurements as the lesson's.

课文为编者 L. G. 亚历山大为教材自撰。城镇和集市都没点名，但这只盘子几乎可以肯定是以一只真盘子为原型的：约克郡的丹比代尔村从十八世纪末起就在特殊场合烤巨型馅饼，而为该村 1964 年那只馅饼打造的盘子，一般记载的尺寸与课文一致。

🌍 Where it comes from出处与背景

Denby Dale's pies became a local tradition, each one marking an event — a royal occasion, the end of a war, a village anniversary — and each larger than the last, which explains why the village needed a dish the size of a small boat. The 1964 pie was baked to celebrate several royal births that year, and its dish was later kept in the village and planted with flowers. We have not found a record that it sank in a canal, so the salvage episode in the lesson may be the writer's own addition or an exaggerated local story. The insurance side of the lesson is accurate in principle. Insurers have long offered cover against unusual risks, from rain on the day of an outdoor event to injury to a performer's voice or hands, and the London market at Lloyd's is famous for taking on such policies. The rule the lesson states — a bigger risk means a higher premium — is the basic arithmetic of the trade: the premium is set by how likely a loss is and how large it would be.

丹比代尔的馅饼成了当地的传统，每一只都纪念一件事——王室庆典、战争结束、村庄周年——而且一只比一只大，这就是为什么这个村子需要一只小船那么大的盘子。1964 年那只馅饼是为庆祝当年王室几位新生儿诞生而烤的，那只盘子后来留在村里，种上了花。我们没找到它在运河里沉没的记录，所以课文里的打捞情节可能是作者自己加的，或是一个被夸大了的地方故事。课文里关于保险的部分在原理上是准确的。保险公司很早就为不寻常的风险提供保障，从户外活动当天下雨到演员的嗓子或双手受伤都有；伦敦劳合社的市场就以承保这类保单出名。课文说的那条规则——风险越大，保费越高——是这一行最基本的算术：保费由损失发生的可能性和损失的大小共同决定。

✨ How it is written赏析

The lesson begins like a dry article about insurance and turns into slapstick, and the join is a single word: unique. Having set up the respectable world of property, premiums and sunken ships, the writer drops a pie dish into it, and the contrast between the solemn vocabulary — local authority, committee, claim, salvage — and the absurd object is the source of the comedy. The story then follows the shape of every good rescue: easy at first, then a setback, then a second try with a better method, then a last-minute danger. The near success on the bank is the best moment, and the writer slows it down with a long phrase of suspense before the dish slides back. The final threat, a wave bouncing off the far bank, is almost scientific, and the men beat it by speed rather than skill. Notice that nobody is blamed: the committee is in the pub, the teenagers are shivering, and the insurer, we assume, pays.

这一课开头像一篇枯燥的保险文章，后来变成了一出滑稽戏，连接两者的只有一个词：「绝无仅有」。作者先搭起一个体面的世界——房产、保费、沉船——然后把一只馅饼盘扔了进去；那些庄重的词汇——地方当局、委员会、索赔、打捞——与这件荒唐物件之间的反差，就是喜剧的来源。接着故事沿着每一次好的营救的形状走：起初容易，然后受挫，然后用更好的办法再试一次，最后是千钧一发的危险。在岸边差一点成功是最精彩的一刻，作者用一个长长的悬念短语把它放慢，然后才让盘子滑回水里。最后的威胁——一道从对岸弹回来的浪——几乎带点科学味道，而那几个人靠的是速度而不是技巧战胜了它。注意，故事里没有人被责怪：委员会在酒馆，少年们在发抖，至于保险公司，我们猜，会照付。

🧭 What it argues, and does it still hold?解读

The lesson treats insurance as a joke background, and the joke depends on something worth noticing: the committee was right to insure the dish. They could not have known that teenagers would dance on it, but they knew that moving a large, awkward object by water was risky, and they bought cover for exactly that trip. That is what insurance is for — not the risks you expect, but the ones you cannot predict in detail. A modern reader might ask a sharper question: would the insurer actually pay? A policy covers what it says, and many policies exclude damage caused while property is left unattended or used for something other than its purpose; a dish used as a dance floor might well fall outside the terms. So the real lesson of the story is the one the writer does not draw. Buying a policy is the first step; knowing what it does and does not cover is the second, and it is usually the one people skip.

课文把保险当成一个玩笑背景，而这个玩笑依赖于一件值得注意的事：委员会给盘子上保险是对的。他们不可能知道会有少年在上面跳舞，但他们知道用水路运一个又大又笨的东西有风险，于是恰好为那一趟行程买了保障。这正是保险的用途——不是为你预料到的风险，而是为你无法具体预测的风险。今天的读者可能会问一个更尖锐的问题：保险公司真的会赔吗？一份保单只保它写明的内容，许多保单不保财物在无人看管时、或被挪作他用时受到的损失；一只被当成舞池的盘子，很可能就落在条款之外。所以这个故事真正的教训，是作者没有点出的那一个：买保险是第一步；弄清它保什么、不保什么是第二步——而人们通常跳过的，恰恰是这第二步。

  2. Write 100 words about an attempt to fix something that went wrong twice before it went right.

④ Grammar in this lesson本课语法

the + comparative ..., the + comparative ...: the more ..., the more ...the + 比较级 …, the + 比较级 …：越……越……
Each half of the sentence begins with the plus a comparative, showing that as one thing changes, the other changes with it. A noun can follow the comparative, as in the bigger the risk. In speech the verbs are often left out: The sooner, the better.
两个分句各以 the + 比较级开头，表示一方变化时另一方随之变化。比较级后面可接名词（the bigger the risk）。口语里常省略动词：The sooner, the better。

本课：第 4 句：风险越大，保费越高。

  • The more you read, the more you learn.
  • The older he gets, the wiser he becomes.
  • The sooner, the better.
so that / in order that with can, could, will or would: purposeso that / in order that + can / could / will / would：目的
so that introduces a clause of purpose, usually with a modal: can, will or may after a present or future main clause, and could, would or might after a past one. Do not confuse it with so ... that, which shows result.
so that 引导目的状语从句，从句里常用情态动词：主句现在/将来时用 can / will / may，过去时用 could / would / might。不要和 so … that（结果）混淆。

Sentence 8 gives the reason the dish was bought, and sentence 23 the reason for the clamps, both with so that and could.

本课：第 8 句用 so that … could be baked 说买盘子的目的；第 23 句也用 so that 加 could 说装夹子的目的。

  • I'll leave the key under the mat so that you can get in.
  • She spoke slowly so that everyone could understand.
  • We left early so that we would not miss the train.
It is not uncommon to do: a soft double negativeIt is not uncommon to do；双重否定的委婉
not plus a negative word such as uncommon, unusual, impossible or without makes a positive meaning, but a softer and more cautious one: not uncommon is roughly fairly common, and not impossible means possible.
not + 否定词（uncommon、unusual、impossible、without）构成双重否定，意思是肯定，但语气更委婉、更有保留：not uncommon ≈ fairly common；not impossible ≈ possible。

⑥ Dictation and retelling听写与复述

  1. Listen to the whole recording once with the book closed. Say in one sentence what it is about.合上书完整听一遍录音，用一句话说出讲了什么。
  2. Dictate sentence by sentence: pause after each sentence and write it down. Replay a sentence at most three times.逐句听写：每句后暂停写下来，同一句最多重放三遍。
  3. Check against the text in your coursebook. Mark every mistake by type: a word you did not know, a word you knew but did not hear (linking, weak forms), or spelling.对照教材课文自查，把每个错按类型标出：不认识的词、认识却没听出的词（连读、弱读）、拼写。
  4. Words you did not hear go into your word book above. Re-listen to those sentences and shadow them.没听出的词加进上面的生词本；把那几句再听、跟读。

Summary writing摘要写作

In no more than 80 words, tell how the pie dish sank and how it was raised.

用不超过 80 个词讲述馅饼盘是怎样沉下去又是怎样被打捞上来的。

Points a good summary covers好摘要要覆盖的要点
  • A council bought a huge pie dish for its yearly fair, insured it and sent it by canal.一个地方政府为年度集市买了一只巨大的馅饼盘，投保后经运河运送。
  • While the committee celebrated in a pub, teenagers danced on the dish and tipped it into the water.委员们去酒馆庆祝时，一群少年在盘子上开派对跳舞，把它弄翻沉入水中。
  • Rescuers soon found it, but its smooth sides made it hard to lift, and the first time it slid back off the bank.救援人员很快找到了盘子，但盘边太滑，第一次吊到岸边又滑回水里。
  • The second time they fixed metal clamps to both sides, lifted it upright and got it ashore before the wave came back.第二次他们在两侧装上金属夹，把它竖着吊出水面，赶在大浪弹回之前拖上了岸。
Our model summary本站范文

A council bought an enormous pie dish for its yearly fair and insured it for a canal journey. While the committee celebrated in a pub, teenagers held a party on it, and their dancing overturned it. Divers found the dish easily, but its smooth sides made it hard to raise, and the first time it slipped back into the canal. Finally they fixed clamps to both sides, lifted it upright and pulled it ashore before the wave it made returned.（80 词）

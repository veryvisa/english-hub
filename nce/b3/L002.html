Lesson 2 · Thirteen Equals One 十三等于一

📘 New Concept English · Book 3 · Lesson 2 “Thirteen Equals One”新概念英语 第三册 Lesson 2《Thirteen Equals One》

B2🧠 19 new words个新词📝 Full notes有完整讲解

🎯 Before you open the lesson开课前
A light village story: the church clock has been out of order for years; one night the vicar is woken by it and counts thirteen strokes at one o'clock. He climbs the tower and finds the grocer, Bill, who has been secretly mending the bell as a surprise. The vicar's closing line — thirteen is worse than one, but still better than none — is both funny and generous. The telling is close to speech, which makes it the right place to learn the tone of direct speech (Whatever are you doing…?, You certainly did give me…) and participle phrases used as adverbials (Looking at his watch…, Armed with a torch…).
一则轻松的小故事：村里的钟坏了多年，牧师半夜被钟声惊醒，一点钟却敲了十三下；上钟楼一看，原来是杂货店老板比尔偷偷来修，想给牧师一个惊喜。结尾牧师一句「十三虽然不如一，总比没有好」，幽默又宽厚。叙事语言很口语，正好学直接引语里的语气（Whatever are you doing…?, You certainly did give me…）和分词作状语（Looking at his watch…, Armed with a torch…）。
Questions to keep in mind as you read读的时候带着这几个问题：
  1. Why had the church clock been silent for so many years?教堂的钟为什么多年没有响？
  2. Whom did the vicar find up in the bell tower that night, and what was he busy with?牧师半夜上钟楼看到了谁？他在做什么？
  3. What was still wrong with the clock after it was mended, and how did the vicar take it?钟修好后有什么问题？牧师怎么回应？

🌍 Where it comes from出处与背景

In an English parish the church clock belongs to the parish, and keeping it going is paid for out of collections and village fundraising rather than by the state. That is why the man in this story spends his life raising money and still never has enough for the one repair he wants. The thirteenth stroke is not pure invention either. London has an old tale about one, and its earliest printed trace is an obituary from 1770: a soldier in the reign of William and Mary was court-martialled for sleeping at his post on the terrace at Windsor, and swore in his defence that he had heard the clock of the cathedral in the City strike thirteen at midnight. The court thought that impossible, Windsor being some twenty-four miles away, but people living nearer town swore the clock had indeed struck thirteen that night, and the man was pardoned. The story rests on that obituary and on later retellings; no court record has been found, so it belongs to legend rather than to history.

在英格兰，教堂的钟属于教区，维修钱靠捐款和村里的集市义卖，不是政府出。所以故事里那位牧师一辈子在筹钱，偏偏凑不出修钟这一笔。「钟敲十三下」也不是凭空编的。伦敦有个老传说，目前能查到最早的书面痕迹是 1770 年的一则讣告：威廉与玛丽在位时，一名士兵因在温莎的平台上值勤睡觉被送上军事法庭，他辩称自己听见城里那座大教堂的钟在午夜敲了十三下。法庭认为不可能——温莎离伦敦有二十四英里左右——但住得近些的人出具证词，说那一夜那座钟确实敲了十三下，他因此获赦。这件事只靠那则讣告和后世转述流传，没有找到法庭记录，所以只能当传说，不能当史实。

✨ How it is written赏析

Notice how little the writer explains. We are never told that the parish is poor, that the village is small, or that Bill is a kind man; all of that arrives as plain fact — money raised for one cause after another, a bell that has not sounded for years, a grocer who has climbed the tower in the dark for weeks. Then the whole weight of the piece is handed to four short speeches, and each does a different job: one is startled, one explains, one teases, one accepts. The funniest line is also the most generous one, it comes last, and it is the shortest in the lesson. If you want to write a comic anecdote in English, copy this shape. Lay the facts out flat and in order. Bring the voices in at the end. Build the joke out of acceptance rather than mockery. And spend no sentence at all telling the reader how to feel.

留意作者解释得多么少。他从没说教区穷、村子小、比尔心好；这些全是靠事实自己站出来的——为这样那样的事业筹款、一口多年不响的钟、一个连着几星期半夜爬钟楼的杂货商。然后全篇的重量交给四句简短的对话，每句干不同的活：一句吃惊，一句解释，一句打趣，一句接受。最好笑的那句同时也是最宽厚的一句，它放在最后，而且是全课最短的。想用英文写一则幽默小品，照这个形状来：先把事实平铺直叙地摆好；人声留到结尾；笑点落在「接受」而不是「取笑」上；一句话也不要花在告诉读者该有什么感受。

🧭 What it argues, and does it still hold?解读

The joke leans on something that has quietly gone away. A striking clock was once public property in the strongest sense: it told the whole parish the hour, and anybody within earshot could only be wrong about the time if the bell was wrong. That is why one stroke too many was worth a story at all, and why the old Windsor tale could hang on what a clock had sounded at midnight. Today hardly anyone takes the time from a bell, so a clock that strikes thirteen is a curiosity rather than a problem, and the sting goes out of the humour. What survives is the vicar's rule for anything made by a volunteer: thirteen is worse than one, and better than none. That is worth holding on to in a world where a flawed gift can be sent back with two taps on a screen, and where the man who spent weeks in a cold tower gets no hearing at all.

这个笑话靠的是一件已经悄悄消失的事。报时的钟曾经是最彻底意义上的公共财产：它告诉整个教区现在几点，听得见钟声的人要弄错时间，除非钟本身错了。正因为这样，多敲一下才值得讲成一个故事，那则温莎传说也才能全靠「钟在午夜敲了几下」立住。今天几乎没人靠钟声看时间，于是「敲十三下」只是件稀罕事，不再是问题，幽默的锋利处也就钝了。留下来的是牧师对待一切义务劳动成果的那条规矩：十三不如一，但好过没有。在一个「不满意的礼物两下点击就能退回」的世界里，这条规矩值得留着——毕竟在冷风里爬了几星期钟楼的那个人，是连说话的机会都没有的。

③ How the hard sentences are built难句结构

Sentences are counted in the lesson text, title not included. Find the sentence in your book, then read our notes on its structure.句子按课文正文数（不含标题）。在教材里找到这一句，再看本站的结构讲解。

Sentence 2第 2 句 (begins开头 “but he has …”)

  • has never managed: present perfect, so up to this moment he still has not succeeded.has never managed：现在完成时，到现在为止一直没做成。
  • have + object + past participle: arrange for someone else to do the work for you, not do it yourself (Book 4 Lesson 15).have + 宾语 + 过去分词：请人把钟修好（不是自己修，见第四册第 15 课）。
  • to have ...: an infinitive of purpose, saying what the money would be for.to have…：不定式表目的，修饰 money。

Sentence 3第 3 句 (begins开头 “The big clock …”)

  • used to do: a regular habit or state in the past that no longer holds.used to do：过去常常（现在不了）。
  • The two halves carry different tenses on purpose: the past simple pins the damage to a stated time in the past, while the present perfect carries the silence right up to the present.was damaged（一般过去时，有具体时间 many years ago）与 has been silent ever since（现在完成时，从那以后一直）并列，时态各有道理。

Sentence 5第 5 句 (begins开头 “Looking at his …”)

Sentence 7第 7 句 (begins开头 “Armed with a …”)

  • armed with: a past participle phrase saying what someone is carrying as they go.armed with：过去分词短语作伴随状语（带着）。

Sentence 8第 8 句 (begins开头 “In the torchlight, …”)

  • whom opens a relative clause and is the object of the verb inside it; in speech people use who or leave it out altogether.whom 引导定语从句，作 recognized 的宾语（口语可用 who 或省略）。
  • our local grocer: an appositive, a second noun phrase naming the same man.our local grocer：同位语。

Sentence 13第 13 句 (begins开头 “'You certainly did …”)

④ Grammar in this lesson本课语法

Present perfect against past simple现在完成时 vs 一般过去时
Use the past simple when a definite past time is named (ago, yesterday, in 2010). Use the present perfect for experience up to now, or for a state that still holds (since, ever since, never, for weeks now).
有明确过去时间（ago, yesterday, in 2010）用一般过去时；强调到现在为止的经历或持续状态用现在完成时（since, ever since, never, for weeks now）。
  • I lost my key yesterday. / I have lost my key.（现在还没找到）
  • She has lived here since 2010.
Participle phrases used as adverbials (an introduction)现在分词与过去分词作状语（入门）
When a sentence opens with a participle phrase, the subject of the participle is the subject of the main clause: use doing for an active meaning, done for a passive meaning or a state (Book 4 Lessons 3 and 9 take this further).
分词短语开头的句子，分词的逻辑主语就是主句主语：主动用 doing，被动或状态用 done（第四册第 3、9 课有进阶）。
  • Hearing the news, she burst into tears.
  • Tired after the long walk, we went to bed early.
Question word + ever: Whatever / However / Whoever for surprise疑问词 + ever：Whatever / However / Whoever 表惊讶
In a question, adding ever to what, how, who, where or why shows surprise, impatience or alarm, close to 'what on earth are you doing?'. It can be written as one word (whatever) or two (what ever).
疑问句里 what/how/who/where/why 后加 ever，表示惊讶、着急或不耐烦：「你到底在干什么？」，ever 可连写（whatever）或分写（what ever）。

Do not mix this up with the whatever that means 'no matter what' and opens a clause of concession (Book 4 Lesson 18).

注意：这与引导让步从句的 whatever（无论什么）用法不同（第四册第 18 课）。

  • However did you manage to find me?
  • Wherever have you been?

⑤ Patterns to reuse可以套用的句型

  • X is always … for one cause or another X 总在为这样那样的事业……
    She is always collecting clothes for one charity or another.
  • … was damaged … ago and has been … ever since ……前被损坏，从那以后一直……
    The bridge was damaged ten years ago and has been closed ever since.
  • X woke up with a start: … X 猛然惊醒：……
    I woke up with a start: someone was knocking at the door.
  • A is not as good as B, but it's better than nothing. A 虽然不如 B，但总比没有好
    A small flat is not as good as a house, but it's better than nothing.

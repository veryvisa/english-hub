Lesson 9 · A Cold Welcome 冷遇

📘 New Concept English · Book 2 · Lesson 9 “A Cold Welcome”新概念英语 第二册 Lesson 9《A Cold Welcome》

B1🧠 15 new words个新词📝 Full notes有完整讲解

🎯 Before you open the lesson开课前
On a Wednesday evening, the final day of the year, a big crowd stands beneath the Town Hall clock to hear it strike midnight. Five minutes before the hour it stops, and the minute hand stays still. People wait and wait; then somebody calls out that it is already two minutes into the new year, and a glance at a watch confirms it. The clock has declined to greet the New Year, and the crowd just laughs and starts singing. The grammar is telling the time in the British way, with past and to, and the prepositions of time on, at and in.
一年的最后一天，星期三晚上，一大群人聚在市政厅的大钟下面等着它敲响十二点。还差五分十二点时，钟停了，分针一动不动。大家等了又等，什么也没发生；突然有人喊：已经十二点过两分了！作者一看表，果然。大钟拒绝迎接新年——这时大家都笑了起来，唱起了歌。本课学钟点的说法（five to twelve、two minutes past twelve）和时间介词 on / at / in。

本课由编者 L. G. 亚历山大为《新概念英语》（朗文，1967）自撰；没写是哪座城镇，场景是虚构的。

🌍 Where it comes from出处与背景

In Britain and many other countries, people like to see in the New Year in a public place, often under a well-known clock or in a main square, and the striking of midnight is the signal to cheer, kiss and sing. In Britain the usual song is Auld Lang Syne, sung in a circle with crossed arms. Big public clocks were wound and checked by hand, and cold weather could slow or stop them. A real case is often told about the most famous clock in London: on the last night of 1962, snow and ice on the hands are said to have slowed it, so that it rang in the new year some minutes late. We have not checked this against a primary record, but it shows that the lesson's scene was not far-fetched.

在英国和许多国家，人们喜欢在公共场所迎接新年，常常聚在某座有名的大钟下或主广场上，午夜钟声一响，就欢呼、亲吻、唱歌。英国通常唱的是《友谊地久天长》，大家手臂交叉围成一圈。大型公共时钟当年要靠人工上弦、校准，天冷时可能变慢甚至停摆。关于伦敦最有名的那座钟，常有人讲一件真事：1962 年的最后一夜，指针上的冰雪据说让它变慢，结果晚了几分钟才敲响新年。我们没有拿一手记录核对过，但它说明课文的场景并不离谱。

✨ How it is written赏析

The story is about waiting, and the writing makes the reader wait as well. It gives a series of times, twenty minutes to go, then fifteen minutes passing, then five to twelve, and then it slows right down: a hand that does not move, a repeated verb, a short line saying that nothing happened. Then a shout breaks the silence, and a quick check confirms it. The title is a play on words. A cold welcome usually means an unfriendly greeting from a person, but here it is the clock that gives it, on a winter night when it was literally cold. The last sentence turns the disappointment into a party, so the reader ends with a smile rather than a sigh.

这个故事讲的是等待，而写法也让读者跟着等。它给出一串时间——还差二十分钟、过去十五分钟、差五分十二点——然后一下子放慢：一根不动的指针、一个重复的动词、一句短短的「什么也没发生」。接着一声喊叫打破沉默，一看表，证实了。标题是个双关：「冷遇」通常指一个人不友好的接待，可这里给冷遇的是钟，而且是在一个真正冷的冬夜。最后一句把失望变成了狂欢，读者读完是一笑，而不是一叹。

🧭 What it argues, and does it still hold?解读

Today almost nobody needs a public clock to know the time, because every phone shows it to the second. Yet crowds still gather at midnight under famous clocks and towers, which shows that the point was never the information. It was the shared moment. The lesson's ending makes the same point: once the clock fails, the crowd does not go home; it makes its own celebration. Chinese readers will recognise the feeling from Spring Festival, when the family counts down together in front of the television, and from city squares where people wait for the New Year bell. The ritual matters more than the machine.

如今几乎没人需要靠公共时钟看时间，手机能精确到秒。可每到午夜，人群仍然聚在著名的大钟和塔楼下，说明重点从来不是「知道几点」，而是大家一起度过的那一刻。课文的结尾讲的也是这一点：钟坏了，人群并没散，而是自己开起了庆祝会。中国读者在春节一定有同感：一家人对着电视一起倒数，城市广场上人们等着新年钟声。仪式本身比那台机器更重要。

④ Grammar in this lesson本课语法

Telling the time: past and to钟点的说法：past 与 to
In British English, minutes after the hour use past, as in ten past six, and minutes before it use to, as in five to twelve. Use o'clock for the exact hour, half past for thirty minutes, and a quarter past or to for fifteen. You can also simply read the numbers, as in eleven fifty-five.
英式说法：过了几分用 past（ten past six = 6:10），差几分用 to（five to twelve = 11:55）；整点用 o'clock，半点用 half past，一刻用 a quarter past / to。也可直接读数字：eleven fifty-five。

Sentence 6 has five to twelve, and sentence 11 has two minutes past twelve.

本课：第 6 句 five to twelve；第 11 句 two minutes past twelve。

  • The train leaves at a quarter to nine (8:45).
  • We had lunch at half past one (1:30).
Prepositions of time: on, at and in时间介词 on、at、in
Use on for a particular day, at for a clock time or a point in time, and in for months, years, seasons and longer periods. in with a length of time can also mean after that much time.
具体某天用 on（on Wednesday、on New Year's Day），钟点和时刻用 at（at five to twelve、at that moment），月、年、季节和较长时段用 in（in May、in 2020）；in + 一段时间 也可表示「……之后」（in twenty minutes' time）。

Sentence 1 has on with a day, sentence 6 and 16 have at with a time, and sentence 4 has in for a time still to come.

本课：第 1 句 On Wednesday evening；第 6 句 at five to twelve；第 16 句 At that moment；第 4 句 in twenty minutes' time。

  • I was born in 1998, on the fifth of May, at six in the morning.
  • The film starts in ten minutes.

⑤ Patterns to reuse可以套用的句型

  • a big crowd had gathered … 一大群人已聚集在……
    A big crowd of fans had gathered outside the stadium.
  • It would … in twenty minutes' time. 再过二十分钟就要……
    The bus would leave in ten minutes' time.
  • X and X, but nothing … 一再……，却毫无结果
    We knocked and knocked, but nobody answered.
  • At that moment, all of us began to … 就在那时，大家都开始……

On New Year's Eve we joined a big crowd under the Town Hall clock to hear it strike midnight. But at five to twelve it stopped. We waited until someone shouted that it was already after twelve. It was true, and everyone started laughing and singing.（46 词）

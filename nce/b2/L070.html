Lesson 70 · Red for Danger 红与黑

📘 New Concept English · Book 2 · Lesson 70 “Red for Danger”新概念英语 第二册 Lesson 70《Red for Danger》

B1🧠 18 new words个新词📝 Full notes有完整讲解

🎯 Before you open the lesson开课前
In the middle of a bullfight, a drunk man stumbled out into the centre of the arena. People yelled, but he had no idea of the danger. The bull, busy with the matador, suddenly noticed this newcomer, who was calling out insults and flapping a red cap. As if hurt by the remarks, it left the matador and rushed at the man. A hush fell. The drunk, however, looked perfectly calm, and at the last moment he stepped awkwardly out of the way. The spectators cheered and he took a bow, but three men had already run in and pulled him clear. Even the bull seemed to pity him, watching kindly until he had gone before going back to the matador. This is a scene told as if the animal had feelings. The grammar is adjective phrases used as adverbials, catch sight of, break into and turn one's attention to.
斗牛进行中，一个醉汉突然晃进了斗牛场中央。观众大喊，他却全然不知危险。公牛本来在对付斗牛士，忽然看见这个醉汉一边嚷着粗话、一边挥着一顶红帽子，它好像受不了批评，把斗牛士忘得一干二净，朝醉汉冲去。全场一下子安静了。醉汉倒是很有把握，等牛冲到跟前，笨手笨脚地往旁边一闪，让它冲了过去。观众欢呼起来，醉汉还鞠了一躬。这时三个人已经进场，飞快地把他拖到安全的地方。连公牛似乎都同情他，一直同情地看着，等醉汉离开了，才又把注意力转回斗牛士身上。本课是一段拟人化的场面描写，学形容词短语作状语、catch sight of、break into 和 turn one's attention to。

📖 Understanding the piece读懂这篇

Unsigned and written for the course; no particular bullfight is named, and the scene reads like a traveller's tale rather than a news report.

本课未署名、为教材自撰，没有指明是哪一场斗牛；读来更像旅行见闻，而不是新闻报道。

🌍 Where it comes from出处与背景

The title borrows the name of a famous French novel by Stendhal, though the story has nothing to do with it; here the red is the drunk's cap and the black is the bull. One detail is a well-known myth: bulls are believed to be largely colour-blind to red, and they charge at the movement of the cloth, not its colour. The traditional red cape is used partly because it hides bloodstains. Bullfighting remains legal in Spain and some other countries, but it is controversial. The Canary Islands banned it in 1991, Catalonia passed a ban that was later overturned by the Spanish constitutional court, and polls in Spain show that many younger people now oppose it.

标题借用了司汤达一部法国名著的书名，故事却和它毫不相干：这里的红是醉汉的帽子，黑是那头公牛。有个细节是著名的误解：一般认为牛基本分辨不出红色，它冲的是布的晃动，而不是颜色；传统上用红布，部分原因是能遮住血迹。斗牛在西班牙和另外几个国家仍然合法，但争议很大。加那利群岛 1991 年禁止斗牛，加泰罗尼亚通过的禁令后来被西班牙宪法法院推翻，西班牙的民调显示，如今很多年轻人反对斗牛。

✨ How it is written赏析

The whole joke rests on giving the bull human feelings. The writer calls it apparently sensitive to criticism, as if the rude remarks hurt its pride, and at the end it watches sympathetically and waits politely before going back to work. Against this gentle bull the drunk looks foolish and the crowd looks fickle, shouting, falling silent, cheering. The rhythm of the crowd's reactions moves the story along: shout, silence, cheers. The word however appears twice, each time just before the story turns, first at the drunk's calm, then at the rescue.

整个笑话建立在赋予公牛人的感情上。作者说它「显然受不了批评」，仿佛那些粗话伤了它的自尊；结尾它又同情地看着，礼貌地等着，然后才回去干活。和这头温和的牛一比，醉汉显得可笑，观众显得善变：喊叫、沉默、欢呼。观众的反应节奏推动着故事：喊、静、欢呼。however 出现了两次，每次都在故事转折之前：一次在醉汉的镇定，一次在救援。

🧭 What it argues, and does it still hold?解读

The lesson is light and amusing, but today many readers will notice what it leaves out: in a real bullfight the bull is usually killed. The kind, patient bull of the story is a fantasy that makes it easier to enjoy the scene. Reading it now, it is fair to ask whether we would laugh at the same story set in a zoo. Chinese readers may know the bull-fighting festivals of some southern regions, where two bulls push against each other and are rarely killed, which shows that a tradition can keep its excitement and lose its cruelty. The story can still be enjoyed as comedy while we think more carefully about the custom it describes.

这一课轻松好笑，但今天很多读者会注意到它没说的事：真正的斗牛里，牛通常会被杀死。故事里那头善良、耐心的牛是一种想象，让人更容易享受这个场面。现在读来，不妨问一问：如果同一个故事发生在动物园，我们还会笑吗？中国读者可能知道南方一些地方的斗牛节，是两头牛互相顶，很少致死，这说明一种传统可以保留刺激而去掉残忍。我们仍可以把这个故事当喜剧来欣赏，同时更认真地想一想它描写的这种习俗。

③ How the hard sentences are built难句结构

Sentences are counted in the lesson text, title not included. Find the sentence in your book, then read our notes on its structure.句子按课文正文数（不含标题）。在教材里找到这一句，再看本站的结构讲解。

Sentence 1第 1 句 (begins开头 “During a bullfight, …”)

  • wander into: walk in without any plan.wander into：漫无目的地走进。
  • a drunk: a noun here, meaning a drunk man.a drunk：名词，醉汉。

Sentence 4第 4 句 (begins开头 “but it suddenly …”)

  • be busy with: be occupied with.be busy with sb/sth：忙于对付……。
  • catch sight of: suddenly see.catch sight of：瞥见。
  • A relative clause with two -ing verbs sharing one was.who was shouting … and waving …：定语从句里两个进行时动词并列。

Sentence 5第 5 句 (begins开头 “Apparently sensitive to …”)

  • An adjective phrase at the front gives a reason; its subject is the bull. apparently: so it seemed.形容词短语作状语，说明原因，逻辑主语是 the bull。apparently：看起来。
  • forget all about: forget completely. charge at: rush at to attack.forget all about：完全忘了。charge at：冲向。

Sentence 9第 9 句 (begins开头 “When the bull …”)

  • to let it pass: an infinitive of purpose; let is followed by the base form without to.to let it pass：不定式表目的。let sb do，不带 to。

Sentence 10第 10 句 (begins开头 “The crowd broke …”)

  • break into: suddenly start cheering, singing or running.break into：突然开始（欢呼、歌唱、奔跑等）。

Sentence 13第 13 句 (begins开头 “for it looked …”)

  • look on: watch without taking part. until: up to the time when.look on：旁观。until：直到……才。

④ Grammar in this lesson本课语法

Adjective phrases used as adverbials形容词（短语）作状语
An adjective phrase can stand at the front or end of a sentence to describe the state of the subject or give a reason. Its subject must be the subject of the main clause, as in Sure that ..., Tired and hungry, ...
形容词或形容词短语放在句首或句末，说明主语的状态或原因，逻辑主语必须是句子的主语。常见：Sure that …、Apparently sensitive to …、Tired and hungry, …。

Sentence 5 uses an adjective phrase to explain why the bull charged, as if describing a person.

本课：第 5 句用形容词短语说明公牛为什么冲向醉汉，像在写一个人。

  • Tired and hungry, we sat down by the road.
  • Unable to sleep, she read until dawn.
Phrases for sudden starts and changes of attention表示「突然开始」与「转向」的短语
break into plus a noun or -ing: suddenly start, as in break into a run or cheers; burst into plus a noun: suddenly start crying, laughing or burning; turn one's attention to: start to deal with something else.
break into + 名词/-ing：突然开始（break into a run / song / cheers / laughter）；burst into + 名词：突然（大哭、大笑、起火）；turn one's attention to：把注意力转向。

Sentence 10 has the crowd's sudden cheering, and the last sentence has the bull turning back to its work.

本课：第 10 句观众突然欢呼；最后一句公牛把注意力转回斗牛士。

  • When the bus came, she broke into a run.
  • After lunch, let's turn our attention to the budget.

⑤ Patterns to reuse可以套用的句型

  • During a …, a … suddenly wandered into … ……期间，一个……突然晃进了……
    During a concert, a dog suddenly wandered onto the stage.
  • X was busy with … at the time, but suddenly caught sight of … X 当时正忙着……，突然瞥见……
    The cook was busy with the soup at the time, but suddenly caught sight of a mouse.
  • Apparently sensitive to …, X forgot all about … 似乎对……很敏感，X 完全忘了……
    Apparently sensitive to the noise, the baby forgot all about her milk.
  • The crowd broke into … and X … 观众突然……，X 则……
    The crowd broke into laughter and the singer blushed.
  • X waited … until … before turning back to … X 一直……地等着，直到……，才又转回……
    The teacher waited patiently until the noise stopped before turning back to the lesson.

Our model summary本站范文

During a bullfight, a drunk man stumbled into the arena. The bull saw him shouting insults and waving a red cap, so it left the matador and rushed at him. The crowd went silent, but the man calmly stepped aside at the last moment. As people cheered and he bowed, three men ran in and pulled him to safety. The bull watched kindly until he had gone, then went back to the matador.（73 词）

Lesson 96 · The Dead Return 亡灵返乡

📘 New Concept English · Book 2 · Lesson 96 “The Dead Return”新概念英语 第二册 Lesson 96《The Dead Return》

B1🧠 19 new words个新词📝 Full notes有完整讲解

🎯 Before you open the lesson开课前
Once a year Japan holds a festival for those who have died. It is a happy time, because the dead are believed to come home and the living welcome them. Since they must be hungry after travelling so far, families put out food, and paper lights hang by every door to show them the way. People dance and sing through the night. At dawn the food goes into a river or the sea, since eating it would bring bad luck. In towns on the coast the small lights from the streets are set on the water, and thousands float away, leading the dead back. People on the beach watch until the last one is gone. This is an account of a custom told in time order. The grammar is the passive with is held, are said to, are expected to and is considered, the past perfect passive, and -ing phrases after watch.
日本每年都有一次亡灵节。这是个欢快的日子：据说这一天亡灵回家，活着的人出来迎接。亡灵走了很远的路，想必饿了，家家摆上食物；屋外挂起特制的灯笼，给亡灵照路。人们通宵跳舞唱歌。清晨，摆给亡灵的食物被扔进河里或海里，因为活人吃了不吉利。海边的城镇在节日结束时，把前一晚挂在街上的小灯笼放进水里，成千上万盏灯笼慢慢漂向大海，引着亡灵回到另一个世界。岸上的人群一直望着，直到再也看不见。本课是一篇按时间顺序写的风俗说明，学被动语态（is held、are said to、are expected to、is considered）、过去完成时被动和 watching … drifting 这类分词结构。
Questions to keep in mind as you read读的时候带着这几个问题：
  1. What is done with the lanterns when the festival ends?节日最后，灯笼怎么样了？
  2. Why is the festival a happy one?为什么说这个节日是欢快的？

📖 Understanding the piece读懂这篇

Unsigned and written for the course as a short account of a custom; it does not name the festival, which is Obon, held in Japan in summer.

本课未署名，是为教材自撰的风俗简述；没有点出节日的名字，它就是日本夏天的盂兰盆节（Obon）。

🌍 Where it comes from出处与背景

Obon is a Buddhist festival for the spirits of family ancestors, held in most places around the middle of August and in some areas in July. Its name and roots go back to the same Buddhist story as the Ghost Festival kept in China on the fifteenth day of the seventh lunar month, so readers from China, Hong Kong or Taiwan will recognise much of it. Families clean the graves, light small fires at the gate to welcome the spirits, and at the end light others to send them off; the most famous send-off is in Kyoto, where great fires in the shapes of characters and other figures are lit on the hills on the sixteenth of August. The circle dances, called bon odori, are held in town squares, and the floating of lanterns, toro nagashi, is the closing scene in many river and coastal towns. The lesson's food thrown into the water is the older practice; today many towns collect the lanterns downstream, and offerings are usually taken away rather than thrown in, to keep rivers clean.

盂兰盆节是祭祀祖先之灵的佛教节日，大多数地方在八月中旬前后过，有的地区在七月。它的名称和来历与中国农历七月十五的中元节（盂兰盆会）出自同一个佛教故事，所以来自中国大陆、香港或台湾的读者会觉得很多地方眼熟。人们扫墓，在门口点小火迎接亡灵，节日结束时再点火送他们走；最有名的送火在京都，八月十六日山上点起字形和图形的大火。圆圈舞叫「盆踊」，在城镇广场上跳；放灯笼叫「灯笼流」，是许多河边和海边城镇的压轴一幕。课文说把食物扔进水里，是较老的做法；如今很多城镇在下游把灯笼收回，供品通常也拿走而不扔进水里，以保持河流清洁。

✨ How it is written赏析

The account moves through one day and one night in strict order, from the welcome to the farewell, and the passive keeps attention on the custom rather than on any person. The tone changes quietly. It begins with the word cheerful and with dancing, so the reader does not expect sadness, and only in the last two sentences does the writer use the word moving and slow everything down: thousands of lights, slowly, drifting, until nobody can see them. The final words fade away just as the lanterns do. A text about death that starts with joy and ends in silence is a small model of how the festival itself feels.

文章按严格的时间顺序走完一天一夜，从迎接到送别；被动语态让注意力停在风俗上，而不是停在哪个人身上。语气悄悄变化。开头用的是「欢快」和跳舞，读者不会预料到伤感；直到最后两句，作者才用了「感人」这个词，把节奏放慢：成千上万盏灯、慢慢地、漂远、直到再也看不见。最后几个词像灯笼一样淡出。一篇写死亡的文章以欢乐开始、以寂静结束，本身就是这个节日给人的感受的缩影。

🧭 What it argues, and does it still hold?解读

Many cultures set aside a time to remember the dead, and they choose very different moods for it. China's Qingming in spring is mostly quiet and serious, a day for sweeping graves; the Ghost Festival in the seventh month is more cautious, with offerings to calm wandering spirits; Mexico's Day of the Dead at the start of November is bright and full of music, much like the Japanese festival in the lesson. In Canada, families from these traditions often keep their customs in a smaller way, and November the eleventh, Remembrance Day, gives the whole country a shared, silent moment for those who died in war. Comparing them is a good way to see that grief and celebration are not opposites; each of these days holds both.

很多文化都留出一段时间纪念死者，而选择的情绪大不相同。中国春天的清明大多安静、庄重，是扫墓的日子；七月的中元节更谨慎，用供品安抚游荡的亡灵；墨西哥十一月初的亡灵节色彩鲜艳、充满音乐，很像课文里的日本节日。在加拿大，来自这些传统的家庭往往以小一些的规模保留各自的习俗，而十一月十一日的阵亡将士纪念日，给全国一个共同的、静默的时刻，纪念战争中的死者。把它们放在一起比较，能看出悲伤和庆祝并不对立；这些日子里两者都有。

③ How the hard sentences are built难句结构

Sentences are counted in the lesson text, title not included. Find the sentence in your book, then read our notes on its structure.句子按课文正文数（不含标题）。在教材里找到这一句，再看本站的结构讲解。

Sentence 1第 1 句 (begins开头 “A Festival for …”)

  • be held: take place, in the present simple passive because it happens every year.be held：（活动）举行，一般现在时被动，讲每年都这样。

Sentence 3第 3 句 (begins开头 “for on this …”)

  • be said to do: people say that; the dead and the living use the plus an adjective for a group.be said to do：据说……；the dead / the living：the + 形容词表示一类人。

Sentence 4第 4 句 (begins开头 “As they are …”)

  • as: because; be expected to do: people suppose that they will; lay out: set out neatly.as：因为；be expected to do：被认为会……；lay out：摆出。

Sentence 5第 5 句 (begins开头 “Specially-made lanterns are …”)

  • help someone do or to do; find your way: get to the place without getting lost.help sb (to) do：帮某人做，to 可省略。find one's way：找到路。

Sentence 7第 7 句 (begins开头 “In the early …”)

  • had been laid out: the past perfect passive, for what was done the night before.had been laid out：过去完成时被动，指前一晚已摆好。
  • it stands for the infinitive at the end; anyone living puts the participle after the pronoun.it is considered + 形容词 + for sb to do：形式主语 it 代替后面的不定式。anyone living：任何活着的人（分词后置）。

Sentence 11第 11 句 (begins开头 “Thousands of lanterns …”)

  • guiding: an -ing phrase for what goes on at the same time; drift out to sea: float away from land.guiding：现在分词作伴随状语。drift out to sea：漂向大海。

Sentence 13第 13 句 (begins开头 “stand on the …”)

  • watching tells what they do as they stand; watch something doing: see it in progress.watching：伴随状语；watch sth doing：看着……正在……。
  • can be seen no more: can no longer be seen, a literary way of putting it.can be seen no more：再也看不见了（no more 放句末，书面）。

④ Grammar in this lesson本课语法

The passive for what people say and believe被动语态表示「据说、人们认为」
be said, believed, thought, expected or considered plus to do reports what people generally say or think; it is considered plus an adjective plus to do says how people judge an action.
be said / believed / thought / expected / considered + to do：据说、人们认为、预计会……；it is considered + 形容词 + to do：人们认为做……是……。

Sentences 3, 4 and 8 use this pattern to describe belief without saying who believes it.

本课：第 3 句 are said to return；第 4 句 are expected to be；第 8 句 it is considered unlucky。

  • He is said to be the richest man in town.
  • The new road is expected to open next year.
  • It is considered rude to arrive late.
watch or see something doing, and -ing for something at the same timewatch / see sb doing 与分词作伴随
After see, hear or watch, an object plus -ing shows an action in progress. An -ing phrase can also tell what else is happening at the same moment.
感官动词 + 宾语 + -ing：看到动作正在进行；句中的 -ing 短语还可作伴随状语，说明同时发生的事。

Sentence 11 and sentence 13 each add an -ing phrase for what goes on at the same time.

本课：第 11 句 guiding the dead；第 13 句 watching the lanterns drifting away。

  • We sat on the hill watching the sun going down.
  • The children ran off, laughing.

⑤ Patterns to reuse可以套用的句型

  • X takes place every year in … ……每年在……举行
    A dragon boat race takes place every year in Vancouver.
  • On this day, X are believed to … 据信这一天 X 会……
    On this day, the gods are believed to visit every home.
  • Since they are thought to be …, … is put out for them 因为人们认为他们会……，就为他们摆出……
    Since the birds are thought to be hungry in winter, seed is put out for them.
  • It is thought unlucky for … to … ……做……被认为不吉利
    It is thought unlucky for a guest to open an umbrella indoors.
  • People stand on … watching … until … 人们站在……看着……，直到……
    People stood on the bridge watching the boats until it grew dark.

Summary writing摘要写作

In no more than 80 words, describe the customs of this Japanese festival.

Points a good summary covers好摘要要覆盖的要点

Our model summary本站范文

Every year Japan holds a happy festival when the dead are believed to visit their homes. Families put out food for the hungry visitors and hang lanterns outside to light their way. People sing and dance all night. At dawn the food is thrown into rivers or the sea, since it would be unlucky to eat it. In towns by the sea, the lanterns are set on the water, and crowds watch them float away, leading the dead home.（79 词）

Lesson 30 · Football or Polo 足球还是马球？

📘 New Concept English · Book 2 · Lesson 30 “Football or Polo”新概念英语 第二册 Lesson 30《Football or Polo》

🎯 Before you open the lesson开课前
The Wayle is a little river running through a park close to the writer's home, and he likes to sit beside it on fine afternoons. Last Sunday was warm, so off he went to the bank. Children were playing there, and people were out in rowing boats. All at once a child gave the ball a mighty kick, and it flew towards a boat going by. People on the bank shouted a warning, but the man in the boat did not hear. The ball hit him so hard that he almost went over the side. The writer looked round for the children: not one was left, they had all fled. The man just laughed once he saw what had happened, called to them, and threw the ball back. The grammar is so ... that, there weren't any, and the past perfect.
威尔河是一条小河，穿过作者家附近的公园。天气好的下午，他喜欢坐在河边。上星期天很暖和，他照常去河岸上坐着。岸上有些孩子在玩，河上有人划船。突然一个孩子狠狠踢了一脚球，球朝一条经过的船飞去。岸上有人朝船上的人喊，可他没听见。球重重打在他身上，他差点掉进水里。作者回头去看那些孩子，一个也不见了——全跑了！那人明白过来后笑了，朝孩子们喊了一声，把球扔回了岸上。本课学 so … that、there weren't any 和过去完成时 had run away / what had happened。
Questions to keep in mind as you read读的时候带着这几个问题：
  1. What went wrong for the man rowing past?船上的那个人怎么了？
  2. Why did he laugh instead of getting angry?他最后为什么笑了，而不是生气？

📖 Understanding the piece读懂这篇

Written for the course by L. G. Alexander for New Concept English (Longman, 1967); the river Wayle and its park are invented.

本课由编者 L. G. 亚历山大为《新概念英语》（朗文，1967）自撰；威尔河和那座公园都是虚构的。

🌍 Where it comes from出处与背景

Many British towns grew up around small rivers, and parks along their banks became places for Sunday walks, boating and children's games, a pattern that still holds. Rowing boats for hire were a common sight on park lakes and rivers through the twentieth century. The title is a small joke that depends on knowing what polo is: a team game played on horseback with long sticks, and also, in water polo, a team game in a pool. A ball kicked from the bank into a boat seems to belong half to football and half to some kind of polo, and the writer leaves the reader to enjoy the confusion.

很多英国城镇是沿着小河发展起来的，河岸边的公园成了星期天散步、划船和孩子们玩耍的地方，这个格局至今未变。整个二十世纪，公园的湖面和河上出租的划艇都很常见。标题是个小笑话，要懂马球才能会意：马球是骑在马上、用长杆击球的团体运动；水球则是在泳池里进行的团体运动。从岸上踢进船里的一个球，似乎一半属于足球、一半属于某种「球」，作者让读者自己去品味这份混乱。

✨ How it is written赏析

The first three sentences are slow and peaceful on purpose: a small river, fine afternoons, a warm Sunday, sitting as usual. The scene is set with the past continuous, children playing, people rowing, so that it feels like a picture rather than an event. Then a single word, suddenly, starts the action, and the verbs become short and physical: kicked, went, struck. The writer turns to look and finds an empty bank, which is funny because the reader expected an argument. The ending gives the man the last word in action rather than speech. He laughs and throws the ball back, and the peace of the opening returns.

前三句故意写得缓慢平和：一条小河，晴朗的下午，暖和的星期天，照常坐着。场景用过去进行时铺开——孩子们在玩，有人在划船——所以它像一幅画，而不像一件事。然后一个词 suddenly 引出动作，动词变得短促而有力：kicked、went、struck。作者转身去看，岸上空空如也，这很好笑，因为读者等着的是一场争吵。结尾让那个人用动作而不是话语收场：他笑了，把球扔回去，开头的宁静又回来了。

🧭 What it argues, and does it still hold?解读

The man's good humour is the real subject of the story. He has every reason to be angry, he nearly fell into a river, and the children have run off without saying sorry. Yet once he sees it was an accident, he chooses to laugh and give the ball back, which is likely to make the children less afraid to own up next time. Children everywhere run from trouble, and adults everywhere decide how big that trouble is. Readers in China may remember being shouted at for a ball through a window, or being forgiven for one; the lesson quietly asks which of those memories taught you more.

那个人的好脾气才是故事真正的主题。他完全有理由生气：他差点掉进河里，孩子们一声道歉都没有就跑了。可是一看出这是个意外，他就选择笑一笑、把球还回去，这很可能让孩子们下次不那么害怕承认错误。世界各地的孩子都会躲麻烦，世界各地的大人都在决定这个麻烦有多大。中国读者也许记得，自己曾因为球砸破窗户被大声训斥，或者被原谅过；课文静静地问你：这两种记忆，哪一种教会你更多？

💬 Discuss and write讨论与写作
  1. Tell of a time you made a mistake and someone reacted with good humour. How did it make you feel?
    讲一次你犯了错、别人却以好脾气回应的经历。你当时什么感受？
  2. Write the scene from the man in the boat's point of view, in about 80 words.
    用大约 80 词，从船上那个人的角度写出这一幕。

③ How the hard sentences are built难句结构

Sentences are counted in the lesson text, title not included. Find the sentence in your book, then read our notes on its structure.句子按课文正文数（不含标题）。在教材里找到这一句，再看本站的结构讲解。

Sentence 1第 1 句 (begins开头 “The Wayle is …”)

  • that cuts across: a relative clause describing the river, in the present simple for a lasting fact.that cuts across：定语从句修饰 river，一般现在时说长期的事实。

Sentence 5第 5 句 (begins开头 “Suddenly, one of …”)

  • one of the children: the subject is one, so the verb is singular.one of the children：孩子们中的一个，谓语用单数。
  • hard is an adverb meaning with force; hardly means almost not.hard 作副词：用力地（不是 hardly）。
  • a passing boat: a boat going by at that moment.a passing boat：一条经过的船。

Sentence 7第 7 句 (begins开头 “The ball struck …”)

  • so with an adverb and then that: to such a degree that.so + 副词 + that：如此……以至于。
  • nearly fell: he almost fell, but did not.nearly fell：差点掉下去，实际没掉。

Sentence 8第 8 句 (begins开头 “I turned to …”)

  • there weren't any in sight: none could be seen. children is left out after any.there weren't any in sight：一个也看不见，any 后省略了 children。
  • had run away: the past perfect, because they had gone before he turned.had run away：过去完成时，作者转身之前孩子们已经跑了。

Sentence 9第 9 句 (begins开头 “The man laughed …”)

  • what had happened: an object clause in the past perfect, pointing back to the kick.what had happened：宾语从句，过去完成时，指之前踢球的事。

④ Grammar in this lesson本课语法

so with an adjective or adverb, then thatso + 形容词/副词 + that
so ... that shows a result that comes from the degree of something. so is followed by an adjective or adverb, and the that clause gives the result. In speech that is often left out.
so … that 表示程度导致的结果。so 后接形容词或副词，that 从句说结果。口语里 that 常省略。

Sentence 7 uses so hard that: the blow was strong enough nearly to knock him in.

本课：第 7 句 so hard that，打得太重，那人差点落水。

  • He ran so fast that nobody could catch him.
  • The soup was so hot (that) I couldn't eat it.
Negative there is and there are with anythere is / are 的否定与 any
Use any in negatives and questions, and some in positive sentences. The noun after any can be dropped when it is clear. in sight means where you can see.
否定句和疑问句用 any：There weren't any … / Were there any …? 肯定句用 some。any 后面的名词可以省略（there weren't any）。in sight＝看得见的范围内。

Sentence 4 uses some in a positive sentence; sentence 8 uses any in a negative one.

本课：第 4 句 there were some people（肯定，some）；第 8 句 there weren't any（否定，any）。

  • There were some birds on the roof.
  • I looked for milk, but there wasn't any.

⑤ Patterns to reuse可以套用的句型

  • X is a small … that cuts across … X 是一条穿过……的小……
    The Mill Lane is a small road that cuts across the farm.
  • I like sitting by X on fine afternoons. 天气好的下午我喜欢坐在 X 旁边。
    I like sitting by the lake on sunny mornings.
  • X hit Y so hard that Y almost … X 打得 Y 那么重，Y 差点……
    The wave hit her so hard that she almost dropped her phone.
  • X looked round, but no … were in sight. X 四下看，可一个……也看不见。
    She looked round, but no buses were in sight.

Our model summary本站范文

Last Sunday the writer sat by the river in the park. Children were playing on the bank, and people were rowing. One child kicked a ball hard, and it hit a man in a boat, who nearly fell in. The children all ran away, but the man only laughed and threw the ball back.（54 词）

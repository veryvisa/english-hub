Lesson 1 · A Private Conversation 私人谈话

📘 New Concept English · Book 2 · Lesson 1 “A Private Conversation”新概念英语 第二册 Lesson 1《A Private Conversation》

B1🧠 15 new words个新词📝 Full notes有完整讲解

🎯 Before you open the lesson开课前
The writer goes to see a play. His seat is good and so is the play, but a young couple behind him chat loudly all through it and he cannot follow the actors. Staring at them does no good, so at last he complains, and the young man tells him the talk is private and none of his concern. This is the first lesson of Book 2: the past simple to tell the story, the past continuous for the background, and -ly adverbs for feelings.
作者去剧院看戏，座位好、戏也好，可身后一对年轻男女一直大声聊天，他听不见台词。回头瞪眼没用，他终于开口抱怨，对方却说这是私人谈话、不关他的事。本课是第二册的第一课：一般过去时讲故事，过去进行时铺背景，副词 -ly 表情绪。

Written for the course by L. G. Alexander; New Concept English was first published by Longman in 1967, and this is an invented scene, not a report.

本课由编者 L. G. 亚历山大为教材自撰；《新概念英语》1967 年由朗文初版，本课是虚构场景，不是纪实。

🌍 Where it comes from出处与背景

Book 2 opens with stories of about a hundred words, each built so that a learner who knows the basic tenses can follow it and then retell it. The first lesson sets the pattern for the whole book: a short scene, a small conflict and a twist in the final line. The theatre is a good choice for this. In British theatres of the time, talking during a performance was seen as a real breach of manners, and people who had paid for a seat expected silence from those around them. That shared rule is what makes the young man's answer surprising: he is the one breaking it, yet he speaks as if the writer were the intruder.

第二册开头的课文都在百词上下，每篇设计成：掌握基本时态的学习者能读懂，再能复述出来。第一课定下了全书的套路——一个短场景、一点小冲突、最后一句翻转。选剧院做场景很合适：在当时的英国剧院，演出中说话被看作严重失礼，花钱买了座位的人默认周围要安静。正因为这条规矩人人都懂，那个年轻人的回答才出人意料：破坏规矩的明明是他，他却说得好像作者才是闯进别人私事的人。

✨ How it is written赏析

Almost every sentence is short and in the past simple, which gives the story a steady, step-by-step rhythm. The writer's anger grows in stages: he gets cross, turns, stares, waits, turns again and finally speaks. Each stage is one plain sentence, and the reader feels the pressure building. Then the punch line comes in direct speech, and it works because of one word, private. The couple have turned a public hall into a sitting room in their own minds. Notice also how the adverbs carry the feeling: one for the couple's noise, one for the writer's temper, one for the reply. Take them out and the story loses most of its colour.

几乎每句都短，而且都是一般过去时，故事于是有一种一步一步往前走的节奏。作者的火气分几级往上升：生气、回头、瞪眼、等待、再回头、终于开口。每一级都是一句平平的话，读者却能感到压力在积累。最后的笑点用直接引语说出来，靠的是一个词——私人的。那对男女在心里把公共场所当成了自家客厅。还要留意副词怎么承载情绪：一个给那对男女的吵闹，一个给作者的火气，一个给那句回答。把这三个副词拿掉，故事的颜色就去了大半。

🧭 What it argues, and does it still hold?解读

The joke still works today, though the noise has changed. In cinemas and theatres now the usual complaint is a glowing phone screen or a call taken in the middle of a scene, and the reply is often much the same: my phone, my call, my affair. The lesson raises a fair question about where private ends and public begins. Chinese readers may recognise the same tension on fast trains and in restaurants, where loud calls and videos played without headphones are common complaints. The writer's method is worth noting too: he tries silent signals first and speaks only when they fail. That is usually the politest order, and the story shows that it does not always work.

这个笑话今天照样成立，只是噪音换了。如今在电影院和剧场，人们最常抱怨的是亮着的手机屏幕、演到一半接起来的电话，而对方的回答往往差不多：我的手机、我的电话、我的事。课文提出一个合理的问题：私人的边界在哪里，公共的又从哪里开始。中国读者在高铁、饭馆里大概也熟悉类似的冲突——大声打电话、外放视频是常见的抱怨。作者的做法也值得看：他先用不出声的信号，信号无效才开口。这通常是最礼貌的顺序，而故事告诉我们，它并不总是管用。

③ How the hard sentences are built难句结构

Sentences are counted in the lesson text, title not included. Find the sentence in your book, then read our notes on its structure.句子按课文正文数（不含标题）。在教材里找到这一句，再看本站的结构讲解。

Sentence 10第 10 句 (begins开头 “I looked at …”)

  • look at sb: look cannot take an object directly, so at is needed.look at sb：看某人。look 不能直接带宾语，要加 at。
  • angrily is an adverb telling how he looked, and it goes at the end.angrily 是副词，说明「怎么看」，放在句末。

Sentence 11第 11 句 (begins开头 “They did not …”)

  • pay attention (to): to listen or watch carefully. The negative can be did not pay any or simply paid no.pay attention (to)：注意，留心。否定可说 did not pay any attention，也可说 paid no attention。

Sentence 12第 12 句 (begins开头 “In the end, …”)

  • in the end: finally, after a stretch of waiting or trouble.in the end：最后，终于（经过一段时间之后）。
  • could not bear it: could stand it no longer. bear is mostly used after can't or couldn't.couldn't bear it：受不了了。bear 多用在 can't / couldn't 后面。

Sentence 15第 15 句 (begins开头 “'It's none of …”)

  • none of your business: a rude way to say that this does not concern you.none of your business：不关你的事（很不客气）。
  • This is the joke of the lesson: the rude pair turn the complaint round and accuse the writer of interfering.全课笑点：真正失礼的是他们，却反过来指责作者多管闲事。

④ Grammar in this lesson本课语法

The past simple and the past continuous一般过去时与过去进行时
The past simple tells the single finished actions of a story, such as went, turned and said. The past continuous, was or were doing, sets the background: what was going on at that moment.
一般过去时讲过去发生并结束的一个个动作（went、turned、said）；过去进行时 was/were doing 讲某一时刻正在进行、作背景的动作。

Sentences 5 and 6 use the past continuous for the background, and the rest of the story moves forward in the past simple.

本课：第 5、6 句用过去进行时交代背景，其余句子用一般过去时推进情节。

  • It was snowing when we left the house.
  • While she was cooking, the phone rang.
Adverbs from adjectives: -ly形容词变副词：-ly
An adjective describes a noun; an adverb describes a verb. Most adjectives add -ly, so loud gives loudly and rude gives rudely. After a consonant, a final y changes to i, so angry gives angrily.
形容词修饰名词，副词修饰动词。多数形容词加 -ly：loud → loudly，rude → rudely；辅音 + y 结尾的改 y 为 i 再加 -ly：angry → angrily。

In sentence 7, angry follows got and describes the writer, so it is an adjective. In sentences 10 and 14, angrily tells how he acted, so it is an adverb.

本课：第 7 句的 angry 跟在 got 后面，是形容词；第 10、14 句的 angrily 说明动作，是副词。

  • She is a careful driver. She drives carefully.
  • The boy was happy. He sang happily.

⑤ Patterns to reuse可以套用的句型

  • get + adjective (get angry / get tired) 变得……
    The soup got cold while we were chatting.
  • pay no attention to sb/sth 不理睬……
    The cat paid no attention to the barking dog.
  • can't bear sth / doing sth 受不了……
    I can't bear waiting in long queues.
  • none of sb's business 不关某人的事
    How much I paid for it is none of his business.

Points a good summary covers好摘要要覆盖的要点

Our model summary本站范文

Last week I went to see a play. I had a good seat, but a young couple behind me chatted loudly all the time, and I could hardly hear the actors. When I complained, the young man rudely told me that their conversation was private.（45 词）

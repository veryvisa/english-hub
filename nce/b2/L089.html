Lesson 89 · A Slip of the Tongue 口误

📘 New Concept English · Book 2 · Lesson 89 “A Slip of the Tongue”新概念英语 第二册 Lesson 89《A Slip of the Tongue》

B1🧠 17 new words个新词📝 Full notes有完整讲解

🎯 Before you open the lesson开课前
People will put up with almost anything for a free show. When word spread that a bird-seed company was putting on a comedy night at the local cinema, the whole town hurried along. We queued for hours, and there were probably several hundred of us inside by the time it began. Sadly it was one of the dullest evenings ever: those left outside had nothing to regret, as many of the promised performers never turned up. The only laughs came from the company's announcer, who stood shaking at the microphone for some minutes; when he finally spoke, he jumbled the company name and the greeting, and the hall roared. This is a short story that ends in a slip of the tongue. The grammar is must have done, need not have done, should have done and burst out laughing.
人们为了看一场免费演出什么都肯干，哪怕演得很糟。消息传开：P. and U. 鸟食公司要在本地电影院办一场喜剧演出，大家一窝蜂地赶去。我们排了好几个小时的队才进场，开演前场里一定有好几百人。可惜那是我们看过的最乏味的演出之一，没挤进去的人根本用不着失望，因为好多该来的演员都没来。那晚唯一好笑的东西，来自开场时的广告人：他显然非常紧张，在话筒前局促地站了好几分钟，一开口全场就哄堂大笑。我们都知道这可怜的人本该说什么，可他实际上说的是把公司名和「女士们先生们晚上好」都颠倒了的一句话。本课是一个以口误收尾的小故事，学 must have done、need not have done、should have done 和 burst out laughing。
Questions to keep in mind as you read读的时候带着这几个问题：
  1. Who produced the only real laugh that evening, and how?那晚唯一真正好笑的笑话是谁说的？为什么？
  2. Why did the people who could not get in have nothing to regret?没挤进去的人为什么不必失望？

🌍 Where it comes from出处与背景

The announcer's mistake is a spoonerism, a slip in which the first sounds or whole words of two parts of a phrase change places. The name comes from William Archibald Spooner, a clergyman and head of an Oxford college in the late eighteen hundreds and early nineteen hundreds, who was famous for such slips, though most of the examples credited to him were probably made up by students. Psychologists who study speech errors find that swaps like these are common and not random: the exchanged parts usually come from the same position and the same kind of word, which suggests that the brain plans several words ahead and occasionally puts them in the wrong slots. Nervousness, as in this lesson, makes such slips more likely.

广告人的错误叫「首音误置」（spoonerism）：一个短语里两部分的开头音或整个词互换了位置。这个名称来自威廉·阿奇博尔德·斯普纳，他是十九世纪末二十世纪初牛津一所学院的院长、牧师，以这类口误出名，不过记在他名下的例子大多可能是学生编的。研究口误的心理学家发现，这种互换很常见，而且并不随机：互换的部分通常来自相同的位置、相同类别的词，说明大脑会提前规划好几个词，偶尔把它们放错了格子。紧张——正像这一课里那样——会让这类口误更容易发生。

✨ How it is written赏析

The story is a joke with a long setup and a quick ending. The opening line is a general truth told with irony, and every detail after it raises our expectations: crowds, queues, hundreds of people. Then the writer lets them fall, the dullest show, missing performers, and uses modal perfects to look back with mild regret: must have been, need not have felt, should have appeared. Only at the end does the real comedy arrive, from the one person who was not meant to be funny. The punch line is the exact words, printed in full so the reader can hear the mistake and laugh too.

这个故事是一个铺垫很长、收尾很快的笑话。开头一句是带着反讽说出的普遍道理，之后每一个细节都在抬高期待：人群、长队、几百人。然后作者让期待落空——最乏味的演出、缺席的演员——并用情态动词完成式带点遗憾地回顾：must have been、need not have felt、should have appeared。真正的喜剧到最后才来，而且来自唯一一个本不该好笑的人。包袱就是他说的原话，全文印出来，让读者也能听出错误、跟着笑。

🧭 What it argues, and does it still hold?解读

The lesson is kind to the poor man: we all know what he meant, and the writer calls him nervous, not foolish. That kindness is worth copying. Slips of the tongue happen to everyone, especially in a second language and in front of a crowd, and the fear of making them stops many learners from speaking at all. Chinese learners often worry about mixing up sounds that are hard to tell apart in English; the best answer is practice out loud and the knowledge that listeners usually understand you anyway. The lesson also touches on free events: the cost is often paid in time, queues and dull advertising rather than money.

这一课对那个可怜的人很宽容：我们都知道他想说什么，作者说他紧张，没说他愚蠢。这种宽容值得学。口误人人都会犯，尤其是用第二语言在众人面前讲话时；对口误的恐惧让许多学习者干脆不敢开口。中国学习者常担心把英语里难以分辨的音说混；最好的办法是出声练习，并且明白听的人通常照样能听懂你。这一课还触及了免费活动：代价往往用时间、排队和乏味的广告来支付，而不是用钱。

③ How the hard sentences are built难句结构

Sentences are counted in the lesson text, title not included. Find the sentence in your book, then read our notes on its structure.句子按课文正文数（不含标题）。在教材里找到这一句，再看本站的结构讲解。

Sentence 1第 1 句 (begins开头 “People will do …”)

  • will here shows typical behaviour: people tend to; even if: whether or not.will：表习性、倾向，「总会」。even if：即使。

Sentence 2第 2 句 (begins开头 “When the news …”)

  • The that clause explains what the news was; would be presented is the future in the past, passive.that 从句是 news 的同位语从句。would be presented：过去将来时被动。

Sentence 4第 4 句 (begins开头 “We had to …”)

  • must have done: a confident guess about the past.must have done：对过去的有把握的推测。
  • present: an adjective after the noun meaning there in the room.present：形容词，放在名词后，「在场的」。

Sentence 6第 6 句 (begins开头 “Those who failed …”)

  • need not have done: it was not necessary, but they did.need not have done：本来不必（却做了）。
  • should have done: it was expected, but did not happen.should have done：本该做（却没做）。

Sentence 10第 10 句 (begins开头 “As soon as …”)

  • burst out doing has the same meaning as burst into plus a noun: start suddenly.burst out doing = burst into + 名词：突然……起来。

Sentence 11第 11 句 (begins开头 “We all know …”)

  • The two what clauses are set side by side: what he ought to have said and what he did say.what … should have said / what he actually said：两个 what 从句对照。

④ Grammar in this lesson本课语法

Modal verbs with have done: must, need not, should情态动词 + have done：must / need not / should
must have done is a confident guess about the past; need not have done means it was done although it was unnecessary; should have done means it was expected but not done.
must have done 对过去的肯定推测（一定是）；need not have done 本来不必做而做了；should have done 本该做而没做。

Sentence 4 has must have been, sentence 6 has need not have felt, and sentences 7 and 11 have should have.

本课：第 4 句 must have been；第 6 句 need not have felt；第 7、11 句 should have。

  • The ground is wet; it must have rained last night.
  • You needn't have bought milk; we have plenty.
as soon as, burst out doing, and burst into
as soon as means the moment that; burst out laughing or crying means the same as burst into laughter or tears.
as soon as 一……就……；burst out laughing/crying = burst into laughter/tears：突然笑起来/哭起来。

Sentence 10 puts both together: the moment he spoke, the hall laughed.

本课：第 10 句两者连用：他一开口，众人就爆笑。

  • As soon as the baby saw her mother, she burst into tears.
  • We all burst out laughing at his face.

⑤ Patterns to reuse可以套用的句型

  • People will do anything to …, even if … 人们为了……什么都肯做，即使……
    People will do anything to win a prize, even if it is a cheap one.
  • Once word got round that …, everyone … 消息一传开说……，大家都……
    Once word got round that the shop was closing, everyone hurried there.
  • There must have been … people … 当时一定有……人……
    There must have been a thousand people at the market.
  • Those who …ed need not have felt … 那些……的人本不必感到……
    Those who stayed at home need not have felt sorry, as the game was awful.
  • As soon as X opened his mouth, everyone … X 一开口，大家就……
    As soon as the clown opened his mouth, the children started to cheer.

Our model summary本站范文

When we heard that a bird-seed company was giving a free comedy show at our cinema, we all rushed there and waited for hours to get in. Several hundred people were present. Unfortunately, the show was extremely dull, and many performers did not even come. The only funny moment came at the start, when the company's nervous announcer mixed up his words, saying the company's name and his greeting the wrong way round. Everyone burst out laughing.（77 词）

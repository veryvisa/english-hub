📖 Understanding the piece读懂这篇

The lesson is taken from Ruth Benedict's Patterns of Culture, published in Boston by Houghton Mifflin in 1934 - the book that set three societies side by side (the Zuni of the American south-west, the people of Dobu in Melanesia, and the Kwakiutl of the north-west coast) in order to show how small a share of possible human behaviour any one culture takes up. The passage belongs to the opening chapter, where she clears the ground before the comparison begins. We verified the author, title, publisher, year and the three societies from library and encyclopedia records; the printed credit in the older edition of Book 4 is garbled by the scan, which renders her name as Rtrm Bemedict.

课文选自露丝·本尼迪克特（Ruth Benedict）的《Patterns of Culture》，1934 年波士顿 Houghton Mifflin 出版——那本把三个社会并排放在一起的书（美国西南的祖尼人、美拉尼西亚的多布人、西北海岸的夸扣特尔人），为的是显示任何一种文化所取用的，只是全部可能的人类行为中极小的一份。这一段属于开篇一章，她在那里先清场，然后才开始比较。作者、书名、出版社、年份与那三个社会我们核过（图书馆目录与百科）；旧版第四册的署名行被扫描糟蹋了，把她的名字印成 Rtrm Bemedict。

🌍 Where it comes from出处与背景

Benedict (1887-1948) came late to the subject: she entered graduate school at Columbia in 1921, in her thirties, and worked under Franz Boas, whose lifelong argument was that differences between peoples are differences of culture and not of blood. Read the date on the book - 1934 - and the stakes of this quiet opening chapter become visible. Theories of racial hierarchy were then respectable in European and American universities and were about to become state policy in Germany, and the sentence in this lesson that insists our own institutions be considered together with everybody else's, our own among the rest, was written into that. The book was also written for the general reader rather than for colleagues, which is why a paragraph of this difficulty still has a joke in it about a child's baby talk. It sold steadily for decades and did more than any other single book to put the word culture into ordinary English in the sense we now use without thinking.

本尼迪克特（1887–1948）入行很晚：1921 年、三十多岁才进哥伦比亚读研究生，师从弗朗茨·博厄斯——博厄斯一生的论点是，族群之间的差别是文化的差别，不是血统的差别。看一眼书的年份，1934，这平静的开篇一章的分量就显出来了：那时种族等级的理论在欧美大学里还是体面的学说，并且即将在德国变成国家政策；而本课里那句「必须把我们自己的制度和别人的放在一起考虑，我们自己也在其中」，就是写进那个处境里的。这本书也是写给普通读者、不是写给同行的——所以一段这么难的文字里还会有一个关于小孩儿语的玩笑。它连卖了几十年，而且比任何一本书都更有力地把 culture（文化）这个词按我们今天不假思索的用法，送进了日常英语。

✨ How it is written赏析

The hard thing on this page is not the vocabulary; it is that the sentences are built back to front, and on purpose. Look at the opening: the object comes first, so that the brain and custom stand side by side like two exhibits before the verdict is read out. Then watch what the inserted phrases do. They are not padding. One of them puts the opinion she is about to demolish into the reader's own mouth, as the way we tend to think, so that the correction, when it comes, is not an attack on anyone. Another, the whole world over, quietly widens the claim from a few societies to the species. A third, in all seriousness, is doing defensive work: she is about to quote a comparison so extreme that the reader will suspect a figure of speech, and she says in advance that Dewey meant it as arithmetic. The best single stroke is the verb in edited. One word turns culture from an atmosphere into an agent with a pencil, working on the page before you ever see it, and the whole argument of the chapter is carried in it. Two things to take away for your own writing: a metaphor hidden inside a verb does more work than one announced with like; and if you want to correct the reader, put the error in the first person plural first.

这一页难的不是词汇，而是句子是倒着搭的，而且是故意的。看开头：宾语先出场，于是「大脑」和「风俗」像两件展品那样并排摆着，判词还没念。然后看那些插入语在干什么——它们不是填充物：其中一个把她即将拆掉的看法塞进读者自己嘴里（「我们往往认为」），于是等纠正来的时候，它不是对任何人的攻击；另一个「就全世界而言」，不声不响地把断言从几个社会扩到整个物种；第三个「极其严肃地」在做防守工作：她马上要引一个极端的比较，读者会怀疑那只是修辞，于是她预先说明，杜威是把它当算术讲的。最漂亮的一笔是 edited 这个动词：一个词就把文化从一种氛围变成了一个拿着铅笔的执行者——在你看见那一页之前它已经在上面改过了；而整章的论证就扛在这一个词上。有两件事可以搬进自己的写作：藏在动词里的比喻，比用 like 宣布出来的比喻干更多活；想纠正读者，就先用「我们」把那个错误说一遍。

🧭 What it argues, and does it still hold?解读

Her central complaint is the one claim here that can be measured, and the measurement is not kind to us. She says that in the study of man, and only there, the major social sciences substituted one local variation - Western civilization - for the whole. A survey of six leading psychology journals published in 2008 found that about 68 per cent of samples were from the United States and about 96 per cent from Western industrialized countries, in a world where those countries hold a small minority of the people; a 2010 paper gave the sample a name, noting that these are among the least representative populations available for generalizing about human beings; and when the same journals were counted again for 2014 to 2018, authors and samples were still a little over 60 per cent American, with the rest of the world supplying four or five per cent. Ninety years after this chapter, her accusation is a statistic rather than an opinion. Where the lesson is harder to defend is in what the comparing is for. If no culture may be given extra weight, how is any practice to be criticized? Benedict's generation left that question open, and it is now the liveliest quarrel in the field; her own answer, in her later writing, was a claim about which arrangements let more people live well, which is not a neutral comparison at all. And there is an irony on her own record that no honest reading can skip. Her most widely read book, the 1946 study of Japan, was written for a wartime government office without her ever visiting the country, learning the language, or interviewing its people, from films, novels and newspaper cuttings - a whole industrial society explained by a few generalizations about culture, which is close to the error this chapter warns against. Japanese scholars were among the first to say so, and the argument has run ever since. For Chinese readers the line she asks us to put down will look familiar from a long way back: the division between the civilized centre and the barbarians beyond it is one of the oldest organizing ideas in classical Chinese writing, as it was in Greek, and her point is not that one culture invented the habit but that the study of human beings cannot begin until somebody stops drawing it.

她的核心指控，恰恰是这一页上唯一可以测量的主张，而测量结果对我们并不客气。她说：唯独在研究人自身的时候，主要的社会科学用一种地方性的变体——西方文明——顶替了全体。2008 年一项对六家主要心理学期刊的清点发现，约 68% 的样本来自美国、约 96% 来自西方工业化国家，而这些国家在世界人口中只占少数；2010 年一篇论文给这个样本起了名字，并指出它们是用来概括人类时最不具代表性的人群之一；而当同样的期刊按 2014 至 2018 年再数一遍，作者与样本仍有六成多是美国的，世界其余地方只供给百分之四到五。这一章写完九十年后，她的指控是一个统计量，不再是一种看法。这一课比较难站住的地方，是「比较为了什么」。如果任何文化都不许被加权，那还怎么批评任何一种做法？她那一代人把这个问题留着没答，而它如今是这个领域里最热的争吵；她本人后来的写作给的答案，是一个关于「哪种安排让更多人活得好」的主张——而那根本不是中立的比较。她自己的记录上还有一处讽刺，任何诚实的读法都不该跳过：她流传最广的那本书，1946 年那部关于日本的研究，是为战时政府机构写的，而她从未去过那个国家、不会那门语言、也没有访谈过那里的人，材料是电影、小说和报纸剪报——用关于「文化」的几条概括去解释一个完整的工业社会，这离本章所警告的那个错误已经很近。最早这么说的人里就有日本学者，而争论从此没停过。对中国读者，她要我们放下的那条线，从很远的地方看就认得：以文明的中心与其外的「夷」相区分，是中国古典文献里最古老的组织性观念之一，希腊也是如此；而她的意思不是哪一种文化发明了这个习惯，而是：研究人这件事，要等到有人停止画这条线才能开始。

💬 Discuss and write讨论与写作
  1. Take one custom you grew up with and had always thought of as simply normal, and describe it as an outsider would - without the word strange, and without defending it. Six sentences. Then say what the exercise cost you.
    挑一件你从小就有、一直觉得「本来就该这样」的风俗，照一个外来者会看到的样子写出来——不用「奇怪」这个词，也不为它辩护，六句话。然后说一说，这个练习让你付出了什么。
  2. If no culture is to be given extra weight, on what ground can any practice be criticized? Answer in 120 words, and set out the strongest version of the position you reject before you reject it.
    如果任何一种文化都不许被加权，那还能站在什么立场上批评任何一种做法？用 120 词回答，并在否定之前先把你所否定的那个立场最有力的版本写出来。
  3. Her best-known book described a country she had never entered. When is it legitimate to write about a place from the outside, and what would have to be true of the writing? Give your rule, then test it on something you have read.
    她最有名的那本书，写的是一个她从未踏入的国家。什么时候从外部写一个地方是正当的？这样的写作需要满足什么条件？先给出你的准则，再拿你读过的某样东西检验它。

③ How the hard sentences are built难句结构

Sentences are counted in the lesson text, title not included. Find the sentence in your book, then read our notes on its structure.句子按课文正文数（不含标题）。在教材里找到这一句，再看本站的结构讲解。

Sentence 2第 2 句 (begins开头 “The inner workings …”)

  • feel something to be ...: object plus complement (Lesson 34).feel sth to be …：复合宾语（L34）。

Sentence 6第 6 句 (begins开头 “is a mass …”)

  • A past participle phrase dropped in as an adverbial: looking at the whole world.taken the world over：过去分词短语作插入状语，「就全世界而言」。
  • A comparative phrase after the noun, with a what-clause after than.more astonishing than …：形容词比较级短语后置修饰 behaviour；than 后接 what 从句。
  • A reduced clause: no matter how aberrant his actions may be.no matter how aberrant：省略句，= no matter how aberrant his actions are。

Sentence 9第 9 句 (begins开头 “The fact of …”)

  • The complement is two nouns in parallel, each carrying its own relative clause, and in the second the relative pronoun has been left out.表语是两个并列名词 role … and varieties …，各带一个定语从句（that custom plays … / (that) it may manifest）。

Sentence 12第 12 句 (begins开头 “He sees it …”)

  • see something done: object plus past participle as complement. Culture is being compared to an editor, which is the force of the image.see sth done：宾语 + 过去分词作补语，「看到它是被……编辑过的」——把文化比作编辑，生动有力。

Sentence 16第 16 句 (begins开头 “as against any …”)

  • The backbone of the that-clause is: the part ... is as A against B, that is, stands in the same proportion as A to B.that 从句主干：the part … is as A against B（「之比，就像 A 比 B」）。
  • A past participle phrase after the noun, doing the work of a relative clause.played by custom in shaping …：过去分词作后置定语。
  • as against: set beside, by way of contrast. And in which he can affect ...: preposition plus which, from affect custom in a way.as against：与……相对比；in which he can affect …：介词 + which（affect custom in a way → in which）。
  • The sense: what custom does to the individual, set against what the individual does to custom, is in the same proportion as the whole of his mother tongue set against the two or three words of baby talk that his family has adopted.意思：风俗对个人的影响，与个人对风俗的影响之比，就像整个母语词汇与孩子自创、被家里采用的几个儿语词之比。

Sentence 30第 30 句 (begins开头 “There is no …”)

  • no plus a comparative plus than works as a superlative (Lesson 28): no duty is more pressing than understanding this one.no + 比较级 + than 表最高级（L28）：理解风俗的作用是我们最义不容辞的责任。
  • it is incumbent upon us to understand: it is the formal subject. The whole of this is a relative clause describing problem, with the relative pronoun left out, since it is the object of understand.it is incumbent upon us to understand：it 形式主语；这一整句作 problem 的定语从句，省略了 that（that 在从句里作 understand 的宾语）。

Sentence 35第 35 句 (begins开头 “In the first …”)

  • require that plus (should) plus the bare infinitive: the subjunctive, which is why there is be rather than is.require that + (should) + 原形：虚拟语气，there be 用原形。
  • preferential weighting: giving one of them more importance than the others.preferential weighting：偏重、厚此薄彼。
  • A relative clause with that left out, describing series.it selects for its consideration：省略 that 的定语从句修饰 series。

④ Grammar in this lesson本课语法

Fronting the object (topicalization)宾语前置（话题化）
For contrast or for a smoother link, the object (or the complement) is moved to the front of the clause while subject and verb keep their order. This is not inversion; it only changes which piece of information comes first.
为了对照或衔接，把宾语（或表语）提到句首，主谓语序不变——这不是倒装，只是改变信息的先后。

Tell it apart from inversion: in inversion the verb comes before the subject (Never have I seen ...), whereas here the subject and verb are still in order.

与倒装区分：倒装是谓语提到主语前（Never have I seen…）；宾语前置后仍是「主语 + 谓语」（we feel）。

It is common in speech, for emphasis: This I cannot accept. / What he said I'll never forget.

常见于口语强调：This I cannot accept.／ What he said I'll never forget.

  • Some things you never forget.
  • That question I can't answer.
require / insist / suggest that plus (should) do: the subjunctive after verbs of ordering and advisingrequire / insist / suggest that + (should) do：命令建议类虚拟
After verbs of requiring, ordering, advising and insisting (require, demand, insist, suggest, propose, recommend, order), the that-clause takes (should) plus the bare infinitive, and American English usually drops the should.
表示要求、命令、建议、坚持的动词（require, demand, insist, suggest, propose, recommend, order）后的 that 从句用「(should) + 动词原形」，美式常省 should。

Person and tense make no difference: He insisted that she go home, not goes or went.

人称和时态都不影响：He insisted that she go home.（不是 goes / went）

The matching nouns and adjectives work the same way: It is essential/important/necessary that he (should) be told; my suggestion that we (should) leave.

相应的名词和形容词同样：It is essential/important/necessary that he (should) be told.／ my suggestion that we (should) leave

One exception: when insist means maintain that something is a fact, and when suggest means indicate, there is no subjunctive: His face suggested that he was angry.

例外：insist / suggest 表「坚持认为某事是事实」「暗示」时不用虚拟：His face suggested that he was angry.

  • The doctor suggested that he (should) stop smoking.
  • It is essential that every student attend the meeting.
Inserted phrases: the punctuation marks of a long sentence插入语：读懂长句的「标点」
An inserted phrase sits in the middle of a sentence and adds the speaker's attitude, a source or an example. When reading, lift it out first to find the backbone, then put it back and ask what it is doing.
插入语夹在句子中间，补充说话人的态度、出处或举例，读的时候先把它拿掉找主干，再回来理解它的作用。

Common ones: I think, I believe, it seems, so to speak, as it were (Lesson 31), to my certain knowledge (Lesson 39), that is to say, in other words.

常见插入语：I think / I believe / it seems / so to speak / as it were（L31）/ to my certain knowledge（L39）/ that is to say / in other words。

An inserted phrase does not affect agreement: the verb still follows the real subject.

插入语不影响主谓一致：Custom, we think, is …（谓语跟 custom）

  • The plan, I am afraid, will not work.
  • He is, so to speak, a walking dictionary.

  3. Check against the text in your coursebook. Mark every mistake by type: a word you did not know, a word you knew but did not hear (linking, weak forms), or spelling.对照教材课文自查，把每个错按类型标出：不认识的词、认识却没听出的词（连读、弱读）、拼写。
  4. Words you did not hear go into your word book above. Re-listen to those sentences and shadow them.没听出的词加进上面的生词本；把那几句再听、跟读。

Keywords for retelling复述关键词

  • custom plays the predominant role in experience and belief
  • nobody sees the world with pristine eyes; it is "edited" by custom
  • Dewey's comparison: mother tongue vs a few baby words
  • from birth the child is shaped by his culture: its habits are his habits
  • scientific study: no preferential weighting; drop "us vs the primitive"; compare our own culture among the rest

Summary writing摘要写作

In no more than 80 words: what part does custom play in a person's life, and what has to be granted before it can be studied scientifically?

用不超过 80 个词概括：作者认为风俗在人的生活中起什么作用？科学地研究风俗需要什么前提？

Points a good summary covers好摘要要覆盖的要点
  • People take custom for ordinary, but it is more astonishing than anything one person could produce, and it governs both experience and belief.人们常认为风俗是平常之事，其实风俗比任何个人行为都惊人，在经验和信仰中起主导作用。
  • Nobody sees the world with clean eyes; from birth each of us is shaped by the customs of the society we are in, and its habits become ours.没有人用纯净的眼睛看世界；个人从出生起就被所在社会的风俗塑造，文化的习惯成为他的习惯。
  • Without understanding custom, the main complicated facts of human life cannot be understood at all.不理解风俗，就无法理解人类生活中的主要复杂事实。
  • A scientific study cannot give extra weight to one culture: the division between us and the savage has to go, and our own culture must be compared with the rest.科学研究风俗不能偏重某一种文化，必须放下「我们与野蛮人」的区分，把我们的文化与其他文化一起比较。
Our model summary本站范文

Custom is usually thought unimportant, but in fact it shapes experience and belief more than anything an individual can do. Nobody sees the world with fresh eyes; from birth each person is moulded by the customs of his society, until its habits and beliefs become his own. We cannot understand human life without understanding custom. To study it scientifically, we must stop treating Western civilization as special and compare all cultures, including our own, without prejudice.（76 词）

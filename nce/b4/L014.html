🎯 Before you open the lesson开课前
From James Gleick's Chaos. Why is a weather forecast useless once it reaches beyond a week? The author runs a thought experiment: suppose a perfect sensor every foot across the whole planet, and a computer with no limit — you still cannot say whether it will rain over Princeton a month from now, because the small motions hidden between the sensors grow stage by stage until they are as wide as the planet itself. What to learn here: building a hypothesis with suppose, and using the future perfect to say what will already have happened by a given moment.
选自詹姆斯·格雷克的《混沌》。天气预报为什么超过一周就没用了？作者做了一个思想实验：假设全球每隔一英尺放一个完美的传感器，再配一台无限强大的计算机——还是算不出一个月后普林斯顿下不下雨，因为传感器之间那些微小波动会一级一级放大到全球尺度。学这一课要学会用 suppose 构造假设、用将来完成时描述「到某时将已经……」。
Questions to keep in mind as you read读的时候带着这几个问题：
  1. Why is a forecast worth nothing once it runs beyond six or seven days?为什么天气预报超过六七天就没有价值？
  2. What kind of perfect observing system does the author ask us to imagine?作者设想了一个怎样的理想观测系统？
  3. Even with that system, how would errors still appear and grow?即使有这个系统，误差还会怎样产生和扩大？

🌍 Where it comes from出处与背景

The idea the passage dramatises was found by accident. A meteorologist running an early computer model restarted a calculation from printed numbers rounded to three decimal places instead of the six the machine held, and the forecast that came out had nothing to do with the first one. Out of that came a question with a famous shape: does the flap of a butterfly's wings in Brazil set off a tornado in Texas? The question was the title of a talk given at a scientific meeting in Washington in December 1972, and it is worth knowing that the butterfly was not the speaker's own image. He had used a seagull in earlier drafts, the title was supplied by a colleague when no title arrived in time, and he never answered the question. Asked again not long before he died in 2008, he still said he did not know.

本段渲染的那个发现，其实是偶然撞上的。一位气象学家在早期计算机模型上重跑一次计算，用的是打印出来的、四舍五入到小数后三位的数，而机器内部存的是六位——跑出来的预报和第一次毫无关系。由此有了一个句式著名的问题：巴西一只蝴蝶拍一下翅膀，会不会在得克萨斯掀起一场龙卷风？这问题是 1972 年 12 月华盛顿一次学术会议上一场报告的标题，而值得知道的是：蝴蝶并不是报告人自己的意象。他早先的稿子里用的是一只海鸥；标题是同事在截稿前替他起的；他自己从未回答这个问题。2008 年去世前不久再被问到，他仍说不知道。

✨ How it is written赏析

This is how you argue by thought experiment, and the method is worth copying. First grant your opponent everything: not a better network of stations but a perfect one, a sensor every foot all the way up, every reading exact, and a computer with no limit at all. Granting too much is the whole trick, because a conclusion that survives it cannot be escaped by saying we need more data. Then come the fake decimal points. The clock ticks 12.01, 12.02, 12.03, and the spurious precision of those numbers does the work an abstract word like amplification never could: you watch the error walk outward one foot, ten feet, and out to the planet. Notice too the two scales set side by side at the start, dust devils and eddies a continent wide, so that the word small has already been stretched before the argument begins.

这是怎样用思想实验来论证，值得照搬。第一步先把一切都让给对方：不是更好的观测网，而是一个完美的网——每一英尺一个传感器，一直铺到大气顶，每个读数都准确，再配一台毫无上限的计算机。让得过头正是全部诀窍：一个在这种条件下仍然成立的结论，就再也不能用「数据还不够多」来躲开。接着是那几个假小数点。钟走到 12.01、12.02、12.03，这种虚假的精确做到了「放大」这类抽象词永远做不到的事：你眼看着误差往外走一英尺、十英尺，一直走到整个行星。还要留意开头并置的两个尺度——尘卷风，和横跨一个大陆的涡旋——于是在论证开始之前，small 这个词已经被拉开了。

🧭 What it argues, and does it still hold?解读

The ceiling is real, and the floor under it keeps rising. The grid he describes was about sixty miles across; the European centre's physics-based model now runs at nine kilometres, and 2025 brought its largest single jump in skill in more than a decade. Something he could not have foreseen has also arrived: models that learn from decades of past weather rather than solving the equations. The same centre put a machine-learning forecast into operation in February 2025 and an ensemble version that July, running on a coarser thirty-one-kilometre grid and still beating the fine physics model on a number of fields, with rainfall gaining as much as a day of useful lead time. None of this touches his argument. Resolution cannot close a gap, because between any two sensors there is always a smaller motion that none of them sees. What the new models change is the price of each extra day, not whether the days run out. The honest summary for a reader today: better than the passage says in the first week, exactly as hopeless as it says in the second month.

天花板是真的，而底板一直在升。他说的那种网格约六十英里一格；欧洲中期天气预报中心的物理模型现在是 9 公里一格，2025 年还拿到了十多年来单次最大的技能跃升。还有一件他无法预见的事已经发生：有些模型不再解方程，而是从几十年的历史天气里学。同一家机构 2025 年 2 月把一个机器学习预报投入业务，同年 7 月又上了集合版本；它跑在更粗的 31 公里网格上，却在多项要素上胜过那个精细的物理模型，降水方面拿到多达一天的有效预报时效。这些都没有碰到他的论证。提高分辨率填不上缝隙，因为任意两个传感器之间，总有一个它们都看不见的更小的运动。新模型改变的是每多争一天要付多少代价，不是这些天会不会用光。给今天读者的如实总结：第一周比本文说的好得多，第二个月和本文说的一样没指望。

Sentence 5第 5 句 (begins开头 “and to a …”)

For small pieces of weather — … — any prediction deteriorates rapidly.

  • An explanation inserted between dashes: for somebody forecasting the whole globe, small can be as big as a thunderstorm. The unexpected sense of scale is the point.破折号插入说明：对全球预报员来说，「小」可以是雷暴和暴风雪那么大——用意外的比例感强调尺度。
  • small is used as a noun here, standing for the phrase already given.small 在这里作名词用（= small pieces of weather）。

Sentence 7第 7 句 (begins开头 “Errors and uncertainties …”)

  • The -ing form gives an accompanying action or a result.cascading…：现在分词作伴随/结果状语。
  • from ... up to ...: a range running from the smallest to the largest.from … up to …：表示范围由小到大。
  • A relative clause describing the eddies.that only satellites can see：定语从句修饰 eddies。

Sentence 12第 12 句 (begins开头 “But suppose the …”)

  • suppose + a past form (could be): a supposition contrary to fact, with the tone of 'just imagine it were possible'.suppose + 过去式（could be）：与事实相反的假设，口气是「假如真能……」。

Sentence 14第 14 句 (begins开头 “Suppose every sensor …”)

  • Here suppose is followed by the present tense: the author now treats the supposition as a case to reason inside, which is a step more committed than the could be of the sentence before.这里 suppose 后用一般现在时：作者把假设当成可设想的情况来展开推理（比上一句的 could be 更「投入」）。
  • A relative clause with the relative pronoun left out.a meteorologist would want：省略关系代词的定语从句。

Sentence 21第 21 句 (begins开头 “By 12.01, those …”)

  • by + a point in the future + the future perfect: by then it will already have happened.by + 将来时间点 + 将来完成时：到……时将已经……。

④ Grammar in this lesson本课语法

The future perfect: will have done将来完成时：will have done
By a given moment in the future, the action will already be complete. Common with by + a time, and with by the time + a clause in the present tense.
「到将来某时为止，某动作将已经完成」，常与 by + 时间、by the time + 从句（一般现在时）连用。

A common mistake: the by the time clause takes the present tense while the main clause takes the future perfect: By the time you arrive, I will have finished.

易错：by the time 从句用一般现在时，主句用将来完成时：By the time you arrive, I will have finished.

  • By next June, I will have worked here for ten years.
  • The film will have started by the time we get there.
Hypotheses with suppose / supposing / imaginesuppose / supposing / imagine 引导假设
suppose/supposing (that) ... puts up a case and then reasons from it. If the case could really happen, use the present tense; if it is contrary to fact or very unlikely, use a past form.
suppose/supposing (that) … 用于提出假设并推理；假设可能成立用一般现在时，假设与事实相反或可能性小用过去式（虚拟）。

In speech suppose can also make a suggestion: Suppose we go for a walk?

口语里 suppose 也可提建议：Suppose we go for a walk?

  • Suppose you won the lottery, what would you do?
  • Supposing it rains, shall we still go?
apart / away / of the order of: distance and rough quantityapart / away / of the order of：距离与数量的表达
For the gap between two things: A and B are + distance + apart. For distance from a place: distance + away (from). For a rough figure: of the order of (British), roughly, or some + number.
两物相距用 A and B are + 距离 + apart；离某处多远用 距离 + away (from)；约数可用 of the order of（英式）、roughly、some + 数字。

apart stresses the gap between things; away stresses the distance from a point.

注意 apart 强调「彼此」间隔，away 强调「离某处」。

  • The two towns are 20 miles apart.
  • The station is two miles away.

⑤ Patterns to reuse可以套用的句型

  • Beyond …, … are …, and beyond … they are … 超过……就……，超过……就根本……
    Beyond five minutes, a speech is tiring, and beyond twenty it is unbearable.
  • … is the reason. 原因就是……（短句收束）
    Lack of sleep is the reason.
  • Suppose …. Suppose …. … will still be unable to … 假设……，再假设……，……仍然无法……
    Suppose you had all the money in the world. Suppose you had perfect health. You would still be unable to buy more time.
  • By …, … will already have … 到……时，……就已经……
    By the time the letter arrives, the decision will already have been made.

Summary writing摘要写作

In no more than 80 words, explain why even perfect data and an unlimited computer could not give an accurate forecast a month ahead.

用不超过 80 个词解释为什么即使有完美的数据和计算机，也不能准确预报一个月以后的天气。

Points a good summary covers好摘要要覆盖的要点
  • A forecast is already guesswork after two or three days and worth nothing after six or seven, and the cause is the effect this lesson is named after.天气预报两三天后就是推测，六七天后就毫无价值，原因是蝴蝶效应。
  • Errors at the smallest scale grow stage by stage until they reach swirls as wide as a continent.小尺度的误差会层层放大，一直放大到大陆尺度的涡流。
  • Even a perfect sensor every foot across the planet, with an unlimited computer, could not say whether one town will be wet or dry a month later.即使全球每隔一英尺都布满完美传感器，并有无限强大的计算机，也预测不了一个月后的晴雨。
  • Between the sensors there are still small movements the computer knows nothing about, and minute by minute the errors widen until they cover the whole planet.因为传感器之间仍有计算机不知道的微小波动，误差会一分钟一分钟地扩大到全球。
Our model summary本站范文

Weather forecasts become guesswork after two or three days and useless after a week because of the Butterfly Effect: small errors multiply and spread to ever larger scales. Even if the earth were covered with perfect sensors a foot apart and an infinitely powerful computer used their data, it still could not predict rain a month ahead, because tiny unseen fluctuations between sensors would grow minute by minute until they affected the whole globe.（74 词）

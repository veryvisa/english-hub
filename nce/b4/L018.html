Lesson 18 · Porpoises 海豚

📘 New Concept English · Book 4 · Lesson 18 “Porpoises”新概念英语 第四册 Lesson 18《Porpoises》

C1🧠 26 new words个新词📝 Full notes有完整讲解

🎯 Before you open the lesson开课前
Sailors have long told each other that these animals push drowning men up to the surface and ring them round to keep the sharks off. The biologists at the aquarium say something smaller: when one of them pushes a person ashore it is far more likely to be curiosity or play than rescue. The second half is a piece of close observation, and funny: a group of young animals treat a sea turtle as a toy, turning it over and butting it until it pulls its feet in and gives up. The first half is argument that takes a belief apart (state the story, then give the better explanation); the second is comic description. The structures to take away are no sooner ... than, whether it be ..., and with + object + participle.
水手间流传海豚会救溺水者、会围成阵型保护人免遭鲨鱼——海洋馆的生物学家却说，海豚推人上岸多半出于好奇或好玩，不是为了救人。后半篇是生动的观察记录：一群小海豚把海龟当玩具，翻它、撞它，直到海龟把脚缩进壳里认输。前半是辟谣式论证（先摆传说，再给更合理的解释），后半是幽默的描写，句式上要学 no sooner … than、whether it be…、with + 宾语 + 分词。
Questions to keep in mind as you read读的时候带着这几个问题：
  1. What do sailors say about these animals, and what do the biologists make of it?水手们对海豚有什么传说？生物学家怎么看？
  2. Why might these animals seem to be keeping sharks away from a person?海豚为什么会「保护」人免遭鲨鱼？
  3. How do they play with a sea turtle?海豚是怎样戏弄海龟的？

🌍 Where it comes from出处与背景

The setting explains the writing. These are not animals watched at sea but animals watched through glass, for hours, day after day, by people whose job was to film them, which is why the second half of the passage can tell you what one particular young animal especially enjoyed doing. That is also the limit of the material. Everything here comes from a tank, from a small number of animals, and from a period when nobody was asking whether the tank was part of the finding. Notice too that the writer uses two names, porpoise and dolphin, for the same animals in the same paragraph; that was ordinary American usage at the time, and biologists today keep the two names for different families.

场景解释了文风。这些不是在海上看到的动物，而是透过玻璃、一连几个小时、一天又一天被人观察的动物——观察者的工作就是拍它们，所以本篇后半能告诉你某一只小海豚「特别爱」做什么。这同时也是材料的边界。这里的一切都来自一个水池、少数几只动物，以及一个还没人追问「水池本身算不算结论的一部分」的年代。还要留意：作者在同一段里用两个名字 porpoise 和 dolphin 指同一批动物；这在当时的美式用法里很平常，而今天的生物学家把这两个名字留给不同的科。

✨ How it is written赏析

The first half is a model of how to take a popular belief apart without calling anybody a fool. Step one, state the belief fully and fairly, and in the strongest version people actually hold. Step two, concede the quality that makes it plausible, which is what however intelligent they may be does. Step three, offer a smaller explanation that covers the same facts: curiosity, play. Step four, and this is the move most writers miss, produce the case that the kind explanation cannot cover, a soaked mattress pushed to the beach, which nobody can be said to have rescued. Then the tone changes completely. The second half has no argument in it at all, only one scene told in the present tense with the timing of comedy: the turtle gets his balance back and is hit again at once, and the sentence is built so that the second blow arrives before you expect it. Argue first, then show; and let the showing carry the feeling.

前半是「怎样拆掉一个流行说法而不骂任何人蠢」的范本。第一步，把这个说法完整公道地摆出来，而且摆的是人们真正持有的最强版本。第二步，承认那个让它显得合理的品质——however intelligent they may be 做的就是这件事。第三步，给一个更小的解释，它能覆盖同样的事实：好奇、玩。第四步——这一步多数写作者会漏——举出那个「善意解释覆盖不了」的例子：一张浸透水的床垫被推上海滩，谁也不能说它是被救的。接着语气完全转变。后半没有任何论证，只有一个用现在时讲出来的场景，而且是喜剧的节奏：海龟刚找回平衡，立刻又被撞一下，句子的搭法让第二下比你预期的更早到来。先论证，再展示；让展示去承担情绪。

🧭 What it argues, and does it still hold?解读

Seventy years on, the biologists quoted here still have the better of the argument, and the reason is not new evidence but an old rule: you can film the behaviour and you cannot film the motive. The best-known modern case shows how little that has changed. In 2008 on a beach in New Zealand a bottlenose dolphin that local people had named Moko turned up while rescuers were failing, after more than an hour, to get a stranded pygmy sperm whale and her calf off a sandbank, and by the rescuers' account the two whales then followed it out through a narrow channel to deep water. A scientist at the national museum called the report believable. There is still no study of the episode; the evidence is the word of the people on the beach, which is exactly the kind of evidence the passage is warning about. What has changed is the question underneath. Canada made keeping and breeding whales, dolphins and porpoises a criminal offence in 2019, with fines up to two hundred thousand dollars and an exemption for animals already held, which at the park in Niagara Falls meant about sixty-one of them, mostly belugas. So the charming second half of this passage now reads two ways at once. The young animal pushing a turtle about is still funny, and the fact that both of them are in a tank is no longer invisible.

七十年过去，本篇引用的生物学家仍然占理，理由不是新证据，而是一条老规矩：行为拍得到，动机拍不到。最有名的现代案例恰好说明这一点几乎没变。2008 年新西兰一处海滩，救援者花了一个多小时也没能把一头搁浅的小抹香鲸和它的幼崽弄离沙洲；一只被当地人起名 Moko 的宽吻海豚这时出现，按救援者的说法，两头鲸随后跟着它穿过一道窄水道游到深水。国家博物馆的一位研究者说这份报告可信。至今没有关于这件事的研究；证据就是海滩上那些人的说法——而这正是本篇提醒要当心的那类证据。变了的是底下那个问题。加拿大 2019 年把饲养与繁育鲸、海豚和鼠海豚定为刑事罪，罚款最高二十万加元，已在饲养中的动物获豁免；在尼亚加拉瀑布那家园区，这意味着约 61 头，多数是白鲸。所以本篇讨人喜欢的后半，今天同时有两种读法。那只推海龟玩的小海豚仍然好笑，而它和海龟都在一个水池里这件事，已经不再是看不见的了。

💬 Discuss and write讨论与写作
  1. The writer accepts the behaviour and refuses the motive. Try the same split on a story about an animal you have been told, and say what you would need to see to decide.
    作者接受行为、拒绝动机。拿别人跟你讲过的某个动物故事做同样的切分，并说你需要看到什么才能判断。
  2. Write 120 words taking apart a belief that many people hold: state it fairly, grant what makes it plausible, offer a smaller explanation, and end with the case the kind explanation cannot cover.
    用 120 词拆解一个很多人相信的说法：公道地摆出它、承认它合理之处、给一个更小的解释，最后举出善意解释覆盖不了的那个例子。
  3. Most of what this passage knows comes from watching animals in a tank. What can that setting show you, and what must it always get wrong?
    本篇所知的大部分来自观察水池里的动物。这种场景能让你看到什么，又必然会把什么看错？

③ How the hard sentences are built难句结构

Sentences are counted in the lesson text, title not included. Find the sentence in your book, then read our notes on its structure.句子按课文正文数（不含标题）。在教材里找到这一句，再看本站的结构讲解。

Sentence 1第 1 句 (begins开头 “There has long …”)

  • that opens a clause in apposition, spelling out what the belief actually is (see Lesson 11); the phrase naming the people who hold it comes in between.that 引导同位语从句，说明 superstition 的内容（见第 11 课）；中间隔着 among mariners。
  • There has long been: the present perfect of there be, for something that has gone on for a long time and still does.There has long been：现在完成时的 there be，「长期以来一直有……」。

Sentence 3第 3 句 (begins开头 “Marine Studio biologists …”)

  • however + adjective + subject + may be: a clause of concession, no matter how much of that quality they have.however + 形容词 + 主语 + may be：让步状语从句，无论多么……
  • it is a mistake to do: an empty subject.it is a mistake to do：形式主语。

Sentence 5第 5 句 (begins开头 “On the occasions …”)

  • The verb and its adverbial come first and the long object follows (see Lesson 13).push to shore + 长宾语后置（见第 13 课）。
  • out of + noun gives the reason; for + noun gives the purpose.out of curiosity / for sport：表原因与目的。
  • Words are left out after as: the full form would repeat what they do.as in riding…：as 后省略，= as (they do) in riding the bow waves。

Sentence 8第 8 句 (begins开头 “If, as has …”)

  • as here is a relative pronoun standing for the whole of the preceding clause: just as has been reported.as has been reported：as 作关系代词，指代整个 if 从句的内容，「正如已报道的那样」。
  • A guess about the past, with two because clauses side by side as the complement.it may have been because…：对过去的推测，两个 because 从句并列作表语。

Sentence 11第 11 句 (begins开头 “It is possible …”)

  • with + noun + participle: an accompanying or resulting circumstance; being driven is passive and in progress.with + 名词 + 分词：with 复合结构作伴随或结果状语；being driven 表被动进行。

Sentence 12第 12 句 (begins开头 “Whether it be …”)

  • whether it be ... or ...: a formal and slightly old-fashioned clause of concession; be is the bare form, where ordinary English would say is.whether it be … or …：虚拟形式的让步从句，be 用原形，= whether it is…；正式、略古雅。

Sentence 20第 20 句 (begins开头 “He no sooner …”)

  • no sooner ... than: one thing happens and the next follows at once. Note that the printed text has a plural where the verb form calls for a singular.no sooner … than：一……就……。课文录入有误，porpoises 应为单数 porpoise（comes）。
  • hit somebody another crack: give him one more blow.hit sb another crack：再给他一下。

④ Grammar in this lesson本课语法

no sooner ... than / hardly ... when, and inversionno sooner … than / hardly … when 与倒装
For one thing following straight after another: no sooner ... than, hardly or scarcely ... when. Usually the past perfect in the first clause and the simple past in the second; and when the negative word stands first, the subject and auxiliary are inverted.
「一……就……」：no sooner … than，hardly/scarcely … when；常用过去完成时 + 一般过去时；否定词放句首时要倒装。

Inverted: No sooner had he recovered than the next one hit him. / Hardly had I arrived when it started to rain.

倒装：No sooner had he recovered than the next porpoise hit him. / Hardly had I arrived when it started to rain.

Keep the pairs straight: no sooner goes with than, hardly goes with when.

注意：no sooner 后配 than，hardly 后配 when，不要混。

  • I had no sooner sat down than the phone rang.
  • Scarcely had the game begun when it started to rain.
Concession: however + adjective / whatever / whether it be ...让步：however + adj. / whatever / whether it be …
however + adjective or adverb + subject + verb (may strengthens it) means no matter how much. whatever and whoever open the same kind of clause. whether it be A or B is the formal version, with the bare form of the verb.
however + 形容词/副词 + 主语 + 谓语（may 可加强）＝无论多么……；whatever/whoever 引导让步；whether it be A or B 是虚拟形式的正式让步（无论是 A 还是 B）。
  • However hard he tried, he couldn't open the door.
  • All living things, whether they be plants or animals, need water.
with + object + participle / adjective / prepositional phrasewith + 宾语 + 分词/形容词/介词短语
The with structure works as an adverbial of accompanying circumstance, cause or result. If the object does the action, use the -ing form; if it undergoes it, the past participle; if it is undergoing it now, being done.
with 复合结构作状语，表伴随、原因或结果；宾语与分词是主动关系用 doing，被动用 done，正在被做用 being done。
  • He sat there with his eyes closed.
  • With prices rising so fast, we can't afford a holiday.

Keywords for retelling复述关键词

  • sailors' superstition: porpoises save drowning men, protect them from sharks
  • biologists: no life-saving motive; curiosity or sport (bow waves, mattress in 1928)
  • sharks and porpoises: natural enemies; scent of a meal
  • porpoises are intrigued by anything alive
  • games with turtles: raising, shoving, turning over
  • swoop and butt → turtle knocked to the floor → gives up

Summary writing摘要写作

In no more than 80 words, write a summary: first the sailors' belief and the biologists' explanation, then an outline of how the animals play with a turtle.

用不超过 80 个词写一段摘要：先说明水手的传说与生物学家的解释，再概述海豚戏弄海龟的情形。

Points a good summary covers好摘要要覆盖的要点
  • Sailors believe these animals push drowning men up and keep sharks off them.水手迷信海豚会救溺水者、保护人免遭鲨鱼。
  • The biologists think a person pushed ashore is more likely a matter of curiosity or play; the same animals once worked hard to push a soaked mattress up the beach.生物学家认为海豚把人推上岸更可能出于好奇或好玩；它们也曾拼命推一张浸水床垫上岸。
  • If they ever did keep sharks off somebody, curiosity may have drawn them in while the smell of food drew the sharks, and the two kinds are enemies anyway.若海豚真的「保护」过人，可能只因好奇被吸引，而鲨鱼被食物气味引来，两者本是天敌。
  • They are curious about anything alive, and they tease turtles for hours: lifting them, turning them over, butting them from above, until the turtle pulls its feet in and gives up.海豚对一切活物好奇，常戏弄海龟：顶它、翻它、从上面撞它，直到海龟缩脚认输。
Our model summary本站范文

Sailors have long believed that porpoises save drowning men and protect them from sharks, but biologists think porpoises push people ashore out of curiosity or for fun—they once pushed a soaked mattress ashore. If they have fought sharks near humans, it was probably because both were attracted for different reasons. Porpoises are curious about anything alive and enjoy bullying turtles, butting them down to the bottom until the turtles give up.（71 词）

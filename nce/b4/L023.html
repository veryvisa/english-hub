Lesson 23 · Bird Flight 鸟的飞行

📘 New Concept English · Book 4 · Lesson 23 “Bird Flight”新概念英语 第四册 Lesson 23《Bird Flight》

C1🧠 25 new words个新词📝 Full notes有完整讲解

🎯 Before you open the lesson开课前
A naturalist's essay written with a poet's ear. The writer divides bird flight roughly into two schools: the gliders, with the albatross as their king, which can hold station beside a ship for an hour on the air that the hull throws upward; and the powered fliers, led by the duck, whose steel-like muscles drive short wings against a head wind for great distances. The swallow has the gifts of both schools, crossing thousands of miles twice a year and feeding its young on the wing. The style is deliberately old-fashioned (a verb followed straight by not; reading the future in the way birds fly), and the plan is classify, give an example, compare, praise. What this lesson teaches best is comparison inside description.
一篇抒情色彩很浓的博物随笔。作者把鸟的飞行粗分两类：滑翔派以信天翁为王，借船舷上升的气流可以一小时不扇翅；动力派以野鸭为首，钢铁般的肌肉能顶着大风长途飞行。而燕子兼有两派之长，一年往返六千英里，边飞边喂雏。文风古雅（动词后直接加 not 的否定式；从鸟的飞行里「取兆」这类旧说法），用分类—举例—比较—赞美展开。这类文章最值得学的是「描写中的比较」。
Questions to keep in mind as you read读的时候带着这几个问题：
  1. Into which two classes does the writer divide bird flight, and which bird stands for each?作者把鸟的飞行分成哪两类？各以什么鸟为代表？
  2. Why can the albatross stay beside a ship for so long almost without beating its wings?信天翁为什么能长时间跟着船飞而几乎不扇翅膀？
  3. Why does the writer say the swallow has the gifts of both schools?为什么说燕子兼有两派的长处？

🌍 Where it comes from出处与背景

Sir William Beach Thomas (1868-1957) made his living in two very different ways. He was one of the small group of accredited British correspondents in France in the First World War, and he was knighted for that work; he also said afterwards that he had been deeply ashamed of his Somme dispatches, for the plain reason that they were untrue. The other half of his life was spent writing about hedges, weather and birds for British papers, and that is the voice you hear in this lesson: unhurried, fond of ranking things, at ease with an old-fashioned turn of phrase. Nature writing of this kind was a weekly fixture in British journalism between the wars, written for readers who knew the names of the birds and expected prose with a little music in it. The scene with the ship is itself a period detail - a passenger crossing an ocean by sea had days in which to watch one bird.

威廉·比奇·托马斯爵士（1868–1957）靠两种很不一样的活计谋生。一战时他是少数获准随军的英国记者之一，并因此获封爵士；后来他自己说，对索姆河战役的那些报道他「深感羞耻」，理由很直白：那些报道不真实。他另一半生涯用来给英国报纸写树篱、天气和鸟，这一课听到的就是那个声音：不慌不忙，爱给事物排座次，用旧式说法也很自在。两次大战之间，这类博物随笔是英国报刊每周的固定栏目，写给认得鸟名、也期待文章里有点音乐感的读者。船上的那个场景本身就是时代细节——乘船横渡大洋的人，才有连着好几天盯住一只鸟的工夫。

✨ How it is written赏析

This is a classification essay, and it does its sorting with rank words rather than with headings. One bird is a king, another is first of all in the opposite school, a third group is made up of humbler followers, and the swallow is given the gifts of both at the highest level. The hinge of the whole piece is a sentence of eight words that announces the second school after a long paragraph on the first; short sentences are worth saving for turns like that. Two other things to notice. The writer puts one word inside quotation marks when he reports what people say about machines beating the air, and that pair of marks does the work of a whole sentence of disagreement - a cheap and very effective tool. And the ending leaves the birds altogether: we no longer read the future in the direction a bird takes, and nobody now lifts his hat to a magpie. An essay that closes on what we have stopped believing says more about the reader than another paragraph of description could.

这是一篇分类文章，而它靠的是「排座次」的词，不是小标题。一只鸟是「王」，另一只在相反的流派里「居首」，第三群是「较卑微的追随者」，而燕子被给了两派的长处，还是最高的程度。全篇的枢纽是一个八词的短句：在整段写完第一派之后，用它宣布第二派——短句值得省下来，专用在这种转折处。另有两点可看。作者在转述人们夸耀机器征服天空时，把一个词放进引号，这对引号干了一整句反驳的活，又便宜又有效。还有结尾完全离开了鸟：我们不再从鸟飞的方向读未来，也没人再对喜鹊脱帽问好。一篇以「我们已不再相信什么」收尾的文章，比再来一段描写更能说出读者自己的处境。

🧭 What it argues, and does it still hold?解读

The description is still a pleasure, but the explanation in it has been replaced. The writer thinks the albatross gets its free ride from the ship: air pushed upward by the hull holds the bird up. Tracking work since the 1980s shows the bird is doing something quite different and would do it with no ship in sight. It uses dynamic soaring - climbing across the layer where wind speed changes sharply near the sea, turning downwind, descending into the trough of a wave, and repeating the loop, so the energy comes from the wind gradient and the waves rather than from any updraught. Studies of tracked wandering albatrosses even suggest the birds hold their speed well below what the physics would allow, probably to keep the load on their wings safe. Two of his other claims have been overtaken as well. The swallow that tires not has a rival: a 2016 study with tiny loggers found common swifts staying airborne for about ten months at a stretch, more than 99 per cent of the period, some of them never landing at all. And the six thousand miles depends on which population you follow: British ringing has long linked those swallows with southern Africa, while geolocators on central European birds show most of them stopping in the Congo basin, so the distance is a range, not a figure. The last line holds something for a Chinese reader. In Britain the magpie was the bird of ill luck, which is why the villagers greeted it politely - you spoke to it so that it would not harm you. A near relative of the same bird, in China, is the bird of good news, and a wedding ornament. Same family, opposite omen; the superstition the writer says we have outgrown was never one superstition.

这些描写今天读来依然有味，但里面的解释已经被换掉了。作者认为信天翁搭的是船的便车：船舷把气流向上顶，托住了鸟。上世纪八十年代以来的追踪研究显示，这鸟干的是另一回事，而且没有船它照样干：它用的是「动力翱翔」——斜着爬升穿过海面附近风速急变的那一层，转向顺风，再俯冲回波谷，如此循环；能量来自风速梯度和海浪，不是上升气流。对戴标的漂泊信天翁的研究甚至提示，它们把速度压在物理上限之下，大概是为了让机翼承受的力保持安全。他另外两处说法也被超过了。「不知疲倦」的燕子有了对手：2016 年一项用微型记录器的研究发现，普通雨燕可以连续约十个月待在空中，超过 99% 的时间不着陆，有的干脆一次没落地。而「六千英里」要看你跟的是哪一群：英国的环志长期把当地燕子与非洲南部联系起来，而给中欧燕子戴的光敏记录器显示，多数停在刚果盆地——所以这是一个区间，不是一个数。最后一句对中国读者还有点意思：在英国，喜鹊是报凶的鸟，所以村民才要客客气气地跟它打招呼——跟它说话，是为了让它别来害你。同一科的近亲在中国是报喜的鸟，还上婚庆图样。同样的鸟，相反的兆头；作者说我们已经摆脱的那种迷信，从来就不是一种迷信。

    在你长大的地方，有哪种鸟、动物或数字算吉或算凶？人们实际上会为它做什么，他们真信吗？
  2. Write 120 words sorting something you know well into two schools - cooks, drivers, students, players - give one example of each and one case that belongs to both.
    写 120 词，把你熟悉的一类人或事分成两派（厨师、司机、学生、球员都行）：各举一例，再举一个同时属于两派的例子。
  3. The writer explains the albatross with the wrong mechanism but describes it exactly. How much does a wrong explanation damage a piece of writing like this, and what would you keep if you were editing it today?
    作者把信天翁的机制说错了，可描写分毫不差。对这样一篇文章来说，错误的解释有多大损害？如果今天由你来编，你会保留什么？

  • quite the same: exactly the same; do not confuse it with not quite, which means almost.quite the same：完全一样；与 not quite（不完全）不同。

  • will here states a habit or a characteristic: this is simply what they do.will 表习性、惯常（它们就是会这样）。

④ Grammar in this lesson本课语法

Complete and partial negatives: no two / none / not all全部否定 vs 部分否定：no two / none / not all
no, none, neither, never and nothing make a complete negative; not all, not every and not both make a partial one (see Lesson 7). No two ... the same is complete: no pair at all is alike.
no, none, neither, never, nothing 表全部否定；not all, not every, not both 表部分否定（见第 7 课）。No two … the same 是全部否定：任何两个都不一样。

Compare: Not all birds fly the same way (some do, some do not).

对比：Not all birds fly the same way.（有的一样，有的不一样）

  • No two people have the same fingerprints.
  • None of the answers was right.
Old-fashioned forms and rhetoric: tires not / nor does it / will for a habit古雅与修辞：tires not / nor does it / will 表习性
Literary English sometimes keeps the old negative (verb + not); nor and neither at the head of a clause cause inversion; will and would can state a habit or a characteristic.
文学性文字偶尔用古式否定（verb + not）；nor/neither 句首倒装；will/would 表示习惯性行为或特性。

In modern writing use It never tires instead of tires not; will for a habit is still ordinary English: Boys will be boys.

现代写作中可用 It never tires 代替 tires not；will 表习性在现代英语仍常用：Boys will be boys.

  • Oil will float on water.（特性）
  • He would sit for hours by the river.（过去习惯）
as well as / like: adding and comparingas well as / like：附加与类比
as well as means in addition to and joins two equal parts; when it joins two subjects the verb agrees with the first. like + noun is a preposition meaning in the same way as; and like them means the same is true of them.
as well as＝除……之外还，连接两个对等成分，作主语时谓语与前一个主语一致；like + 名词＝像……一样（介词），and like them…＝它们也一样。

Agreement: The teacher, as well as the students, is coming.

主谓一致：The teacher, as well as the students, is coming.

  • She speaks French as well as English.
  • Like his father, he became a doctor.

  • In the contrary school, Y is supreme. 在相反的一派中，Y 首屈一指
    In the contrary school, the slow and steady reader is supreme.
  • … with such … power that … 以如此……的力量，以至于……
    The wind blew with such force that trees fell across the road.

Summary writing摘要写作

In no more than 80 words, compare the two classes of flight and say what is special about the swallow.

用不超过 80 个词比较两类鸟的飞行，并说明燕子的特点。

Points a good summary covers好摘要要覆盖的要点
  • Bird flight differs endlessly, but it falls roughly into two classes.鸟的飞行千差万别，但大致可分两类。
  • The gliders are led by the albatross, which rides the air thrown up by a ship and hardly beats its wings, but has to give way to a head wind.滑翔派以信天翁为王，借船舷上升的气流几乎不扇翅膀就能跟船飞行，但必须向逆风让步。
  • The powered fliers are led by the duck, with the pigeon beside it: strong muscles carry them long distances against a gale, while partridges push hard but tire early.动力派以野鸭为首，鸽子也是，肌肉强健，能顶着大风长途飞行；鹧鸪等飞得有力但容易累。
  • The swallow has the strengths of both: it does not tire, it crosses thousands of miles twice a year, it feeds its young in the air, and it makes way even against the wind.燕子兼有两派之长：不知疲倦，往返六千英里，边飞边喂雏，逆风中也能穿行。
Our model summary本站范文

Birds' flight varies endlessly, but there are two broad classes. Gliders, led by the albatross, use rising air currents, such as those from a ship's sides, to fly for long periods almost without moving their wings, though they must give way to opposing winds. Powerful fliers like ducks and pigeons use strong muscles to beat through gales. The swallow combines both: tireless, it flies thousands of miles and feeds its young in flight.（73 词）

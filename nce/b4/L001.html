🎯 Before you open the lesson开课前
How do peoples without writing keep their history? The author moves from spoken legends to stone tools: legends reach back about two thousand years, and before that only stone, which does not rot, is left. This first lesson of Book 4 is a classic chain of reasoning in expository prose — each sentence answers the question the sentence before it leaves open. Watch the linking words but / so / fortunately, however, and the logic of the whole piece falls into place.
没有文字的民族靠什么保存历史？作者从口头传说讲到石器：传说能追溯两千年，再往前只剩不会腐烂的石头工具。本课是第四册的开篇，典型的说明文推理链——每一句都在回答上一句留下的问题。读的时候盯住 but / so / fortunately, however 这些转折词，整篇的逻辑就出来了。
Questions to keep in mind as you read读的时候带着这几个问题：
  1. How do peoples without writing keep their history?没有文字的民族用什么方式保存历史？
  2. Why is it stone tools that survive, rather than wood, leather or even human bone?为什么最后留下来的是石器，而不是木器、皮革，甚至人骨？

🌍 Where it comes from出处与背景

The piece belongs to the popular-archaeology writing of the mid-twentieth century, when the main evidence for human origins was still bones and worked stone. Its picture of the Pacific reflects that period: it puts the Polynesians' homeland in Indonesia and their arrival about two thousand years ago. Since then two new kinds of evidence have arrived. Radiocarbon dating and ancient DNA now trace the ancestors of the Polynesians back through Island Southeast Asia to Taiwan, with the first settlers of Tonga and Vanuatu arriving about 3,000 years ago and the far east of Polynesia (Hawaii, New Zealand, Rapa Nui) settled only in the last thousand years or so. The stone-tool record has also grown far older: tools found at Lomekwi in Kenya are dated to about 3.3 million years, older than our own genus.

这篇属于二十世纪中叶的通俗考古写作，那时研究人类起源的主要证据还是骨头和打制石器。文中的太平洋图景也停在那个年代：说波利尼西亚人来自印度尼西亚、约两千年前到达。此后多了两类新证据。放射性碳测年和古 DNA 把波利尼西亚人的祖先经东南亚岛屿一路追到台湾：汤加、瓦努阿图的第一批定居者约在三千年前到达，而波利尼西亚最东端（夏威夷、新西兰、复活节岛）要到最近一千年左右才有人定居。石器记录也大大提前：肯尼亚洛迈奎（Lomekwi）出土的石器约有 330 万年，比「人属」本身还早。

✨ How it is written赏析

The paragraph is built as a ladder of questions. Each sentence closes one gap and opens the next: writing is recent, so how did older peoples remember? By legends. How far back do legends reach? Not far enough for the first modern humans. Then what is left? Only what cannot rot. Notice how the turn words carry the argument — but, so, fortunately, however — and how the last sentence ends on a strong contrast placed inside a when-clause: the tools have outlasted even the bones of the hands that made them. That final image is what you remember. When you write an explanation of your own, try the same shape: begin with what the reader already accepts, remove one possibility at a time, and save the concrete, surprising fact for the end.

这一段是一架「问题梯子」：每一句堵上一个缺口，又打开下一个——文字出现得晚，那更早的人靠什么记事？靠传说。传说能往回追多远？追不到最早的现代人。那还剩什么？只剩不会腐烂的东西。整篇的推理靠转折词撑着：but、so、fortunately、however；最后一句把强烈的对比放进 when 从句：工具比造工具的那双手的骨头还活得久。读完记住的正是这个画面。自己写说明文时可以照搬这个结构：从读者已接受的事实出发，一次排除一种可能，把具体又出人意料的事实留到最后。

🧭 What it argues, and does it still hold?解读

The author's logic still works, but one premise has changed. He assumes that without writing or legends, stone is almost the only witness. Today ancient DNA lets scientists read relationships and migrations directly from bones and even from cave sediment, and this work won Svante Pääbo the 2022 Nobel Prize. So the honest update is: stone tools remain the longest record of human behaviour, but no longer the only record of human history. For Chinese readers the contrast is useful. Chinese culture is proud of a long written record, from oracle bones onwards, yet it is only about 3,300 years deep; everything before it, in China as everywhere, has to be read from tools, pots, bones and genes.

作者的推理至今成立，但有一个前提变了。他默认：没有文字、没有传说，几乎只能靠石头作证。今天，古 DNA 能直接从骨头、甚至从洞穴沉积物里读出亲缘和迁徙，斯万特·帕博因此获得 2022 年诺贝尔生理学或医学奖。所以如实的更新是：石器仍是人类行为最长的记录，但已不是人类历史唯一的记录。对中国读者来说这个对照很有意思：我们以悠久的文字记录自豪，从甲骨文算起也不过约 3300 年；在此之前，中国和世界各地一样，都得从工具、陶器、骨头和基因里去读。

④ Grammar in this lesson本课语法

Modal + have done: guessing about the past情态动词 + have done：对过去的推测
may/might/must/can't + have + past participle = '(at that time) possibly / certainly / could not have ...' — a guess about the past.
may/might/must/can't + have + 过去分词＝「（当时）可能／一定／不可能已经……」，推测的是过去。

From most to least certain: must have done (certainly) > should/would have done (it is likely) > may/might/could have done (possibly) > can't/couldn't have done (impossible).

把握程度从高到低：must have done（一定）＞ should/would have done（按理会）＞ may/might/could have done（可能）＞ can't/couldn't have done（不可能）。

Note that should have done has a second meaning: something that ought to have happened but did not (regret or blame). Decide from the context.

注意 should have done 还有另一个意思：本应该做却没做（带遗憾、责备）。看上下文判断。

  • He must have left early — his coat is gone. 他一定早走了。
  • She can't have seen us; she didn't wave. 她不可能看到我们。
  • You should have told me. 你本该告诉我的（但没说）。
neither ... nor ... and noneneither … nor … 与 none 的用法
neither A nor B = not A and not B; none = not one, out of three or more (or of an uncountable whole).
neither A nor B＝A 和 B 都不；none 指三者及以上（或不可数整体）中「一个也没有」。

When neither ... nor ... joins two subjects, the verb agrees with the nearer one: Neither the teacher nor the students were there.

neither … nor … 连接两个主语时，谓语动词与靠近的那个一致（就近原则）：Neither the teacher nor the students were there.

When neither opens a negative reply, the subject and auxiliary are inverted: Neither did I. / Neither can she.

neither 放句首表否定时要倒装：Neither did I. / Neither can she.

With none of + plural noun as subject, formal writing often uses a singular verb; in speech both are fine: None of them was/were ready.

none of + 复数名词作主语，正式文体常用单数谓语，口语单复数都可：None of them was/were ready.

  • Neither my brother nor I am good at maths.（就近与 I 一致）
  • "I don't like it." — "Neither do I."
Participle phrases after a noun名词后的分词短语（后置定语）
A present participle (-ing) after a noun is active or ongoing; a past participle (-ed) is passive or completed. Both are shortened relative clauses.
现在分词（-ing）作后置定语表主动/进行，过去分词（-ed）表被动/完成；都相当于省略了的定语从句。

How to decide: make the noun the subject and ask whether it does the action or has it done to it. Legends are handed down, so handed; people live on the islands, so living.

判断方法：把被修饰的名词当主语，看它是「做」还是「被做」。传说「被」传下来 → handed；人们「住」在岛上 → living。

  • The man standing at the door is my uncle.
  • Most of the goods made in this factory are exported.

⑤ Patterns to reuse可以套用的句型

  • The only way (that) … is to do … ……的唯一办法就是……
    The only way to learn a language well is to use it every day.
  • neither A nor B 既没有 A 也没有 B
    The village had neither running water nor electricity.
  • … when even … （对比） 连……都已……，……却还在
    The tools remained when even the bones had disappeared.
  • Fortunately, however, … 不过幸运的是……（转回正面）
    The road was closed. Fortunately, however, there was another way.

Summary writing摘要写作

In no more than 80 words, answer: why can archaeologists study the first modern men only through stone tools? (The Book 4 method: find the main point of each paragraph, put it in your own words and join the points with linking words; do not copy long sentences.)

用不超过 80 个词写一段摘要，回答：为什么考古学家只能靠石器来研究最早的现代人？（第四册的摘要写法：先找出每段的主干信息，用自己的话、用连接词串成一段，不照抄长句。）

Points a good summary covers好摘要要覆盖的要点
  • Some peoples cannot write and keep their history only in legends passed down by word of mouth.有的民族没有文字，只能靠代代口传的传说保存历史。
  • The first modern men lived so long ago that no legends survive.最早的现代人年代太久，连传说也没有留下。
  • Only the stone tools have survived, and they are the archaeologists' evidence.只有石器保存了下来，成为考古学家的线索。

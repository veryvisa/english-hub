📖 Understanding the piece读懂这篇

The credit in the older edition of Book 4 reads: Winston Churchill, Painting as a Pastime - the small book that Odhams Press brought out in 1948. But the words are older than the book, and the lesson's own title tells you how much older. The essay first appeared in the Strand Magazine in two parts: Hobbies in December 1921 and Painting as a Pastime in January 1922, and this passage belongs to the first of them. So the title given in the textbook is the one the author gave this half of the piece when he published it, twenty-seven years before the book that is credited for it. We verified the two Strand parts, their months and the 1948 volume from the Churchill Centre's account and from a bookseller's description of the first edition; we have not seen the 1921 magazine itself.

旧版第四册的署名是：温斯顿·丘吉尔《Painting as a Pastime》——Odhams Press 1948 年出的那本小书。但文字比书老得多，而本课自己的标题正好说明老多少：这篇随笔最早分两部分刊于《Strand Magazine》——1921 年 12 月的 Hobbies 与 1922 年 1 月的 Painting as a Pastime，而这一段属于前一部分。所以教材给的标题，正是作者当年发表这半篇时给它起的名字，比署名里那本书早了二十七年。两部分的刊期与 1948 年成书我们核过（丘吉尔中心的说明与一份首版售书描述）；1921 年那期杂志本身没有看到。

🌍 Where it comes from出处与背景

This is not general advice. It is a man describing his own treatment, and the dates make that plain. He had begun to paint in the summer of 1915, in a rented farmhouse in Surrey, in the weeks after the Dardanelles disaster had cost him the Admiralty and, as he thought at the time, his career; a sister-in-law put a brush in his hand and he kept it for the rest of his life. The essay was written six years later, and 1921 had been the worst year he would have until the war: his mother died suddenly that June, and in August his youngest child, Marigold, died of an infection, two and a half years old. The piece appeared in a popular monthly that December. Read the first paragraph again with that in view. The mind that fastens on one thing and refuses to loosen, the reasoning that does no good, the thing that has to be slipped gently into its grip - none of it is a figure of speech, and the cheerfulness of the rest of the page is the kind that has been paid for.

这不是泛泛的劝告，而是一个人在描述他自己的疗法；年份把这件事说清了。1915 年夏天他开始画画，地点是在萨里郡租的一处农舍，时间是达达尼尔海峡的惨败让他丢掉海军部、而他当时以为连政治生涯也一起丢掉之后的那几个星期；一位妯娌把画笔塞进他手里，他拿了一辈子。这篇随笔写在六年之后，而 1921 年是直到那场战争之前他最糟的一年：那年 6 月母亲骤然去世，8 月最小的孩子玛丽戈尔德因感染去世，两岁半。文章当年 12 月刊在一份大众月刊上。带着这些再读第一段：那个抓住一样东西不肯松手的心、那种讲道理没有用、那个必须被轻轻塞进它掌心的东西——没有一样是修辞；而这一页其余部分的那种明快，是付过代价的明快。

✨ How it is written赏析

Everything on this page is built in pairs and threes, because it was written to be heard. The three classes of people arrive in three clauses of identical shape, each ending in the same three words, so that the list falls like three hammer blows. Then the famous line turns one clause inside out to make the next - doing what you like against liking what you do - and the trick works because the words are the same and only the order has changed; nothing can be forgotten that is built that way. Look also at the extended figure. A hobby is not described as being like a seed; it is simply treated as one, through three sentences - chosen, planted in good ground, tended - and because the metaphor is never announced, it never has to be defended. And notice the one soft place, which is the best writing on the page: slipping something gently into a grip that will not open. It is a physical action, it is exactly the right size for the idea, and it is offered instead of an argument. Two things to carry away: if you want a sentence remembered, reverse it; and if you want a reader to accept a comparison, do not tell him it is one.

这一页上的每样东西都是成对、成三地搭起来的，因为它本来是写给耳朵的。三类人用三个同构的分句出场，每句都以同样的三个词收尾，于是这份名单像三记锤子落下。接着那句名言把一个分句翻过来造出下一句——「做你喜欢的事」对「喜欢你做的事」——这一手之所以成立，是因为词一样、只换了次序；这样搭起来的句子忘不掉。再看那个展开的比喻：爱好并不是「像」一粒种子，而是直接被当成种子处理，连着三句——挑选、落在好地里、精心照料；而正因为这个比喻从未被宣布，它也就从不需要被辩护。还要注意那一处柔软的地方，它是全页最好的文字：把一样东西轻轻塞进一只不肯打开的手里。它是一个物理动作，大小恰好配得上那个念头，而且它是被拿来代替论证的。两件事可以带走：想让一个句子被记住，就把它翻过来；想让读者接受一个比喻，就不要告诉他这是比喻。

🧭 What it argues, and does it still hold?解读

The main claim has since been measured, and it holds. The largest study of the question pooled five long-running surveys covering 93,263 people aged 65 and over in sixteen countries and found that having a hobby went with fewer depressive symptoms and with higher happiness and life satisfaction; the effect was small but consistent across every country in the set. The authors are careful about what that does and does not show: the data are observational, and the relationship probably runs both ways, since people in better spirits are also more likely to take something up. Two of his other claims come out less well. The first is his timetable. He says a hobby cannot be got up on the spur of the moment, and that beginning it late in life is no good at all; the evidence gives him no support there: the study that found the benefit was a study of people over sixty-five, measuring whether they had a hobby rather than when they began one, so the strongest finding we have concerns exactly the age at which he says it is too late. The second is his sociology. He tells the manual labourer not to want football on a Saturday and the clerk not to want more sitting down, and the logic - that what rests you is the opposite of what tires you - is tidy enough to be suspicious; the same study found the share of people with a hobby running from about half in one country to nearly all in another, which looks less like the three human types of this essay than like differences in what each society makes available and respectable. That survey also carries one number worth pausing on for Chinese readers: among the sixteen countries, China's reported figure was the lowest, around 38 per cent, and the authors warn in the same breath that respondents there were asked only about hobbies done with other people, so the figure is not comparable with the rest. It is a useful lesson in reading statistics about one's own country: the number is real, the comparison is not. And the classical suspicion of pastimes - the old warning that a man absorbed in his playthings loses his purpose - is the position this essay was written against, from a man who left several hundred canvases and a reputation that did not suffer for them.

核心主张后来被测量过，而它站住了。关于这件事最大的研究汇总了五项长期调查、覆盖十六个国家 93,263 名 65 岁以上的人，发现「有一项爱好」与更少的抑郁症状、更高的幸福感和生活满意度相关；效应不大，但在样本里的每个国家都一致。作者对这说明了什么、不说明什么很谨慎：数据是观察性的，关系很可能是双向的——心情较好的人也更可能去拾起一样东西。他另外两个主张的成绩就差一些。第一个是他的时间表：他说爱好不能临时拼凑、上了年纪才开始没有用；而证据在这一点上并不支持他——发现益处的那项研究研究的正是六十五岁以上的人，量的是「有没有」爱好而不是「什么时候开始」，于是我们手上最强的发现，恰好落在他说「太晚了」的那个年龄段上。第二个是他的社会学：他叫体力劳动者周六不要想着足球、叫坐办公室的不要再坐着，而那个逻辑——让你休息的东西是让你累的东西的反面——整齐得有点可疑；同一项研究发现，有爱好的人口比例从一个国家的约一半到另一个国家的近乎全部，这看起来不太像本文那三种人性类型，更像各个社会所提供的、以及所认为体面的东西不一样。那项调查里还有一个数值得中国读者停一下：十六国之中，中国报出的比例最低，约 38%，而作者在同一句话里提醒，那里的受访者只被问了「与别人一起做的爱好」，所以这个数与其余各国不可比。这是关于「怎么读与自己国家有关的统计」的一堂有用的课：数字是真的，比较不是。至于古来对玩物的那份疑心——「玩物丧志」的老话——恰恰是这篇文章要反对的立场；而写它的人留下了几百幅画布，名声也并未因此受损。

💬 Discuss and write讨论与写作
  1. Write the first paragraph of this essay again in your own words, for somebody you know who is worrying about one thing and cannot stop. Six sentences, and no abstract nouns.
    把这篇随笔的第一段用你自己的话重写一遍，写给你认识的、正为一件事反复忧虑而停不下来的某个人。六句话，不许用抽象名词。
  2. He says the rest you need is the opposite of the work that tires you. Test it against your own week: what actually rests you, and does it fit his rule? If it does not, what rule would you write instead?
    他说你需要的休息，是让你疲惫的那种工作的反面。拿你自己的一个星期检验一下：真正让你恢复的是什么？它符合他的规则吗？如果不符合，你会改写成什么规则？
  3. There is an old warning that a person absorbed in a pastime loses sight of what matters, and this essay argues the reverse. Take a side in 120 words, and put the strongest version of the other side first.
    有一句老话警告说，沉迷于玩乐的人会丢掉正事；而这篇随笔主张的恰恰相反。用 120 词选一边，并先把另一边最有力的版本写出来。

Sentence 2第 2 句 (begins开头 “the mind catches …”)

  • catch hold of: seize. And will here means habitual refusal, not the future: it simply will not let go.catch hold of：抓住；will not：此处 will 表习性、固执（「就是不肯」），不表将来。

Sentence 4第 4 句 (begins开头 “The stronger the …”)

  • the plus a comparative, twice over (Lesson 37), with is left out of both halves.the + 比较级, the + 比较级（L37），两个分句都省略了 is：= The stronger the will is, the more futile the task is.
  • The sense: the harder you push, the less good it does.意思：意志越强，跟忧虑硬拼就越徒劳。

Sentence 6第 6 句 (begins开头 “And if this …”)

  • Two if-clauses stand side by side in front of the main clause, which gives the sentence its rising movement.两个 if 条件从句并列在前，主句在后，形成递进的节奏。
  • be attended by: accompanied by.be attended by：伴随着。
  • Two adverbs that pull in opposite directions, placed together: slowly, and yet often very fast.gradually, and often quite swiftly：两个对立的副词并列，表示「慢慢地，又常常很快地」。

Sentence 12第 12 句 (begins开头 “they must be …”)

  • A hobby is treated as a seed: chosen, planted in good ground, carefully looked after - three parallel musts.把爱好比作种子：选种、落在好地里、精心照料——三个 must 排比。
  • if plus be to do, in the sense of wanting to (Lesson 42).if … are to be：if + be to do 表「如果想要」（L42）。
  • A reduced time clause: when they are needed.when needed：省略的时间状语从句，= when they are needed。

Sentence 17第 17 句 (begins开头 “It is no …”)

  • It is no use doing: it is the formal subject, and the real subject is the gerund.It is no use doing：做……没用（it 形式主语，真正主语是动名词）。
  • The two halves are each other turned around, a figure the Greeks called chiasmus.前后两半句互为倒置（do what you like / like what you do），修辞上叫「回文式对照」（chiasmus）。
  • have got to is the spoken British form of have to.have got to = have to（英式口语）。

Sentence 22第 22 句 (begins开头 “It is no …”)

  • invite somebody to do something: here a non-defining relative clause has been dropped in between the object and the infinitive.invite sb to do：动名词 inviting 的宾语与不定式补语之间夹了一个非限制性定语从句。
  • The present perfect continuous: it has been going on for six days.has been working or worrying：现在完成进行时，表持续了六天。

④ Grammar in this lesson本课语法

It is no use / no good doing: there is no point in itIt is no use / no good doing：做……没用
It is no use / no good / useless plus a gerund means there is no point in doing it. There is no point (in) doing is another way to say it, and it is the formal subject.
It is no use / no good / useless + 动名词：「做……是没用的」；也说 There is no point (in) doing；it 是形式主语。

Note the form: no use takes a gerund, never an infinitive. But the adjective useless does take one: It is useless to do.

注意：no use 后接动名词，不接不定式（✗ It is no use to start）；但 It is useless to do（形容词 useless 后接不定式）。

Related: It is worth doing; and the rhetorical question What's the use of doing ...? (Lesson 32).

相近：It is worth doing（值得做）；What's the use of doing …?（做……有什么用？——反问，L32）

  • It's no use crying over spilt milk.（覆水难收）
  • There's no point in arguing with him.
Parallel structure: what you join must match平行结构：并列成分要同类、同形
Items joined by and, or, but, not only ... but also or either ... or should match grammatically - noun with noun, gerund with gerund, clause with clause. That is what makes a long sentence clear and strong.
用 and, or, but, not only … but also, either … or 等连接的成分，要在语法上对等（名词对名词、动名词对动名词、从句对从句），这样句子才清楚有力。

A common fault: She likes swimming, to read and cooking - it should be swimming, reading and cooking.

常见错误：She likes swimming, to read and cooking.（✗）→ swimming, reading and cooking。

Things compared must match too: The climate here is milder than that of Toronto (Lesson 35).

比较对象也要平行：The climate here is milder than that of Toronto.（L35）

  • He came, he saw, he conquered.
Modals of obligation: must / ought to / have got to / need表义务的情态：must / ought to / have got to / need
must gives the speaker's own demand; have (got) to points to an outside necessity; ought to and should give advice about what is right; need states what is necessary, and needn't means there is no need.
must 强调说话人的主观要求；have (got) to 强调客观需要；ought to / should 表示「理应」的建议；need 表必要（否定 needn't＝不必）。

The negatives are far apart in meaning: mustn't forbids, while don't have to and needn't only say there is no need.

否定差别很大：mustn't＝禁止；don't have to / needn't＝不必。

must has no past form; for a past necessity use had to.

过去时：must 没有过去式，过去的「必须」用 had to。

  • You must stop smoking.（医生的要求）
  • I have to get up at six because the train leaves early.（客观需要）
  • You needn't come if you're busy.

Points a good summary covers好摘要要覆盖的要点

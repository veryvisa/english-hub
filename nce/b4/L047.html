📘 New Concept English · Book 4 · Lesson 47 “The Great Escape”新概念英语 第四册 Lesson 47《The Great Escape》

🎯 Before you open the lesson开课前
People assume that camping is about saving money, and then you look at the cars parked beside the tents: a Bentley, a Mercedes. What really draws people, the writer argues, is escape - escape from the grand hotel where you never know how much to hand the doorman, and the pleasure of being able to leave whenever you choose. Critics say campers hide in their tents and never meet the people whose country they are in; he answers that a campsite is a great deal more international than a holiday hotel, where at supper time the smell of German cabbage competes with the smell of French garlic. This is social comment with a smile, and the sentence shapes are worth collecting: adjectives and nouns thrown to the front to make a concession (Affluent he may be), that-clauses doing duty as subjects, Granted conceding a point before it is taken away.
人们以为露营是图便宜，其实营地里停着宾利、奔驰；作者认为露营真正的吸引力是一种「逃离」：逃离高级酒店里不知该给门童多少小费的尴尬，享受有车一族「想走就走」的自由。有人批评露营者躲在帐篷里、不接触当地人，作者反驳说营地比度假酒店国际化得多——德国酸菜和法国大蒜的味道在饭点争奇斗艳。本课是带幽默的社会评论，句式灵活：形容词、名词提到句首的让步倒装（Affluent he may be），that 从句作主语，Granted 让步，处处是可以借用的书面表达。
Questions to keep in mind as you read读的时候带着这几个问题：
  1. What draws people to camping, besides the saving?除了省钱，露营还有哪些动机？
  2. In what way does camping free the modern traveller from fear?为什么说露营让现代旅行者摆脱了「恐惧」？
  3. How does the writer answer the charge that campers never get to know the local people?作者怎样反驳「露营者无法了解当地人」的说法？

📖 Understanding the piece读懂这篇

The credit in the older edition of Book 4 reads: Nigel Buxton, The Great Escape, from The Weekend Telegraph, the colour magazine of the Daily Telegraph. Buxton (1924-2015) joined the Sunday Telegraph as a travel columnist in 1961 and edited its travel pages for some twenty-eight years; he also wrote A Penguin Guide to Travel in Europe (1965) and a wine-country walking book that won a prize in 1993. So this is a professional travel editor writing in the middle of his career, not an enthusiast with a tent. We verified his dates, his post and the books from an encyclopedia entry and press reports; we did not find the issue or the date of the article.

旧版第四册的署名是：Nigel Buxton，The Great Escape，出自 The Weekend Telegraph——《每日电讯报》的彩色杂志。Buxton（1924–2015）1961 年进《星期日电讯报》做旅游专栏作家，主持其旅游版约二十八年；他还写过《A Penguin Guide to Travel in Europe》（1965）和一本 1993 年获奖的葡萄酒产区徒步书。所以写这篇的是一位职业旅游编辑、正当盛年，不是一个带着帐篷的爱好者。生卒年、职务与著作我们核过（百科条目与报道）；文章的期号与日期没有查到。

🌍 Where it comes from出处与背景

The piece sits at the beginning of something, which is why it reads as reporting rather than nostalgia. Mass holiday travel was then new and growing fast: international arrivals worldwide were about 25 million in 1950 and about 278 million by 1980, so the years this was written in are the steep part of that curve, and the 1.4 billion of 2024 was unimaginable. What was new in particular was the car. The sentences about owning the means of arriving and the means of escape only make sense in a decade when ordinary families were acquiring, for the first time, a machine that let them leave the country on a whim; the campsite was the hotel for people who had a car before they had a travel agent. This also explains the odd social anxiety in the middle of the page, the business of the doorman and the chambermaid. The grand hotel had been built for a class that knew its rules, and here is a new kind of customer who can afford the bill and not the etiquette. He is not being laughed at. He is the reader.

这篇文章站在某件事的开头，所以它读起来像报道，而不是怀旧。大众度假旅行在当时是新事物，而且长得很快：全世界国际旅客到访数在 1950 年约 2,500 万，到 1980 年约 2.78 亿——写这篇的年份正落在那条曲线陡起的一段上，而 2024 年的约 14 亿当时无法想象。新的东西里头，最新的是汽车。那些关于「到达的手段和撤离的手段都归自己掌握」的句子，只有放在普通家庭第一次拥有一台能让他们随兴出国的机器的年代里才讲得通；营地是给那些先有了车、后有旅行社的人准备的旅馆。这也解释了这一页中段那股古怪的社交不安——门童和客房女佣那一段。高级酒店当初是为一个懂得其规矩的阶层建的，而这里来了一位新客人：账单付得起，礼数不懂。作者不是在笑他。他就是读者本人。

✨ How it is written赏析

The page opens with a trick worth stealing: it does not deny the common assumption, it allows it and then parks a Bentley next to it. One concrete car, and the assumption cannot be stated with a straight face again. After that the method is concession all the way down. Twice he takes the opposing case, grants it in full, and then shows that granting it costs him nothing; once he lets an objection be stated at length and kills it in a single sentence, saying that the opinion does not last five minutes at a real site. That is the shape of the whole essay: never refuse the other side its facts, only its conclusion. Then there is the ear. The smell of one country's cabbage set against the smell of another's garlic does more to prove a campsite international than any statistic could, because the proof arrives through the nose. The best sentence on the page is the one in which a grown man has no idea at what point he may safely say boo to a hotel manager - that one word, boo, is doing all the work, since nothing more dignified would have shown the size of the fear. And notice the phrase that has outlived the article: a snobbery based upon equipment. He saw the thing coming before it had a name. Two lessons: concede everything you can afford to concede, and spend your most specific word on your weakest-looking claim.

这一页开头有一手值得偷：它不否认那个通行的假设，而是先承认它，然后在它旁边停一辆宾利。一辆具体的车，那个假设就再也没法一本正经地被说出来。此后的办法一路都是让步：两次把对方的说法整段接下来、完整承认，再显示承认它对自己毫无损失；还有一次让反驳把话说完，然后用一句话打死——那种看法在真正的营地待不了五分钟。这就是全文的形状：从不拒绝对方的事实，只拒绝对方的结论。还有耳朵这一项：一个国家的酸菜味对着另一个国家的蒜味，比任何统计数字都更能证明营地的国际性，因为这个证明是从鼻子进来的。全页最好的句子是「不知道什么时候该对酒店经理哼一声」那一句——boo 这个词干了全部的活，因为任何更体面的词都显不出那份怯意的大小。还要注意那个比文章活得更久的说法：一种基于装备的势利。他在这件事还没有名字的时候就看见它来了。两条可学：凡是你承担得起的让步，全部让掉；把最具体的那个词，花在你看起来最弱的那个主张上。

🧭 What it argues, and does it still hold?解读

His diagnosis has aged better than his subject. The claim that camping is bought for freedom rather than for cheapness is now the ordinary explanation of why people go outdoors at all, and the health research points the same way: a study of nearly twenty thousand people in England found that those with at least two hours a week of contact with nature were more likely to report good health and high wellbeing, with the benefit appearing at that threshold and levelling off somewhere past three or four hours, and with the two hours working just as well split into short visits. The authors call it a correlation and warn against reading it as a prescription, and they add that the findings are English and should not be stretched round the world. What he got exactly right was the joke he made in passing. He granted that camping had already grown a snobbery of its own, founded on equipment, and that is now an industry with a vocabulary: the kit is specified, the brands are ranked, and a form of camping with beds and electricity has acquired its own name and price list. The paradox he handed to the cynic has simply gone on widening. Two things in his argument are weaker than they look. The first is the rest it promises: a modern holiday carrying a screen in its pocket is harder to escape into, and the freedom he describes was partly a freedom from being reachable, which the tent no longer supplies. The second is the thing he was defending camping against. Idealists in his day complained that campers never met the local people; the complaint now runs the other way, that there are too many visitors in too few places and that the people who live there would rather be left alone - and the numbers are the reason, since arrivals worldwide have gone from the tens of millions of his lifetime to about 1.4 billion in 2024. His closing warning was that the future of camping would depend on how campers treated the countryside, and on that he was right in a larger way than he meant: it is the whole industry that now turns on that question. For Chinese readers there is an exact parallel to watch rather than a difference, since the mass car holiday, the crowded scenic spot and the argument about what the visitors leave behind arrived there within one generation, a few decades later and at several times the speed.

他的诊断比他的题材老得更好。「露营买的是自由，不是便宜」这个说法，如今已是「人为什么要到户外去」的通常解释，而健康研究指向同一边：一项覆盖英格兰近两万人的研究发现，每周与自然接触至少两小时的人，更可能报告健康良好、幸福感高；益处在那个门槛上出现，到三四个小时之后趋平；而那两小时拆成几次短途也同样有效。作者称之为相关性，并提醒不要当成处方；他们还补充说，这是英格兰的结果，不宜直接推到全世界。他真正说准的，是那个顺口开的玩笑：他承认露营自己已经长出了一种势利，根基是装备——而今天这已是一个有自己词汇的行业：装备有规格、牌子有排序，而一种带床和电源的露营还得了自己的名字和价目表。他交给愤世嫉俗者的那个悖论，只是一路变宽了。他的论证里有两处比看上去更弱。第一是它承诺的休息：今天的假期口袋里装着屏幕，更难「逃进去」；而他描述的那种自由里，有一部分是「别人找不到你」的自由，而帐篷已经不再提供这一项。第二是他当年为露营辩护所针对的那件事：那时的理想主义者抱怨露营者从不接触当地人；而今天的抱怨反了过来——太多的客人挤在太少的地方，住在那里的人宁愿没人来；原因就在数字上：全世界的到访数已从他在世时的几千万，涨到 2024 年的约 14 亿。他结尾的警告是，露营的前途取决于露营者怎样对待乡间；在这一点上他说对了，而且对得比他本意更大：如今是整个行业都悬在这个问题上。对中国读者，这里要看的是一处精确的平行、而不是差异：大众汽车度假、挤爆的景区、以及「客人留下了什么」的争论，在那里也在一代人之内到齐了，只是晚了几十年，而速度是好几倍。

💬 Discuss and write讨论与写作
  1. He says the real attraction is not saving money but being able to leave whenever you want. Think of a choice of your own that you explain to people on grounds of cost. What is the real reason, and why is cost the answer you give?
    他说真正的吸引力不是省钱，而是想走就能走。想一件你自己的选择，你对别人的解释是「便宜」。真正的原因是什么？而你为什么给出的是「便宜」这个答案？
  2. Part of the freedom he describes was the freedom of not being reachable, and a tent does not give that any more. Write 120 words on whether a holiday can still be an escape, and make the strongest case for the other view before your own.
    他描述的自由里，有一部分是「别人找不到你」的自由，而帐篷如今给不了这一项。写 120 词谈一谈假期还能不能算一种逃离，并在你自己的看法之前，先把相反的看法用最有力的方式写出来。
  3. In his day the complaint was that visitors kept away from local people; now it is often that there are too many visitors. Take a place you know that has become crowded, and say what you would actually change - and who would lose by it.
    在他那个年代，抱怨是「客人不接触当地人」；今天的抱怨常常是「客人太多」。挑一个你熟悉、已经变得拥挤的地方，说出你真会改变什么——以及谁会因此受损。

③ How the hard sentences are built难句结构

Sentences are counted in the lesson text, title not included. Find the sentence in your book, then read our notes on its structure.句子按课文正文数（不含标题）。在教材里找到这一句，再看本站的结构讲解。

Sentence 3第 3 句 (begins开头 “But, contrary to …”)

  • far from (being): nothing like. And one here stands for motive.far from (being)：远非，根本不是；one = motive。
  • An inserted prepositional phrase: against what people generally assume.contrary to a popular assumption：与一般看法相反（插入的介词短语）。

Sentence 6第 6 句 (begins开头 “That the equipment …”)

  • That introduces a subject clause: the whole clause is the subject, and is is the verb.That 引导主语从句，整个 that 从句作主语，谓语 is。
  • A second complement in parallel with the first. get away from it all: leave the whole business behind.a brighter promise …：与 an entertaining paradox 并列的第二个表语；get away from it all：远离尘嚣。
  • The paradox: the people who mean to get away from everything take more and more elaborate equipment with them.悖论在于：「逃离一切」的人反而带着越来越复杂的设备。

Sentence 11第 11 句 (begins开头 “Affluent he may …”)

  • Concessive inversion: the complement goes to the front, meaning although he may be well off.让步倒装：表语 affluent 提到句首 = Although he may be affluent。
  • by no means: not in the least.by no means：绝不。
  • what to tip ...: question word plus infinitive, as object.what to tip …：疑问词 + 不定式作宾语。

Sentence 19第 19 句 (begins开头 “To this pleasure …”)

  • The prepositional object has been brought forward; in ordinary order it would come at the end. Putting it first links the sentence to the independence and freedom of the one before (compare the fronting in Lesson 44).间接宾语前置：正常语序是主语 camping 在前、介词短语放到句末；把 to this pleasure 提前，与上一句的 independence and freedom 衔接（L44 宾语前置）。

Sentence 21第 21 句 (begins开头 “Not only are …”)

  • With Not only at the front, the first half inverts (the verb comes before the subject); the half after but does not (Lesson 28).Not only 放句首，前半句部分倒装（are 提前）；but 后的后半句不倒装（L28）。
  • Paradise is set against hell, and the bracket gives the two forms that hell can take.holiday paradise 与 holiday hell 对照，括号里是 hell 的两种情形。

Sentence 37第 37 句 (begins开头 “Whether the remarkable …”)

  • Whether introduces a subject clause, and is hard to say is the predicate.Whether 引导主语从句；谓语 is hard to say。
  • A subject clause of this kind takes whether, never if.主语从句只能用 whether，不能用 if。

④ Grammar in this lesson本课语法

Concessive inversion: adjective or noun, plus (as/though), plus subject, plus may be让步倒装：adj./n. + (as/though) + 主语 + may be
The complement (an adjective, or a noun with no article) or an adverb is moved to the front, followed by as or though plus subject and verb, and the sense is although. It is a literary pattern, and as or though is sometimes left out.
把表语（形容词、不带冠词的名词）或副词提到句首，后接 as/though + 主语 + 谓语，表示「虽然……」；文学性强，as/though 有时也省略。

The standard forms: Rich as/though he is, he is not happy. / Child as he is, he knows a lot (the article drops away when the noun is fronted).

标准形式：Rich as/though he is, he is not happy.／ Child as he is, he knows a lot.（名词提前去掉冠词 a）

With a verb: Try as he might, he could not open the door.

动词形式：Try as he might, he could not open the door.（无论他怎么试）

Note: only as or though will do in this pattern, never although.

注意：这种句式里只用 as 或 though，不用 although。

  • Tired as she was, she kept on working.
  • Much as I admire him, I cannot agree.（虽然我很佩服他）
Subject clauses with that and whetherthat / whether 引导的主语从句
Put that in front of a complete statement, or whether in front of a yes-no question, and the clause can serve as the subject of a sentence. A subject clause takes a singular verb.
一个完整的陈述句前加 that、一个是非问句前加 whether，就能作句子的主语；从句作主语时谓语用单数。

When the clause is long, it is more usual to move it to the end behind a formal it (Lesson 37): It is hard to say whether ... A whether-clause at the front is quite common; a that-clause at the front sounds formal and emphatic.

主语从句较长时多改用 it 作形式主语（L37）：It is hard to say whether …；但 whether 从句作主语放句首很常见，that 从句放句首则显得正式、强调。

In subject position if cannot replace whether, and that cannot be left out.

主语位置不能用 if 代替 whether；that 在主语从句里不能省。

Lesson 25 has what-clauses as subjects, where what also does a job inside the clause; that does no such job.

L25 讲过 what 主语从句（what 在从句中充当成分）；that 在从句里不充当成分。

  • That he is honest is clear to everyone.
  • Whether we go depends on the weather.
Granted, ... but ...: concede first, then answerGranted, … but …：先让步，再反驳
Granted (that) means admittedly, or even if. You first grant whatever is right in the other side's case, and then turn back to your own with but. It is the commonest concessive move in argument.
Granted (that) …＝「诚然、就算……」；先承认对方说法中对的部分，再用 but 转回自己的观点——这是议论文最常用的让步手法。

The same family: Admittedly, ...; It is true that ..., but ...; To be sure, ..., but ...

同类：Admittedly, …；It is true that …, but …；To be sure, …, but …

  • Granted, the hotel is expensive, but the service is excellent.
  • It is true that he is young, but he is very experienced.

⑤ Patterns to reuse可以套用的句型

  • contrary to a popular assumption, … 与一般的看法相反，……
    Contrary to a popular assumption, cats can be trained.
  • … go hand in hand ……相伴而行，密不可分
    Wealth and worry often go hand in hand.

Summary writing摘要写作

In no more than 80 words: why do people like camping, apart from the saving, and how does the writer answer the charge that campers never meet the local people?

用不超过 80 个词概括：除了省钱，人们为什么喜欢露营？作者怎样回应「露营者不接触当地人」的批评？

Points a good summary covers好摘要要覆盖的要点
  • Money is neither the only motive nor the main one, as the expensive cars at the sites show.省钱并非露营的唯一或最大动机，营地里不乏豪车。
  • Camping takes away the discomfort a grand hotel produces, such as not knowing how much to tip.露营让旅行者逃离高级酒店带来的不安，如不知该给多少小费。
  • It extends the independence that owning a car gives: you can set off when you please, and leave a place that disappoints you just as freely.露营延伸了有车一族的独立与自由：随时出发，也随时可以离开不如意的地方。
  • A campsite mixes nationalities more thoroughly than a holiday hotel does, and the future of camping turns on whether campers treat the people of the countryside properly.营地比度假酒店更国际化，各国游客混居；露营的前途取决于露营者是否尊重乡间居民。
Our model summary本站范文

Camping is popular not only because it is cheap; many campers are wealthy. It frees travellers from the anxieties of grand hotels, such as not knowing what to tip, and it adds to the freedom that car ownership brings, since campers can arrive or leave whenever they like. Critics say campers never meet local people, but campsites are far more international than hotels. Its future depends on campers respecting the countryside.（71 词）

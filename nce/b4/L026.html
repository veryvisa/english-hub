Lesson 26 · The Past Life of the Earth 地球上的过去生命

📘 New Concept English · Book 4 · Lesson 26 “The Past Life of the Earth”新概念英语 第四册 Lesson 26《The Past Life of the Earth》

C1🧠 25 new words个新词📝 Full notes有完整讲解

🎯 Before you open the lesson开课前
Why are fossils mostly of water creatures, while land animals are so rare? There is really only one answer: quick burial. Mud and sand keep settling on the bed of a river or a sea, while a body on land rots or is eaten within days, so land animals survive only through some accident - a fall into an ice crack, as with the Siberian mammoths, or into the tar at Rancho la Brea in what is now a suburb of Los Angeles. This lesson is a textbook case of the cleft sentence It is ... that: a short piece uses it four times, and the thing worth watching is what the writer chooses to stress each time.
为什么化石里多是水生动物、陆地动物却少见？关键只有一个：快速掩埋。水底有泥沙不断沉积，陆地上的尸体却很快腐烂或被吃掉，只有掉进冰缝（西伯利亚猛犸象）或沥青坑（洛杉矶拉布雷亚）这种「特殊情况」才留下完整遗骸。本课是强调句 It is … that 的教科书级范例——一篇短文用了四次，读的时候注意作者每次在强调什么。
Questions to keep in mind as you read读的时候带着这几个问题：
  1. What is the one necessary condition for remains to survive, and why is it met more easily in water than on land?保存遗骸最必要的条件是什么？为什么水里比陆上更容易满足？
  2. Why do we so seldom see a dead bird or a dead field mouse?我们平时为什么很少见到鸟或田鼠的尸体？
  3. Why does the tar at Rancho la Brea hold both elephants and sabre-toothed cats?拉布雷亚沥青坑里为什么既有大象又有剑齿虎？

🌍 Where it comes from出处与背景

Errol Ivor White (1901-1985) was a fossil-fish man at the Natural History Museum in London, deputy to the keeper from 1939, later a Fellow of the Royal Society and president of the Linnean Society; a Devonian armoured fish carries his name. So this page is written by someone who had spent his working life opening slabs of rock, and it shows: the first thing he tells a reader is not what fossils prove but what has to happen before there is anything to study at all. That emphasis belongs to the state of the science at the time. Before radiocarbon dating was in general use, and long before any genetic work, the rock record was almost the only witness, which made its gaps the central professional problem. A reader today meets fossils as finished exhibits in a hall; the writer wants you to see them as the survivors of a process that destroys almost everything.

埃罗尔·伊沃·怀特（1901–1985）是伦敦自然史博物馆研究化石鱼的人，1939 年起任该部副主管，后为皇家学会会士、林奈学会会长；一种泥盆纪的盾皮鱼以他命名。所以这一页出自一个一辈子在劈开岩板的人：他对读者讲的第一件事不是化石能证明什么，而是在有东西可研究之前，必须先发生什么。这个重点属于当时的学科状况——放射性碳测年尚未普遍使用，遗传学手段更要等很久，岩层几乎是唯一的证人，于是它的缺口就成了这一行最核心的问题。今天的读者在展厅里见到的是成品；而作者要你看见的，是一个几乎摧毁一切的过程留下的幸存者。

✨ How it is written赏析

The whole page is built on one grammatical device used four times. The cleft sentence lets a writer put the stress exactly where he wants it: on the creatures of the water, on the place, on the part played by luck, on the special circumstance. Read the four in a row and you have the argument in outline, without any of the examples. That is a useful test of your own writing: if the stressed parts of your sentences, read one after another, do not add up to your argument, the stress is in the wrong place. Two more things are worth taking. First, the writer argues from what is missing. He asks you to count the birds you see flying and then to notice that you almost never find one dead, and the ordinary observation carries more weight than any statistic would. Second, the examples come in a rising order of strangeness: mud on a river bed, then a cave, then ice, then a pit of tar, with the last one holding a small drama in it - the drinkers stuck fast, the hunters that came for them stuck in their turn. End with the strangest case and the reader remembers the principle through the picture.

整页建在一个语法手段上，用了四次。强调句让作者把重心放在他想放的地方：水里的生物、地点、运气的作用、特殊情况。把这四处连着读，你就得到了论证的提纲，连例子都不需要。这也是检验自己文章的好办法：如果把你每句话的重心连起来读，加不出你的论点，那重心就放错了。另有两点值得拿走。第一，作者从「缺失」来论证：他让你数一数天上飞的鸟，再注意你几乎从没见过一只死鸟——这个寻常的观察比任何统计都有力。第二，例子按「怪异程度」递升：河床的泥、山洞、冰、沥青坑，最后一个还带着一出小戏——来喝水的陷住了，来吃它们的也跟着陷住。把最怪的例子放在最后，读者就靠这幅画面记住了那条原理。

🧭 What it argues, and does it still hold?解读

The principle has held up: quick burial is still the first condition, and the best fossil beds in the world are places where something covered the bodies at once. The Jehol beds of northeastern China are the plainest modern demonstration - feathers, stomach contents and skin outlines survive there because falls of volcanic ash buried whole lake communities within hours. Several of the writer's details, though, do not survive checking. The famous Siberian carcass was found in 1900 and dug out in 1901; the published accounts describe a broken foreleg rather than a broken back, grasses and buttercups among its teeth rather than fir, and the specimen stands in the Zoological Museum of the Russian Academy of Sciences, not in a palaeontological one. The Rancho la Brea story has been revised too: the asphalt is sticky, not deep, and experiments suggest a large body would need months to go under, so the picture of an animal promptly sinking is wrong even though the trap itself was real, and the catch was heavily weighted towards hunters - more than half the excavated skeletons are carnivores, which is what you would expect if each stuck animal drew others in. Finally, the rock is no longer the only witness. Ancient DNA can now be read from bone and from sediment with no bones in it at all, including material about two million years old from northern Greenland, so the gap the writer called hopeless has been partly filled from a direction he could not have foreseen.

原理站住了：快速掩埋仍是第一条件，世界上最好的化石层，都是尸体被立刻盖住的地方。中国东北的热河层是最清楚的现代例证——羽毛、胃内容物、皮肤轮廓都留了下来，因为火山灰在几小时内埋掉了整个湖泊群落。不过作者的若干细节经不起核对。那头著名的西伯利亚猛犸 1900 年被发现、1901 年挖出；已发表的记述说它折断的是前腿而不是脊背，牙缝里是禾草和毛茛而不是冷杉，标本现存俄罗斯科学院动物博物馆，而不是某个古生物博物馆。拉布雷亚的故事也被修订了：沥青是黏，不是深，实验提示一具大型尸体要几个月才会沉下去，所以「立刻陷下去」的画面是错的——尽管陷阱本身是真的，而且捕获严重偏向猎食者：挖出的骨架中一半以上是食肉动物，这恰恰是「每陷住一只就引来更多」所应有的结果。最后，岩层已不是唯一的证人：古 DNA 如今能从骨头、甚至从完全没有骨头的沉积物里读出来，包括格陵兰北部约两百万年前的材料——作者当年认为无望的那个缺口，已经从他想不到的方向被部分填上了。

💬 Discuss and write讨论与写作

    作者用「缺失」来立论。就你熟悉的一个题目写 120 词，照样做：说清缺的是什么证据，以及它使谁说不出什么话。
  3. Several details in this lesson are wrong while its main argument is right. When you read an old textbook, how do you tell which is which?
    这一课有几处细节错了，主论点却是对的。读旧教材时，你用什么办法分辨哪一类是哪一类？

③ How the hard sentences are built难句结构

Sentences are counted in the lesson text, title not included. Find the sentence in your book, then read our notes on its structure.句子按课文正文数（不含标题）。在教材里找到这一句，再看本站的结构讲解。

Sentence 1第 1 句 (begins开头 “It is animals …”)

  • A strict cleft sentence uses only that or who, never whose. This one is of the type It is + noun + relative clause: it looks like a cleft and does the work of one, but the whose-clause is really a relative clause describing the noun. Do not copy it as a model in an examination.严格的强调句只用 that/who 连接，不用 whose；这句是「It is + 名词 + 定语从句」的强调型句式：形式像强调句、起强调作用，但 whose 从句实际是修饰 animals and plants 的定语从句。考试里不要把它当标准强调句模板套用。

Sentence 5第 5 句 (begins开头 “that bodies and …”)

  • and the like: and other things of that sort.and the like：以及诸如此类的东西。

Sentence 10第 10 句 (begins开头 “whether it be …”)

  • whether it be ... or ...: a clause of concession with the bare form be - a formal and old-fashioned subjunctive, the same as whether it is ...whether it be … or …：让步状语从句，be 用原形——古旧/正式文体的虚拟式，= whether it is …。
  • live by doing something: to stay alive by that means.live by doing：靠……生存。

Sentence 16第 16 句 (begins开头 “it is very …”)

  • Here the cleft stresses how often: hardly ever.强调句强调频率状语 very rarely：「很少会碰到」。
  • not to mention ...: an inserted addition, meaning and still more so, followed by a noun phrase.not to mention …：插入的补充，「更不用说……」，后接名词短语。
  • which you do not see: a relative clause describing the small animals.which you do not see：定语从句修饰 small animals。

Sentence 23第 23 句 (begins开头 “In his mouth …”)

  • An adverbial of place is moved to the front and the whole verb goes before the subject. The plain order would put the remains first and the place last.地点状语提前 + 完全倒装：正常语序是 The remains of fir trees were in his mouth。
  • The subject is long and the place is short, so the inversion gives the sentence a light head and a heavy tail, and it points the reader's eye at the mouth.主语长、地点短，倒装后句子头轻脚重，读起来更平衡，也把读者的视线引向「嘴里」。

Sentence 27第 27 句 (begins开头 “Apparently what happened …”)

  • what happened is a noun clause used as the subject; that opens the clause which completes it, and inside that clause three actions follow one another: the water gathered, the animals walked out, they stuck fast.what happened 主语从句；that 引导表语从句，后面三个动作 collected / ventured / were bogged 依次推进。
  • the apparently firm surface: here apparently means seemingly, and the surface was not firm at all; at the head of the sentence Apparently means it seems that, which is a different sense.the apparently firm surface：apparently 在这里是「看似」（其实不结实），与句首 Apparently（「显然、据说」）意思不同。

④ Grammar in this lesson本课语法

The cleft sentence It is ... that ...强调句 It is … that …
It is/was + the stressed part + that/who + the rest of the sentence. If you can remove It is and that and still have a complete sentence, it is a cleft.
It is/was + 被强调部分 + that/who + 句子其余部分；去掉 It is 和 that 句子仍然完整，就是强调句。

You can stress the subject, the object or an adverbial (time, place, manner, reason); you cannot stress the verb - for that use do/does/did.

可以强调主语、宾语、状语（时间、地点、方式、原因），不能强调谓语动词（强调谓语用 do/does/did）。

who is possible when a person is stressed; for time and place a cleft still uses that, never when or where.

强调人时可用 who；强调时间、地点也只用 that，不用 when/where。

  • It was in 1956 that the ship was discovered.
  • It is not money that makes people happy.
Full inversion after an adverbial of place地点状语前置引起的完全倒装
When a phrase of place or direction stands first and the verb is an intransitive one such as be, lie, stand, come or sit, the whole verb goes in front of the subject.
表地点、方位的介词短语放句首，谓语是 be、lie、stand、come、sit 等不及物动词时，整个谓语提到主语前（完全倒装）。

The rule holds only when the subject is a noun; with a pronoun there is no inversion - Here he comes, not Here comes he.

条件：主语是名词；主语是代词时不倒装——Here he comes. / In his mouth it was.（✗）

The verb agrees with the subject that follows it: In the box were two old letters.

谓语与后面的主语一致：In the box were two old letters.

A common related form: Here/There + verb + noun subject - There goes the bus! / Here comes the teacher.

另一种常见形式：Here/There + 动词 + 名词主语：There goes the bus! / Here comes the teacher.

  • On the hill stands an old church.
  • Under the tree sat a little boy.
whether it be ... or ...: the bare subjunctive in a concessionwhether it be … or …：让步中的原形虚拟
whether it be A or B is the same as whether it is A or B; the bare form be belongs to formal written English and means no matter whether it is A or B.
whether it be A or B ＝ whether it is A or B，be 用原形，是正式/书面的虚拟式，表示「无论是 A 还是 B」。

Of the same family: be it ever so humble; Be it right or wrong, ... (inverted, more formal still); Come what may.

同类：be it ever so humble（尽管再简陋）、Be it right or wrong, …（倒装形式，更书面）、Come what may（无论发生什么）。

In your own writing whether it is ... or ... is enough; recognising the bare be as formal style, not as a mistake, is what matters.

日常写作用 whether it is … or … 就够了；能认出 be 原形不是错误，是更正式的文体。

  • Every citizen, whether he be rich or poor, has the right to vote.
  • Be it ever so humble, there's no place like home.

⑤ Patterns to reuse可以套用的句型

  • It is only in/by … that … 只有在/靠……才……
    It is only by hard work that you can master a language.
  • whether it be A or B 无论是 A 还是 B
    Every animal must eat, whether it be plant or meat.
  • …, not to mention … ……，更不用说……
    The trip was expensive, not to mention tiring.
  • What happened was that … 事情是这样的：……
    What happened was that the bus broke down on the way.

Summary writing摘要写作

In no more than 80 words, answer: why do the remains of water plants and animals survive more often than those of land animals, and in what circumstances can a land animal be preserved?

用不超过 80 个词概括：为什么水生动植物的遗骸比陆地动物更容易保存？陆地动物在什么情况下才能保存下来？

Points a good summary covers好摘要要覆盖的要点
  • Remains last only if something covers them quickly, and that happens easily in water, where mud and sand keep settling.保存的必要条件是快速掩埋，只有在泥沙不断沉积的水中才容易做到。
  • Even in the best conditions most bodies rot or are eaten by animals that feed on the dead.即使在有利条件下，大多数尸体也会腐烂或被食腐动物吃掉。
  • On land almost nothing covers a body, so it breaks down or is eaten within days.陆地动物很少被覆盖，尸体很快分解或被吃掉。
  • Only an accident preserves a land animal: a fall into an ice crack, as in Siberia, or into the tar at Rancho la Brea.只有特殊情况才能保存：掉进冰缝（西伯利亚猛犸象）或陷入沥青坑（拉布雷亚）。
Our model summary本站范文

Remains are preserved only if they are buried quickly, and this usually happens in seas, rivers and lakes, where mud and silt keep settling. Even there, most dead creatures decay or are eaten first. On land, bodies are rarely covered, so they soon decompose or are eaten. Land animals survive only in special cases, such as mammoths frozen in Siberian crevasses or animals trapped in the tar pits of Rancho la Brea.（72 词）

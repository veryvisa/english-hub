📘 New Concept English · Book 4 · Lesson 3 “Matterhorn Man”新概念英语 第四册 Lesson 3《Matterhorn Man》

🎯 Before you open the lesson开课前
Today's climbers pick the hard line, and the harder it is, the more credit it brings; the pioneers wanted the easiest way up. The writer opens with a then-and-now contrast, then spends the second half on detail — filthy inns, bread a year old, rough wine — so that we feel the hardship instead of being told about it. Two techniques to take away: arguing by contrast, and building a picture out of concrete nouns.
今天的登山者挑难路，越难越光彩；早年的先驱者却只求最容易登顶的路。作者用一组古今对比开头，再用大段细节（脏乱的旅店、放了一年的面包、粗劣的酒）让我们感受先驱的艰苦。学这一课要抓住两种写法：对比论证，和用具体名词堆出画面感的描写。
Questions to keep in mind as you read读的时候带着这几个问题：
  1. What were the early climbers chiefly after, and how does that differ from climbers today?早期登山者的主要目标是什么？与现代登山者有何不同？
  2. What were the Alpine villages like at the time?阿尔卑斯山村当时是什么样子？
  3. Why was this life especially hard on those particular climbers?为什么说这种生活对那些登山者「格外」艰苦？

📖 Understanding the piece读懂这篇

The lesson is credited to Walter Unsworth, from Matterhorn Man (attribution line in the original Book 4). The book is Unsworth's life of Edward Whymper, published in London by Victor Gollancz in 1965.

课文署名 Walter Unsworth，选自《马特霍恩峰人》（Matterhorn Man）（据旧版第四册课末署名）。该书是 Unsworth 为爱德华·温珀（Edward Whymper）写的传记，1965 年由伦敦 Victor Gollancz 出版。

🌍 Where it comes from出处与背景

The man behind the title is Edward Whymper, and the climb behind the book is the first ascent of the Matterhorn on 14 July 1865. Seven men reached the summit: Whymper, Lord Francis Douglas, Charles Hudson, Douglas Hadow, the Chamonix guide Michel Croz, and the two Zermatt guides, Peter Taugwalder father and son. On the way down one of them slipped on the rope and four were pulled to their deaths; only Whymper and the two Taugwalders came back. That ascent is taken as the close of the golden age of Alpine climbing, because the Matterhorn was the last of the great peaks still unclimbed. Walt Unsworth, who wrote this in 1965, exactly a century later, also wrote a life of A. F. Mummery and a shelf of climbing histories, much of it aimed at young readers — which is why the prose is so plain and so heavy with physical detail. Zermatt and Chamonix, the two villages the lesson lets off, are the two that had already found the tourist trade; they are now among the best-known resorts in Europe.

书名里的那个人是爱德华·温珀，书背后的那次攀登是 1865 年 7 月 14 日人类首登马特霍恩峰。七个人登上峰顶：温珀、弗朗西斯·道格拉斯勋爵、查尔斯·哈德森、道格拉斯·哈道、夏蒙尼向导米歇尔·克罗，以及采尔马特的老少两位陶格瓦尔德向导。下山时一人滑脱，绳上四人被拖下山崖身亡，只有温珀和两位陶格瓦尔德回来。这次攀登被当作阿尔卑斯登山黄金时代的终点，因为马特霍恩是最后一座尚未被征服的大峰。Walt Unsworth 在整整一百年后的 1965 年写下这本书，他还写过 A. F. Mummery 的传记和一系列登山史，多半面向青少年读者——这解释了这篇文字为什么句子朴素、实物细节密集。课文唯一放过的采尔马特与夏蒙尼，正是当时已经做起旅游生意的两个村子，今天是欧洲最有名的度假地之一。

✨ How it is written赏析

The piece runs on one move repeated: abstract claim first, then physical proof. The claim is that the pioneers had a different aim from ours; the proof is the state of the inns. Notice that the hardship is never named. The writer does not write that conditions were primitive; he gives you cheese and bread a year old and wine you have to wash it down with, and lets you supply the word. That is the difference between telling and showing, and it is the reason the paragraph stays in the memory while the opening generalisation does not. Notice too where the real force of the ending lies. Hardship means nothing by itself — it only exists next to something. So the last sentence puts seven courses and linen sheets beside the dirt, and the whole paragraph suddenly has a measure. If you are describing a hard place, find the thing your reader already knows and set it alongside; and prefer nouns a reader can see to adjectives a reader must accept.

这篇文章靠一个动作反复推进：先给抽象判断，再给实物证据。判断是先驱者的目标与我们不同；证据是旅店的样子。注意作者从没说出「艰苦」二字。他不写条件原始，他给你放了一年的面包和奶酪、给你必须就着往下咽的粗酒，让你自己把那个词填上。这就是「讲出来」和「摆出来」的区别，也是为什么那一段留在记忆里，而开头的概括留不住。再注意结尾的劲道落在哪里。艰苦本身没有意义，它只存在于对照之中。所以最后一句把七道菜的晚餐和细亚麻床单摆到脏污旁边，整段突然有了尺度。写一个艰苦的地方时，去找读者本来熟悉的那样东西摆在旁边；多用读者看得见的名词，少用需要读者认领的形容词。

🧭 What it argues, and does it still hold?解读

The contrast the lesson draws is real but it has been pushed further than the author could have guessed, and in two opposite directions at once. The hard-line principle he describes has hardened: difficulty itself is now the currency, measured in grades, in style (no bolts, no oxygen, no fixed ropes, alone, in winter), and sometimes in speed. Yet the other half of the sport has gone the opposite way. The great peaks the pioneers starved to reach can now be reached by people with no mountain skill at all, because for a fee the hard parts are done for them: ropes already fixed, routes already prepared, porters, guides, and a helicopter if things go wrong. So the two kinds of climber the lesson sets side by side both still exist, but they no longer meet on the same mountain in the same way. One thing to be careful of in the lesson's own picture: it draws the poverty of the Alpine valleys from the outside, in the voice of a visiting gentleman, and the villagers appear only as dirt, fleas and bad bread. The guides on whom those visitors wholly depended — Croz, the Taugwalders — came from exactly those villages, and two of them survived the Matterhorn because they knew the mountain better than the client who hired them.

课文画的这个对比是真的，但后来被推得比作者能料到的更远，而且是朝相反的两个方向同时推。他描述的「越难越光彩」变得更极端：难度本身成了货币，用难度等级来量，用风格来量（不打岩钉、不用氧气、不挂固定绳、独攀、冬攀），有时还用速度来量。可这项运动的另一半走向了相反的方向。先驱者挨着饿才能到的那些大峰，今天可以由完全没有登山技术的人到达，因为只要付钱，难的部分有人替他做完：绳子已经固定好，路线已经修整好，有背夫，有向导，出事还有直升机。所以课文并列的两种登山者今天都还在，只是不再以同一种方式在同一座山上相遇。课文自己的画面里有一点要留神：它是从外部、用一位到访绅士的口吻来写阿尔卑斯山谷的贫困，村民只以脏污、跳蚤和劣质面包的形象出现。而那些访客完全依赖的向导——克罗、陶格瓦尔德父子——恰恰来自这些村子；马特霍恩那场事故里有两个人活下来，是因为他们比雇他们的客人更懂这座山。

💬 Discuss and write讨论与写作
  1. Describe a place you have been that was uncomfortable, in 150 words, without using the words dirty, poor, cold or hard. Make the reader supply those words from the things you name.
    用 150 词写一个你去过的、不舒服的地方，但不许用「脏」「穷」「冷」「苦」这几个词。让读者从你列出的东西里自己把这些词填上。
  2. If someone is carried up a mountain with fixed ropes, bottled oxygen and a guide, has that person climbed it? Where would you draw the line, and what is your line actually measuring?
    如果一个人靠固定绳、瓶装氧气和向导被带上山顶，他算登顶了吗？你会把界线划在哪里，而你那条界线实际在量什么？
  3. The lesson treats the pioneers as the people worth writing about. Write 120 words telling the same story with a Zermatt guide as the main figure — what changes?
    课文把先驱者当作值得写的人。换成以一位采尔马特向导为主角，用 120 词重讲这个故事——什么变了？

④ Grammar in this lesson本课语法

the + comparative ..., the + comparative ...the + 比较级 …, the + 比较级 …
To say 'the more X, the more Y', move both comparatives to the front of their own clauses and put the in front of each. The first clause gives the condition, the second the result.
「越……就越……」：两个比较结构都要提到各自分句的句首，前面加 the；前一个分句表条件，后一个表结果。

When the clauses are very short the verbs can be dropped: The sooner, the better. The bigger, the better.

句子很短时可省略谓语：The sooner, the better. The bigger, the better.

Two common slips: leaving out the second the, and leaving the comparative where it was (not: the more it is difficult).

常见错误：漏掉第二个 the；把比较级留在原位（× the more it is difficult）。

  • The harder you work, the more you will achieve.
  • The older he grew, the less he talked.
such ... as, and relative clauses opening with assuch … as 与 as 引导的定语从句
When the noun in front is modified by such, the same or as, the relative clause opens with as; inside the clause as works as subject, object or complement.
先行词被 such / the same / as 修饰时，定语从句用 as 引导；as 在从句里作主语、宾语或表语。

the same ... as against the same ... that: as points to the same kind, that usually to the very one. This is the same pen as I lost (the same model) against This is the same pen that I lost (that very pen).

the same … as 与 the same … that：the same … as 指「同类」，the same … that 常指「同一个」：This is the same pen as I lost.（同款）/ This is the same pen that I lost.（就是那支）

Keep this apart from such as meaning 'for example': such as is followed by instances, not by a clause.

注意与「such as 例如」区分：such as 后接例子，不引导从句。

  • I have never heard such a story as he told.
  • Take such books as you need.
Participle phrases as adverbials, and the absolute construction分词短语作状语与独立主格
A participle phrase takes the subject of the main clause as its own. If the participle has a subject of its own, the result is an absolute construction: noun or pronoun + participle.
分词短语的逻辑主语必须是主句主语；若分词有自己的主语，就成了「独立主格」（名词/代词 + 分词）。

First case in this lesson: They often faced dangers ..., equipped in a manner which ... — the understood subject is they, and they were equipped, so the past participle is used.

本课 ①：They often faced dangers…, equipped in a manner which… —— 逻辑主语 they，被装备，用过去分词。

The mistake to avoid is the dangling participle: not Walking along the street, a car hit him (the car was not walking), but Walking along the street, he was hit by a car.

误用「悬垂分词」：× Walking along the street, a car hit him.（车没有在走路）→ Walking along the street, he was hit by a car.

  • Seen from the hill, the town looks beautiful.
  • The work done, we went home.（独立主格）

⑤ Patterns to reuse可以套用的句型

  • the more …, the more … 越……越……
    The more you practise, the more confident you become.
  • … would make sb shudder at the thought ……会让某人一想到就发抖
    The prices would make any student shudder at the thought.
  • For people accustomed to …, … must have been … 对习惯了……的人来说，……一定非常……
    For people accustomed to city life, the village must have been very lonely.

Summary writing摘要写作

In no more than 80 words, explain how the early climbers differed from climbers today, and describe the living conditions the pioneers met in the Alps.

用不超过 80 个词说明早期登山者和现代登山者的不同，并描述先驱者在阿尔卑斯山遇到的生活条件。

Points a good summary covers好摘要要覆盖的要点
  • Climbers today want hard lines, and the harder the line the higher the standing; the early climbers wanted the easiest way up, because getting to the top was the whole point.现代登山者追求难路，越难越受推崇；早期登山者只求最容易登顶的路，峰顶本身就是目标。
  • The pioneers met great danger with poor equipment, yet they did not look for risk on purpose.先驱者虽然也遇到极大危险、装备简陋，但并不刻意冒险。
  • Apart from Zermatt and Chamonix, the mountain villages were poor and shut off, their inns were filthy, and often there was no inn at all.除采尔马特、夏蒙尼外，山村贫困闭塞，旅店肮脏，常常根本没有旅店。
  • They lodged with penniless priests or with shepherds, and lived on stale bread and rough wine.他们只能借住在穷神父或牧羊人家里，吃陈面包、喝粗酒。
Our model summary本站范文

Modern climbers prefer difficult routes, but the pioneers simply wanted the easiest way to the summit. Though poorly equipped and often in danger, they did not seek excitement. Apart from Zermatt and Chamonix, Alpine villages were poor and isolated, with dirty inns or none at all, so climbers stayed with priests or shepherds and ate stale bread and cheese. For men used to comfort, this must have been very hard.（70 词）

📖 Understanding the piece读懂这篇

The attribution line in the original Book 4 reads 'Gordon Barrie and Aubrey L. Diamond, The Consumer Society and the Law'. The book is really 'The Consumer, Society and the Law', a Penguin paperback first published in 1964, and the first author's name is spelt Borrie: Gordon Borrie, then a law teacher at Birmingham University and, from 1976 to 1992, Britain's Director General of Fair Trading. The textbook has turned Borrie into Barrie.

旧版第四册课末署名作「Gordon Barrie and Aubrey L. Diamond, The Consumer Society and the Law」。该书实为《The Consumer, Society and the Law》（企鹅/鹈鹕版，1964 年初版）；第一作者姓氏应作 Borrie——戈登·博里，当时在伯明翰大学教法律，1976 至 1992 年任英国公平贸易总署署长。教材把 Borrie 印成了 Barrie。

🌍 Where it comes from出处与背景

The passage comes from a paperback written for ordinary readers rather than for lawyers: a 1964 Penguin book that set out, chapter by chapter, where you stood in law when you bought a kettle, signed a hire-purchase form or kept money at a bank. Britain in the sixties was just starting to treat the shopper and the account holder as people with rights worth naming, and cheap paperbacks were how that idea reached the public. Banking then was almost entirely paper. A salary arrived as a cheque, rent went out as a cheque, and a clerk somewhere compared the ink on each one against a card kept in a drawer. The rule this passage turns on, that handwriting which is not yours cannot move your money, mattered every single day, because the only proof of your consent was a few inches of ink.

这段选自一本写给普通读者、不是写给律师的平装书：1964 年的企鹅版小书，一章一章讲你买个水壶、签一份分期付款合同、在银行存钱时，法律上站在什么位置。六十年代的英国才刚开始把买东西的人和开户的人当作「有权利可讲」的人，而廉价平装书正是这种观念走向大众的渠道。那时的银行业务几乎全靠纸：工资是一张支票，房租是一张支票，某个柜员在后台把每张票上的墨迹同抽屉里的留样卡比对。本篇立论所靠的那条规则——不是你写的字不能动你的钱——在当年天天用得上，因为能证明你同意付款的，只有几英寸长的一行墨水。

✨ How it is written赏析

Three habits of legal writing are on show. First, nouns do the work that verbs do in speech: a bank does not simply owe you things, it owes obligations; money is not paid back, there is repayment of it. Second, a definition is dropped in behind a dash and left standing there, so one sentence can carry a second idea without starting over. Third, a whole conditional is squeezed into half a dozen words where ordinary English would want two clauses, and you have to slow down and unpack it. Then the last sentence does the opposite: after all that formality it swings into the emphatic it is ... which shape and lands on one short verb. Notice how the formal and the blunt take turns. When you write about rules yourself, keep the exact wording for the rule and save a plain short sentence for what follows from it.

这里有三个法律文体的习惯。第一，口语里用动词的地方，它用名词：银行不是「欠你东西」，而是 owe obligations；钱不是「还回来」，而是 there is repayment of it。第二，定义被塞在破折号后面就地放着，一个句子因此能顺手带出第二层意思，不必重新起句。第三，普通英语要两个分句才讲清的条件关系，被压缩成五六个词，你得慢下来把它拆开。接着最后一句走了反方向：前面那么正式，到这儿忽然用强调句式 it is ... which，落在一个很短的动词上。留意正式与直白的轮换。自己写规则类文字时也这样：规则本身用精确的措辞，规则导致的结果用一句大白话。

🧭 What it argues, and does it still hold?解读

The core rule has held. In Canada the Bills of Exchange Act says a forged signature is wholly inoperative, so no claim can be built on it, and the Supreme Court applied exactly that in BMP Global Distribution v Bank of Nova Scotia: the paying bank could not stand on money it had handed over against a forged drawer's name. What has changed is how seldom any of this arises. Payments Canada reports that personal cheque transactions fell twelve per cent in 2024 alone, and that cheque volumes dropped by about a fifth across five years while cards and transfers took the traffic. Meanwhile the loss people actually suffer today is the mirror image: money they sent themselves, after a convincing phone call or message. A forged signature is the bank's problem; a payment you authorised has always been yours. Britain moved on that in October 2024, making reimbursement compulsory up to eighty-five thousand pounds and splitting the cost between the sending and the receiving firm. Canada has no equivalent rule, so the old protection stands and the new gap is wide open. Keep the authors' question, then, and put it to the new instrument: who carries the loss when the instruction was genuine and only the reason for giving it was a lie?

核心规则至今站得住。加拿大《汇票法》第 48 条规定伪造签名「完全无效」，任何权利都不能据它成立；最高法院在 BMP Global Distribution 诉 Bank of Nova Scotia 案里正是这么判的：付款行凭伪造的出票人签名付出去的钱，不能主张付得有效。变了的是这种事还有多少机会发生。加拿大支付协会的数据：个人支票笔数 2024 年一年就降了 12%，五年间支票总笔数降了约两成，流量被卡和转账接走。而今天人们真正亏掉的钱，是反过来的那一种：被一个像真的电话或短信说服后，自己把钱转出去。伪造签名是银行的麻烦；你自己授权的付款，一向算你自己的事。英国 2024 年 10 月在这件事上动了手，规定强制赔付、上限八万五千英镑，成本由汇出方与收款方机构对半承担。加拿大没有对应规则，所以旧的保护还在，新的缺口大开着。那就把两位作者的问题留下，拿去问新的支付工具：当指令是真的、只有给出指令的理由是假的，损失该由谁承担？

  • either ... or ...: here the two phrases joined are both adverbials of manner.either … or …：连接两个方式状语。

Sentence 4第 4 句 (begins开头 “who is which …”)

  • that of: that stands in for the noun already used, so it is not repeated.that of：that 代替 the relationship，避免重复。

Sentence 8第 8 句 (begins开头 “cannot complain that …”)

  • The subject and its verb are pulled apart by the inserted phrase.主语与谓语被插入语隔开。

Sentence 10第 10 句 (begins开头 “When, for example, …”)

  • instruct somebody to do something: to give somebody a formal order.instruct sb to do：指示某人做。
  • A past participle used after the noun; only limits the whole prepositional phrase, not the verb.drawn by himself：过去分词后置定语；only 修饰 in respect of… 短语。

Sentence 12第 12 句 (begins开头 “that the bank …”)

  • that opens a clause in apposition which spells out what the rule says (see Lesson 11).that 引导同位语从句，解释 rule（见第 11 课）。

  • may have been: a guess about the past (see Lesson 1); one stands for the noun already named.may have been：对过去的推测（见第 1 课）；one 代替 forgery。

④ Grammar in this lesson本课语法

Relative clauses opened by preposition + which / whom介词 + which / whom 引导的定语从句
Pick the preposition that belongs with the verb, noun or adjective inside the clause. It may also stay at the end; but once it moves to the front, which or whom is the only choice.
介词的选择看从句中的搭配：介词跟哪个动词/名词/形容词搭配就用哪个；介词也可后置（which 不能换成 that 时，介词必须前置）。

Rule: with the preposition in front you can only use which/whom, never that; with the preposition at the end, that is allowed and may even be dropped: the house (that) I live in.

规则：介词前置时只能用 which/whom，不能用 that；介词后置时可用 that 或省略：the house (that) I live in。

  • The man to whom I spoke was the manager.
  • This is the reason for which he left.（= why）
it as an empty subject: It makes no difference / It is no use / It matters ...it 作形式主语：It makes no difference / It is no use / It matters…
When the real subject is a that clause, a wh- clause, an infinitive or an -ing form, English often parks it in the subject slot and sends the long subject to the end.
真正的主语是 that 从句、wh- 从句或不定式/动名词时，常用 it 先占主语位置，把长主语移到句尾。

Others of the same shape: It doesn't matter whether...; It is no use/good doing...; It is worth doing...; It is said that...

常见：It doesn't matter whether…；It is no use/good doing…；It is worth doing…；It is said that…

  • It makes no difference whether you go or not.
  • It is no use crying over spilt milk.
Set phrases of legal and business prose法律／商业文体的常用短语
Formal texts reach for nouns and fixed phrases: in respect of (about), in favour of (payable to), give rise to (cause), owe obligations to (have duties towards), have no right or authority to do (not be allowed to).
正式文本偏爱名词化和固定短语：in respect of（关于）、in favour of（以……为收款人）、give rise to（引起）、owe obligations to（对……负有义务）、have no right or authority to do（无权做）。

Spoken equivalents: in respect of = about; give rise to = cause; facilitate = make easier.

口语对应：in respect of = about；give rise to = cause；facilitate = make easier。

  • Payment will be made in respect of all goods delivered.
  • The decision gave rise to much criticism.

⑤ Patterns to reuse可以套用的句型

  • Primarily, A is that of B and C 从根本上说，A 是 B 与 C 之间的关系
    Primarily, the teacher-student relationship is that of guide and traveller.
  • X, unlike, say, Y, cannot complain that … 与比如说 Y 不同，X 不能抱怨……
    A tenant, unlike, say, a hotel guest, cannot complain that the rules are unfair.
  • It makes no difference that … 即便……也没有区别
    It makes no difference that you didn't mean it; the damage is done.
  • If this …, it is A which will lose, not B 如果……，吃亏的是 A，不是 B
    If prices fall, it is the seller who will lose, not the buyer.

Summary writing摘要写作

In no more than 80 words, set out the basic relationship between a bank and its customer and the bank's duty over signatures. End by explaining why a customer runs no risk when his name is already printed on the cheque forms.

用不超过 80 个词说明银行与储户的基本关系，以及银行在签名问题上的责任。最后解释为什么在支票上印储户名字对储户无风险。

Points a good summary covers好摘要要覆盖的要点
  • Opening a current account means lending the bank money, which you can ask for at any time in cash or by cheque.储户开活期账户就是把钱借给银行，可以随时要求以现金或支票方式偿还。
  • The basic relationship is debtor and creditor, and which is which depends on the state of the account.基本关系是债务人与债权人，谁是谁取决于账户有余额还是透支。
  • The two sides also owe each other many duties; the bank must obey its own customer and nobody else.双方还互相负有许多义务；银行必须只听储户本人的指令。
  • The bank must know the customer's signature and may not pay on a forged one, however skilful the copy.银行必须认得储户签名，伪造签名的支票即使仿得再像，银行也无权付款。
  • So even if a printed name makes forgery easier, the loss falls on the bank.因此印储户名字即使便于伪造，损失也由银行承担。
Our model summary本站范文

A customer who opens a current account lends the bank money, which he can withdraw at any time. The bank and the customer are debtor and creditor, depending on whether the account is in credit or overdrawn, and they also owe each other many obligations. The bank must obey only its customer and must recognize his signature: if it pays on a forged cheque, however skilful the forgery, the bank bears the loss.（73 词）

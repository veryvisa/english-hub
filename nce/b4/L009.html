Lesson 9 · Royal Espionage 王室谍报活动

📘 New Concept English · Book 4 · Lesson 9 “Royal Espionage”新概念英语 第四册 Lesson 9《Royal Espionage》

C1🧠 28 new words个新词📝 Full notes有完整讲解

🎯 Before you open the lesson开课前
A piece of English history: in the ninth century Alfred the Great dressed as a travelling singer, spent a week inside the camp of the Danish invaders as his own spy, saw that their discipline was loose and their supplies disorganised, and then, instead of fighting a pitched battle, wore them down by harassment until they gave in within the month. The shape is the shape of narrative: reconnaissance, judgement, decision, outcome, with cause running through each step. In the language, watch thither (the old word for 'to that place'), participles used as adverbials, and The force there assembled, where the participle follows the noun.
一段英国历史：九世纪的阿尔弗雷德大帝扮成吟游诗人，混进入侵者丹麦人的营地当了一周「间谍」，看清对方纪律松弛、补给混乱，回去后不打硬仗而是骚扰拖垮敌军，一个月内迫使丹麦人投降。叙事文的写法：侦察—判断—决策—结果，每一步都有因果。语言上注意 thither（古语「到那里」）、分词作状语、The force there assembled（后置的过去分词）等书面结构。
Questions to keep in mind as you read读的时候带着这几个问题：
  1. Why did Alfred go in dressed as a minstrel?阿尔弗雷德为什么扮成吟游诗人？
  2. What weaknesses did he see in the Danish camp?他在丹麦营地看到了哪些弱点？
  3. What did he do about them, and how did it end?他据此采取了什么战术，结果如何？

🌍 Where it comes from出处与背景

The events sit in the spring of 878, in the worst year of Alfred's reign. The Danish army under Guthrum had taken Chippenham in a surprise winter attack, Wessex had collapsed, and Alfred was reduced to a fortified island in the Somerset marshes, Athelney. From there he gathered what men he could and fought the battle of Edington, after which Guthrum accepted baptism and withdrew. Those facts are solid; they come from the Anglo-Saxon Chronicle and from Asser's life of the king, both close to the events. The minstrel episode is a different matter. It does not appear in those early sources at all; the earliest surviving versions are post-Conquest, two centuries later, from the generation of Anglo-Norman historians who turned Alfred into a national hero and supplied him with the stories he is now remembered by. The burnt cakes come from the same workshop. By the nineteenth century the harp scene was popular enough to be painted — Daniel Maclise's canvas of Alfred in the Danish camp dates from 1852 — and popularity of that kind is how a late anecdote becomes something everybody knows.

事件发生在 878 年春天，阿尔弗雷德在位最糟的一年。古斯鲁姆率领的丹麦军队在一次冬季突袭中占了奇彭纳姆，韦塞克斯崩溃，阿尔弗雷德退到萨默塞特沼泽中的一座设防小岛阿塞尔尼。他从那里尽力召集人手，打了埃丁顿之战，此后古斯鲁姆接受洗礼并撤离。这些事实是可靠的，出自《盎格鲁-撒克逊编年史》和阿塞尔为国王写的传记，两者都离事件很近。吟游诗人那一段则是另一回事。它在这些早期史料里完全没有；现存最早的版本出自诺曼征服之后、两百年后那一代盎格鲁-诺曼史家——正是他们把阿尔弗雷德塑造成民族英雄，并为他配上了今天人们记住他的那些故事。烤糊的饼出自同一间工坊。到十九世纪，竖琴那一幕已经流行到被画成油画——丹尼尔·麦克利斯画阿尔弗雷德在丹麦营地那幅作于 1852 年——而正是这种流行，让一则晚出的轶事变成人人都知道的事。

✨ How it is written赏析

The paragraph is built as an intelligence report, and that is why it reads faster than its sentence length suggests. Every observation is followed by the inference drawn from it, and every inference by the action taken. Discipline was slack, so security was casual; they lived off raiding, so easy living had taken the edge off them; therefore they could not sustain a long fight, and therefore no pitched battle but constant harassment; therefore the raiding parties were stopped, and therefore hunger did the rest. Nothing in the chain is decorative. Note the economy of the detail. The writer gives us exactly three things about the disguise — ballads, acrobatic tricks, simple conjuring — and one sentence about why minstrels could walk in anywhere. That is the whole of the characterisation, and it is enough, because what the reader needs is not Alfred's inner life but the plausibility of the cover story. Note too the archaic touches: thither Alfred went, the harp was their passport, The force there assembled. They are doing a job. The old word order and the old vocabulary tell you that you are a long way back in time, which is a cheaper and quicker way of establishing a period than any amount of description. Then the last sentence breaks the register deliberately, calls the whole thing an epic of royal espionage, and lets the writer admire his own story out loud.

这一段是按情报报告的结构搭的，这就是为什么它读起来比句子长度显示的要快。每一项观察后面跟着由它得出的推断，每一项推断后面跟着据此采取的行动。纪律松弛，所以防备随便；靠劫掠为生，所以安逸的生活让他们变软；因此他们打不了持久战，因此不打正面仗而持续袭扰；因此劫掠队被拦住，因此饥饿完成了剩下的事。链条上没有一环是装饰。注意细节的节省。关于那套伪装，作者只给三样东西——民谣、杂技、简单魔术——以及一句话说明吟游诗人为什么到处进得去。这就是全部的人物刻画，而且够了，因为读者需要的不是阿尔弗雷德的内心，而是这个掩护身份的可信度。还要注意那些古语：thither Alfred went、竖琴就是通行证、The force there assembled。它们在干活。老的语序和老的词汇告诉你时间很远，这比任何篇幅的描写都更便宜、更快地立起一个年代。然后最后一句故意换了调门，把整件事称作「王室谍报的一部史诗」，让作者放声赞赏自己的故事。

🧭 What it argues, and does it still hold?解读

The history is sound; the story in it probably is not. The siege, the retreat to Athelney, the victory and the Danish surrender are attested in sources written within living memory of the events. The visit to the camp in disguise is not: it is absent from the Anglo-Saxon Chronicle and from Asser, and first appears some two hundred years later, in writers who had every reason to embellish. Historians of Alfred treat it as legend, and the usual judgement is that both the harp and the burnt cakes belong to the making of a national myth rather than to the reign. This is worth sitting with, because the lesson does not merely retell the anecdote; it uses it as evidence. The reasoning runs: he went in, therefore he saw the weaknesses, therefore he chose harassment over battle. Remove the visit and the conclusion survives untouched — the Danish army's reliance on raiding and its unfitness for a long campaign would have been obvious to any commander in Wessex, and Alfred's tactics are exactly what a weaker force does against a stronger one that cannot feed itself. The story explains nothing that needs explaining. That is the signature of an anecdote added later: it is too neat, it supplies a motive for something already accounted for, and it makes a general truth about a campaign into a personal feat by the king. Which leaves a fair question about what the piece is for. As history it is unreliable in its central scene; as a demonstration of how reconnaissance turns into strategy it is excellent, and as a specimen of how national heroes acquire their stories it is better still. Read it for the chain of reasoning, and keep the harp in quotation marks.

历史是站得住的；里面那个故事大概不是。围城、退守阿塞尔尼、取胜、丹麦人投降，都有写于事件尚在世人记忆之内的史料佐证。乔装进营则没有：《盎格鲁-撒克逊编年史》和阿塞尔都没有它，它最早出现在约两百年后的作者笔下，而那些人有充分的理由加工。研究阿尔弗雷德的史家把它当作传说，通常的判断是：竖琴和烤糊的饼都属于民族神话的制造，而不属于那段在位期。这一点值得停下来想想，因为课文不只是重讲这则轶事，它把它当作证据在用。推理是这样走的：他进去了，所以他看到了弱点，所以他选择袭扰而不是决战。把那次进营抽掉，结论毫发无损——丹麦军队依赖劫掠、打不了长仗，这对韦塞克斯的任何一位指挥官都是明摆着的；而阿尔弗雷德的战术，正是一支弱势部队对付一支养不活自己的强敌时会做的事。这个故事并没有解释任何需要解释的东西。这正是后来添加的轶事的签名：它太齐整，它为一件本已说得通的事补上一个动机，并把一场战役的普遍道理变成国王个人的功绩。于此留下一个公道的问题：这篇东西用来干什么？当历史读，它最核心的那一幕不可靠；当「侦察如何变成战略」的示范读，它相当出色；而当「民族英雄如何获得他的故事」的标本读，它更好。读它的推理链条，把那把竖琴放在引号里。

③ How the hard sentences are built难句结构

Sentences are counted in the lesson text, title not included. Find the sentence in your book, then read our notes on its structure.句子按课文正文数（不含标题）。在教材里找到这一句，再看本站的结构讲解。

Sentence 1第 1 句 (begins开头 “Alfred the Great …”)

  • visiting ...: a present participle phrase saying how he did it, with Alfred as its subject and the action his own.visiting…：现在分词短语作伴随/方式状语，主语 Alfred 主动地去。
  • disguised as a minstrel: a past participle phrase, the form of disguise oneself as, that is, be disguised as.disguised as a minstrel：过去分词短语作状语，Alfred 被打扮成（disguise oneself as → be disguised as）。

Sentence 5第 5 句 (begins开头 “While Alfred's little …”)

  • The While clause means 'at the same time as'; himself puts the weight on the king doing it in person.While 从句表「与此同时」；himself 强调「国王亲自」。

Sentence 6第 6 句 (begins开头 “These had settled …”)

  • These refers back to the Danish invaders in the sentence before.These 指前句 the Danish invaders。
  • thither Alfred went: the adverb of place comes first; thither is archaic and lends weight to the history. The subject is a noun but is not inverted here, which keeps the tone plain.thither Alfred went：地点副词提前；thither 为古语，增加历史叙事的庄重感；主语是名词但这里没有倒装（went Alfred 也可），语气更朴素。

Sentence 11第 11 句 (begins开头 “The force there …”)

  • there assembled: the participle assembled follows the noun, with the adverb there in front of it, = the force which was assembled there.there assembled：过去分词 assembled 作后置定语，副词 there 提前，= the force which was assembled there。
  • compared with ...: a participle phrase working as an adverbial of comparison.compared with…：过去分词短语作状语，表比较。

Sentence 12第 12 句 (begins开头 “But Alfred had …”)

  • Two noun clauses with that stand in parallel, and the second that cannot be dropped, or the clause would read as a new main sentence.两个 that 宾语从句并列，第二个 that 不能省（否则会读成主句）。
  • had deduced: the past perfect — he had worked this out before deciding what to do.had deduced：过去完成时，「在决定战术之前已经推断出」。

Sentence 13第 13 句 (begins开头 “So, faced with …”)

  • faced with ...: a past participle phrase giving the cause or the moment; the phrase is be faced with, to be up against.faced with…：过去分词短语作原因/时间状语，be faced with 面临。
  • not ... but ...: not the first, but the second (see Lesson 6).not … but …：不是……而是……（见第 6 课）。
  • risk takes a noun or a gerund after it: risk open battle, risk losing.risk 后接名词或动名词：risk open battle / risk losing。

④ Grammar in this lesson本课语法

Participle phrases as adverbials: doing / done / having done分词短语作状语：doing / done / having done
A participle phrase used as an adverbial takes the subject of the main clause as its own: doing when the action is active, done when it is passive, and having done when it was completed before the main action.
分词短语作状语时，逻辑主语就是主句主语；主动用 doing，被动用 done，动作在主句之前完成用 having done。

The perfect form: Having learned the Danes' weaknesses, Alfred returned — he learned first and went back afterwards.

完成式：Having learned the Danes' weaknesses, Alfred returned.（先学到，再回去）

  • Seeing the danger, he stopped.
  • Faced with difficulties, she never gave up.
The past perfect: had done过去完成时：had done
Use had done for an action already complete before some point in the past. It goes naturally with before, by the time and already, and with conclusions drawn or results reached in the past.
过去某时之前已经发生的动作用 had done；常与 before, by the time, already 或「过去的推断/结果」连用。

Note that you do not need the past perfect when simply telling events in the order they happened: He got up, dressed and went out.

注意：按时间顺序叙述时不必用过去完成时：He got up, dressed and went out.

  • By the time we arrived, the film had started.
  • She told me she had seen the film.
compared with and compared to, and expressions of the trivial kindcompared with / compared to 与 trivial 一类的比较表达
compared with sets two things side by side to show the difference; compared to likens one thing to another, though American usage also employs it for plain comparison. Either phrase can stand at the beginning of the sentence or inside it, as an adverbial.
compared with（与……相比，比较差异）、compared to（把……比作；美式也常用作「与……相比」）；放在句首或句中作状语。

Related phrases: in comparison with, by comparison, and as opposed to (meaning 'and not').

相关表达：in comparison with / by comparison；as opposed to（而不是）。

  • Compared with last year, prices have risen sharply.
  • Life is often compared to a journey.（比作）

⑤ Patterns to reuse可以套用的句型

  • act as one's own … 亲自充当……
    The director acted as his own cameraman.
  • … was trivial compared with … 与……相比……微不足道
    Our losses were trivial compared with theirs.
  • X did not risk …, but … X 不冒险……，而是……
    The general did not risk a direct attack but cut off the enemy's supplies.
  • faced with …, sb did … 面对……，某人……
    Faced with rising costs, the company cut staff.
  • within a month … had … 不到一个月……就已经……
    Within a week the rumour had spread to every school.

Our model summary本站范文

Disguised as a wandering minstrel, Alfred the Great entered the Danish camp at Chippenham and stayed a week. He saw that the Danes were undisciplined, careless about security and softened by an easy life of raiding. Realizing they could not fight a long war and had no organized supplies, Alfred avoided open battle. Instead he harried them, stopped their raids and starved them, and within a month they surrendered.（69 词）

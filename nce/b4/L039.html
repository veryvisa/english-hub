📖 Understanding the piece读懂这篇

The lesson is an extract from an essay by John le Carré, What every writer wants, published in Harper's Magazine in November 1965 at pages 142-145 and reprinted in the Cornhill Magazine the following winter. We did not get behind the magazine's paywall, so we have confirmed the citation but not read the original pages.

课文节自约翰·勒卡雷（John le Carré）的随笔 What every writer wants，1965 年 11 月刊于《Harper's Magazine》第 142–145 页，次年冬季由《Cornhill Magazine》转载。我们没有越过该刊的付费墙，所以核到了出处，但没有读到原刊页面。

🌍 Where it comes from出处与背景

Read the date beside the name and the essay turns personal. Two years earlier his third novel had made him famous all over the world; he was leaving the service he had worked in, and he was writing under a name that was not his own while being asked everywhere to explain himself. So the warning in the middle of this page - that the temptation to close the distance between yourself and your reader can be a writer's undoing - is not advice handed down from a height. It is a man describing the thing that was happening to him that year. The account of method is also his own, and the archive has since confirmed it in detail: he wrote by hand, his wife typed what he had written, and then he cut the typescript up and fastened the pieces into new arrangements and wrote over them again by hand. The breaking and remaking in this lesson is not a metaphor. It is a description of his desk.

把名字旁边的日期读进去，这篇随笔就变成私人的了。两年前他的第三部小说让他在全世界出名；他正在离开他供职的那个机构；他用一个不属于自己的名字写作，却到处被要求解释自己。所以这一页中间那句警告——想要缩短自己与读者之间的距离，可能成为一个作家的毁灭——不是居高临下传下来的训诫，而是一个人在描述那一年正发生在他身上的事。文中关于方法的说法也是他自己的，后来档案把细节也证实了：他手写，妻子把手写稿打成字，然后他把打字稿剪开、把碎片重新拼贴固定，再在上面手写一遍。这一课里的「拆了重做」不是比喻，是他书桌上的实况。

✨ How it is written赏析

The essay moves by metaphor, and each metaphor is allowed to do one job and then dropped. The journey carries the whole first half, so that destination and setting off and changing course all read as plain language; then the painter arrives for exactly two sentences, because a blurred image that goes when you add a brushstroke is the only way to say what the sentence needs; then comes the yeast, which does the work no other word would, since it says that the thing inside a writer is alive, is not under his control, and will go on rising after the loaf has been baked. The mirror does the cruellest work: writers who read only their own books are put beside adolescents, and the comparison is both affectionate and withering. Then watch the turn. The sentence that condemns the bore is followed at once by incidentally, an unforgivable bore - the author putting himself among the guilty by the lightness of the aside - and the paragraph ends not in judgment but in diagnosis, naming the disease as the wish to be liked. The lesson for your own writing is to let each image carry one point and get off the stage, and to spend the sharpest sentence on yourself.

这篇随笔靠比喻推进，而每个比喻只干一件事就退场。旅程支撑了整个前半，于是「目的地」、「出发」、「改道」都读起来像平常话；接着画家只出现两句，因为「加一笔，模糊的影像就不见了」是唯一能说出那句话需要的东西；然后是酵母，这个词干的是别的词干不了的活——它同时说出三件事：作家体内那个东西是活的、不受他控制、而且面包烤好之后它还会继续发。镜子那一下最狠：只读自己作品的作家被摆在青春期少年旁边，这个比较既有温情又极刻薄。然后看那个转折：谴责「话痨」的句子之后，紧跟着一句轻轻的插话——顺便说，他还是个不可饶恕的闷人——作者用插话的轻，把自己也放进了被告席；而这一段结束时给的不是判决，是诊断：他已经开始为取悦而写。可以搬进自己写作的是两条：让每个意象只承担一个点，承担完就下台；把最锋利的那一句留给自己。

🧭 What it argues, and does it still hold?解读

Two of his claims have been tested by time, and they have come out differently. The claim about method was always too strong. He claims to have met nobody who ever drew up a bare plan in advance, and plenty of good writers do - some work from index cards, some from spreadsheets of chapters and timelines, and the division between those who find the book by writing it and those who find it before writing it looks like a difference in temperament rather than a difference between art and school exercise. What survives is the smaller, harder point inside it: that the first version is not the place where the quality is decided. His other claim has not weakened; it has been industrialized. He warns against covering the distance between the writer and the reader, against studying your image in the eyes of strangers. Every writer now does that for a living: the interview, the festival, the thread, the video, the follower count, the daily reading of what strangers say about a book. The condition he described as a temptation is now a contractual obligation, and his word for its consequence - writing to please - names something a writer can now watch happening to a sentence in real time. And there is a new question his formula cannot settle. If what a writer is born with shows in the first version and what he has learned shows in the revisions, what is left when a machine will produce that first version on demand, in four seconds, in the style of your choice? One reading is that his hierarchy is vindicated, since what he called art is exactly the part that cannot be ordered up. The other is that the organic process he prized - finding in your material what you did not know was in it - never happens at all if the material was not yours to begin with. For Chinese readers the comparison worth making is with the classical habit of revision: the poets' stories about changing a single character, the long tradition that treats polishing as the whole of the craft rather than an afterthought. On that view his sentence about talent and art is not modern or Western at all. It is a thousand-year-old working rule, arriving in English in 1965.

他的两个主张都被时间检验过，结果不同。关于方法的那一个，本来就说得太重：他说从没听说有人列骨架，可许多好作家确实列——有人用卡片，有人用记着章节与时间线的表格；而「边写边找到这本书」与「写之前就找到这本书」的分别，看起来是性情之别，不是「艺术」与「学校作业」之别。活下来的是它里面那个更小、更硬的点：质量不是在第一稿上定的。他另一个主张没有变弱，而是被产业化了。他警告不要去缩短作者与读者之间的距离、不要在陌生人的眼睛里端详自己的形象；而今天每个作家都以此为业：访谈、书展、长帖、视频、关注数、每天读陌生人怎么说你的书。他当年称之为「诱惑」的处境，如今是合同义务；而他给后果起的名字——为取悦而写——命名的那件事，今天一个作家可以实时看着它发生在一个句子上。还有一个他的公式解决不了的新问题：如果才华在初稿、艺术在之后的修改稿，那么当机器能在四秒钟内按你指定的风格交出初稿时，还剩下什么？一种读法是他的等级被证实了，因为他称为「艺术」的那一部分，恰恰是点不来的；另一种读法是，他所珍视的那个有机过程——在自己的材料里发现原先不知道在里面的东西——如果材料本来就不是你的，就根本不会发生。对中国读者，值得拿来对照的是古典的修改传统：诗人为一个字反复推敲的那些故事，那个把「改」当作手艺全部、而不是事后补救的长久传统。按这个看法，他那句关于才华与艺术的话既不现代也不西方，而是一条上千年的老规矩，1965 年才用英文说了一遍。

💬 Discuss and write讨论与写作
  1. Take something of your own - an essay, a report, an email you rewrote - and ask which parts came from the first version and which only appeared later. Does his division between talent and art hold for your own page?
    拿你自己写过的一样东西——一篇文章、一份报告、一封改了又改的邮件——问问哪些部分来自第一稿，哪些是后来才出现的。他那个「才华对艺术」的分法，在你自己的纸面上成立吗？
  2. He thinks a writer who explains his own book is doomed. But a reader who hears the author explain it often enjoys the book more. Write 120 words taking one side, and allow the other side its best argument first.
    他认为解释自己作品的作家注定毁灭。可听过作者解释的读者，往往更喜欢那本书。写 120 词支持一方，但先把另一方最有力的论证写出来。
  3. If a first draft can now be had on demand, where should a writer put the work? Answer in your own case: what part of your writing would you refuse to hand over?
    如果初稿现在随要随有，作家该把功夫下在哪里？就你自己回答：你写作里的哪一部分，你不肯交出去？

④ Grammar in this lesson本课语法

Leaving out the relative pronoun; those who and those whom关系代词的省略与 those who / those (whom)
When the relative pronoun is the object of its clause it can be dropped (that, which, whom); when it is the subject it cannot. those + a relative clause means 'the people who ...'.
关系代词在定语从句中作宾语时可以省略（that/which/whom）；作主语时不能省。those + 定语从句 ＝「那些……的人」。

In this lesson the first clause drops the pronoun and the second keeps whom: with two clauses side by side, the pronoun marks where the second one starts.

本课：第一个从句省了 whom，第二个保留 whom——两个从句并列，作者用 whom 标出第二个从句从哪里开始。

Compare: Most animals that we commonly observe (Lesson 37, where the pronoun was dropped); the drafts that follow (here it is the subject and cannot go).

又：Most animals (that) we commonly observe（L37）；the drafts that follow（that 作主语，不能省）。

those who takes a plural verb; in this lesson it refers to the people who have no idea who he is.

those who ＋ 复数谓语：本课把它用在「不认识他的那些人」身上。

The test: look at what follows the pronoun. the book (that) I read — a subject follows, so it may go; the man who called — a verb follows, so it must stay.

判断能否省：看关系词后面有没有主语。the book (that) I read（后面有主语 I → 可省）；the man who called（后面直接是动词 → 不可省）。

  • The film (that) we saw last night was excellent.
  • Those who work hard will succeed.
might as well do: may just as wellmight as well do：还不如、不妨
may or might as well do means 'there is nothing better to do, so let us do this'. It also carries irony: you might as well do this other, equally absurd thing.
may/might as well do ＝「（既然没有更好的选择）不妨、还不如」；might as well 还常带讽刺：「那还不如去做（一件同样荒唐的事）」。

Here it is the ironic use: the two things he says a writer might as well attempt are both hopeless.

本课用的是讽刺义：might as well try 后面接的是「解释一桩罪行或一段恋情」——同样徒劳。

The suggesting use: The bus has gone; we may as well walk.

建议用法：The bus has gone; we may as well walk.（反正车走了，不如走路）

The ironic use: You might as well talk to a wall.

讽刺用法：You might as well talk to a wall.（你还不如跟墙说话）

Keep it apart from might well or may well, 'very probably' (Lesson 29). The little as changes everything.

与 might/may well（很可能，L29）要区分：中间有没有 as 意思完全不同。

  • There's nothing on TV. We might as well go to bed.
  • If you're not going to listen, I might as well not speak.
be of + abstract noun = be + adjectivebe of + 抽象名词 ＝ be + 形容词
of + an abstract noun, used as a complement or as a modifier, does the work of the matching adjective and sounds more formal: of importance = important, of use = useful, of value = valuable.
of + 抽象名词作表语或定语，相当于对应的形容词，语气更正式：of importance = important，of use = useful，of value = valuable。

In this lesson the phrase is of an indescribable fascination stands for is indescribably fascinating.

本课：This organic process … is of an indescribable fascination.（= is indescribably fascinating）

It takes modifiers: of great importance, of no use, of little value.

可加修饰：of great importance（非常重要）、of no use（毫无用处）、of little value（价值不大）。

It can also follow the noun it describes: a matter of importance; a man of courage; a woman of few words.

也可作后置定语：a matter of importance；a man of courage；a woman of few words（话很少的女人）。

  • The book is of great value to students.
  • Your advice was of no help at all.

⑤ Patterns to reuse可以套用的句型

  • have little idea where/what … 不太清楚……在哪儿/是什么
    I had little idea what the job would involve.
  • to my certain knowledge 据我确切所知
    To my certain knowledge, he has never been abroad.
  • I never heard of anyone doing … 我从没听说有人……
    I never heard of anyone learning English in a week.
  • … will not rest till … 不……决不罢休
    The police will not rest till they catch the killer.
  • make order out of … 从……中理出秩序
    A good editor makes order out of a mass of notes.

Points a good summary covers好摘要要覆盖的要点

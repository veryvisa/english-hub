Lesson 32 · Galileo Reborn 伽利略的再生

📘 New Concept English · Book 4 · Lesson 32 “Galileo Reborn”新概念英语 第四册 Lesson 32《Galileo Reborn》

C1🧠 30 new words个新词📝 Full notes有完整讲解

🎯 Before you open the lesson开课前
The Galileo of the textbooks is a simple figure: a hero who trusted only experiment, scorned book learning and dropped iron balls from the tower at Pisa. Historians of science have since found that the story is not that simple. The Galileo of the record has been born again as a more tangled man, and his opponents have been granted a little sympathy: in an age when curved glass was what you used to produce illusions, why should anyone trust what a tube of it showed? This is a piece of historical criticism. The author sets up the old portrait, knocks it down with But, and then defends Galileo's opponents with three rhetorical questions.
课本里的伽利略形象很简单：一个只信实验、蔑视书本、在比萨斜塔上扔铁球的英雄。但近代科学史家发现事情没那么简单——历史上的伽利略「再生」成了一个更复杂的人物，人们对他的对手也多了几分同情：在那个年代，弯曲的玻璃是制造幻象的东西，凭什么相信望远镜里看到的？本课是史学评论文，作者先立「旧形象」，再用 But 推翻，最后用三个反问句替伽利略的对手辩护。
Questions to keep in mind as you read读的时候带着这几个问题：
  1. What did the old portrait of Galileo look like?「旧的伽利略形象」有哪些特征？
  2. What changed the way people see Galileo?是什么改变了人们对伽利略的看法？
  3. Why does the author think those who distrusted the telescope can be forgiven?作者为什么认为可以原谅那些不相信望远镜的人？

📖 Understanding the piece读懂这篇

The passage is by Michael Hoskin (1930-2021), the Cambridge historian of astronomy who founded the Journal for the History of Astronomy and ran the university's department of history and philosophy of science from 1975 to 1986. It appeared in the BBC weekly The Listener under the title Galileo Reborn; we have not fetched the issue itself, so the exact date is not verified.

课文作者是迈克尔·霍斯金（Michael Hoskin，1930–2021），剑桥的天文学史家，《天文学史杂志》的创办人，1975–1986 年主持剑桥科学史与科学哲学系。文章以 Galileo Reborn 为题刊于 BBC 周刊 The Listener；我们没有取到该期原件，确切日期未核。

🌍 Where it comes from出处与背景

The lesson is a report from inside a quarrel among historians, not a quarrel about religion. For most of the twentieth century the standard story was the one the author calls uncomplicated: a man who tested things and trusted only what he saw. Then historians read the notebooks more carefully and noticed how much of the work was mathematics and argument rather than measurement, how much he owed to the very scholars he mocked, and how skilfully he handled patrons and publicity. The tower at Pisa is the clearest case. No account of it survives in anything he wrote; the story reaches us through a biography written by his last secretary, who is the only source that puts the test on that tower, and most historians now read it as a demonstration of something already worked out, or as an experiment carried out in the mind. He does say in his books that he tried a cannonball against a musket ball, but he does not mention the tower.

这一课报道的是史学界内部的争论，不是宗教争论。二十世纪的大半时间里，通行的说法就是作者所说的「简单得可爱」：一个只肯动手试、只信自己所见的人。后来史家更细地读他的笔记，发现其中数学推演与论辩远多于实测，发现他欠了那些被他讥笑的学者很多，也发现他极善于经营金主和声势。比萨斜塔是最清楚的一例：他自己所有著作里都没留下这件事的记载；故事来自他晚年秘书写的传记，而这是唯一把实验放在那座塔上的来源，今天多数史家把它读作「把已经算出来的结论演示给人看」，或者干脆是一次在头脑里完成的实验。他书里确实说过拿炮弹和火枪弹比过，但没有提那座塔。

✨ How it is written赏析

The architecture is worth copying. One paragraph builds the old portrait as sympathetically as it can - note delightfully, which smiles at it rather than sneering - and the next takes it apart with a single long sentence whose subject and verb are a whole line apart, so that the revision arrives slowly, like the work it describes. Then comes the defence. Instead of asserting that the opponents were reasonable, the author asks three questions in a row and lets the reader supply the answers; and he strengthens the case by moving the charge forward two hundred years, to astronomers who dismissed what they saw through a much better telescope as scratches from the polishing machine. That is the move to steal: before you blame people in the past for failing to see, find someone in a later and better-equipped age who failed in the same way. It converts a quarrel about one man into a point about how seeing works.

这篇的结构值得照搬。前一段尽量体谅地把旧形象立起来——注意 delightfully 这个词，是带笑意的，不是讥讽；后一段用一个长句把它拆掉，而这个长句的主语和谓语隔了整整一行，于是「修正」来得很慢，正像它所描述的那种工作。接着是辩护。作者不去断言对手其实有道理，而是连问三个问题，让读者自己填答案；再把指控往后挪两百年，挪到那些用更好的望远镜看见了旋涡星云、却说那是磨镜刮痕的天文学家身上。这一手值得偷：在责备古人「看了却没看见」之前，先找一个时代更晚、器材更好却同样看错的人。于是关于一个人的争吵，变成了关于「看见」这件事如何发生的论点。

🧭 What it argues, and does it still hold?解读

Three things have moved since this was written. First, the picture the author calls new is now the ordinary one among specialists, while the simple hero still rules in school books and in films - which is itself worth noticing: popular history lags behind the research by decades. Second, the quarrel with the church has a formal ending. A commission set up in 1981 reported in 1992, and on 31 October that year the pope told the Pontifical Academy of Sciences that the theologians of the day had failed to separate scripture from the reading of scripture; the cardinal presenting the report called the judges' mistake an error of judgment. Newspapers read this as a pardon, and some historians answered that a pardon is the wrong shape for the case, since what was wrong was the claim to judge at all. Third, the author's defence of the doubters has become a general lesson about instruments: every new instrument, from the telescope to the brain scanner, produces pictures that nobody yet knows how to read, and the first readings are usually part artefact. For Chinese readers there is a sharp parallel. The telescope reached China within a decade of Galileo's discoveries, carried by the same Jesuit order that was prosecuting him at home; the earliest Chinese book on the instrument has a preface dated 1626, and what it taught Chinese astronomers was not the sun-centred system but the compromise model of Tycho Brahe. The tool crossed the world in ten years. The theory it supported was held back at the border.

这篇写成之后有三件事变了。第一，作者所说的「新图景」在专家里已成常识，而那个简单的英雄仍然统治着教科书和电影——这件事本身值得留意：通俗史落后于研究往往几十年。第二，与教会的那场官司有了正式的收尾：1981 年成立的委员会在 1992 年提交报告，同年 10 月 31 日教宗对宗座科学院说，当年的神学家没能把圣经与对圣经的解释分开；代为宣读报告的枢机把当年审判者的错误称为「判断上的错误」。报纸把这读成平反，也有史家回应说「平反」这个形状本身就不对，因为错的是「有权审判」这个前提。第三，作者为怀疑者所作的辩护，如今成了关于仪器的通则：每一种新仪器，从望远镜到脑部扫描，产出的都是还没人会读的图像，最早的读法里总有一部分是器械造出来的假象。对中国读者，有一个很尖锐的对照：望远镜在伽利略发现之后十年内就到了中国，带它来的正是在欧洲起诉他的那个修会；现存最早的中文望远镜专书序作于 1626 年，而它教给中国天文学家的不是日心说，是第谷的折中模型。工具十年就跨过了半个地球，它支持的那套理论却被挡在了关口。

💬 Discuss and write讨论与写作
  1. The author forgives the people who looked through the telescope and saw nothing. Think of a recent case where experts looked at the same data and drew opposite conclusions. What would it take for you to decide which side was careless and which was merely early?
    作者原谅了那些从望远镜里什么也没看出来的人。想一个近年的例子：专家看同一批数据得出相反结论。要怎样你才能判断哪一方是草率、哪一方只是早了一步？
  2. Write 120 words on a hero from your own school books whose story you later found was tidier than the facts. What was gained by the tidy version, and what was lost?
    写 120 词：你自己课本里的某个英雄，后来你发现他的故事比事实整齐得多。这个整齐的版本带来了什么，又遮掉了什么？
  3. The instrument arrived in China quickly and the theory did not. Pick a technology that has spread faster than the ideas behind it, and say what follows from the gap.
    仪器很快到了中国，理论没有。挑一样传播得比它背后的观念更快的技术，说说这个落差会带来什么。

③ How the hard sentences are built难句结构

Sentences are counted in the lesson text, title not included. Find the sentence in your book, then read our notes on its structure.句子按课文正文数（不含标题）。在教材里找到这一句，再看本站的结构讲解。

Sentence 1第 1 句 (begins开头 “In his own …”)

  • the scientific dust has settled: a metaphor — the quarrel inside science died down long ago.the scientific dust has settled：比喻「科学上的争论尘埃早已落定」。
  • Tense against tense: the simple past for his own day, the present perfect for what has been true since.时态对比：was（当年）与 has long since settled（到现在早已平息）。
  • The phrase in something like means 'close to'. It is the author's reservation: not that we now see the affair clearly, only that we almost do.后半句的 in something like …＝「近乎……」，是作者的保留语气：不说「完全看清了」，只说「差不多看清了」。

Sentence 7第 7 句 (begins开头 “who put his …”)

  • Three who clauses after the colon, all describing the same man: the parallel gives the sentence its drive.冒号后连用三个 who 从句，都修饰前面的 a man，形成排比，节奏有力。
  • put questions to somebody: to ask them. In instead of to the ancients the second to is kept so that it balances to nature.put questions to sb：向……提问；instead of to the ancients 中保留 to，与 to nature 对称。

Sentence 23第 23 句 (begins开头 “if we remember …”)

  • A rhetorical question: on the surface it asks whether we may blame them; what it means is that we may not.反问句：表面问「我们能责怪……吗？」，实意是「我们不能责怪」。
  • those who looked ...: a relative clause with a what clause nested inside it as the object.those who looked …：定语从句里又套一个 what 宾语从句（看了，却没看见伽利略看见的东西）。

Sentence 24第 24 句 (begins开头 “Was the philosopher …”)

  • The second rhetorical question: was the philosopher who would not look any more to blame than the people who, two centuries later, dismissed the spiral nebulae as marks left by the polishing machine? The implication: getting it wrong is not a fault of the old alone.第二个反问：「那位拒绝看望远镜的哲学家，难道比两百年后把星云说成磨镜刮痕的人更该受责备吗？」——言下之意：犯错的不只是古人。
  • those who alleged that ...: a relative clause with an object clause inside, and two past participle phrases used as modifiers after their nouns.those who alleged that …：定语从句套宾语从句；两个过去分词短语（用某架大望远镜观测到的、磨镜留下的）都作后置定语。

Sentence 28第 28 句 (begins开头 “and if a …”)

  • how much more ...?: a rhetorical step up — 'then two of them would distort it even more, would they not?'how much more …?：反问式递进，「那么两片就更会扭曲了，不是吗？」。
  • In the second half the verb is left out after would, understood from the first half, and would stands in front of its subject because the sentence is a question.后半句的谓语后省略了 distort nature（承前省略）；would 提到主语前，是疑问句语序。
  • The sentence before uses not A but B: curved glass was then a device for producing illusions rather than truths.前面一句用 not A but B：弯曲的玻璃在当年是制造幻象、而不是制造真相的器具。

④ Grammar in this lesson本课语法

Rhetorical questions: a question in form, a strong statement in meaning反问句：用疑问形式表达强烈的肯定或否定
A rhetorical question expects no answer. A positive one means a negative, a negative one means a positive; it is used to defend a view and to pull the reader onto your side.
反问句不求回答，肯定形式表示否定的意思，否定形式表示肯定的意思；常用来为观点辩护、引读者认同。

The lesson ends with three of them in a row: can we blame ...? (we cannot), was he more culpable ...? (he was not), and how much more ...? (two pieces would distort it even more).

本课结尾连用三个：Can we blame those who …?（＝我们不能责怪他们）／ Was the philosopher … more culpable than …?（＝他并不更该受责备）／ how much more …?（＝两片玻璃扭曲得更厉害）。

A negative rhetorical question means a positive: Isn't it obvious? (it is) / Who doesn't love a holiday? (everybody does)

否定形式的反问表示肯定：Isn't it obvious?（= It is obvious.）／ Who doesn't love a holiday?

In writing, a rhetorical question is at its strongest at a turn or at the end; too many in one paragraph and the effect goes. Here the run of three is deliberate: it builds the case for the defence.

写作中反问句用来收尾或转折最有力，但一段里不宜太多；本课作者是有意连用，制造辩护的气势。

  • What's the use of worrying?（= It's no use worrying.）
  • Who could have foreseen such a disaster?（= No one could.）
not A but B, and instead ofnot A but B 与 instead of
not A but B means 'not A; B instead', where A and B are parallel in form. instead of A means 'in place of A' and is followed by a noun, a pronoun or an -ing form.
not A but B ＝「不是 A 而是 B」，A、B 是平行的同类成分；instead of A ＝「代替 A、而不是 A」，后接名词、代词或动名词。

Two places in this lesson: the glass produced not truth but illusion, and he asked nature rather than asking the ancients.

本课两处：弯曲的玻璃是制造 not truth but illusion 的器具；他把问题提给自然，instead of 提给古人。

When not A but B is the subject, the verb agrees with B: Not the students but the teacher is to blame.

not A but B 作主语时，谓语与 B 一致：Not the students but the teacher is to blame.

Keep the two halves parallel: It is not that I dislike him, but that I don't trust him. (a that clause answering a that clause)

注意平行：It is not that I dislike him, but that I don't trust him.（that 从句对 that 从句）

instead as an adverb goes at the end: He didn't go to Paris; he went to Rome instead.

instead（副词）放句尾：He didn't go to Paris; he went to Rome instead.

  • The problem is not money but time.
  • Let's walk instead of taking a taxi.
how much more / how much less: arguing a step furtherhow much more / how much less：递进推理
'If that is so for A, then it is all the more so for B.' After a positive statement use much more or still more; after a negative one use much less, still less or let alone.
「既然 A 如此，B 就更……了」——肯定句后用 much more / still more，否定句后用 much less / still less / let alone。

In this lesson: one curved glass already distorts nature, so what about two? The rhetorical question pushes the reasoning one step further.

本课：一片弯玻璃就能扭曲自然，那么两片呢——how much more …？用反问把推理再推一级。

The negative pattern: He can't afford a bicycle, much less a car. / I didn't even read the first page, let alone the whole book.

否定式：He can't afford a bicycle, much less a car.／ I didn't even read the first page, let alone the whole book.

let alone is the commonest of them, and what comes before it must be negative or carry a negative sense.

let alone 最常用，前面必须是否定或含否定意义的句子。

  • It is hard enough to learn one foreign language, still more two.
  • She can't boil an egg, let alone cook a meal.

⑤ Patterns to reuse可以套用的句型

  • the dust has long since settled （争论）早已尘埃落定
    The dust has long since settled on that election.
  • see sth in its proper perspective 恰如其分地看待某事
    Try to see this problem in its proper perspective.
  • … calls for long experience and … ……需要长期经验和……
    Repairing old clocks calls for patience and skill.
  • If A …, how much more … B? 如果 A 都……，B 岂不更……？
    If one mistake costs so much, how much more would ten?

Summary writing摘要写作

Write no more than 80 words: what was the traditional portrait of Galileo, how has modern research changed it, and why does the author sympathize with his opponents?

用不超过 80 个词概括：传统的伽利略形象是怎样的？现代研究怎样改变了这一形象？作者为什么同情伽利略的对手？

Points a good summary covers好摘要要覆盖的要点
  • The old view: a hero of experiment who scorned book learning and drew his conclusions boldly, overturning the ancients with a telescope and with falling weights.旧观点：伽利略是重实验、蔑视书本知识、敢于下结论的英雄，用望远镜和落体实验推翻了古人。
  • Closer work on the evidence, and a better sense of the period and of its philosophical currents, has revised that portrait; a more tangled Galileo has appeared.更深入的研究和对时代背景、哲学暗流的理解，修正了这一形象，出现了更复杂的伽利略。
  • Sympathy for his opponents has grown.人们对他的对手多了些同情。
  • A telescope takes experience to use; later observers made mistakes of the same kind; and in his day curved glass was thought to produce illusions.用望远镜需要经验；后人也犯过类似错误；当时弯曲的玻璃被认为只制造幻象。
Our model summary本站范文

Galileo used to be seen simply as a fearless experimenter who scorned tradition, overthrew the ancients with his telescope and discovered the law of free fall. Closer study of the evidence and of the age has produced a more complex picture, and more sympathy for his opponents. Using a telescope well required skill; later scientists made similar mistakes; and in Galileo's day curved glass was believed to create illusions, so doubting what it showed was understandable.（76 词）

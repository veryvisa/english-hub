Lesson 42 · Recording an Earthquake 记录地震

📘 New Concept English · Book 4 · Lesson 42 “Recording an Earthquake”新概念英语 第四册 Lesson 42《Recording an Earthquake》

C1🧠 27 new words个新词📝 Full notes有完整讲解

🎯 Before you open the lesson开课前
An earthquake arrives like a burglar at night, with no warning, so what people needed was an instrument that never dozes. The earliest ones were a table of wooden rods of different heights and thicknesses: a light shock knocked over a few, a heavy one knocked over them all. The real breakthrough came from something anybody has felt - a bus pulls away and you fall backwards, because your feet have gone and your head has not. Hang a pen on a heavy weight, and the ground moves, the paper moves and the pen does not: that is a seismograph. This lesson is popular science at its best: it starts from the problem, then explains an abstract idea with an experiment the reader can do at home.
地震像夜里的小偷，来时毫无预兆，所以人们需要「从不打盹」的仪器。最早的装置是一桌高矮粗细不同的木棍：震动轻只倒几根，重则全倒。真正的突破来自一个日常观察——公共汽车突然开动，人会往后倒，因为脚动了、头没动。把笔挂在重锤上，地动纸动、笔不动，就能把震动画在纸上：这就是地震仪的原理。本课是科普说明文的典范：由问题引出原理，用读者能亲手做的小实验解释抽象概念。
Questions to keep in mind as you read读的时候带着这几个问题：
  1. How did the early rod device record an earthquake?早期用木棍记录地震的装置是怎样工作的？
  2. Why was the sudden-start-in-a-bus observation the key to the whole problem?「人在车上突然开动时会往后倒」这个观察为什么是解决问题的关键？
  3. Which three waves does an instrument far from the centre pick up?离震中 700 英里以外的地震仪会记录到哪三种波？

📖 Understanding the piece读懂这篇

The lesson comes from The Earth Beneath Us by H. H. Swinnerton, published in London by Frederick Muller in 1955 and reviewed in the Spectator that June; a Pelican paperback followed in 1958. Henry Hurd Swinnerton (1875-1966) held the chair of geology at Nottingham from 1910 to 1946, so the book was written in his late seventies, by a man who had spent thirty-six years explaining rocks to first-year students. We verified author, publisher, year and post from review and catalogue records, and have not read the chapter itself.

课文出自 H. H. Swinnerton 的《The Earth Beneath Us》，1955 年伦敦 Frederick Muller 出版，同年 6 月《Spectator》有书评；1958 年出了 Pelican 平装本。Henry Hurd Swinnerton（1875–1966）1910 至 1946 年任诺丁汉地质学讲席，所以这本书写于他七十多岁时——一个给大一学生讲了三十六年岩石的人。作者、出版社、年份与职位我们核过（书评与图书馆目录），原章未读。

🌍 Where it comes from出处与背景

The instruments in this lesson are older than the book by two generations. The rods on a table, the weight on a string, the pen on a slowly turning drum: that is the apparatus of the 1880s and 1890s, when a small group of British engineers teaching in Japan built the first machines that could draw a shock, and the museum photograph printed beside the lesson belongs to the same world. By 1955 those records had already been read for half a century, and what came out of them is the reason the last paragraph matters. The two wave trains that travel through the rock, rather than round the surface, are what told us what is under our feet: a core inferred in 1906 from the way the waves failed to arrive, its depth put at about 2,900 kilometres a few years later, the outer core shown to be liquid in 1926 because one of the two waves will not pass through it, and an inner core found in 1936 inside the first. An old professor writing for the general reader describes a pen, a weight and a piece of string, and does not mention that this is the only instrument we have ever had for seeing the inside of the planet.

这一课里的仪器比这本书还老两代。桌上的木棍、绳上的重锤、缓慢转动的鼓上那支笔，是 1880、1890 年代的装置——那时一小批在日本任教的英国工程师造出了最早能把震动画下来的机器；印在课文旁边的那张博物馆照片也属于同一个世界。到 1955 年，这类记录已经被读了半个世纪，而读出来的东西正是最后一段的分量所在：穿过岩石（而不是沿地表走）的那两列波，告诉了我们脚底下是什么——1906 年有人从「波没有按时到」推断出地核，几年后把界面深度定在约 2,900 公里；1926 年因为两种波里有一种过不去，证明外核是液态；1936 年又在里面发现了一个内核。一位老教授给普通读者写文章，讲的是一支笔、一个重锤和一截绳子，却没有提：这是人类唯一一件能看见行星内部的仪器。

✨ How it is written赏析

The order of this page is the whole lesson in how to explain something. It begins with the difficulty, not the answer: an event with no warning, so the watchman cannot be a man. Then the crude first attempt, which works a little and shows what is missing. Then the requirement, stated as a problem the reader can feel is hard - the table moves, the paper moves, the hand moves, so how can anything draw a straight line? Then, and only then, the ordinary observation that unlocks it, taken from a bus rather than from a laboratory. Then an experiment with a weight and a piece of string, written in the imperative so that the reader's hands do the proving. Only at the end does the machine appear, by which point it is obvious. Two smaller things are worth stealing. He puts himself on the page - his own pen moving while the paper stays - which is the only moment in this part of the book where the writer is visibly holding something; and he lets the reader hear how daft the idea sounds before he defends it, which buys him all the trust he needs. The opening comparison, an instrument that never dozes and never sleeps, is borrowed from a psalm, and the borrowed solemnity is a joke at the expense of the sleeping scientist who appears two sentences later.

这一页的顺序本身就是一整课「怎么解释一件事」。它从困难开始，而不是从答案开始：一个毫无预兆的事件，所以守夜人不能是人。接着是粗糙的第一次尝试，它有点用，并且显出缺了什么。然后把要求说成一个读者能感到难的问题：桌子在动、纸在动、手也在动，那还怎么画出一条直线？然后——而且只在这时——那个解开死结的日常观察出场了，而它取自一辆公共汽车，不是取自实验室。再然后是一个用重锤和一截绳子做的实验，用的是命令式，让读者自己的手去完成证明。机器直到最后才出现，而那时它已经显而易见。还有两件小事值得偷：他把自己写进了纸面——他自己的笔在动而纸不动——这是这一部分书里唯一一处作者手里明显拿着东西的时刻；他还让读者先听见这个主意有多傻，然后才去为它辩护，这一下就买到了他需要的全部信任。开头那个比喻——一件既不打盹也不睡觉的仪器——是从一首诗篇里借来的，而借来的庄严，是在拿两句之后出场的那位睡着的科学家开玩笑。

🧭 What it argues, and does it still hold?解读

Everything here still works, and that is the interesting part. The principle is inertia and relative motion, and it has not been improved on; what has changed is the size of the weight. The pendulum became a spring, the spring became a chip, and the chip now travels in a pocket. A paper in Science in July 2025 reported that ordinary telephones had been turned into that network: a system running on Android handsets delivered warnings in 98 countries for shocks of magnitude 4.5 and above, detected more than 11,000 earthquakes in three years, sent over 1,200 alerts, and raised the number of people with access to early warning roughly tenfold, to about two and a half billion. The authors are careful to say such sensors are less sensitive than a proper station, which is exactly the trade his rods already made. Note what the old sentence has become: the watchman that never sleeps is now a device that sleeps all day while the thing inside it does not. The other afterlife is political. Six years after the book, the first global network of standardized instruments was built under an American programme whose purpose was to detect underground nuclear tests, about a hundred and twenty stations of them, and the science of the earth's interior rode along on arms control. The home experiment with the string ended up in treaty verification, and today a test in a closed country is identified by the same two wave trains in the last paragraph of this lesson. For Chinese readers the comparison is almost too neat. An instrument of AD 132, described in the Book of the Later Han, registered the direction of a distant shock by dropping a bronze ball from one of eight dragons; the text does not say how the inside worked, and the reconstructions are guesses, including the one that puts an inverted pendulum in the middle. Swinnerton's rods are a cousin of it: both answer the question of direction without answering the question of how much, and both are beaten by the machine that can draw. What his page teaches, in the end, is that the leap was not the instrument. It was noticing that you can measure movement only against something that refuses to move.

这里的一切今天都还成立，而有意思的恰恰是这一点。原理是惯性与相对运动，没有被改进过；变的只是那个重物的大小：摆变成了弹簧，弹簧变成了芯片，而芯片现在装在口袋里走路。2025 年 7 月《Science》上的一篇论文报告，普通手机已经被组成了那样一个网络：一套跑在安卓手机上的系统在 98 个国家发出 4.5 级以上地震的预警，三年里探测到一万一千多次地震、发出一千二百多次警报，把能收到预警的人数提高了约十倍，到二十五亿左右。作者谨慎地说明，这类传感器不如正规台站灵敏——而这正是他那些木棍早就做过的那笔交易。注意那句老话变成了什么：那个永不睡觉的守夜人，如今是一台整天睡着、而里面那个东西不睡的装置。另一段后世则是政治的。书出版六年后，第一个全球标准化台网在一项美国计划下建起来，目的是探测地下核试验，大约一百二十个台站；地球内部的科学就搭在军备控制的车上走了一程。那个用绳子做的居家实验，最后落到了条约核查上；今天一个封闭国家的试验，正是靠本课最后一段里的那两列波被认出来的。对中国读者，这个对照几乎太整齐了：公元 132 年的一件仪器，《后汉书》记载它以八条龙中的一条吐出铜丸来指示远处地震的方向；原文没有说内部怎么工作，各种复原都是猜测，包括那个把倒立摆放在中间的方案。Swinnerton 的木棍是它的表亲：两者都回答了「从哪个方向来」而回答不了「有多大」，两者也都被会画线的机器打败了。他这一页最终教的其实不是仪器。是那个发现：你只能拿一个拒绝移动的东西，来量移动。

💬 Discuss and write讨论与写作
  1. Do the experiment in the lesson - a heavy thing on a long string, your hand moving sideways but not up and down - and then write six sentences explaining what you saw to someone who has not done it. You may not use the word inertia.
    把本课里的实验做一遍：一截长绳吊一个重物，手只左右动、不上下动。然后用六句话向一个没做过的人解释你看到了什么。不许用「惯性」这个词。
  2. The writer solves his problem by remembering a bus. Think of something you understand because of an everyday experience rather than a definition, and write the explanation the way he does: difficulty first, homely observation next, the answer last.
    作者靠回想一辆公共汽车解决了他的问题。想一件你是靠日常经验、而不是靠定义才真正懂的事，照他的办法写出来：先说困难，再放那个家常的观察，答案放最后。
  3. An instrument built to watch the earth was paid for in order to watch other countries. Does it matter why a piece of science gets funded, if the science is good? Take a side in 120 words, and state the strongest form of the other side first.
    一件为观察地球而造的仪器，是为了观察别的国家才被付钱的。如果科学本身是好的，它为什么被资助还重要吗？用 120 词选一边，并先把另一边最有力的说法写出来。

③ How the hard sentences are built难句结构

Sentences are counted in the lesson text, title not included. Find the sentence in your book, then read our notes on its structure.句子按课文正文数（不含标题）。在教材里找到这一句，再看本站的结构讲解。

Sentence 2第 2 句 (begins开头 “It was necessary, …”)

  • it is the formal subject; the real subject is the infinitive that follows.it 形式主语，真正主语是不定式 to invent …。
  • The relative clause pairs two near-synonyms. Saying both is a rhetorical device, and the words echo a line from the Psalms about a keeper who does not slumber or sleep.that neither slumbered nor slept：定语从句；slumber 和 sleep 意思几乎相同，连用是一种修辞强调（化用《圣经》诗篇「他必不打盹，也不睡觉」）。

Sentence 9第 9 句 (begins开头 “recorded for the …”)

  • The verb has two objects, and both have been pushed to the back by the for-phrase.recorded 的两个宾语 the strength … 和 the direction … 被 for the slumbering scientist 推到后面。
  • too weak to waken him：too … to（L34）；from which it came：come from a direction → from which。

Sentence 11第 11 句 (begins开头 “But instruments far …”)

  • A comparative phrase after the noun, with far strengthening the comparative.far more delicate than that：形容词比较级短语后置修饰 instruments；far 修饰比较级。
  • A passive form of be to do, used inside the if-clause to mean if anybody wanted to make one.was to be made：be to do 的被动，在 if 从句里表「如果要……的话」。

Sentence 16第 16 句 (begins开头 “That sounds a …”)

  • sound plus a noun is possible in British English; American English more often says sounds like.sound + 名词：英式英语可直接接名词（美式多说 sounds like）。
  • A past participle after the noun, working like a relative clause.adopted in …：过去分词作后置定语修饰 idea。

Sentence 26第 26 句 (begins开头 “It will be …”)

  • An impersonal passive: it stands in for anybody, and sounds more objective than You will find.It will be found that …：无人称被动句，「你会发现……」，比 You will find 更客观。
  • but here means only, and the phrase that follows takes it one step further: or nothing at all.but slightly：but = only，「只是轻微地」；or not at all＝或者根本不动。

Sentence 31第 31 句 (begins开头 “It was upon …”)

  • An emphatic it was ... that sentence (Lesson 26), throwing the weight onto the adverbial of means.强调句（L26）：强调方式状语 upon this principle。

④ Grammar in this lesson本课语法

How be to do is usedbe to do 的用法
be to do can carry four meanings: (1) a plan or arrangement; (2) an order or a rule; (3) inside an if-clause, if somebody wants to; (4) with was or were, a fate, something that turned out later.
be to do 可以表示 ① 计划安排（将要）；② 命令、规定（应该）；③ 在 if 从句里表「如果想要……」；④ 注定（was/were to do，后来发生的事）。

(1) A plan: The President is to visit Canada next week.

① 计划：The President is to visit Canada next week.

(2) An order: You are to finish this by Friday.

② 命令：You are to finish this by Friday.

(4) A fate: He was never to see his home again.

④ 命运：He was never to see his home again.（他从此再也没能回家）

The passive is be to be done, as in this lesson.

被动形式：be to be done——本课 was to be made。

  • If we are to catch the train, we must leave now.
  • The meeting is to be held on Monday.
Impersonal passives: It is said / It will be found that ...无人称被动句：It is said / It will be found that …
it + be + past participle + that ...: it states a view or a finding without saying whose, and so sounds objective. The usual verbs are say, believe, report, find, know, think and expect.
It + be + 过去分词 + that …：说出看法、发现而不说「谁」认为，语气客观；常见动词 say, believe, report, find, know, think, expect。

The same thing can be said with subject + be + past participle + infinitive: It is said that he is rich becomes He is said to be rich (Lesson 34).

可换成「主语 + be + 过去分词 + to do」：It is said that he is rich. → He is said to be rich.（L34 复合宾语的被动）

If the action came first, use a perfect infinitive: It is believed that he left last week becomes He is believed to have left last week.

时间先于主句用完成不定式：It is believed that he left last week. → He is believed to have left last week.

  • It is reported that three people were injured.
  • It is believed that the painting is genuine.
The passive of the continuous: was being done进行时的被动：was being done
The passive of a continuous tense is be being done, and it stresses that at that moment the thing was having something done to it. In the past it is was or were being done.
进行时的被动式＝be being done，强调某个时刻「正在被……」；过去进行被动 was/were being done。

Compare with the simple past passive: The drum was shaken reports the result; The drum was being shaken describes what was going on at the time.

与一般过去被动比较：The drum was shaken.（被摇了——结果）／ The drum was being shaken.（当时正在被摇——过程）

There is no passive for the perfect continuous (not has been being done): say it another way.

完成进行时没有被动形式（✗ has been being done），要用别的说法。

  • The road is being repaired, so we have to go round.
  • I felt that I was being watched.

Keywords for retelling复述关键词

  • earthquakes come without warning → instruments that never sleep
  • early device: rods on a table — how many fell, which way they fell
  • better idea: keep the pen still and let the paper move
  • everyday observation: a sudden start in a bus — feet move, head stays

  • pen on a weight + paper on a rotating drum → wriggling line; three waves recorded far from the centre

Summary writing摘要写作

In no more than 80 words, explain the principle of the early seismograph, from the table of rods to the design in which the pen stays still and the paper moves.

用不超过 80 个词说明早期地震仪的原理：从木棍装置讲到「笔不动、纸动」的设计。

Points a good summary covers好摘要要覆盖的要点
  • Earthquakes give no warning, so the instrument has to be on duty at all times; the earliest one used rods of different sizes, and how many fell, and which way, gave the strength and the direction.地震没有预兆，需要随时工作的仪器；早期装置用高矮不同的木棍，倒下的数量和方向表示震动强度和方向。
  • A better instrument had to draw the movement of the ground on paper with a pen, and the way to do it was to keep the pen still and move the paper.更精密的仪器要用笔在纸上记录地面的运动；办法是让笔不动而纸动。
  • The principle is inertia: when a bus starts, the feet go and the head stays; a heavy weight on a long string barely moves when the hand does.依据是惯性：车突然开动时人的脚动而头不动；挂在长绳上的重锤在手晃动时几乎不动。
  • With the pen fixed to the weight and the paper wrapped round a slowly turning drum, a shock draws a crooked line; better instruments record three directions and three kinds of wave separately.笔挂在重锤上，纸绕在缓慢转动的鼓上，地震时画出扭动的曲线；更完善的仪器能分别记录三个方向和三种波。
Our model summary本站范文

Because earthquakes come without warning, scientists needed instruments that were always on watch. An early device used rods of different sizes: how many fell, and which way, showed a shock's strength and direction. Better instruments used a pen and the principle of inertia: a heavy weight hung on a string stays almost still while its support moves. With a pen fixed to such a weight, paper on a rotating drum recorded the vibrations as a wavy line.（77 词）

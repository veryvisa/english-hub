Lesson 43 · Are There Strangers in Space 太空中有外星人吗？

📘 New Concept English · Book 4 · Lesson 43 “Are There Strangers in Space”新概念英语 第四册 Lesson 43《Are There Strangers in Space》

C1🧠 31 new words个新词📝 Full notes有完整讲解

🎯 Before you open the lesson开课前
Give the universe a planet even roughly like ours and life will almost certainly begin; and since the stars run to something like 3x10^20, intelligent life must be out there. So why has nobody called? The writer offers several answers. Perhaps they came long ago and found us too crude to be worth a second look. Perhaps they left an automatic messenger behind. The real difficulty is distance: even if such a messenger picked up our broadcasts from the 1920s, the reply would still be only half way home. The way out, he says, is to agree on one frequency - the 21-centimetre wavelength of hydrogen. This is a piece of reasoning, not a report: it moves from a premise to something it calls almost inevitable, then answers the objections. Watch the words that grade his confidence: almost certain, bound to, virtual certainty, may have, might well.
只要有一颗大致像地球的行星，生命几乎必然会出现；宇宙里的恒星多达 3×10²⁰ 颗，智慧生命一定存在。那为什么从没人来访？作者给出几种可能：也许他们早就来过、对原始的我们不感兴趣；也许留下了「自动信使」；最大的障碍是距离——就算信使收到我们 1920 年代的广播，回传的信号现在也才走了一半。出路是用氢原子的 21 厘米波长作宇宙间的「约定频率」。本课是推理型科普文：从前提推到「几乎必然」，再回答反驳，注意作者表示确定程度的词：almost certain, bound to, virtual certainty, may have, might well。
Questions to keep in mind as you read读的时候带着这几个问题：
  1. Why is the writer sure that intelligent life exists somewhere else?作者为什么认为宇宙中一定存在其他智慧生命？
  2. Why has nobody visited us? What answers does he offer?外星人为什么至今没有来访？作者给出了哪些解释？
  3. What does the one rational way of getting in touch depend on?「唯一合理的通讯方式」依靠的是什么？

🌍 Where it comes from出处与背景

Two things behind this page are worth knowing, because both are real and both are named in it. The first is the paper by Ronald Bracewell, then at Stanford, which appeared in Nature on 28 May 1960 and argued that a civilization far older than ours would not send radio messages across the galaxy at all; it would send machines, and one of them might already be sitting in our own solar system, waiting for somebody here to become worth talking to. The idea now carries his name. The second is the frequency. In March 1951 two physicists at Harvard, Harold Ewen and Edward Purcell, first detected radiation from hydrogen in interstellar space at a wavelength of about 21 centimetres, and the detection was published in Nature that September, held back until Dutch and Australian groups could confirm it. So when this writer says the figure must be known to any radio astronomer anywhere, he is talking about something that had been in the literature for barely a dozen years. The lesson is a report from the first decade of a real research programme, not a flight of fancy.

这一页背后有两件事值得知道，因为两件都是真的，而且课文都点了名。第一件是 Ronald Bracewell（当时在斯坦福）的论文，1960 年 5 月 28 日发表在《Nature》上：他主张一个比我们古老得多的文明根本不会把无线电讯号撒遍银河，它会派机器；而其中一台也许已经停在我们自己的太阳系里，等着这里有人变得值得搭话。这个设想现在以他的名字命名。第二件是那个频率：1951 年 3 月，哈佛的两位物理学家 Harold Ewen 与 Edward Purcell 第一次测到星际空间氢原子在约 21 厘米波长上的辐射；论文当年 9 月才在《Nature》刊出，因为要等荷兰和澳大利亚的同行先确认。所以当作者说宇宙里任何一位射电天文学家都必然知道这个数时，他谈的是一件写进文献才十几年的事。这一课是一份来自某个真实研究计划第一个十年的报告，不是凭空的遐想。

✨ How it is written赏析

This page is an argument, and the first thing to learn from it is how carefully the writer prices his own confidence. Within a few sentences he moves from almost certain through bound to and virtual certainty down to may have and might well, and the gradations are not decoration: the strong words sit on the astronomy, which was solid, and the weak ones sit on the biology and the guesswork, which was not. Read the lesson with a pencil, marking each phrase of this kind, and you have a map of what was actually known in 1965. The second move is structural. He states the conclusion early, then spends the rest of the page answering the one objection a reader is certain to raise - if they are out there, where are they? - and he answers it three times over, each answer weaker and more interesting than the last. The third move is the joke. He says that what any other civilization would make of us, having watched our television, is better not discussed, and then goes straight on. One short clause, no elaboration, and the reader is invited to finish it himself. That is the whole craft of dry humour in a piece of exposition: say less than you could, and move on. The closing image does the opposite job: the galaxy becomes a city, and the problem of searching it becomes the problem of meeting a friend in a place that big with no arrangement made. An abstraction has been turned into an afternoon anybody has wasted.

这一页是一个论证，首先该从它身上学的，是作者怎样小心给自己的信心定价。几句话之内，他从「几乎肯定」走到「必定」「几乎可以肯定」，再降到「可能」「很可能」；这些层次不是装饰：重的词押在天文学上，那部分是硬的；轻的词押在生物学和猜测上，那部分不硬。拿支笔把这类短语一个个标出来，你就得到一张 1965 年「哪些是真知道的」地图。第二手是结构：他很早就把结论摆出来，然后用剩下的篇幅回答读者必然会提的那一个反驳——如果他们在，他们人呢？——而且他回答了三遍，一遍比一遍弱，也一遍比一遍有意思。第三手是那个玩笑：他说别的文明看过我们的电视之后会怎么想我们，还是不说为好——然后径直走开。一个短句，不作展开，让读者自己把它补完。这就是说明文里冷幽默的全部手艺：少说一点，然后走开。结尾的比喻干的是相反的活：银河变成一座城市，搜索它的难题变成了在那么大的地方、事先没有约定、要碰见一个朋友。一个抽象的东西被换成了谁都浪费过的一个下午。

🧭 What it argues, and does it still hold?解读

Sixty years on, the two halves of his argument have come apart. The astronomy went his way, and more completely than he could have hoped. When he wrote, planets around other stars were an assumption; the first one around a sun-like star was announced in 1995, its discoverers took the Nobel prize in 2019, and by September 2025 the confirmed count had passed six thousand. His premise about numbers is no longer a premise. The biology did not go his way, because it has not moved. We still know of life starting exactly once, and the step from a planet roughly like ours to life on it remains the one term in the whole calculation with no number in it. That is why his phrase almost certain is the weakest word on the page: it was a hope wearing the clothes of a measurement. His picture of the neighbourhood has also been redrawn. He rules out Mars for dryness, Venus and Mercury for heat, and the outer planets for cold, which assumes life must be where Earth-like warmth and free oxygen are. Nobody frames it that way now: the places that interest biologists most are under the ice of Jupiter's and Saturn's moons, where there is liquid water, no sunlight and no oxygen worth speaking of. And the silence he was explaining away is still there, with better instruments to measure it. The frequency he recommends remains the obvious place to listen, and the listening has been done: one recent programme searched more than thirteen hundred nearby stars and the centres of ninety-seven galaxies, and found nothing to report. Three of his explanations for the silence are still the main ones on offer, which tells you how little has been settled. For Chinese readers there is a sharper version of his final image. The world's largest single-dish radio telescope now stands in Guizhou, and this kind of search is one of the things it is used for. The lesson's problem has become an instrument, a budget and a staff rota, and the question has not changed: where do you point it, and what would you accept as an answer?

六十年后，他论证的两半已经分开了。天文学那一半站到了他这边，而且比他敢期望的更彻底：他写作的时候，别的恒星周围有行星只是一个假设；第一颗围绕类太阳恒星的行星 1995 年宣布，发现者 2019 年拿了诺贝尔奖，而到 2025 年 9 月，确认数已过六千。他关于数量的前提，今天不再是前提。生物学那一半没站到他这边，因为它根本没动：我们知道的生命起源仍然只有一次；从「一颗大致像地球的行星」到「它上面有生命」这一步，是整个算式里唯一一个填不进数字的项。所以「几乎肯定」是这一页上最弱的词——它是一个穿着测量外衣的希望。他对邻居的描绘也被重画了。他以干燥排除火星，以炎热排除金星与水星，以寒冷排除外行星，这背后的假设是：生命必须出现在有类地温度和自由氧的地方。今天没有人这么划线了：最让生物学家感兴趣的地方，是木星与土星某些卫星的冰层之下——那里有液态水，没有阳光，也谈不上氧。而他当年要解释掉的那片沉默仍然在，只是测量它的仪器更好了：他推荐的那个频率依然是最该去听的地方，而且已经听过了——近年一个计划搜了一千三百多颗近邻恒星和九十七个星系的中心，没有可报告的东西。他为沉默给出的三种解释，今天仍是主要的候选，这正说明被解决的事有多少。对中国读者，他最后那个比喻有一个更锋利的版本：世界上最大的单口径射电望远镜现在立在贵州，而这一类搜索正是它的用途之一。课文里的难题已经变成了一台仪器、一份预算和一张排班表，而问题本身没变：你把它指向哪里，以及你会接受什么算作一个回答？

💬 Discuss and write讨论与写作
  1. Go through the lesson marking every phrase that grades the writer's confidence, from the strongest to the weakest. Then say which of his claims are carried by the strong words and which by the weak ones, and whether he put them in the right places.
    把课文里每一个表示确定程度的短语都标出来，从最强排到最弱。然后说一说：他哪些主张是由强词扛着的、哪些是由弱词扛着的，以及他有没有把它们放在对的位置上。
  2. His weakest step is the one everybody skips: that life is almost certain to begin on any suitable planet. We still have one example. Write 120 words arguing that one example is enough to reason from, or that it is not - and give the other side its best case first.
    他最弱的一步正是人人都跳过的那一步：只要行星合适，生命几乎必然开始。而我们至今只有一个例子。写 120 词论证「一个例子足以用来推理」或者「不足以」——先把另一方最有力的说法写出来。
  3. Suppose a machine of the kind described here really were parked in the solar system, listening. What would you want it to have recorded of the last hundred years, and what would you rather it had missed? Answer in six sentences.
    假设这里说的那种机器真的停在太阳系里，一直在听。过去一百年里，你希望它记录下什么，又希望它漏掉什么？用六句话回答。

Sentence 2第 2 句 (begins开头 “that given a …”)

  • The that-clause is the object, and it has been pushed away from its verb by the long from-phrase.conclude … that …：that 宾语从句与动词被 from the work of … 隔开。
  • be almost certain to do: it will happen, as near as makes no difference.be almost certain to do：几乎肯定会。

Sentence 4第 4 句 (begins开头 “Mars is too …”)

  • is has been left out in the middle clause, because the verb would be the same as the one before it.Venus far too hot：省略了 is（与前一分句谓语相同，省略避免重复）。
  • so + be + subject means the same is true of this one as well: inversion in agreement, not emphasis.and so is Mercury：so + be + 主语，「水星也是如此」（倒装表示肯定的附和）。
  • poor in oxygen: short of it. Compare be rich in something.poor in oxygen：缺乏氧气；be poor/rich in …。

Sentence 6第 6 句 (begins开头 “But other suns, …”)

  • An inserted phrase in which as is the relative pronoun, equal to which is what they call them. The printed start and as is are misprints for stars and as.stars as the astronomers call them：插入语，as 是关系代词（= which is what astronomers call them）。课文中的 start / as is 是印刷错误，应为 stars / as。
  • be bound to do: certain to.be bound to do：必定。
  • virtual certainty: as good as certain (virtual, Lesson 37).virtual certainty：几乎可以肯定（virtual，L37）。

Sentence 12第 12 句 (begins开头 “so vast is …”)

  • With so + adjective at the front, the main clause inverts: the subject follows the verb.So + adj. 放句首，主句倒装：so vast is the number = the number is so vast。
  • The that-clause gives the consequence.that 引导结果状语从句。
  • Although concedes the small percentage; the sentence then overwhelms it with the number of planets.Although 让步：虽然只有百分之一……，但行星数量太多了。

Sentence 18第 18 句 (begins开头 “may have left …”)

  • may have done: a guess about the past (Lesson 1).may have done：对过去的推测（L1）。
  • The infinitive gives the purpose.to await …：不定式作目的状语。

Sentence 21第 21 句 (begins开头 “although what impression …”)

  • The -ing phrase works as an adverbial of condition or time: if, or when, it picked them up.receiving our … signals：现在分词作状语（条件/时间）。

Sentence 30第 30 句 (begins开头 “though good enough …”)

  • have no chance of doing: it is simply not possible.have no chance of doing：根本不可能。
  • let alone: and still less (Lesson 32).let alone：更不用说（L32）。

④ Grammar in this lesson本课语法

So / Such at the front of the clause, and the inversion that followsSo / Such 放句首引起倒装
When so + adjective or adverb, or such + be, is moved to the front for emphasis, the main clause inverts (be or the auxiliary goes first), and a that-clause of consequence usually follows.
「so + 形容词/副词」或「such + be」放到句首加以强调时，主句要倒装（be 或助动词提前），后面常接 that 结果从句。

With an ordinary verb, borrow did: So fast did he run that nobody could catch him.

助动词形式：So fast did he run that nobody could catch him.（实义动词借 did）

With such: Such was his anger that he couldn't speak, which is the same as His anger was such that ...

such 形式：Such was his anger that he couldn't speak.（= His anger was such that …）

  • So difficult was the exam that few students passed.
  • Such was the force of the storm that trees were uprooted.
given / assuming / provided: conditions set up with a participle or a conjunctiongiven / assuming / provided：用分词或连词表条件
given (a preposition, taking a noun or a that-clause) means if there is, or allowing for; assuming (that) means supposing; provided or providing (that) means only if.
given（介词，后接名词或 that 从句）＝「假如有、考虑到」；assuming (that) …＝「假定……」；provided/providing (that) …＝「只要、如果」。

given can also mean allowing for: Given his age, he did very well.

given 也可表「考虑到」：Given his age, he did very well.（考虑到他的年龄，他做得很好了）

provided insists on the condition and sounds firmer: You can go, provided you finish your homework.

provided 强调前提条件，口气较强：You can go, provided you finish your homework.

  • Given enough time, anyone can learn to swim.
  • Assuming (that) the train is on time, we'll arrive at six.
Grading your confidence: be bound to / be certain to / be likely to / may have done表示确定程度的说法：be bound to / be certain to / be likely to / may have done
The writer scores each inference with a different phrase: be bound to and be certain to (it must be so) are stronger than be likely to (probably), which is stronger than may or might (well) (possibly), and may have done makes the guess about the past.
作者用不同的词给推断「打分」：be bound to / be certain to（必然）＞ be likely to（很可能）＞ may / might (well)（可能）＞ may have done（过去可能）。

be bound to takes the bare infinitive and means destined to; be bound for means travelling to: The ship is bound for Shanghai.

be bound to 后接动词原形，表「注定、一定会」；be bound for 表「开往」：The ship is bound for Shanghai.

be sure/certain to do gives the speaker's judgement, while be sure/certain that ... or be sure of ... gives the subject's own feeling: He is sure to win (I have no doubt) against He is sure that he will win (he has no doubt).

be sure/certain to do 是说话人的判断；be sure/certain that … 或 be sure of … 是主语自己的感觉：He is sure to win.（我肯定他会赢）／ He is sure that he will win.（他自己有把握）

  • It's bound to rain — look at the clouds.
  • She is likely to be late.

⑤ Patterns to reuse可以套用的句型

  • We must conclude from … that … 我们从……一定会得出结论：……
    We must conclude from these results that the drug is safe.
  • given …, … is almost certain to … 只要有……，……几乎肯定会……
    Given good weather, the event is almost certain to succeed.
  • … is best left unsaid ……还是不说为好
    What he thought of the food is best left unsaid.
  • … have no chance of …, let alone … 根本不可能……，更不用说……
    He has no chance of winning, let alone breaking the record.

Summary writing摘要写作

In no more than 80 words: why does the writer believe in other intelligent life, what is the biggest obstacle to reaching it, and what is the way round?

用不超过 80 个词概括：作者为什么相信存在其他智慧生命？与他们联系的最大障碍是什么？可能的解决办法是什么？

Points a good summary covers好摘要要覆盖的要点
  • Any planet roughly like ours will almost certainly produce life, and with so many stars, intelligent life has to exist.只要有类似地球的行星，生命几乎必然产生；恒星数量巨大，智慧生命必然存在。
  • Visitors may have come long ago, found human beings too crude to be interesting, and perhaps left a machine behind to listen.外星人可能早已来过，觉得原始的人类无趣，或许留下了自动信使。
  • The real obstacle is the distance: a radio message takes centuries, and today's rockets cannot even reach the nearest star.最大的障碍是遥远的距离；无线电信号要走几百年，现有火箭也到不了最近的恒星。
  • The rational way to talk is to use the frequency of hydrogen, at a wavelength of 21 centimetres, as the channel everyone can be expected to know.合理的通讯方式是利用氢原子 21 厘米波长的频率作为宇宙间公认的广播频率。
Our model summary本站范文

Since life almost certainly begins on any Earth-like planet, and the universe contains countless stars, intelligent life must exist elsewhere. Visitors may have come long ago and found us too primitive, perhaps leaving an automatic messenger behind. The real obstacle is distance: signals take centuries to travel, and our rockets cannot reach even the nearest star. The rational solution is to use the 21-cm wavelength of hydrogen, which any radio astronomer would know, as an agreed interstellar frequency.（78 词）

Lesson 2 · Spare That Spider 不要伤害蜘蛛

📘 New Concept English · Book 4 · Lesson 2 “Spare That Spider”新概念英语 第四册 Lesson 2《Spare That Spider》

C1🧠 21 new words个新词📝 Full notes有完整讲解

🎯 Before you open the lesson开课前
A model piece of advocacy: it opens with a question (what have spiders ever done for us?), then builds its case by contrast (birds and beasts against spiders), by clearing up a mistake (a spider is not an insect), and by numbers, and closes on one startling estimate. The skeleton — question, reasons, correction, data, conclusion — is worth borrowing the next time you have to argue for something in writing.
一篇典型的「为某物辩护」的说明文：先抛问题（蜘蛛凭什么是朋友），再用对比（鸟兽 vs 蜘蛛）、澄清误解（蜘蛛不是昆虫）、数字（每英亩 225 万只）层层加码，最后用一个惊人的估算收尾。学写议论文时，这种「问题→理由→纠错→数据→结论」的骨架可以直接借用。
Questions to keep in mind as you read读的时候带着这几个问题：
  1. Why does the writer call insects some of our worst enemies?为什么说昆虫是人类最大的敌人之一？
  2. How can you tell a spider from an insect at one look?蜘蛛和昆虫怎么一眼区分？
  3. Which numbers does the writer use to show how much work spiders do?作者用了哪几个数字来说明蜘蛛的作用？

🌍 Where it comes from出处与背景

The Listener was the BBC's weekly magazine, launched in January 1929 so that broadcast talks could survive in print, and closed with its last issue on 3 January 1991. That tells you what kind of writing this is: a radio talk for a general audience, spoken aloud before it was ever read. It explains the direct address, the rhetorical question at the start, the round numbers, and the single memorable image at the end. Three of the pieces in Book 4 come from the same magazine, which was for decades where British readers met scientists, critics and travellers speaking in their own voices. The particular argument also belongs to its moment. In the 1950s the standard way to defend an unloved animal was to show that it was useful to farmers; today the same case is usually made in terms of ecosystems and biodiversity rather than crop protection.

《听众》是 BBC 的周刊，1929 年 1 月创刊，用意是让广播里的谈话留下文字，1991 年 1 月 3 日出最后一期停刊。知道这一点，就知道这篇是什么文体：面向普通听众的广播讲话，先被说出来，才被读到。直接称呼读者、开篇的设问、整数、结尾那个让人记住的画面，都是讲话稿的做法。第四册里有三篇出自这同一本杂志——几十年间，英国读者就是在这里听科学家、批评家和旅行者用自己的声音说话。它的论证方式也属于那个年代：1950 年代替不受欢迎的动物辩护，标准做法是证明它对农民有用；今天同样的辩护通常改用生态系统与生物多样性来讲，而不是护住庄稼。

✨ How it is written赏析

Watch how the writer earns the right to his numbers. He does not begin with the census; he begins with a question he puts in your mouth, then answers it with the largest claim he can make, then spends the middle of the piece removing your objections one at a time. Are there not other animals that do this job? Yes, but their share is small. Are spiders not themselves pests? No, they leave your things alone. Are they not insects, so part of the problem? No, and here is how you can see it for yourself in one second. Only when every objection is gone does he bring out the figures, and he saves the strangest comparison for the last sentence. Two habits are worth stealing. First, he gives the reader a test that costs nothing: count the legs. A fact you can check yourself buys more trust than a fact you have to take on authority. Second, he converts a number nobody can picture into a weight set against something everybody can picture.

注意作者怎么为他的数字赢得信任。他不从普查数字开始，而是先把一个问题塞到你嘴里，再用他能给的最大断言回答它，然后用全篇的中段一个一个拆掉你的反驳。难道没有别的动物干这活吗？有，但份额很小。蜘蛛本身不是害虫吗？不是，它不动你的东西。它不就是昆虫、本身就是问题的一部分吗？不是，而且你一秒钟就能自己看出来。等反驳全部清空，他才摆出数字，并把最奇怪的那个对比留到最后一句。两个习惯值得偷：第一，他给读者一个零成本的验证办法——数腿。读者能自己验的事实，比只能听权威的事实更能换来信任。第二，他把一个谁都想象不出的数量，换算成一个重量，去对比一件人人都想象得出的东西。

🧭 What it argues, and does it still hold?解读

The claim has held up, and modern work has made it much bigger. A 2017 study in The Science of Nature estimated that the world's spiders kill something between 400 and 800 million tonnes of prey a year — set against roughly 400 million tonnes of meat and fish eaten each year by all human beings — from a standing spider biomass of about 25 million tonnes. Two of the study's findings cut against the lesson's framing, though. Forests and grasslands account for more than 95 per cent of that kill, while the farmland the lesson is anxious about contributes under 2 per cent, because ploughed, sprayed, harvested fields are poor habitat and carry little spider weight. So spiders matter enormously, but mostly where we are not farming; the honest version of the argument is no longer 'spiders guard your dinner' but 'spiders are one of the largest predators on earth, and intensive farming has largely pushed them out'. One number in the lesson we could not trace: the authority who counted 2,250,000 spiders to the acre in a southern English field is unnamed in the text, and the figure usually travels without a citation. The classic British grassland counts are W. S. Bristowe's, but we did not find this exact figure in a first-hand source, so treat it as unverified.

这个判断站住了，而且现代研究把它放得更大。2017 年《自然科学》（The Science of Nature）上的一项研究估算，全世界的蜘蛛每年捕食 4 亿到 8 亿吨猎物——相比之下，全人类每年吃掉的肉类与水产约 4 亿吨——而全球蜘蛛的现存生物量只有约 2500 万吨。不过该研究有两个结论和课文的框架相左：森林与草地占了这个捕食量的 95% 以上，而课文最担心的农田贡献不足 2%，因为翻耕、打药、收割过的地块是糟糕的栖息地，承载不了多少蜘蛛。所以蜘蛛极其重要，但主要重要在我们不耕作的地方；如实的说法不再是「蜘蛛守着你的饭碗」，而是「蜘蛛是地球上最大的捕食者类群之一，而集约农业基本上把它们挤了出去」。课文里有一个数我们没能追到源头：那位在英格兰南部一块草地上数出每英亩 225 万只蜘蛛的「权威」，原文没有点名，这个数字在后来的引用里也往往不带出处。英国草地蜘蛛密度的经典数据出自 W. S. Bristowe，但 225 万这个具体数字我们没在一手文献里找到，请当作未核。

💬 Discuss and write讨论与写作
  1. Pick an animal, plant or practice your family dislikes, and write 150 words defending it the way this writer does: one question, one largest claim, objections removed one at a time, numbers last.
    挑一种你家里人讨厌的动物、植物或做法，照这篇的路子写 150 词替它辩护：一个设问、一个最大的断言、一条条拆掉反驳、数字放在最后。
  2. The lesson defends spiders on the grounds that they are useful to us. What happens to an animal when that argument fails — when it turns out to be useless, or costly? Is usefulness a good basis for protection?
    课文替蜘蛛辩护的理由是它对我们有用。如果这个理由不成立——某种动物没用，甚至有害——它会怎样？「有用」是保护一个物种的好理由吗？
  3. Find a claim on the internet that comes with a big round number and no source, and try to trace it to a first-hand document. How far do you get, and what does that tell you about the numbers you repeat?
    在网上找一个带着整数大数字、却没有出处的说法，试着把它追到一手文献。你能追到哪一步？这件事对你平时转述的数字意味着什么？

③ How the hard sentences are built难句结构

Sentences are counted in the lesson text, title not included. Find the sentence in your book, then read our notes on its structure.句子按课文正文数（不含标题）。在教材里找到这一句，再看本站的结构讲解。

Sentence 0第 0 句 (begins开头 “Why, you may …”)

Why should spiders be our friends?

  • you may wonder: an aside that turns the writer's own question into the reader's, a common way to open a piece of exposition.you may wonder：插入语，把作者的提问变成「替读者问」，常见于说明文开头。
  • Why is a question word, so the auxiliary still comes before the subject: should spiders be ...; the aside sits between the two, with a comma on each side.Why 是疑问词，问句照常倒装：should spiders be…；插入语夹在疑问词和助动词之间，两边用逗号。

Sentence 4第 4 句 (begins开头 “if it were …”)

  • if it were not for + noun: 'were there no ...'. This is the unreal present, so the main clause takes would + bare infinitive.if it were not for + 名词：「要不是有……」，与现在事实相反的虚拟语气，主句用 would + 动词原形。

Sentence 6第 6 句 (begins开头 “but all of …”)

  • put together: a past participle phrase after the noun, meaning 'added up', = when they are put together.put together：过去分词短语作后置定语，「加在一起」，= when they are put together。
  • destroyed by spiders: another past participle phrase, this one describing the number, = which is destroyed by spiders.destroyed by spiders：过去分词作后置定语修饰 the number，= which is destroyed by spiders。

Sentence 8第 8 句 (begins开头 “Spiders are not …”)

Spiders are not insects, nor (are they) even nearly related to them.

  • as many people think: an aside opening with as, 'as many people believe'. In a negative sentence as applies only to the part being denied — what people believe is the opposite.as many people think：as 引导的插入从句，「如许多人以为的那样」——注意否定句里 as 只修饰被否定的部分：人们以为它们是昆虫。
  • After nor the writer leaves out are they; written in full it would be nor are they even nearly related, with the auxiliary first.nor 后省略了 are they；完整倒装是 nor are they even nearly related。

Sentence 9第 9 句 (begins开头 “One can tell …”)

  • for: a co-ordinating conjunction meaning 'because', adding a reason. It needs a comma in front and cannot start the sentence.for：并列连词「因为」，补充理由，前面要有逗号，不能放句首。

Sentence 16第 16 句 (begins开头 “It has been …”)

  • It is a dummy subject; the real subject is the that clause. The passive It is estimated / believed / said that ... keeps the tone impersonal.It 是形式主语，真正的主语是 that 从句；用被动 It is estimated/believed/said that… 显得客观。

④ Grammar in this lesson本课语法

The unreal conditional: if it were not for / but for虚拟语气：if it were not for / but for
if it were not for + noun (unreal present) or if it had not been for + noun (unreal past) = 'were it not for ...'; the main clause takes would do or would have done.
if it were not for + 名词（与现在相反）／if it had not been for + 名词（与过去相反）＝要不是……；主句用 would do / would have done。

In writing you can drop if and put the verb first: Were it not for your help, ... / Had it not been for the rain, ...

书面可省 if 并倒装：Were it not for your help, … / Had it not been for the rain, …

A shorter way to say the same thing: But for / Without + noun, with the same unreal main clause.

更简洁的说法：But for / Without + 名词，主句同样用虚拟。

  • Had it not been for his advice, I would have failed.（与过去相反）
  • But for the bad weather, we would have arrived on time.
The dummy object it: make it + adjective + for sb + to do形式宾语 it：make it + adj. + for sb + to do
When the object is an infinitive or a clause and something has to follow it, English puts it in the object slot first and moves the real object to the end: make / find / think / consider + it + adjective or noun + to do or that ...
当宾语是不定式或从句而后面还有宾补时，用 it 先占宾语位置，真正的宾语放到后面：make/find/think/consider + it + 形容词/名词 + to do/that…

You cannot say make impossible for us to live ... (no dummy object), and make us impossible to live ... means something else: that we ourselves are impossible.

不能说 make impossible for us to live…（缺形式宾语）；也不能说 make us impossible to live（意思变成「使我们不可能」）。

  • I find it hard to concentrate in a noisy room.
  • She made it clear that she disagreed.
Carrying a negative on: nor / neither with inversion, and not ... the least否定的延续：nor / neither + 倒装；not the least
When one negative statement is followed by a second, use nor or neither and put the auxiliary before the subject. The least, used as an adjective in a negative sentence, means 'not even a little'.
前句否定，接着再否定一件事时用 nor/neither，其后助动词提前（倒装）；the least（作形容词）用在否定句里＝丝毫都不。

not ... the least = not ... at all. Do not confuse it with at least, which means 'no less than'.

not … the least ＝ not … at all；不要与 at least（至少）混淆。

  • He doesn't smoke, nor does he drink.
  • I haven't the least idea what you mean. 我一点也不明白你的意思。

⑤ Patterns to reuse可以套用的句型

  • Why, you may wonder, … ? 你也许会问，为什么……？（引出话题）
    Why, you may wonder, should we save water in a rainy country?
  • It has been estimated that … 据估计……
    It has been estimated that a third of all food is wasted.

Summary writing摘要写作

In no more than 80 words, explain why spiders deserve to be called our friends. Cover all three steps: the harm insects do, how spiders compare with the other animals that eat insects, and their numbers and appetite.

用不超过 80 个词说明为什么蜘蛛是人类的朋友。要求把「昆虫的危害」「蜘蛛与其他食虫动物的对比」「数量与食量」三层都写到。

Points a good summary covers好摘要要覆盖的要点
  • Insects are great enemies of ours, and without animals that eat them they would strip our fields and kill our farm animals.昆虫是人类大敌，没有食虫动物的保护，它们会吃光庄稼、害死牲畜。
  • Birds and beasts eat insects too, but added up they account for only a small share of what spiders take, and spiders leave us and our things alone.鸟兽也吃昆虫，但加起来也只是蜘蛛消灭量的一小部分，而且蜘蛛从不伤害人和财物。
  • A spider is not an insect at all: eight legs against six.蜘蛛不是昆虫（八条腿对六条腿）。
  • Their numbers are enormous: over 2,250,000 to the acre in one English grass field.数量极多：英格兰一块草地每英亩超过 225 万只。
  • In a year the insects they take weigh more than everyone living in Britain.一年里它们吃掉的昆虫总重量超过英国全国人口的体重。
Our model summary本站范文

Spiders are our friends because they destroy huge numbers of insects, which would otherwise ruin our crops and kill our animals. Birds and beasts also eat insects, but together they kill far fewer than spiders do, and spiders never harm us. There may be over two million spiders in one acre, and the insects they eat in Britain each year outweigh the whole population.（64 词）

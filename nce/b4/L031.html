Lesson 31 · The Sculptor Speaks 雕塑家的语言

📘 New Concept English · Book 4 · Lesson 31 “The Sculptor Speaks”新概念英语 第四册 Lesson 31《The Sculptor Speaks》

C1🧠 29 new words个新词📝 Full notes有完整讲解

🎯 Before you open the lesson开课前
Why is sculpture called the hardest of the arts to appreciate? Henry Moore, the British sculptor, answers: most of us read only flat outlines. A child learns to judge distance well enough to keep itself safe, and there it stops. The sculptor, by contrast, carries a solid body in his mind — front and back at the same time, its balance point, the air it pushes aside. The viewer has the same work to do: to see an egg as pure shape, not as breakfast and not as a bird to come. This is an essay on art, thick with abstract nouns (appreciation, perception, completeness). Read it this way: whenever an abstract noun turns up, change it back into a verb (perception to perceive) and ask who is doing what to what.
为什么说雕塑是最难欣赏的艺术？作者亨利·摩尔（英国雕塑大师）给出的答案是：大多数人只会看「平面的形」，孩子学会判断距离以后就停住了；雕塑家却要把一个形体「握在掌心」，同时感知它的正反面、重心、体积。欣赏者也一样，要学会把一个蛋只看成一个形体，而不是食物或未来的小鸟。本课是艺术论说文，抽象名词密集（appreciation, perception, completeness），读法是：每遇到一个抽象名词，就把它还原成动词想一想（perception → perceive）。
Questions to keep in mind as you read读的时候带着这几个问题：
  1. What ability does appreciating sculpture call for first, and why is it harder than looking at a painting?欣赏雕塑首先需要什么能力？为什么说它比欣赏绘画难？
  2. How does a sculptor take hold of a form inside his head?雕塑家在头脑中是怎样「把握」一个形体的？
  3. Why does the author bring in the egg?作者为什么举「蛋」的例子？

📖 Understanding the piece读懂这篇

The lesson comes from an article Henry Moore wrote for the BBC weekly The Listener of 18 August 1937 (pages 338-40), printed under the title A Sculptor Speaks and reprinted in the 1966 collection of his writings. He altered the title while the piece was in the press, so bibliographies list it both as A Sculptor Speaks and as The Sculptor Speaks.

课文取自亨利·摩尔为 BBC 周刊 The Listener 写的文章，1937 年 8 月 18 日刊出（第 338–340 页），当时题为 A Sculptor Speaks，1966 年收进他的文集。排印期间他改过题目，所以书目里既作 A Sculptor Speaks 也作 The Sculptor Speaks。

🌍 Where it comes from出处与背景

Moore was thirty-nine when he wrote this, the son of a Yorkshire coal miner, trained in Leeds and then in London, and still a disputed figure in British art. The weekly that printed him belonged to the BBC and existed to put serious talk in front of ordinary readers, which is why the piece explains itself so patiently instead of arguing with other critics. The 1930s were the years when British sculptors were turning away from academic bronze portraits and towards carving stone and wood directly, letting the material show. Moore had spent years in the British Museum in front of Mexican, Sumerian and African carving rather than Greek marble, and that choice is audible here: he never once asks whether a figure looks like anybody. What he asks instead is whether you can feel its bulk.

写这篇时摩尔三十九岁，约克郡矿工的儿子，先在利兹、后在伦敦受训，在英国艺术界还是个有争议的人物。刊登他的周刊属于 BBC，办刊的意思是把严肃的讨论端到普通读者面前——所以文章一路耐心解释，不跟别的批评家争辩。1930 年代的英国雕塑正从学院派的青铜肖像转向直接雕凿石头与木头、让材料自己说话。摩尔多年泡在大英博物馆里看墨西哥、苏美尔和非洲的雕刻，而不是希腊大理石，这个选择在文章里听得出来：他一次都没问「这个像不像某个人」，他问的是「你感觉到它的体量了吗」。

✨ How it is written赏析

The piece opens with a definition and then widens like a ripple. First a child is called as witness, because everyone has been one. Then a word is coined, form-blind, and set beside a real medical term so that the invented word borrows the authority of the real one. Then comes the centre of the essay: a run of verbs, each more inward than the last, strung on semicolons so that reading it feels like one unbroken mental act. It closes by naming sixteen homely solids - shell, nut, plum, mushroom, bone - and a lofty argument lands back on the kitchen table. Two lessons for your own writing. When you explain an abstract skill, hand the reader one object to hold. And when you want a sentence to feel like a process rather than a list, use semicolons and keep the subject the same: the repetition is what makes it feel continuous.

文章从一个定义开始，然后像水波一样一圈圈扩大。先让孩子出庭作证，因为人人当过孩子。接着造一个词 form-blind，把它摆在一个真实的医学词旁边，让生造的词借来真词的权威。然后是全文的核心：一串动词，一个比一个更往里走，用分号串起来，读下来像一次不间断的心理活动。收尾点出十六样家常的实体——贝壳、坚果、李子、蘑菇、骨头——一篇高蹈的论说就落回了厨房的餐桌。两点可以搬进自己的写作：解释一种抽象能力时，先递给读者一个能握住的东西；想让句子读起来像一个过程而不是一张清单，就用分号、保持主语不变，重复本身就制造了连续感。

🧭 What it argues, and does it still hold?解读

Two things have changed since 1937. The first helps Moore. What he describes - holding a solid in the mind and turning it - is now a measured ability, tested by asking people to decide whether two drawn objects are the same shape rotated. The second corrects him. He writes as though most people simply stop developing, which leaves form-blindness sounding permanent. A 2013 review of 217 training studies found that spatial skill improves with practice by about half a standard deviation on average, and that the gains carry over to tasks the training did not cover. So the honest reading today is: untrained, not incurable. There is also a new way to test his claim. A sculptor can now rotate a scan of a figure on a screen and have it printed, which does part of the work he said had to happen inside the head - and yet the thing no screen has replaced is the hand, since touch is still how most of us learn depth at all. For Chinese readers the comparison is worth making. In the scholar tradition the prestigious art was the flat brushed surface, calligraphy and painting, while the great carving - the Buddhist caves, the buried army at Xian - was mostly anonymous craft. Sculpture as a signed, authored art came back from Paris with people like Liu Kaiqu, who studied there in the 1920s and began teaching in Hangzhou in the early 1930s. If an entire tradition could rank flat form first, then what Moore calls blindness looks less like a fault in the eye and more like a habit of training.

1937 年以来变了两件事。第一件对摩尔有利：他描述的那种能力——在脑子里托着一个实体并转动它——今天是可测量的，办法是给人看两个画出来的物体，让他判断是不是同一个形状转了个角度。第二件要纠正他：他写得像是大多数人就此停止发育，于是「形盲」听起来是永久的。2013 年一份汇总 217 项训练研究的综述发现，空间能力经练习平均提高约半个标准差，而且提高会迁移到训练里没练过的任务上。所以如实的说法是：不是治不好，是没练过。还多了一条检验他的新路径：雕塑家现在可以在屏幕上转动一个扫描模型再打印出来，这替掉了他所说「必须在头脑里完成」的一部分工作；但屏幕替不掉的是手——绝大多数人最初学会判断深度，靠的就是触摸。对中国读者，这个对照值得一提：文人传统里体面的艺术是平面的笔墨，书法与绘画；而最伟大的雕刻——佛教石窟、西安地下的军阵——多半是无名工匠的手艺。署名的、作者个人的雕塑艺术是从巴黎带回来的，比如 1920 年代留法、1930 年代初到杭州任教的刘开渠。如果一整个传统都能把平面的形排在第一位，那么摩尔说的「盲」，就不太像眼睛的缺陷，更像训练的习惯。

💬 Discuss and write讨论与写作
  1. Pick up the nearest small object and describe it out loud without once naming what it is for: only its weight, its curves, the space it pushes aside. How long can you keep going before the use of the thing creeps back in?
    随手拿起身边最近的一个小东西，出声描述它，但一次都不许说它有什么用——只说重量、曲面、它占掉的那块空间。你能坚持多久，才会不自觉地说回它的用途？
  2. Moore assumes appreciation can be learned. Think of something you once could not perceive and now can - a tone in a foreign language, a wrong note, a line in a painting - and write 120 words on what actually changed.
    摩尔的前提是「欣赏可以学」。想一件你以前感知不到、现在感知得到的东西——外语里的一个声调、一个走音、一幅画里的一根线条——写 120 词说清到底变了什么。
  3. Is a language learner form-blind in the same way: able to read the flat meaning of a sentence but not its shape? Argue for or against, with one example from your own English.
    外语学习者是不是也有一种「形盲」：读得懂句子的字面意思，却感觉不到句子的形状？支持或反对，用你自己英语里的一个例子说明。

③ How the hard sentences are built难句结构

Sentences are counted in the lesson text, title not included. Find the sentence in your book, then read our notes on its structure.句子按课文正文数（不含标题）。在教材里找到这一句，再看本站的结构讲解。

Sentence 3第 3 句 (begins开头 “certainly it is …”)

  • That is why ...: a predicative clause carrying the conclusion.That is why …：表语从句，引出结论。
  • shape in only two dimensions: an appositive explaining what flat forms means.shape in only two dimensions：flat forms 的同位语，解释什么叫「平面的形」。

Sentence 4第 4 句 (begins开头 “Many more people …”)

  • many more + plural noun: 'far more ...'. Use many more with countable nouns, much more with uncountable ones.many more + 复数名词：「多得多的……」，修饰可数名词比较级用 many more，不可数用 much more。
  • 'form-blind' is coined on the model of colour-blind; the quotation marks show the author made the word up himself.'form-blind' 仿照 colour-blind 造词，引号表示作者自创。

Sentence 7第 7 句 (begins开头 “But having satisfied …”)

  • having satisfied ...: a perfect participle working as an adjunct of time or cause — that action is over before the main verb.having satisfied …：完成式分词作时间/原因状语，表示「满足了……之后」，动作先于 go no further。
  • go no further: to stop at that point and advance no more.go no further：不再前进，意为「就此止步」。

Sentence 9第 9 句 (begins开头 “they do not …”)

  • Though for concession: may carries the concessive tone, 'they may well be able to ...'.Though 让步：may 表示让步语气（「也许能……」）。
  • needed to comprehend ...: a past participle phrase after effort, the same as which is needed to comprehend ...needed to comprehend …：过去分词短语作后置定语修饰 effort，= which is needed to comprehend …。

Sentence 12第 12 句 (begins开头 “He gets the …”)

  • whatever its size: a concessive adjunct with is left out (= whatever its size is / may be).whatever its size：让步状语，省略了 is（= whatever its size is / may be）。
  • as if + were holding: the subjunctive, against present fact — he is not really holding anything.as if + were holding：虚拟语气，与现在事实相反（他并没有真的握着）。
  • completely enclosed: a past participle phrase as object complement, describing it.completely enclosed：过去分词短语作宾语补足语，修饰 it。
  • In the sentence before, as it were is parenthetical, 'so to speak': it warns you that a comparison is coming.前面一句的 as it were 是插入语，「可以说」——给比喻打个招呼。

Sentence 14第 14 句 (begins开头 “he knows while …”)

  • what ... is like: what something is like, with statement word order inside the clause.what … is like：……是什么样子，从句用陈述语序。

④ Grammar in this lesson本课语法

The subjunctive after as if / as thoughas if / as though 后的虚拟语气
If the as if clause describes something untrue or unlikely, use the subjunctive: a past form for the present (be is always were), had done for the past.
as if 引导的从句如果说的是「不真实或不太可能」的情况，用虚拟：与现在相反用过去式（be 一律用 were），与过去相反用 had done。

In this lesson the clause as if he were holding marks the unreal present: nothing is actually in his hand.

本课：… as if he were holding …（他并没有真的握着——虚拟语气，与现在相反，用过去进行式）

If what you describe may well be true, keep the indicative: The sky looks as if a storm is coming. (it really has clouded over)

如果说的是可能真实的情况，就用陈述语气：The sky looks as if a storm is coming.（天真的阴了）

Matching the time: He talks as if he knew everything. (untrue now) / He talked as if he had seen a ghost. (untrue, and earlier than the main verb)

时间对应：He talks as if he knew everything.（现在不真实）／ He talked as if he had seen a ghost.（先于谓语动作的不真实）

In speech as if he was ... is common too, but formal writing keeps were.

口语里 as if he was … 也常见，但正式写作用 were。

  • She treats me as if I were a child.
  • He looked as if he hadn't slept for days.
Concessive clauses with whatever / howeverwhatever / however 引导的让步状语
whatever (= no matter what), however (= no matter how) and whoever open a concessive clause meaning 'it makes no difference ...'; be inside the clause is often left out.
whatever (= no matter what)、however (= no matter how)、whoever 等引导让步从句，表示「无论……」；从句里的 be 常可省略。

In this lesson whatever its size stands for whatever its size is / may be.

本课：… whatever its size …＝whatever its size is / may be。

whatever is followed by a noun or fills a slot in the clause; however is followed by an adjective or adverb: However hard he tries, he cannot do it. (not However he tries hard)

whatever 后接名词或作从句成分；however 后接形容词或副词：However hard he tries, he can't do it.（✗ However he tries hard）

The shortened form is common in writing: whatever the reason, whatever the cost, however difficult the task.

省略形式在书面语常见：whatever the reason、whatever the cost、however difficult the task。

Note that whatever can also open a noun clause (= anything that): Take whatever you want. That one is not concession.

注意 whatever 还能引导名词从句（= anything that）：Take whatever you want.——这里不是让步。

  • Whatever the weather, we'll go.
  • However tired she is, she always reads before bed.
The perfect participle having done完成式分词 having done
having + past participle as an adjunct stresses that the action was over before the main verb; the passive is having been done.
having + 过去分词作状语，强调该动作在主句动作之前已完成；被动形式是 having been done。

In this lesson the practical need is met first; only then comes the main verb.

本课：But having satisfied …，most people go no further.（先满足了实用需要，然后止步）

Compare the simple participle: Walking along the street, I met an old friend. (at the same time) / Having finished my work, I went home. (one after the other)

与一般分词对比：Walking along the street, I met an old friend.（同时）／ Having finished my work, I went home.（先后）

The implied subject must be the subject of the main clause: the people who satisfied the need are the same people who stop.

逻辑主语要和主句主语一致：having satisfied 的主语就是 most people。

not goes in front of having: Not having received a reply, he wrote again.

否定放在 having 前：Not having received a reply, he wrote again.

  • Having lived in Paris for ten years, she speaks perfect French.
  • Having been told the news, he rushed to the hospital.

⑤ Patterns to reuse可以套用的句型

  • … depends upon the ability to … ……取决于……的能力
    Learning a language depends upon the ability to listen carefully.
  • That is perhaps why … 这也许就是……的原因
    He grew up by the sea. That is perhaps why he loves swimming.
  • Many more … than … ……比……多得多
    Many more people read online than in print.
  • think of A as if … 把 A 想象成好像……
    He thinks of the company as if it were his own child.
  • quite apart from … 完全撇开……不谈
    Quite apart from the cost, the plan is too risky.

Summary writing摘要写作

Write no more than 80 words: why is sculpture hard to appreciate, and what must the sculptor and the viewer each do?

用不超过 80 个词概括：为什么欣赏雕塑很难？雕塑家和欣赏者分别要做到什么？

Points a good summary covers好摘要要覆盖的要点
  • Appreciating sculpture means reacting to a body in three dimensions, which is harder than reading flat shapes; far more people are blind to form than to colour.欣赏雕塑需要对三维形体作出反应，这比欣赏平面形状难，「形盲」的人比色盲多得多。

Our model summary本站范文

Sculpture is hard to appreciate because it requires a response to form in three dimensions, and most people are "form-blind". Children learn to see flat shapes and, later, to judge distances roughly for safety, but most people stop there. The sculptor must hold a whole form in his mind, sensing every side, its weight and volume. The viewer, too, must learn to see shape purely as shape — an egg, for instance, simply as a solid form.（77 词）

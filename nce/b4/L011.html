Lesson 11 · How to Grow Old 如何安度晚年

📘 New Concept English · Book 4 · Lesson 11 “How to Grow Old”新概念英语 第四册 Lesson 11《How to Grow Old》

🎯 Before you open the lesson开课前
The closing part of Bertrand Russell's essay on growing old. He holds that fear of death in an old person is both pitiable and beneath him, and that the cure is to let your interests widen and become less personal, so that the walls of the self fall away — as a river does in passing from a mountain torrent to a broad, quiet mouth. It is one of the best-known pieces in Book 4: a single figure carried through the whole passage (one human life as a river), with long, unhurried sentences and a fine rhythm, well worth learning by heart.
罗素（Bertrand Russell）随笔《如何安度晚年》的结尾部分。他说老人怕死既可怜又不体面，克服之道是把兴趣逐渐扩大、变得「非个人化」，让自我的墙慢慢退去——就像一条河从山涧奔腾到平缓入海。这是第四册最有名的篇目之一：一个贯穿全段的比喻（个人生命＝河流），句子舒展、节奏优美，非常值得背诵。
Questions to keep in mind as you read读的时候带着这几个问题：
  1. Why does the writer allow the young their fear of death but not the old?为什么作者认为年轻人怕死有情可原，老人怕死却不然？
  2. What does he hold to be the best way of getting over that fear?克服对死亡的恐惧，最好的办法是什么？
  3. How does the writer hope to die?作者希望自己怎样死去？

📖 Understanding the piece读懂这篇

The lesson is credited to Bertrand Russell, under the title "How to grow old", from Portraits from Memory (attribution line in the original Book 4). The essay first appeared in the London journal Family Doctor in June 1951, and the fuller version was collected in Portraits from Memory and Other Essays, published by George Allen and Unwin in 1956.

课文署名 Bertrand Russell，题为 How to grow old，选自《记忆中的画像》（Portraits from Memory）（据旧版第四册课末署名）。该文 1951 年 6 月首发于伦敦刊物《家庭医生》（Family Doctor），较完整的版本收入 1956 年由 George Allen and Unwin 出版的《记忆中的画像及其他随笔》。

🌍 Where it comes from出处与背景

Two facts about the author change how the passage reads. The first is his age when he wrote it: Russell was born on 18 May 1872 and wrote this in his eightieth year, for a popular medical magazine, which is to say he was not theorising about old age from the outside. The second is what came after. He died on 2 February 1970, aged ninety-seven, so he had another nineteen years to live when he wrote that he would rather die with the work still in his hands — and he spent most of them at work, on the Russell-Einstein manifesto against nuclear weapons in 1955, on the Pugwash conferences, on the campaign for nuclear disarmament, and at ninety on a public role in the Cuban missile crisis. He kept the promise in the last sentence for nearly two decades. It is also worth knowing what he was not saying. Russell had no religion and expected no afterlife; he had written as much, plainly and often. So the consolation offered here is a deliberately unreligious one. He does not say that you will continue; he says that what you care about will continue, which is a different proposition and the only one available to him.

关于作者的两个事实会改变这段文字的读法。第一个是他写它时的年纪：罗素生于 1872 年 5 月 18 日，这篇写于他的第八十个年头，刊在一本通俗医学杂志上——也就是说，他不是从外部思辨老年。第二个是后来发生的事。他于 1970 年 2 月 2 日去世，享年九十七，所以当他写下「希望在仍在工作时死去」时，他还有十九年可活——而且这十九年里他多半都在工作：1955 年的《罗素—爱因斯坦宣言》反对核武器、帕格沃什会议、核裁军运动，九十岁时还在古巴导弹危机中扮演了公开角色。最后那句承诺，他守了将近二十年。还值得知道他没有在说什么。罗素没有宗教信仰，也不期待来世，这一点他写过，写得直白而频繁。所以这里提供的安慰是刻意非宗教的。他没有说「你会延续」，他说的是「你所关心的事物会延续」——这是另一个命题，而且是他唯一能用的那个。

✨ How it is written赏析

The figure of the river is doing something more exact than decoration, and the way to see it is to notice what it refuses to claim. A river that reaches the sea does not survive. It loses its banks, its speed, its name and its separateness, and the sentence says so: painlessly lose their individual being. Most consolations about death promise the opposite, that something of you persists. Russell takes an image whose whole content is dissolution and makes it feel like an arrival, and he does it with nothing but word choice: the water does not end or vanish, it becomes merged, and the loss is painless, and the join has no visible break. Look at the shape of the sentences as well. The river sentence is built to perform what it describes. It starts short and broken by commas, where the stream is still little and tightly held between its banks, and then the clauses lengthen and the pauses thin out as the water widens, and the last stretch runs on without interruption into the sea. The prose slows down exactly where the river does. That is why the passage is worth learning by heart rather than merely understanding: the rhythm is carrying half the argument. And then the ending refuses to rise. A writer with a weaker ear would close on the sea. Russell comes back from the cosmic image to a desk and an unfinished job, and ends on the flattest words available, saying only that whatever could be done has been. After the river, that plainness is what makes it credible.

河流这个比喻做的事比装饰精确得多，而要看出来，办法是注意它拒绝声称什么。一条到达大海的河并没有存活下来。它失去了河岸、速度、名字和独立性，而句子就是这么说的：无痛地失去它们各自的存在。多数关于死亡的安慰承诺的恰恰相反，说你总有什么会留存。罗素取了一个全部内容都是消解的意象，却让它读起来像一次抵达，而他用的只是选词：水不是终止或消失，而是「融入」；那种丧失是「无痛的」；那个接合处「没有可见的断裂」。也看看句子的形状。写河流那一句是被造出来去表演它所描述的东西的。它起手短、被逗号切断——起初窄小，紧紧束在河岸之间——然后随着水面变宽，分句变长、停顿变稀，最后一段毫无中断地直流入海。散文正好在河流减速的地方减速。这就是为什么这段值得背下来，而不只是读懂：节奏承担了一半的论证。然后结尾拒绝升高。耳朵差一些的作者会收在大海上。罗素从宇宙意象回到一张书桌和一件没做完的活，用最平的几个词结束：能做的都已经做了。在河流之后，正是这种平，让它可信。

🧭 What it argues, and does it still hold?解读

The claim here is testable in a way that is unusual for an essay of consolation, and the evidence has mostly gone his way. Russell makes an empirical prediction, not a moral demand: widen your interests beyond yourself and the fear will loosen. Work in the psychology of ageing since has found something close to it. Older adults report, on average, more positive emotion and less fear of death than the middle-aged, with the strongest effects among people who stay engaged with things outside themselves — other people, work, causes, the next generation. The usual interpretations vary, and this is where honesty requires care: whether the mechanism is Russell's impersonality, or a narrowing of time horizons that makes people prize the present, or simply that those who remain engaged are the healthier ones, the studies do not settle. So treat his claim as supported in its prediction and unproven in its explanation. Two qualifications he does not make. First, the essay assumes that an old man has already done the work he was capable of doing, and that is a large assumption to rest the argument on. Russell had done his work; most people's lives are not shaped so tidily, and for someone whose work was taken from them by illness or circumstance, the advice to widen your interests has a harder edge than he can have intended. Second, the consolation depends on the things you care for continuing. For a philosopher whose causes outlive him, that holds. For a reader whose attachments are mostly particular people, it may offer less than it seems to. What survives all of this is the instruction, which is practical and testable in a single life: the fear of death tracks how much of your attention is spent on yourself, so the lever is not the fear but the attention. That is advice you can act on at any age, and it is cheaper to try than to argue about.

这里的主张在一篇安慰性随笔中少见地可以检验，而证据基本站在他那一边。罗素给的是一个经验性预测，不是一个道德要求：把兴趣扩展到自身之外，恐惧就会松动。此后的老龄心理学研究发现了与此接近的东西。平均而言，老年人报告的正面情绪多于中年人，对死亡的恐惧也更少，而效应最强的是那些仍与自身之外的事物保持联系的人——他人、工作、事业、下一代。对机制的解释不一，而这正是需要谨慎如实的地方：到底是罗素所说的「非个人化」，还是时间视野收窄让人更珍视当下，还是仅仅因为保持投入的人本来就更健康——研究没有定论。所以把他的主张当作「预测得到支持、解释尚未证实」。还有两点他没有加的限定。第一，这篇随笔假定一位老人「已完成了他力所能及的工作」，而把论证架在这个假定上，分量很重。罗素做完了他的工作；多数人的人生没有这么齐整，而对于那些工作被疾病或处境夺走的人，「把兴趣扩大」这句建议会比他本意更锋利。第二，这份安慰依赖于「你所关心的事物会延续」。对一位事业比自己活得更久的哲学家，这成立；对一位牵挂主要是某几个具体的人的读者，它给的可能比看上去少。经得住这一切的是那条操作指令，它务实，而且可以在一个人的一生中检验：对死亡的恐惧，随你把多少注意力花在自己身上而变化——所以可以撬动的不是恐惧，而是注意力。这是任何年纪都能照做的建议，而试一次比争论一场便宜。

💬 Discuss and write讨论与写作
  1. Read the river sentence aloud and mark where it speeds up and slows down. Then write one sentence of your own whose rhythm performs what it describes.
    把写河流的那一句大声读出来，标出它在哪里加速、在哪里减速。然后自己写一句，让它的节奏表演它所描述的东西。
  2. Russell's consolation rests on what you care for continuing. Name three things you care for that will outlast you, and one that will not. Does the difference matter?
    罗素的安慰建立在「你所关心的事物会延续」之上。写出三件你关心、且会比你活得更久的事，再写一件不会的。这个差别重要吗？
  3. Test his claim against someone you know who is old. Is the person who fears death least also the one whose interests reach furthest beyond themselves?
    用你认识的一位老人检验他的说法。最不怕死的那个人，是不是兴趣伸得离自己最远的那个？

④ Grammar in this lesson本课语法

The appositive clause against the relative clause (that)同位语从句 vs 定语从句（that）
An appositive clause spells out the content of an abstract noun in front of it (fact, idea, thought, news, belief, hope and the like), and its that has no job inside the clause. In a relative clause, that works as the subject or the object.
同位语从句解释前面抽象名词（fact, idea, thought, news, belief, hope…）的内容，that 在从句中不作成分；定语从句的 that 在从句中充当主语或宾语。

How to tell them apart: take out that and see what is left. A complete sentence means an appositive clause; a sentence with a hole in it means a relative clause.

判断：去掉 that 后剩下的部分是否是完整句子？完整 → 同位语从句；缺成分 → 定语从句。

  • The news that he had won surprised everyone.（同位语从句）
  • The news that he told me surprised everyone.（定语从句，that 作 told 的宾语）
The double negative and understatement (litotes)双重否定与低调陈述（litotes）
not + a word with a negative prefix (not unwelcome, not uncommon, not impossible) states something positively but more quietly, with a shade of 'in fact' or 'by no means'.
not + 否定前缀词（not unwelcome, not uncommon, not impossible）表达肯定但更含蓄，常带「其实」「未尝不」的语气。

Common cases: no small achievement; not without reason; not a little, meaning a good deal.

常见：no small achievement 不小的成就；not without reason 不无道理；not a little 非常。

  • It is not uncommon for people to change careers at 40.
  • He said so not without reason.
have ... to offer, have reason to do, and other cases of have + noun + infinitivehave … to offer / have reason to do 等「have + 名词 + 不定式」

Keep this apart from the modal have to, meaning must: look at whether have is followed first by a noun or straight by to.

注意与情态 have to（必须）区分：看 have 后面先接的是名词还是直接接 to。

  • I have a lot of work to do.
  • You have no reason to complain.

⑤ Patterns to reuse可以套用的句型

  • In A there is a justification for …; but in B … is … 在 A 身上……情有可原，在 B 身上却……
    In a child there is a justification for impatience; in a manager it is simply bad leadership.
  • X should be like Y — …, …, and … X 应当像 Y 一样——……（三个并列描写）
    A good speech should be like a journey—clear at the start, lively on the way, and quiet at the end.
  • … will be not unwelcome ……也未尝不受欢迎
    A few days of rain will be not unwelcome to the farmers after this dry summer.
  • I should wish to … while still …, knowing that … 我希望在仍……时……，因为知道……
    I should wish to retire while still healthy, knowing that younger people will do the job well.

Summary writing摘要写作

In no more than 80 words, sum up the writer's view of how an old person can get over the fear of death, and explain the figure of the river.

用不超过 80 个词概括作者关于老年人如何克服死亡恐惧的看法，并说明河流的比喻。

Points a good summary covers好摘要要覆盖的要点
  • The best course is to let your interests grow wider and less personal until the walls of the self draw back and your life runs into the life of everything.最好的办法是让兴趣逐渐变宽、变得超越个人，自我之墙慢慢退去，生命融入宇宙生命。
  • One human life is like a river: narrow and headlong at first, broad and quiet later, and at the end entering the sea without a mark.个人生命像河流：起初窄急，后来宽缓，最终无痕入海。
  • An old man who sees his life that way will not fear death, because the things he cares about will go on; and when his strength ebbs, rest will be no unwelcome thought.这样看待生命的老人不会怕死，因为他关心的事物会延续；精力衰退时，休息也是受欢迎的。
Our model summary本站范文

Fear of death is understandable in the young, but in old people who have lived full lives it is unworthy. The best cure is to widen one's interests until the ego fades and one's life merges with the universal life, like a river that grows broader and calmer until it flows painlessly into the sea. Such a person will not fear death, since what he cares for will continue.（69 词）

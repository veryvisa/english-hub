Lesson 141–142 · Sally's First Train Ride 萨莉第一次坐火车

📘 New Concept English · Book 1 · Lesson 141–142 “Sally's First Train Ride”新概念英语 第一册 Lesson 141–142《Sally's First Train Ride》

B1🧠 19 new words个新词📝 Full notes有完整讲解

🎯 Before you open the lesson开课前
The writer took her four-year-old, Sally, to a party by train. Sally had never been on one, so she was thrilled, sat by the window and asked about everything outside. A middle-aged woman got on, took the seat facing Sally and greeted her; Sally gave no reply and just stared with interest at the blue coat and the big, odd hat. Once the train was moving, the woman got out her powder compact and started on her face. Sally asked why. To look nice, came the answer, and the woman put the compact away with a friendly smile. Then Sally told her she still looked ugly. The child found it funny; her mother wanted the floor to swallow her. This is a short story with a punchline that teaches the passive with was invited and was dressed, the past perfect with never and after, make up, the to of purpose, and -ed adjectives such as excited, amused and embarrassed.
讲述人带四岁的女儿萨莉坐火车去参加儿童聚会。萨莉从没坐过火车，兴奋得很，坐在窗边看见什么问什么。一位中年女士上车坐在她对面，跟她打招呼，萨莉不答，只是好奇地盯着她：蓝外套，一顶又大又滑稽的帽子。火车开出车站后，女士拿出粉盒开始化妆。萨莉问她为什么，她说「让自己漂亮点」，收起粉盒，和蔼地笑了笑。萨莉说：「可你还是很丑啊。」萨莉觉得好玩，妈妈却窘得要命。本课是一则带笑点的短叙事，学被动语态 was invited / was dressed、过去完成时 had never travelled、after the train had left、make up、To make myself … 表目的，以及 excited / amused / embarrassed 这类 -ed 形容词。
Questions to keep in mind as you read读的时候带着这几个问题：
  1. Why is Sally so thrilled?萨莉为什么那么兴奋？
  2. What does the woman do on the train?那位女士在车上做了什么？
  3. What does Sally say that upsets her mother?萨莉说了什么让妈妈很难堪？

📖 Understanding the piece读懂这篇

Written for the course by L. G. Alexander; New Concept English, Book 1, Longman, 1967. It is an invented anecdote of a familiar kind, in which a small child says out loud what adults only think.

本课由编者 L. G. 亚历山大为教材自撰，见《新概念英语》第一册（朗文，1967）。这是一则虚构的趣事，属于常见的一类：小孩把大人心里想的话大声说了出来。

🌍 Where it comes from出处与背景

British trains of the 1960s often had compartments, small rooms with two facing benches, so strangers really did sit opposite each other with nothing to look at but the window and one another. Women of the time commonly carried a powder compact with a small mirror and touched up their face in public places. A large decorated hat was still part of dressing up for a trip to town.

1960 年代的英国火车多是包厢式的：一个小隔间里两排座椅面对面，陌生人真的就对坐着，除了窗外就只有彼此可看。那个年代的女性常随身带一个带小镜子的粉盒，在公共场合补妆。进城时戴一顶装饰繁复的大帽子，仍是正式打扮的一部分。

✨ How it is written赏析

The story is told by the mother, which is why the last line works: we see the joke through the eyes of the adult who has to sit there afterwards. The details are chosen for the punchline, a funny hat and a compact, so the reader is already half expecting a rude remark. Sally's silence at the first hello also prepares us: she is a child who looks hard and says exactly what she thinks.

故事由妈妈来讲，这正是最后一句能成立的原因：我们是透过那个事后还得坐在原位的大人的眼睛看这个笑话。细节都是为笑点挑的——一顶滑稽的帽子、一个粉盒——读者心里已经半猜到会有一句失礼的话。萨莉对第一声招呼不吭声，也是铺垫：她是那种盯着看、然后想什么说什么的孩子。

🧭 What it argues, and does it still hold?解读

The -ed and -ing pair is one of the most common errors for Chinese learners, who often say I am boring when they mean they feel bored, and the result can be quite embarrassing in itself. Parents in Canada will recognise the scene: young children often comment aloud on people's looks, and a calm reply such as We don't say that about people, it can hurt their feelings, followed by an apology to the stranger, is the usual way to handle it.

-ed 与 -ing 是中国学习者最常见的错误之一，常把「我觉得无聊」说成 I am boring，这个错本身就够尴尬的。在加拿大当父母的人会认得这一幕：小孩常常大声评论别人的长相，常见的处理方式是平静地说一句「我们不这样说别人，会让人难过」，然后向那位陌生人道歉。

💬 Discuss and write讨论与写作
  1. Tell a partner about an embarrassing moment caused by a child or a friend, using embarrassed and embarrassing.
    跟同伴讲一件因孩子或朋友引起的尴尬事，用上 embarrassed 和 embarrassing。
  2. Describe a stranger you once sat opposite on a bus or train, using was dressed in.
    描述一次你在公交或火车上对面坐着的陌生人，用上 was dressed in。

④ Grammar in this lesson本课语法

-ed and -ing adjectives-ed 与 -ing 形容词
-ed adjectives describe how a person feels: excited, amused, embarrassed, bored, tired. -ing adjectives describe what causes the feeling: exciting, amusing, embarrassing, boring, tiring. I'm boring means people find me dull; I'm bored means I feel dull.
-ed 形容词说人的感受（我觉得……）：excited、amused、embarrassed、bored、tired。-ing 形容词说事物给人的感觉（令人……）：exciting、amusing、embarrassing、boring、tiring。I'm boring 是「我这个人很无聊」，I'm bored 才是「我觉得无聊」。

Sentence 5 has excited, and sentence 17 amused and embarrassed.

本课：第 5 句 excited，第 17 句 amused 和 embarrassed。

  • The film was boring, so we were bored.
  • It was an embarrassing moment, and I felt embarrassed.
The past passive: was or were plus past participle被动语态：was / were + 过去分词
Use the passive when the person or thing affected is the subject: be plus the past participle, with was or were in the past. Add by to say who did it, or leave it out if it does not matter. be dressed in is a common passive phrase for describing clothes.
动作的承受者作主语时用被动：be + 过去分词。过去时用 was / were。要说是谁做的，加 by …；不重要或不清楚就不说。be dressed in 是常用的被动式说法：穿着……。

Sentence 3 has was invited, and sentence 10 was dressed in.

本课：第 3 句 was invited，第 10 句 was dressed in。

  • The house was built in 1912.
  • We were told to wait outside.

⑤ Patterns to reuse可以套用的句型

  • … was invited to a … party. ……被邀请去参加……聚会。
    My son was invited to a birthday party.
  • She had never … before. 她以前从没……过。
    She had never seen snow before.
  • She asked about everything she … 她见什么问什么。
    He asked about everything he heard.
  • He was dressed in … 他穿着……
    He was dressed in a grey suit.
  • The kids were amused, but I was … 孩子们觉得好笑，我却……
    The kids were amused, but I was shocked.

Our model summary本站范文

Sally, aged four, had a party to go to, so her mother took her by train. It was her first time on one, and she was thrilled. A middle-aged woman wearing blue and an odd hat sat facing her. Once they were moving, the woman took out a compact and began to powder her face. She said she wanted to look nice. Sally told her she still looked ugly. Sally laughed, but her mother went red.（76 词）

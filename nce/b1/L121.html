Lesson 121–122 · The Man in a Hat 戴帽子的人

📘 New Concept English · Book 1 · Lesson 121–122 “The Man in a Hat”新概念英语 第一册 Lesson 121–122《The Man in a Hat》

B1🧠 17 new words个新词📝 Full notes有完整讲解

🎯 Before you open the lesson开课前
A customer comes back to a bookshop. Thirty minutes earlier he paid for two costly dictionaries and then walked out without them. The manager asks which assistant dealt with him, and he points to Caroline at the counter. She has no memory of his face; what she remembers is a hat. So the manager asks him to put his hat on, and at once she knows him. This is a dialogue about identifying people and things. It teaches relative clauses with who, which and that, the polite request Would you ... please?, put on, and recognize.
一位男顾客回到书店，说自己半小时前在这里买了两本很贵的词典，走时忘了拿。店长问是谁接待的，他指了指柜台后面的女店员卡罗琳。可卡罗琳想不起来了：她记得当时那位客人戴着帽子。店长请顾客把帽子戴上，卡罗琳一看，马上认出来了。本课是一段辨认人和物的对话，学定语从句 who / which / that（指人、指物）、Would you … please? 礼貌请求、put on 与 recognize。

  2. Why did Caroline fail to know him at first?卡罗琳一开始为什么没认出他？

📖 Understanding the piece读懂这篇

Written for the course by L. G. Alexander; New Concept English, Book 1, Longman, 1967. It is a made-up shop scene built to practise relative clauses.

本课由编者 L. G. 亚历山大为教材自撰，见《新概念英语》第一册（朗文，1967），是为操练定语从句编的书店小场景。

🌍 Where it comes from出处与背景

In the 1960s British bookshops were staffed by assistants who stood behind a counter, and customers often asked for help rather than taking books from open shelves. A man's hat was still a normal part of going out in town, so it was a natural detail for someone to remember. Paying and then forgetting the parcel is an everyday accident; most shops kept such items behind the counter for the owner to collect.

1960 年代英国书店有店员站在柜台后面，顾客常常请店员帮忙，而不是自己从开放书架上拿。男人出门戴帽子仍很普遍，所以帽子是一个很自然会被记住的细节。付了钱却忘了拿东西是日常小事，多数店铺会把这类东西放在柜台后，等失主来取。

✨ How it is written赏析

The whole dialogue is a chain of questions that ask which one: which assistant, which books, which man. Each answer is a noun followed by a relative clause, so the grammar is not decoration but the actual job the speakers need to do. The joke at the end turns on the clause that describes the man by his hat: take the hat away and the description no longer fits him.

整段对话是一串「哪一个」的问题：哪个店员、哪些书、哪个人。每个回答都是「名词＋定语从句」，所以这个语法不是装饰，而是说话人正要完成的事。结尾的笑点就落在用帽子描述那个人的从句上：帽子一摘，这个描述就对不上他了。

🧭 What it argues, and does it still hold?解读

Chinese puts the description before the noun, while English puts it after (the lady who ...). This is why learners often build long phrases in front of the noun and get stuck. A useful habit is to say the noun first, then add who or which and the extra details. In a Canadian store, if you leave something behind, go back with the receipt; staff will usually check with whoever served you, much as Caroline does.

中文把修饰语放在名词前面（站在柜台后面的那位女士），英文放在后面（the lady who …）。所以学习者常在名词前堆一长串，说着说着就卡住。好习惯是先说名词，再接 who 或 which 补细节。在加拿大的商店落了东西，带上收据回去，店员一般会去问当时接待你的人，就像卡罗琳这样。

💬 Discuss and write讨论与写作
  1. Describe three people in your class or office using who: the person who ….
    用 who 描述你班上或办公室里的三个人：the person who …。
  2. Act out the scene with a partner, but change the hat to glasses or a scarf.
    和同伴演这一幕，把帽子换成眼镜或围巾。
  3. Write four sentences about objects on your desk with which or that, then say the same sentences again leaving the relative word out where you can.
    用 which 或 that 写四句话描述你桌上的东西，再把能省关系词的地方省掉，重说一遍。

④ Grammar in this lesson本课语法

Relative clauses with who, which and that定语从句：who / which / that
A relative clause follows a noun and says which one we mean. Use who or that for people and which or that for things. When the relative word is the object of the clause, it can be left out. When it is the subject, it must stay.
定语从句跟在名词后面，说明「是哪一个」。指人用 who（或 that），指物用 which（或 that）。关系词在从句里作宾语时可以省略：the man (who) I served。作主语时不能省：the lady who is standing …

Sentence 5 has who as subject, sentence 7 which as subject, sentence 11 who as object, and sentence 16 that as object.

本课：第 5 句 who 作主语，第 7 句 which 作主语，第 11 句 who 作宾语，第 16 句 that 作宾语。

  • The girl who sits next to me is from Korea.
  • I lost the umbrella (that) you gave me.
Polite requests with Would you ... please?Would you …, please? 礼貌请求
Would you plus the base verb plus please is more polite than Can you. It suits strangers and customers. Answer with Certainly, All right or Of course; to refuse, start with I'm sorry, but.
Would you + 动词原形 + please? 比 Can you …? 更客气，常用于对陌生人、顾客。回答：Certainly. / All right. / Of course. 拒绝时先说 I'm sorry, but …

In sentence 14 the manager asks politely, and in sentence 15 the customer agrees.

本课：第 14 句店长请顾客戴上帽子，第 15 句顾客答应。

  • Would you close the window, please?
  • Would you sign here, please? — Certainly.

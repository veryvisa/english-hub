Lesson 3–4 · Sorry, Sir 对不起，先生

📘 New Concept English · Book 1 · Lesson 3–4 “Sorry, Sir”新概念英语 第一册 Lesson 3–4《Sorry, Sir》

B1🧠 16 new words个新词📝 Full notes有完整讲解

🎯 Before you open the lesson开课前
A man at a cloakroom hands over his ticket and asks for his coat and umbrella. The attendant brings the things for number five, but the umbrella is not his. The attendant says sorry and offers another, which is wrong too; the right one comes on the next try. This lesson is about the most ordinary kind of small mistake and the polite way to deal with it: say what you want, point out what is wrong, apologise, and check again. The grammar is Here is or Here's for handing something over, the negative This is not, and the pair my and your.
一位男士在衣帽间取东西，递上存衣牌，要回自己的大衣和雨伞。服务员照五号牌拿来东西，男士一看：伞不是他的。服务员道歉，再拿一把问是不是，还不是；第三把才对上。本课是生活里最常见的小差错和它的礼貌处理：说清要什么、指出不对、道歉、再确认。学 Here is / Here's 递东西、否定句 This is not … 和 my/your 的对照。
Questions to keep in mind as you read读的时候带着这几个问题：
  1. What does the man use to get his things back?男士是凭什么拿回东西的？
  2. When the wrong umbrella appears, what does each side say?伞拿错了，双方各说了什么？
  3. How many umbrellas does the attendant offer before the right one?服务员一共拿了几次伞？

📖 Understanding the piece读懂这篇

Written for the course by L. G. Alexander; New Concept English, Book 1, Longman, 1967. The scene is invented to drill my, your and the negative of be.

本课由编者 L. G. 亚历山大为教材自撰，见《新概念英语》第一册（朗文，1967）。场景是虚构的，用来操练 my、your 和 be 的否定。

🌍 Where it comes from出处与背景

In the Britain of the 1960s a man going out in the evening would usually wear a coat and carry an umbrella, and theatres, restaurants and dance halls all had a cloakroom with an attendant who gave out numbered tickets. The scene would have been instantly familiar to the first readers. Cloakrooms still exist in theatres, museums and smart restaurants in Canada and Britain, and the system is the same: you hand over the coat, take a ticket, and get the coat back by number.

在 1960 年代的英国，晚上出门的男士通常穿大衣、带雨伞；剧院、餐馆和舞厅都有衣帽间，服务员发编了号的存衣牌。最初的读者一看就懂这个场景。今天加拿大和英国的剧院、博物馆、高档餐馆仍有衣帽间，办法也一样：交外衣、拿号牌、凭号取回。

✨ How it is written赏析

The comedy is gentle and built on repetition. The attendant gets it wrong twice, and each time the customer says the same calm thing. Nobody raises a voice and nobody is blamed; the problem is solved by checking and checking again. For a beginner this is ideal, because each wrong umbrella gives a fresh reason to say the same question and the same short negative. The lesson also hides a lesson in manners: the man never says you are wrong, only that this is not his.

笑点很温和，靠的是重复。服务员错了两次，客人每次都平静地说同一句话。没人提高嗓门，也没人被指责，问题靠一遍遍确认解决。这对初学者正合适：每拿错一次，就多一个理由把同一个问句、同一个否定简略回答再说一遍。课里还藏着一点礼貌：男士从不说「你错了」，只说「这不是我的」。

🧭 What it argues, and does it still hold?解读

The habit of stating the fact rather than accusing the person is worth copying. In many Chinese settings a customer might simply say wrong one or wave the thing away, which is efficient but can sound sharp in English. A plain This isn't mine, I'm afraid, followed by thanks when it is put right, keeps the exchange friendly. The attendant's side is just as useful: a quick Sorry and another try, with no excuses. In service jobs in Canada this is the expected pattern.

「只陈述事实、不指责人」这个习惯值得学。在很多中文场合，客人可能直接说「错了」或摆摆手，效率高，但换成英语就显得冲。说一句 This isn't mine, I'm afraid，改对了再道谢，交流就是友好的。服务员那边也同样有用：一句 Sorry，再试一次，不找借口。在加拿大的服务行业，这正是大家默认的做法。

③ How the hard sentences are built难句结构

Sentences are counted in the lesson text, title not included. Find the sentence in your book, then read our notes on its structure.句子按课文正文数（不含标题）。在教材里找到这一句，再看本站的结构讲解。

Sentence 4第 4 句 (begins开头 “My coat and …”)

  • A noun followed by please is the simplest polite request. please goes at the end, after a comma.名词 + please：最简单的礼貌请求，please 放句末，前面加逗号。
  • my is said twice, because each noun in English needs its own word in front.my 重复两次：英语里每个名词前都要带自己的限定词，不像中文说「我的大衣和雨伞」一个「我的」就够了。

Sentence 5第 5 句 (begins开头 “Here is my …”)

Here is my ticket.

  • Here is ... is what you say as you hand something over. It does not mean this place is.Here is …：递东西给对方时说的「给你……」，不是「这里是」。
  • Sentence 8 uses the short form Here's, which is commoner in speech, even before two things.下文第 8 句用缩写 Here's，口语更常见；后面跟两样东西时也常说 Here's。

Sentence 9第 9 句 (begins开头 “This is not …”)

This is not my umbrella.

  • To make a negative, put not after the verb be. In speech it is usually shortened: This isn't my umbrella.否定句：be 动词后加 not。口语常缩成 isn't：This isn't my umbrella.。
  • To point out someone's mistake, it is enough to state the fact; there is no need to say you are wrong.指出别人出错时，直接陈述事实就够了，不必说「你错了」。

Sentence 13第 13 句 (begins开头 “Is this it? …”)

Is this it?

  • it stands for the umbrella already talked about, so the short question saves repeating the noun.it 指前面说的那把伞：「是这把吗？」比 Is this your umbrella? 更省事。

④ Grammar in this lesson本课语法

The negative: This is not ... / This isn't ...否定句：This is not … / This isn't …
To make be negative, put not straight after is; in speech this becomes isn't. The short no answer to Is this ...? is No, it isn't.
be 动词的否定直接在 is 后加 not；口语缩写 isn't。回答 Is this …? 的否定简略回答是 No, it isn't.。

In sentence 9 the man uses a negative to say the umbrella is wrong, and in sentence 12 he gives the short no answer.

本课：第 9 句男士用否定句指出伞不对，第 12 句用否定的简略回答。

  • This is my pen. → This is not my pen. / This isn't my pen.
  • Is this your car? — No, it isn't.
Here is ... / Here's ...: handing something overHere is … / Here's …：递东西时说
When you hand something to someone, say Here is or Here's and name the thing. To say just here it is, use Here you are.
把东西递给别人时说 Here is + 物品（或 Here's）；只说「给你」用 Here you are.。

In sentence 5 the man hands over his ticket, and in sentence 8 the attendant hands back his things. Both use the same pattern.

本课：第 5 句客人递票，第 8 句服务员递东西，两人用的都是同一个句型。

  • Here is your change, madam.
  • Here's the menu. — Thank you.

⑤ Patterns to reuse可以套用的句型

  • … and …, please. 请给我……和……
    A tea and a sandwich, please.
  • Here is / Here's my … 给你，这是我的……
    Here's my passport.
  • This is not my … 这不是我的……
    This is not my bag; mine is green.
  • Sorry, sir/madam. Is this …? 对不起，先生／女士。是这个吗？
    Sorry, madam. Is this the right one?

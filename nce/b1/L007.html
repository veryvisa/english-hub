Lesson 7–8 · Are You a Teacher 你是教师吗？

📘 New Concept English · Book 1 · Lesson 7–8 “Are You a Teacher”新概念英语 第一册 Lesson 7–8《Are You a Teacher》

B1🧠 17 new words个新词📝 Full notes有完整讲解

🎯 Before you open the lesson开课前
Two new students, Robert and Sophie, get to know each other. He gives his name and she gives hers. He guesses she is from France and is right; she asks if he is from France as well, and he is not: he comes from Italy. Then each asks what the other does for a living. She works at a keyboard, typing, and he is an engineer. This lesson is the second step of a first conversation: after names come nationality and work. The grammar is the yes-no question Are you with the answers Yes, I am and No, I'm not; the wh- questions about nationality and job; and a or an before a job.
新学生罗伯特和索菲互相认识。他先报名字，她也报名字；他猜她是法国人，猜对了；她反问他是不是也是法国人，不是，他是意大利人。接着两人问对方做什么工作：她是键盘操作员（打字员），他是工程师。本课是初次交谈的第二步：介绍完名字，接着问国籍和工作。学 Are you …? 一般疑问句与 Yes, I am / No, I'm not、What nationality …? / What's your job? 特殊疑问句和 a/an 加职业。
Questions to keep in mind as you read读的时候带着这几个问题：
  1. Where is Robert from, and what does he do?罗伯特是哪国人？他做什么工作？
  2. Which questions do the two ask each other, and in what order?两人互问了哪几个问题？顺序是什么？
  3. Why do we say an engineer but a keyboard operator?为什么说 an engineer，而说 a keyboard operator？

🌍 Where it comes from出处与背景

The cast of the language school from Lesson 5 now talk to each other without a teacher in the middle. That is the real goal of these early lessons: after a few pages the learner can carry a first conversation alone. The jobs in this lesson and the drill lesson after it show the world of the book's time, with postmen, policemen, air hostesses and keyboard operators, and even the revision of the book could not keep up with how fast some of those jobs have changed.

第 5 课语言学校里的人物，这一课不用老师居中，自己聊起来了。这正是前几课的真正目标：学过几页，读者就能独立撑起一次初次交谈。本课和后面那课操练里的职业反映的是成书年代的世界：邮递员、警察、空中小姐、打字员。连新版修订也赶不上这些职业变化的速度。

✨ How it is written赏析

The dialogue is a set of mirrors. He gives his name, she gives hers; he asks a question, she asks one back; each one asks about the job. Every question is answered at once and then turned round, which is how polite small talk works in English: you do not only answer, you return the question. The one surprise is that Sophie guesses wrong about Robert, and the wrong guess is what brings out the new question about nationality.

这段对话像一组镜子：他报名字，她也报；他问一句，她反问一句；两人都问对方的工作。每个问题都马上得到回答，然后被抛回去——这正是英语里礼貌闲聊的方式：不能只回答，还要把问题还给对方。唯一的意外是索菲猜错了罗伯特的国籍，正是这一猜错，引出了问国籍的新句子。

🧭 What it argues, and does it still hold?解读

Two updates are worth knowing. Job names in English have become neutral: police officer, flight attendant and mail carrier are now usual, and a keyboard operator would today be called a data entry clerk, if the job exists at all. And in Canada a blunt What's your job? is less common than What do you do? It is a friendly question, but some people do not like to be judged by their work, so it is better asked after a little small talk. Answering with only a job title is also a little flat; adding where or what you work on makes it easier for the other person to reply.

有两点更新值得知道。英语里的职业名称已经趋向中性：police officer、flight attendant、mail carrier 现在更常见；键盘操作员今天大概叫 data entry clerk——如果这个岗位还在的话。另外在加拿大，直接问 What's your job? 不如 What do you do? 常用。这是个友好的问题，但有人不喜欢被工作定义，最好先寒暄几句再问。回答时只报一个职业名也有点干，加上在哪儿做、做哪方面，对方更好接话。

③ How the hard sentences are built难句结构

Sentences are counted in the lesson text, title not included. Find the sentence in your book, then read our notes on its structure.句子按课文正文数（不含标题）。在教材里找到这一句，再看本站的结构讲解。

Sentence 4第 4 句 (begins开头 “I am a …”)

I am a new student.

  • am is the form of be after I, and in speech it becomes I'm.I 后面用 am；口语缩写 I'm。
  • A singular countable noun needs a in front of it. I am new student is not English.单数可数名词前要有 a：a new student，不能说 I am new student。

Sentence 12第 12 句 (begins开头 “What nationality are …”)

What nationality are you?

  • A wh- question puts the question word, here what with a noun, at the front, and then uses question order: are you.特殊疑问句：疑问词 What + 名词放句首，后面接一般疑问句的语序 are you。
  • The answer uses the nationality adjective: I'm Italian, not I'm Italy.回答用国籍形容词：I'm Italian.，不说 I'm Italy.。

Sentence 9第 9 句 (begins开头 “Yes I am. …”)

Yes, I am.

  • In answer to Are you ...?, the subject becomes I: Yes, I am or No, I'm not.回答 Are you …? 时主语换成 I：Yes, I am. / No, I'm not.。
  • The short yes answer cannot be shortened, so Yes, I'm is wrong.肯定简略回答不缩写：不说 Yes, I'm.。

Sentence 19第 19 句 (begins开头 “I'm an engineer. …”)

I'm an engineer.

  • engineer starts with a vowel sound, so it takes an; keyboard starts with a consonant sound, so it takes a. What counts is the sound, not the letter.engineer 以元音音素开头，所以用 an；keyboard 以辅音开头，用 a。看的是读音，不是字母。
  • A job always takes a or an in English: I'm an engineer, never I'm engineer.说职业一定带 a/an：I'm an engineer.，不说 I'm engineer.。

④ Grammar in this lesson本课语法

Are you ...? and its short answersAre you …? 与简略回答
From the statement You are French, move are to the front to ask Are you French? In the answer the subject becomes I: Yes, I am, or No, I'm not.
陈述句 You are French. 把 are 提前成问句 Are you French?；答句主语换成 I：Yes, I am. / No, I'm not.。

Sentences 8 and 10 are two Are you questions, and sentences 9 and 11 give the yes and the no answer.

本课：第 8 句与第 10 句是两个 Are you …? 问句，第 9 句与第 11 句分别是肯定、否定的简略回答。

  • Are you a nurse? — Yes, I am.
  • Are you Canadian? — No, I'm not. I'm American.
a or an before a job职业名词前的 a / an
To give someone's job, use be with a or an and the job word. Before a consonant sound use a, as in a teacher; before a vowel sound use an, as in an engineer.
说某人的职业：be + a/an + 职业名词。辅音音素开头用 a（a teacher），元音音素开头用 an（an engineer）。

Sentence 17 uses a, and sentence 19 uses an.

本课：第 17 句用 a，第 19 句用 an。

  • She's a hairdresser.
  • He's an air steward. / I'm an office worker.

⑤ Patterns to reuse可以套用的句型

  • My name's … 我叫……
    My name's Kevin. I'm from Calgary.
  • Are you … too? 你也是……吗？
    Are you a nurse too?
  • What nationality are you? — I'm … 你是哪国人？——我是……人。
    What nationality is she? — She's Brazilian.
  • What's your job? — I'm a/an … 你做什么工作？——我是……
    What's her job? — She's an architect.

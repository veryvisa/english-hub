Lesson 39–40 · Don't Drop It 别摔了！

📘 New Concept English · Book 1 · Lesson 39–40 “Don't Drop It”新概念英语 第一册 Lesson 39–40《Don't Drop It》

🎯 Before you open the lesson开课前
Sam asks Penny what she plans to do with the vase. She says it is going on the table; Sam tells her not to, takes it from her, and wants it by the window. Penny cries out to him to be careful and not let it fall, then says no, not there either: it belongs on the shelf. At last it is in place, a fine vase with fine flowers in it. This lesson is two people pulling back and forth over a small thing. The grammar is be going to with do with to ask about plans, the negative imperative Don't ..., give it to me, place phrases such as in front of and on this shelf, and the spoken phrase There we are!
萨姆问彭妮打算把那只花瓶怎么办。她说要放在桌上，萨姆说别放那儿，让她把花瓶给他；他想放在窗前。彭妮一边喊「小心，别摔了」，一边又说别放那儿，要放在这个架子上。终于放好了——很漂亮的花瓶，花也很好看。本课是两个人为一件小事来回拉扯，学 be going to 加 do with 问打算、否定祈使句 Don't …、give it to me、in front of 和 on the shelf 这类方位短语，以及 There we are! 这句口语。

Written for the course by L. G. Alexander; New Concept English, Book 1, Longman, 1967.

🌍 Where it comes from出处与背景

Sam and Penny are the couple whose daily life fills much of the middle of Book 1, and this is one of their small domestic scenes. It follows the lesson on be going to, so it reuses that form in its very first line and then adds the negative imperative, the natural next step once a learner can already give orders with plain verbs. The drill lesson that follows has the learner tell someone where to put many objects, which is why the text moves the vase three times.

萨姆和彭妮这对夫妻的日常生活占了第一册中段的很大篇幅，这是他们家里的一个小场景。它紧跟在讲 be going to 的那课后面，所以第一句就重用了这个结构，接着加入否定祈使句——读者已经会用动词原形下指令了，下一步自然是学「别做」。后面那课操练让读者告诉别人把很多东西放到哪里，这也是为什么课文要把花瓶挪三次。

✨ How it is written赏析

Very little happens, and that is the point. The vase moves from table to window to shelf, and each move comes with a new place phrase, so the grammar is carried by a small domestic tug of war. The tension rises in the middle, where three short commands come almost together, and then drops completely with There we are, a phrase that means the fuss is over. The last two lines are pure relief: both people stop arguing and simply admire the result. Anyone who has rearranged a room with a partner will recognise the rhythm.

几乎没发生什么事，而这正是要点。花瓶从桌子挪到窗前、再挪到架子上，每挪一次就带出一个新的方位短语，语法就靠这场家里的小小拉锯来承载。张力在中段升高，三句短促的命令几乎挤在一起；然后 There we are 一句彻底放松下来，意思是「好了，折腾完了」。最后两句纯粹是松了口气：两个人都不争了，只是欣赏结果。和伴侣一起重新摆过房间的人，都认得这个节奏。

🧭 What it argues, and does it still hold?解读

Everything here is still natural English, though the tone deserves a note. Between partners at home, Don't do that and Give it here are normal. Said to a colleague or a stranger in Canada they would sound rude; you would hear Could you pass it to me? or Maybe not there? instead. Chinese speakers sometimes translate a direct Chinese instruction word for word and come across as harsher than they mean. The safe rule is that bare imperatives are for family, close friends and emergencies, and Be careful is fine with anyone.

这里的英语今天仍然自然，不过语气值得说一句。在家里伴侣之间，Don't do that 和 Give it here 很正常；但在加拿大对同事或陌生人这样说，就显得粗鲁，你会听到 Could you pass it to me? 或 Maybe not there? 这类说法。中国读者有时把中文里直接的指令逐字翻成英语，听起来比本意更生硬。稳妥的原则是：光秃秃的祈使句留给家人、好友和紧急情况；Be careful 对谁说都可以。

③ How the hard sentences are built难句结构

Sentences are counted in the lesson text, title not included. Find the sentence in your book, then read our notes on its structure.句子按课文正文数（不含标题）。在教材里找到这一句，再看本站的结构讲解。

Sentence 4第 4 句 (begins开头 “What are you …”)

  • do with something asks what will happen to it or where it will go; be going to asks about the plan.do with sth：拿某物怎么办、放哪儿。be going to 问打算。

Sentence 6第 6 句 (begins开头 “Don't do that. …”)

  • A negative imperative is Don't with the plain verb. It is direct and is common between people who know each other well.否定祈使句：Don't + 动词原形。语气直接，熟人之间常用。

Sentence 9第 9 句 (begins开头 “I'm going to …”)

  • First here, then in front of plus a place to say exactly where.先说 here，再用 in front of 加地点补充具体位置。
  • in front of means outside and ahead of; in the front of means in the front part inside.in front of：在……前面（外部）；in the front of：在……里面的前部。

Sentence 10第 10 句 (begins开头 “Be careful! Don't …”)

Be careful! Don't drop it!

  • A positive imperative with Be and an adjective, then a negative one that names the actual worry.肯定祈使句用 Be + 形容词；紧接着的否定祈使句说出具体的担心。
  • drop takes an object; if the vase came down by itself you would use fall.drop 要带宾语：drop it；花瓶自己掉下用 fall。

Sentence 13第 13 句 (begins开头 “There we are! …”)

There we are!

  • A spoken phrase when something is in place or done at last. There you are and There! are also used.东西放好、事情办成时说的口语：「好了！」也可说 There you are 或 There!

④ Grammar in this lesson本课语法

The negative imperative: Don't with the plain verb否定祈使句：Don't + 动词原形
To tell someone not to do something, use Don't with the plain verb. It is the same for everyone and has no subject. The negative of Be careful is Don't be careless.
不让别人做某事，用 Don't + 动词原形；对所有人都一样，不加主语。Be careful 的否定是 Don't be careless。

Sentences 6, 10 and 11 each have one Don't: once from Sam and twice from Penny.

本课：第 6、10、11 句各有一个 Don't，萨姆说一次，彭妮说两次。

  • Don't touch the stove. It's hot.
  • Don't be late for the meeting.
Putting something somewhere: put with a thing and a place把东西放到某处：put + 物 + 地点
put takes an object and then a place: here, there, on the table, in front of the window. When the object is a pronoun it goes straight after put: put it here.
put + 宾语 + 地点状语（here、there、on the table、in front of the window）。宾语是代词时放在 put 后：put it here。

Sentences 5, 9 and 12 each give a different place for the vase.

本课：第 5、9、12 句三次说要放哪儿，每次换一个位置。

  • Put it on the shelf, please.
  • Don't put your feet on the chair.

⑤ Patterns to reuse可以套用的句型

  • What will you do with …? 你打算拿……怎么办？
    What will you do with all those old clothes?
  • Don't do that — give it here. 别那样，给我吧。
    Don't do that — let me try.
  • I'll leave it …, just in front of the …. 我要把它放在……，就在……前面。
    I'll leave it here, just in front of the door.
  • Be careful! Don't …! 小心！别……！
    Be careful! Don't spill the tea!
  • It's a lovely …. Those … are lovely, too. 这……真漂亮，那些……也很漂亮。
    It's a lovely house. The trees are lovely, too.

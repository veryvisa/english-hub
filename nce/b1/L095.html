Lesson 95–96 · Tickets, Please 请把票拿出来

📘 New Concept English · Book 1 · Lesson 95–96 “Tickets, Please”新概念英语 第一册 Lesson 95–96《Tickets, Please》

B1🧠 17 new words个新词📝 Full notes有完整讲解

🎯 Before you open the lesson开课前
George and Ken buy two return tickets to London and ask when the next train goes: nineteen past eight, from platform two, across the bridge. The clock says they have more than twenty minutes, so they go next door for a drink. When they come back, the ticket collector tells them the train has already gone. Their clock showed a quarter past, but it was running ten minutes behind. The next train is five hours away. This lesson teaches telling the time in two ways, will for timetables, plenty of time, had better, just missed, and in five hours' time.
乔治和肯在车站买了两张去伦敦的往返票，问下一班车几点开：八点十九分，二站台，过天桥。看看钟才七点五十七，时间还多，两人就去车站隔壁的酒吧喝一杯。回到站台，检票员说车刚开走——明明才八点十五，原来那只钟慢了十分钟。下一班要等五个小时。本课学问时间与报时（nineteen minutes past eight = eight nineteen）、will 问班次、We've got plenty of time、had better、just missed 与 in five hours' time。
Questions to keep in mind as you read读的时候带着这几个问题：
  1. What time does the next train go, and from which platform?下一班车几点开？在几号站台？
  2. Why did they go to the bar?他们为什么去了酒吧？
  3. Why did they miss the train?他们为什么没赶上火车？

📖 Understanding the piece读懂这篇

Written for the course by L. G. Alexander; New Concept English, Book 1, Longman, 1967.

本课由编者 L. G. 亚历山大为教材自撰，见《新概念英语》第一册（朗文，1967）。

🌍 Where it comes from出处与背景

British railway stations in the 1960s usually had a large clock over the platforms, and travellers trusted it more than their own watches. Most tickets were bought at a booking office window, and a ticket collector checked them at the barrier before passengers could reach the platform, which is why the man at the gate is the one who breaks the news. Return tickets were cheaper than two singles, so day trippers always asked for a return. On branch lines with few trains, a gap of several hours between services was quite possible. Today the departure times appear on electronic boards and phones, though a station clock is still a familiar sight.

1960 年代英国火车站的站台上方通常挂着一只大钟，旅客信它胜过信自己的手表。车票大多在售票窗口买，检票员在闸口查票，旅客过了闸才能上站台，所以向他们报坏消息的是闸口的人。往返票比两张单程票便宜，当天往返的人总是买往返票。在车次少的支线上，两班车之间隔好几个小时是很可能的。今天发车时间显示在电子屏和手机上，不过车站大钟仍是熟悉的景象。

✨ How it is written赏析

The lesson is built so that the reader trusts the clock as much as the two men do. The time is given twice, first in full and then as numbers, and the clock reading comes with the comfortable word only. Then there is a gap: the bar scene is not shown, and the next line jumps straight to the decision to go back. The reversal arrives through the ticket collector, and the cause is held back for one more line, so the reader learns the bad news before the reason, just as the men do. The last answer, five hours, is the punch line, and it needs no comment.

课文的设计让读者和两个人一样相信那只钟。时间先完整说一遍，再用数字说一遍，钟上的读数还带着一个令人安心的「才」。接着留了一个空：酒吧那一段没写，下一句直接跳到决定回去。反转由检票员带来，原因又往后压了一句，于是读者和两个人一样，先听到坏消息，再知道为什么。最后那个答案「五小时」就是包袱，无需任何评论。

🧭 What it argues, and does it still hold?解读

The moral still holds, though the clock has moved into our pockets: phones keep perfect time, but people still miss trains by trusting a wrong timetable or an old screenshot. In Metro Vancouver, the SkyTrain runs every few minutes, but West Coast Express commuter trains and BC Ferries sailings can leave hours apart, so missing one has a real cost, much like in the lesson. For learners, the two ways of telling the time both matter: announcements and apps use eight nineteen, while friends in conversation often say twenty past eight. Chinese learners often say five past to mean the same as eight five; the spoken form needs oh, as in eight oh five.

道理今天依然成立，只是钟从车站搬进了口袋：手机时间准确无误，人们却仍会因为信了错误的时刻表或一张过期截图而误车。在大温哥华，架空列车几分钟一班，但西海岸快线通勤火车和 BC 渡轮的班次可能相隔几个小时，误一班的代价和课文里差不多。对学习者来说，两种报时法都要会：广播和应用程序说 eight nineteen，朋友聊天常说 twenty past eight。中国学习者读 8:05 时常漏掉 oh，口语要说 eight oh five。

④ Grammar in this lesson本课语法

Two ways to tell the time报时的两种说法
The first way says the minutes first: past for up to half an hour after the hour, and to for the minutes before the next hour. The second way just reads the numbers, as stations and timetables usually do.
① 先说分钟：分钟 ≤30 用 past（ten past eight 8:10、half past eight 8:30），分钟 >30 用 to（twenty to nine 8:40、a quarter to nine 8:45）。② 直接读数字：eight ten、eight forty。车站、广播、时刻表常用第二种。

Sentences 5 and 11 give the same time, first in the long form and then as plain numbers.

本课：第 5 句用第一种，第 10 句用第二种，指的是同一个时刻。

  • It's a quarter past six. = It's six fifteen.
  • The bus leaves at ten to nine. = eight fifty.
had better and have just donehad better 与 have just done
had better plus the base verb, with had better not for the negative, is strong advice about now. have just plus the past participle reports something that happened a moment ago and still matters.
had better + 动词原形（否定 had better not do）：现在最好这样做，否则会有麻烦，比 should 语气强。have/has just + 过去分词：刚刚做完，结果就在眼前。

Sentence 15 has had better, and sentence 18 has the present perfect with just.

本课：第 15 句用 had better，第 18 句用 have just done。

  • You'd better not drive in this snow.
  • The plane has just landed.

George and Ken bought return tickets to London. Their train was due at 8.19, and the station clock said 7.57, so they went for a drink next door. When they got back to the platform, the train had already left. The clock was ten minutes behind, and the next train was five hours later.（54 词）
